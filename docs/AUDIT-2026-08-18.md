# Wolfgang audit — why the output isn't human-quality

Date: 2026-08-18. Method: read the note path end to end (brief → shorthand →
LayerIR → MusicXML/MIDI), ran the real tools on real workspace pieces, and
measured the corpus the agent learns from. Every claim below was checked against
running code or data, not inferred from docs.

## The five root causes

1. **The notation layer can't represent the music.** No triplets, 32nd notes
   silently collide, ties bind the wrong notes, no meter changes, no anacrusis.
   The expressive vocabulary of the whole system is narrower than a beginner
   piano method book. (§A)
2. **The corpus the agent studies is corrupted.** Per-bar key detection is noise,
   so every "interval_from_root" transposition and every Roman numeral is wrong
   at a high rate; 11–51% of left-hand exemplars overflow their own bar; texture
   labels are ~81% one value; 94% of bars are labelled "middle". The agent is
   handed wrong notes and told they are real Beethoven. (§B)
3. **The plan is a grid, not a form.** Every phrase is 8 bars, a cadence lands in
   every 8th bar, a new chord is forced every bar, and the accompaniment texture
   is instructed to change *every bar* by round-robin. Sonata form has no
   development and no recapitulation. (§C)
4. **Nothing measures quality.** `_section_gate` returns `passed: True`
   unconditionally, every `musical_ear` finding is severity `warn` so
   `error_count` is always 0, and `validate_meter` is dead for every phrase after
   the first. Convergence reduces to one LLM grading itself. (§D)
5. **The guidance contradicts itself.** The brief says "never compose to hit a
   number" and then "hit ALL dimensions"; the craft doc says Beethoven changes
   texture 58% of bars while the brief says 26%; §2 says the gate blocks density
   while §9 says nothing blocks. Contradictory instructions produce hedged,
   average output. (§E)

## §B The corpus is corrupted — the agent studies wrong notes (VERIFIED BY MEASUREMENT)

| # | Issue | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | `analyze_score_bars` calls `measure.analyze("key")` **per bar**. Single-bar Krumhansl key-finding is noise. Measured: Chopin's bars are labelled C# 411/4000 times — Chopin did not write 10% of his music in C#. `tonic_pc` comes from this, and `tonic_pc` drives every `interval_from_root`. | critical |
| B2 | Because of B1, exemplars "transposed to Am" are transposed by a *wrong interval* at a high rate. The exemplar bars in every brief are harmonically wrong relative to the phrase's key. | critical |
| B3 | Because of B1, `roman`/`function` per bar are computed against a wrong local key, so the corpus Roman-numeral progression model is trained on noise. This is why a brief for an **A minor** phrase suggests `i │ I │ III │ I │ ii │ iv │ III │ V` — A major and A minor triads alternating, with a major `ii` in minor. | critical |
| B4 | The left hand is flattened with `lh_measure.recurse().notesAndRests` — **no voice splitting** (only the RH gets `_staff_voices`). Any two-voice LH interleaves into one sequential stream. Measured LH shorthand that overflows its own bar: Liszt 51%, Chopin 18.9%, Beethoven 15.5%, Mozart 11.5%, Handel 13%. | critical |
| B5 | `_voice_records(cap=16)` truncates dense bars. Measured: 12.9% of Mozart bars and 6.8% of Beethoven bars hit the cap, so the agent is shown a half-bar and told it is a bar. | high |
| B6 | `rh_texture` classification: the `<=8` branch is `"singing_melody" if any(hasattr(n,"pitch") ...) else "chordal"`, and every `Note` has `.pitch`, so `chordal` is essentially unreachable. Measured: 81% of Chopin bars are labelled `singing_melody`. Texture-scoped retrieval and per-texture density stats are built on a label with almost no information. | critical |
| B7 | Texture labels are pure note-*count* thresholds with no reference to duration or meter, so a 3/8 bar and a 4/4 bar with the same count get the same label, and `alberti` vs `walking_bass` differ only by count. | high |
| B8 | `phrase_position` is derived from position in the whole movement (`mi < 2` → opening, last 4 → cadential). Measured: 94% of bars are `middle`; only ~80 of 4000 Chopin bars are `cadential`. Cadence-scoped retrieval therefore has almost no material, and `musical_ear.detect_no_breathing` — which keys off `phrase_position` — only ever inspects the last 4 bars of a piece. | critical |
| B9 | `parts[0]` is assumed to be the right hand for `rh_display`, `rh_events`, and both densities. The melody part is detected separately *only* for `melody_line`. For bass-first MIDI (Liszt et al.) the "RH exemplar melody" in the brief is the left hand. | critical |
| B10 | RH and LH measures are paired by list index `mi`, which misaligns on pickup bars, repeats, or unequal measure counts. | high |
| B11 | MIDI-sourced corpora carry no ornaments: `has_grace_notes` is structurally near-zero, so ornament targets in briefs are ~0 and the agent is effectively told real Chopin doesn't ornament. | high |
| B12 | `_bar_harmony` takes the single longest sonority per bar as "the" chord — one chord per bar by construction. There is no harmonic-rhythm model anywhere in the system. | high |
| B13 | Four "armed" composers have `roman` coverage of 0% (schubert 82 bars, handel 54, corelli 19, weber 241) and corelli/handel/schubert have too few bars to support any retrieval. They are nonetheless offered as reference composers. | medium |
| B14 | Exemplar sets contain exact duplicates. Verified in a live brief: `[E7]` and `[E8]` are byte-identical bars (sonata02-2 b17 and b48). No dedup. | medium |
| B15 | Exemplars are silently reshaped to the target meter, so a 4/4 Beethoven bar arrives as 3 beats — the idiom being taught is a mutilated one. | high |
| B16 | `LH VOCABULARY` patterns are transposed but not fitted to the meter: `alberti: A2e A3e A2e A3e A2e A3e A2e A3e` is 4 beats, handed to a 3/4 phrase. | high |
| B17 | `LH VOCABULARY` ignores register: `bass_melody: B3s F#4s B4s D5s F#4e` sits above middle C and is offered as a left-hand bass figure in A minor (with an unexplained F#). | high |
| B18 | `PHRASE SHAPE (corpus K.279/i …)` appeared in a **Beethoven** brief — `_phrase_shape` retrieves from the phrase bank without scoping to the composer. Cross-composer leakage. | high |
| B19 | The density arc in `PHRASE SHAPE` has 7 values for an 8-bar phrase. | low |
| B20 | `corpus_metrics.texture_change_pct` — described as the single most important human-vs-AI discriminator — is computed over the B6/B7 texture labels, i.e. over noise. Every z-score and band derived from it is meaningless. | critical |
| B21 | `_DENSITY_DELTA = 4` (a raw note-count shift) defines "texture change" — meter- and tempo-blind. | medium |
| B22 | `melody_density` counts a chord as one event, so block-chordal and single-line bars are indistinguishable in every density statistic. | high |
| B23 | Brief `TARGET STATS` surfaces `LH unclassified: median 4.0` as guidance — a junk bucket presented as an idiom. | medium |
| B24 | `STYLE TARGETS` computes mean ± σ on bounded quantities and prints impossible bands: `triplet_ratio=-0.04–0.34`, `density_cv=-0.3–1.67`, `seventh_chord_ratio=-0.01–0.05`. | medium |
| B25 | `STYLE TARGETS` applies whole-movement metrics to an 8-bar phrase: `melodic_range=34.06–51.98` semitones (≈4 octaves) is unreachable in 8 bars and is presented as a target to hit. | high |
| B26 | `self_evaluate` builds composer bands as `mean ± 2σ` with `lo` clamped to 0 but `hi` unclamped, so ratio bands routinely exceed 1.0 and can never fail. | medium |
| B27 | `self_evaluate`'s hint text for `texture_change_pct` says "real corpus changes texture between ~40-70%" while the band it just applied is Beethoven's own 0.11–0.41. The hint contradicts the test. | medium |
| B28 | `STYLE DOCTRINE (this phrase)` slices are not phrase-relevant: a lyrical bagatelle's contrasting theme was given "Grand pause after dominant chord", "Tritone relationship — maximum tonal distance", "Semitone key juxtaposition". | medium |
| B29 | The brief is ~4,000 tokens, of which the load-bearing parts (feeling, theme, chord frame, 2–3 good exemplars) are maybe 600. Eight LH texture stat lines plus eight self-continuation lines plus 14 exemplars dilute attention. | high |
| B30 | `composer_coverage_tier` / `available_corpus_composers` do not consider record *validity* (LH overflow, roman coverage), only presence. | medium |

## §C The plan is a grid, not a form

| # | Issue | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Every phrase in every form is 8 bars.** `_build_ternary` = 8+8│8+8│8+8, `_build_theme_variations` = 8+8 per variation, `_build_simple` = 8+8, sonata = 8+8+8+8+8+4. No 4-, 5-, 6-, 10-, or 12-bar phrases; no extension, elision, or codetta. Hyper-regular phrase rhythm is by itself a machine tell. | critical |

| C22 | Tempo is one value per slot; no accelerando/ritardando planning, no tempo relation between movements. | medium |

So `error_count` is always 0, and `converged(error_count=0, section_gate_passed=True, ...)` reduces to `critic_approved and critic_quality >= 4`. | D7 | `detect_unresolved_nct` fires only for notes that are chromatic **and** leapt to **and** leapt from. The common failure — a diatonic note that is simply wrong against the chord — is undetectable. It deliberately avoids the roman data (because of B1/B3). | high |

| D12 | The ear listens to the **assembled** score, which has already been quantized and truncated (§A), then reports bar numbers the agent fixes in the un-distorted source. Signal and cause are misaligned. | high |
| D13 | `commit_gate._check_contour` tells the agent the corpus norm for `direction_changes_per_bar` is **1.0–2.0**; the brief's STYLE TARGETS for the same metric says Beethoven's mean is **0.41** (band 0.28–0.53). Two modules disagree by 3–4×. | high |
| D14 | `_check_density` derives its per-bar floors from the round-robin `texture_plan` (C4), so the density floor changes arbitrarily bar to bar. | high |
| D15 | `_check_expression_zero` is disabled for every MIDI-derived composer, since `avg_ornament_density` is 0 (B11). | medium |
| D16 | `validate_voice_leading` aligns only exact `(bar, beat)` matches between `principal_line` and `bass_foundation`. With a one-note-per-bar bass it has ~1 comparison per bar, so parallel-motion detection is effectively blind — and it only warns. | high |
| D17 | `validate_meter` requires an *exact* sum and has no underfull warning path that survives A8; a bar with 2 of 3 beats passes. | medium |
| D18 | `converged` still defaults `quality_threshold=4.0` although the project notes say quality-threshold gating was dropped. | low |
| D19 | `self_evaluate`'s `_PCT_METRICS` "value > 1.5 → divide by 100" is a guess about units rather than a contract. | low |

## Fix order (impact ÷ effort)

1. **§A notation** — triplets/tuplets, quantization grid, tie/slur binding, dead
   `validate_meter`, per-phrase meter, key/TS dedup, StaffGroup, meter/key
   defaults on LayerIR commit. Without this the system cannot notate the music
   it is being asked to write.
2. **§B corpus** — score-level (windowed) key for `tonic_pc`/roman, LH voice
   splitting, raise/remove the 16-event cap, melody-part detection for both

## §E Guidance — the contradictions are gone

- The craft doc no longer claims the gate blocks on density (§9 already said it doesn't), no longer states one composer's LH statistics as universal, and no longer quotes a texture-change rate that contradicts the brief's own measured band. (E5, E6, E9)
- The brief no longer says "never compose to hit a number" and then "hit ALL dimensions". (E7)

- A single-stream left hand is one voice, so the bass line is the bass line. (E2, E3)

**Scope of the bass fix — stated precisely.** Every generated piece had a
`bass_foundation` of exactly one note per bar while the rest of the left hand was
filed as "arpeggiated fill" in a second voice. Verified on
`bagatelle-am-20260621`: 8 notes for 8 bars.

What that broke, and what it did **not**:
- **Broken (verified)**: every per-layer statistic; `validate_voice_leading`,
  which compares `principal_line` against `bass_foundation` and was therefore
  checking the melody against a one-note-per-bar line; the note roles, which were
  a constant; and the engraved staff voicing, which split one line into two voices.
- **Not broken**: the pitches themselves. Both layers export to the bass staff, so
  the *sound* was unchanged. An earlier draft of this audit claimed the fix was
  "the single largest change to what the output sounds like" — that was an
  overclaim, corrected above at E3. It is a large change to what the system can
  correctly *measure and notate*, which is what the rest of the pipeline reasons from.

# §F — Second pass, 2026-08-25: what a critical review of the first pass found

The first pass verified its fixes structurally and reported them as done. Reviewing
that work adversarially found four more defects in the shipped system and four
in the fixes themselves. All are now corrected; the point of listing them is that
the *class* of error keeps recurring.

## Pre-existing, and worse than anything in §A-§E

| # | Issue | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **`texture_density_stats` cached its output to `compiled_packs/<c>/density_stats.json` with no provenance and no invalidation.** Rebuilding the entire corpus did not update the density targets in a single brief. The cache in the repo had been written under an older texture vocabulary, so it was serving targets for textures the corpus no longer produces at all (`dialogue_chords`, `passage_work`, `ornamental_cascade`, `stammer_repeat`) alongside numbers computed from a different set of bars. The commit gate's density floors read the same file. Fixed: the cache carries a schema version and the source bar count, and recomputes when either moves. | critical |
| F2 | **`self_evaluate` judged a count against a band derived from a fraction.** `style_analyzer.direction_changes_per_bar` is a count of contour reversals per bar (1-3 for real music); `corpus_metrics.direction_changes_per_bar` was a fraction of bars whose direction label changes (mean 0.56). ## §G — Found by composing a piece and being told it was boring (2026-08-25)

Writing an actual Mozart-style Andante, being told it was dull, and then reading
K.279/ii bar by bar surfaced a further class of defect: the system was **recording
and rewarding the wrong things about the music**.

| # | Issue | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Every generic discriminator band was a hand-written guess, and every one rejected real music.** Measured against 36 real Mozart/Beethoven/Chopin movements: `texture_change_pct` (0.35, 0.70) rejected **20 of 24**; `rest_ratio` rejected 54%; `direction_changes_per_bar` (1.0, 2.0) rejected **half of all real movements**, including 5 of 12 Mozart sonata movements. The report the critic reads was flagging idiomatic writing as defective across the board, pushing every revision toward the non-idiomatic. All bands recalibrated to the measured 5th-95th percentile, and `test_discriminator_bands_do_not_reject_real_music` now fails the build if any band rejects more than a quarter of real movements. | critical |
| G2 | **Harmony was recorded once per bar; two thirds of Mozart's bars carry more than one.** The corpus stored a single Roman numeral per bar, so the harmonic rhythm of most of the repertoire was discarded — which is exactly why every generated piece changes chord on a metronomic one-per-bar pulse. Bars now carry `harmony_events`: where the harmony moves *inside* the bar. | critical |
| G3 | **Harmony was read from the wrong thing.** The bar's chord was taken as its longest single vertical. In any real texture the chord is stated one note at a time — an Alberti bass or a triplet figure makes every instant a bare dyad, and music21 resolved the missing third to a guess. Bar 1 of an F major movement was reported as a MINOR tonic. Harmony is now read from what a span SPELLS: every pitch sounding anywhere in the beat. K.279/ii's opening went from `v, #ivo, ii6, IV6` to `I, V65, ii6, IV6`. | critical |
| G4 | **The key detector read only the melody staff.** `_windowed_keys` took its pitch evidence from `parts[0]`, discarding the bass — the single strongest evidence for both key and mode. | high |
| G5 | **A local key window had no home-key prior**, so it flipped on any passage that dwelt on the subdominant: bar 1 of K.279/ii, an F major movement, was read as B-flat. The movement's own key now acts as a prior each window must overcome. | high |
| G6 | **My own texture-plan fix overcorrected.** §C4 replaced a round-robin (a new idiom every bar, arbitrarily) with *hold one idiom per phrase* — and Mozart changes left-hand texture on **62%** of bar transitions. One wrong answer swapped for its opposite. The plan now takes its rate from the composer's own `lh_texture_change_pct` and its successor from the corpus transition matrix. | high |
| G7 | New `flat_harmonic_rhythm` detector: a section that never moves harmony within a bar. Falsified against 18 real movements (0 flagged); catches a planted one-chord-per-bar span. | medium |
| G8 | Grace notes registered as minor-2nd clashes against their own principal — chordify merges them at the principal's offset and drops the grace flag, so every statement of a theme opening with an appoggiatura was reported as a clash. | medium |

the left hand lives around F3–C4 and sometimes climbs *above* the right hand;

| I3 | **The PhraseSlot loader hand-enumerated its fields and dropped the rest.** `curves` (the narrative energy arc), `motif_transforms` (the theme placements the theme planner attaches at plan time), `harmony_detail`, `pickup_beats`, `continuation` and `notes` all vanished on the first save/load round-trip — so the narrative arc and the theme recurrence machinery were being computed and then thrown away. This is the **third** time this project has recorded a load-drop bug of exactly this shape. Replaced with generic reconstruction from the dataclass's own declared fields, which recurses into nested dataclasses: adding a field to a model can no longer silently lose it. | critical |

## §J — The dramatic plan: giving every phrase a reason to exist

The remaining complaint was "mechanical, lacks texture or structure". Measured on
a freshly planned piece, the whole dramatic layer was **empty and unread**:
``narrative.sections`` had 0 entries, ``primary_climax_section`` was blank,
``curves.tension``/``register``/``brightness`` were empty lists, ``articulation``
was never set, ``forward_context`` was None, and ``rhetorical_goals`` /
``development_techniques`` had no writer and no reader anywhere in the codebase. A
phrase knew its cadence and its bar count and nothing about why it existed — so
every phrase came out locally optimal and the PIECE had no shape.

| # | Fix | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | New `dramatic_plan.py`: every phrase gets a **dramatic role** — establish, extend, depart, intensify, crisis, retreat, return, confirm, close — from its position in the form, with the intent stated in the composer's terms. | a phrase is told what it is for |
| J2 | The plan **locates the piece's climax** (a development's crisis; else the last intensify; else the phrase before the return) and gives every phrase its distance from it. | "you are 2 phrases before the peak — hold something in reserve" |
| J3 | **Tension, register and brightness curves** are built so they peak at the climax and relax at the extremes, instead of being empty. | the piece has an arc, not nine local arcs |
| J4 | **Energy is built, not scaled.** The per-function defaults return a CONSTANT for continuation phrases, so scaling a flat line by a per-phrase constant left the climax an unchanging *mf*. Energy is now the piece-level arc crossed with a per-role shape — rising for depart/intensify/crisis, falling for retreat/close, arching for lyrical material. | a sonata now opens *pp*, peaks *ff* at the development crisis, and dies to *pp* in the coda |
| J5 | **Return strategies.** A returning phrase gets one concrete way it must differ — reharmonized, registrally displaced, texturally intensified, abbreviated, expanded, dissolved — rotated so successive returns never reuse a device. A return that is the statement plus a trill is the machine tell no statistic catches, because every metric reads "matches the exposition". | the return is changed by what happened in between |
| J6 | New `unvaried_return` detector: compares a return's rhythm and contour against the statement and flags a near-copy. Catches the tell above; quiet on a real variation. | the tell is now measurable |
| J7 | **`forward_context` is populated** — every phrase is told what it leads into and what that phrase's job is, so it can prepare it. It existed on the model and was never written. | sections read as one fabric, not a sequence of validated phrases |
| J8 | **Key journey**: each phrase is marked prolong / depart / arrive, and an arriving phrase is given the tones common to both keys as a pivot. A slot carried one key and no notion of motion, so a modulation teleported. | modulations can be composed rather than declared |
| J9 | **Rhetorical goals and development techniques** per section, from its roles — pivot chord, applied dominant, rising sequence, liquidation, cadential 6-4, plagal colour. Both fields existed with no writer. | the brief names the techniques the moment calls for |
| J10 | **A dynamic shape and a touch instruction** per phrase, derived from the energy curve. The only dynamic guidance in the brief had been the words "avoid flat dynamics". | `b19 mf < b20 f < b21 ff` with hairpin directions |
| J11 | New `flat_register` detector: a phrase whose melody stays inside a fifth when the plan asked for an arc. Falsified at 1 in 40 real Mozart phrases. | the register plan is checked, not just stated |
| J12 | **Theme capture is automatic.** `motif_bank` is empty at plan time so no theme was ever elected, and `capture_theme_surface` was called by one optional workflow only — so on the default path there was NO theme mechanism and a piece was N phrases with nothing binding them. The first `establish` commit now captures the theme, and the development operation comes from the phrase's dramatic role and return strategy rather than a substring match on its section id. | the piece has a recurring idea |
| J13 | **Cadences land where the structure is**, not at the end of every phrase. A cadence per phrase is one every four to six bars for the whole piece — a metronome at the structural level. Continuations now run on (`none`), transitions evade, and only structural arrivals cadence: a ternary went from 9 cadences in 41 bars to 6, a sonata from 17 in 83 to 9. | phrases run into one another |
| J14 | **Three- and four-voice writing.** `//` was capped at two voices per hand, so real counterpoint required hand-written LayerIR and therefore never happened. Any number of voices now parse, engrave as separate staff voices, and are each checked for filling their own bar. | a four-voice chorale is writable in shorthand |
| J15 | `register_monotony` recalibrated from measurement: real Mozart four-bar phrases have a median melodic span of 15 semitones but a 10th percentile of 8, so the "within one octave" test fired on 39% of real phrases. Threshold moved to a fifth. Every gate warning now fires on ≤26% of real music. | the warnings that remain are worth reading |

## §K — Notation and score-presentation gaps found while verifying §J

| # | Fix |
|---|---|
| K1 | **A note longer than its bar was silently truncated.** Audit item A14 — a note crossing a barline simply lost its tail, so sustaining across a barline was impossible unless the composer hand-wrote the tie. Long notes are now split at the barline and **tied**, with attack markings (dynamic, articulation, ornament, expression) kept on the first fragment only and release markings (slur, hairpin) on the last — otherwise one tied note re-articulates its accent in every bar it crosses. |
| K2 | **No structural barlines.** Every engraved score has a double bar where a section ends and a final barline at the close; their absence is one of the first things that makes a printout look machine-generated. Now emitted from the section boundaries. |
| K3 | **The score's title was the first fifty characters of the request prompt and its composer field read "Wolfgang SCALES (mozart)".** Now the piece's own title, and `after <composer>` — this is not by Mozart and the score should not claim to be. |
| K4 | **music21 mangled the title on assignment round-trip.** Reading `md.title` back applies article-stripping and title-casing, so `md.movementName = md.title` turned "Andante grazioso in F major" into "Ndante Grazioso In F Major". Both fields are now assigned from the same local string. |
| K5 | **The chord frame spelled accidentals from the key signature**, so G minor's raised leading tone came out as G♭ and the frame offered `V = D/Gb/A` — not a chord. Pitches are now spelled from the Roman numeral itself, with only the accidental style converted to the shorthand's `Bb` form. |
| K6 | **Within-bar harmony could contradict the cadence.** A sampled pattern ending on V turned a phrase planned as a perfect cadence into one that stops on the dominant. The cadence bar's detail now lands on the planned cadence chord, and consecutive repeats are collapsed so `b3[1:I 2:I]` — telling the composer to change to the chord it is already on — cannot appear. |
| K7 | Augmented sixths and the Neapolitan are parsed with their figured-bass digits (`Ger65`, `Fr43`, `It6`, `N6`, `Cad64`). The table keyed only the bare names, so every one music21 wrote with digits was dropped from the progression model and rendered `?` in the chord frame. |

## The ceiling, measured

Comparing generated pieces to eight real Mozart movements on the system's own
discriminator metrics:

| metric | real Mozart (8 mvts) | generated |
|---|---|---|
| events_per_bar | 11.59 ± 4.10 | 6.60, 18.14 |
| texture_change_pct | 0.350 ± 0.139 | 0.362, 0.453 |
| density_cv | 0.377 ± 0.070 | 0.341, 0.363 |
| melody_direction_change_pct | 0.540 ± 0.129 | 0.660, 0.537 |
| dotted_ratio | 0.213 ± 0.208 | 0.438, 0.344 |
| **grace_ratio** | **0.139 ± 0.081** | **0.021, 0.000** |

**The output already sits inside real Mozart's band on nearly every metric the
system uses to judge itself.** That is the honest answer to "why isn't it
human-quality": not because it fails these measurements, but because passing them
is not what makes music good. This corroborates the project's own earlier finding
that metric-chasing is the ceiling.

The single clear outlier is ornamentation — and the correct response is *not* to
add a grace-note target to the report. A quota would produce exactly the
"ornaments at regular intervals are wallpaper" tell the craft guidance already
names. The gap is real; the fix is not a number.

**What the §A-§E work therefore is:** the removal of hard ceilings, not a
demonstration of better music. The system could not write a triplet, could not
sustain a note across a barline, was studying exemplars transposed by wrong
intervals, planned a cadence in every eighth bar, and discarded two-thirds of
every Bach chorale. Those are floors, not ceilings — a composer working under
them could not produce good music regardless of skill. Whether removing them
produces better music is **unproven**, and needs a full generation run judged by
ear, which this session did not do.

# What is still open

- **B11/D15** — MIDI-sourced corpora carry no ornaments, so ornament targets for
  those composers remain near zero. Fixing it needs score-sourced (not MIDI)
  acquisition.
- **B12/C6** — harmonic rhythm is still one chord per bar in the corpus records
  and the chord frame. Chord *repetition* now expresses prolongation, but a
  half-bar harmony cannot be represented.
- **A13** — the anacrusis exports via music21's padding-rest convention rather
  than `implicit="yes"`; it sounds and bars correctly but a purist pickup measure
  would need bar renumbering that would desynchronise IR bar references.
- **E20** — three or more voices per hand still requires hand-written LayerIR JSON.
- **E17** — the ~3,000-line engine-fallback path (`sketch_proposer`, `realizer`,
  `surface_composer`) is dead weight on the default flow but still has to stay correct.
- **C15** — no modulation model: a slot has one key, so pivot chords and
  tonicization cannot be planned.

## §L — The plan reaches the notes (2026-08-26)

The dramatic plan from §J was written but only half-consumed. Two fixes closed
the gap, and then the piece it produced was revised four times against the
read-back audit until nothing was left to hear.

### L1. Harmony was sampled without knowing what the phrase was for

`corpus_harmony_plan` took a cadence and a bar count and nothing else, so an
opening statement and a development crisis drew from the same pool. The B-flat
Andante's first phrase came out as `I | iv | viio | V` while its own STYLE
DOCTRINE, printed in the same brief, said "clear periodic phrasing, diatonic
harmony". A statement that opens on the borrowed minor subdominant does not
read as a statement.

`sample_progression` now takes `allow_chromatic`, and `corpus_harmony_plan`
takes `role`. Stable roles (establish / confirm / close / return) exclude the
mode's chromatic vocabulary; unstable ones (extend / depart / intensify /
crisis / retreat) keep it. `dramatic_plan.role_for()` resolves the role *before*
the slot is built so the sampler can be told.

**A second bug inside the first:** `_is_chromatic` tested for `b`/`#` in the
symbol, so it called `iv` — the borrowed minor subdominant, the very chord that
started this — diatonic. Mode mixture carries no accidental in its own symbol.
It is now mode-aware: in major, `i`/`iv`/`v` and `III`/`VI`/`VII` are borrowed;
in minor only `I`/`IV` are, since the raised-leading-tone `V` is normal practice.

Also tightened: a stable role may not sit on one chord for three bars. `I6 I6 I6`
is not prolongation, it is stalling.

### L2. Composing the plan, then answering the audit four times

`mozart-andante-bb-20260826` — Andante cantabile, B-flat major, 2/4, ternary,
41 bars — composed by hand from the dramatic plan, every note. The measurement
layer built in §D–§K then found six things a listener would hear:

| Finding | What it caught |
|---|---|
| `out_of_period_register` | The climax reached B♭6, above Mozart's own F6 keyboard |
| `voicing_poverty` | 100% of melody attacks single notes — no moment sounded fuller than any other |
| `tie_absent` | 41 bars, nothing held over a barline |
| `rest_ratio` 0.015 | The melody never breathed |
| `lh_texture_change` 0.25 vs 0.541 | 85% alberti — the accompaniment monoculture again |
| `unvaried_return` | The return repeated the opening with 100% identical rhythm *and* contour |

Fixing those produced two more, one of which was my own over-correction:

| Finding | What it caught |
|---|---|
| `register_stasis` | Melody spanned 19 semitones; no canonical movement measured under 24 |
| `scalar_overuse` | 31% of melody bars were plain scales — and b24 duplicated b21's contour, b34 duplicated b11's |
| `lh_texture_change` 0.825 | **Over-corrected**: monoculture became restlessness, changing character on nearly every barline |
| `third_ratio` z=+3.1 | **Over-corrected**: breaking up the scales turned the melody into arpeggio-noodling |
| `chromatic_ratio` z=-2.2 | Almost purely diatonic — an andante with no chromatic shadow has no colour |
| `unresolved_nct` | The diminished seventh I added to fix that was leapt into *and* out of |

Final state, read back off the assembled file rather than off the IR: 41 bars,
every bar exactly 2 beats, melody spanning F4–F6 (24 semitones, top note exactly
Mozart's ceiling), 2 cross-barline ties, 27% of melody attacks now chords,
17 slurs / 13 hairpins / 20 dynamics. **Ear 0 findings, realism 0 findings,
section gate passed, every generic band clear.**

Two corpus z-scores remain off — `rest_bar_ratio` (−2.3) and `third_ratio`
(+2.5) — and are deliberately left there. They are diagnostics, not revision
targets; chasing them further is the metric whack-a-mole this project already
rejected once.

### What this pass does *not* establish

The detectors are now clean on this piece, which means only that nothing they
can see is broken. Whether it is *good* is a judgement none of them make, and
the fresh-ears critic did not hear this one.

### L3. A rule I wrote in §L1, falsified two hours later

`_ROLE_ALLOWS_CHROMATIC` banned mode mixture from stable phrases outright.
Measuring 3,964 real Mozart bars killed it: mixture appears in **10.7% of his
opening bars**, statistically indistinguishable from its 10.9% rate mid-phrase.
A ban would reject one opening bar in ten of his own music.

The defect the rule was written for was **rate, not presence** — the sampler had
produced `I | iv | viio | V`, half the bars borrowed, five times the corpus rate.
Replaced with `_ROLE_CHROMATIC_SHARE`, a ceiling set above what Mozart actually
does, with a test asserting every role's ceiling exceeds the measured 10.6%.

This is the third time the standing discipline has caught one of my own rules.

### L4. Three numbers called "dotted", none of them labelled

The brief prints Mozart's dotted-rhythm rate three times in three units —
`dotted 0.27` (per bar), `dotted_eighth_ratio 0.01` (share of notes),
`dotted_ratio 0.215` (share of bars). They are consistent; nothing said so, and
`0.0–0.03` beside `0.27` reads as "avoid dotted rhythms". I misread it exactly
as a composer would. Both sites now state their units.

### L5. The rhythmic gap the metrics had not been asked about

Reading K.332/ii — B-flat, the same key — showed a melody saturated with the
dotted-eighth/sixteenth pair and with 32nds. Measured over 5,942 real Mozart
melody bars: **22.0% of his bars carry a dotted rhythm and 12.6% of his melody
notes are a 32nd or faster.** This piece had 4.9% and *zero*. Separately, he
rests in **62% of bars**; it rested in 22%.

After a rhythmic pass and a breathing pass:

| | before | after | Mozart |
|---|---|---|---|
| bars with a dotted rhythm | 4.9% | 26.8% | 22.0% |
| notes 32nd or faster | 0% | 11.3% | 12.6% |
| bars containing a rest | 22% | 51% | 62% |

Adding the rests fixed the eight identical tonic alberti bars at the same time,
and the dotted figures dissolved the arpeggio-heaviness (`third_ratio`) left over
from fixing `scalar_overuse`. **Every corpus metric now sits inside Mozart's own
distribution — `off-corpus: none` — with ear 0, realism 0, gate passed.**

Two ties were silently lost along the way and only surfaced when `tie_absent`
returned: one tied into a rest, and one whose landing pitch a later edit had
changed. A tie is only a tie if something matching catches it.

## §M — Systematic sweep: the doctrine supply (2026-08-26)

§L fixed one piece deeply. This pass swept the codebase for defect *classes*.

### M1. 35 of 47 composers were serving stale, hollow doctrine — 121 fixes

Auditing every compiled pack across 47 composers found **192 hollow
composer/pack combinations** — pack files present but containing no content.
The pattern gave away the cause: the 12 composers with populated packs are
*exactly* the members of the four registered styles. The compiler had only ever
been re-run for style members; everyone else held packs from an older schema.

Recompiling all 47 fixed **121 of them**. This landed where it matters most —
the four packs repaired (`cadence_scripts`, `harmonic_devices`, `melody_priors`,
`figuration_templates`) are the most-read packs in the system, at 14, 14, 11 and
10 importers each, all consumed directly by `composition_brief.py`. Craft items
available across those four packs: **22,517**, median 480 per composer, where 35
composers previously had none.

Of the 71 remaining, **40 are `phrase_prototypes`, which requires a bar corpus** —
only 8 composers have one. That is a data-availability fact, not a defect.

### M2. Three of the four styles had no craft doctrine at all — 60 fixes

`style__baroque`, `style__renaissance` and `style__romantic` carried only
statistics: corpus profile, density stats, progression model. No cadence
scripts, no melody priors, no fingerprints. Composing "in Renaissance style"
got numbers and no craft. Built **48** aggregated craft packs from the styles'
member composers.

`style__classical` *had* packs, and they were far thinner than the union of its
own members — cadence scripts 118 → 454 items, fingerprint rules 1 → 105,
review rubric 0 → 148. **12** more upgraded.

### M3. Six instrument ranges were wrong, all clamping legitimate writing

Trumpet and cornet bottoms sat at written F♯3 rather than sounding E3 — the
written/sounding confusion. The tuba stopped at B♭3, excluding its whole tenor
register. Cello stopped at E5, timpani at A3, xylophone an octave low.
`orchestration_planner` imports the table rather than copying it, so all six
propagate.

### M4. Fifteen documented commands could not run outside an activated shell

Every corpus-rebuild and composer-acquisition command in CLAUDE.md and
ARMING-COMPOSERS.md invoked bare `python3` while the same CLAUDE.md says to use
`.venv/bin/python` for anything touching music21. They work today only because
this shell has the venv activated.

### M5. The fingerprint — the lesson of §L5, made permanent

§L5 found by hand that the andante rested in 22% of its bars against Mozart's
62%. That fact was derivable from the corpus the entire time; the brief carried
it only as a per-note ratio, which is the same truth in a unit nobody reasons
in. `rhythmic_fingerprint()` now measures, per composer, the four surface facts
that most decide whether a phrase sounds played or generated, and the brief
states them in bars and percentages above the statistics.

They separate composers sharply: Bach changes left-hand character on 63% of
barlines, Chopin on 33%; Liszt rests in 77% of bars, Bach in 25%.

**And the feature immediately asserted a falsehood.** Liszt's corpus is 437 bars
from the four lyrical works in the public domain and contains nothing faster
than a sixteenth — rendered as "0% of *his* melody notes are a 32nd or faster",
which is false about Liszt and would push a composer away from his signature
filigree. A thin or narrow corpus is now labelled as the sample, not the man.

### M6. z-scores restated as sentences

`rest_bar_ratio z=-2.32` was reported, read, understood as diagnostic, and left
alone. "22% of your bars contain a rest vs Mozart's 60%" is a fact a composer
acts on. `_rhythmic_gap` says it that way — and, like everything else in the
corpus comparison, names a distance and never a target.

**This one shipped broken too**: it carried a single sentence per metric, written
for the below-case, and reported a piece resting in 79% of its bars as "the music
never stops sounding". Both directions now read correctly, with a test for it.

### What this pass did not do

The stated goal was 400+ fixes. This is roughly **227** — 121 + 60 pack repairs,
6 ranges, 15 commands, and the rest features and rules. The remaining named
gaps are honest ones: four composer profiles are still 2-file stubs (two of them
the whole Renaissance style), and no ear has judged any of the output.

## §O — What the corpus actually is (2026-08-26)

The most consequential finding of the session, and it invalidates the framing of
every statistic this system computes.

### O1. Every armed corpus is a single genre

| Composer | Bars | What the corpus actually contains |
|---|---|---|
| bach | 6,795 | **100% four-part chorales** (music21's 371 + Riemenschneider) |
| haydn | 1,013 | **100% string quartets** (opus1, opus74) — used to teach PIANO writing |
| chopin | 4,853 | **100% mazurkas** — no nocturnes, no études, no ballades |
| mozart | 7,022 | 100% piano sonatas |
| beethoven | 17,757 | 100% piano sonatas |
| palestrina | 60,677 | 100% masses and motets |
| monteverdi | 3,885 | 100% madrigals |

Every number this system derives — density, ornament rates, texture change, the
rhythmic fingerprint, the discriminator bands, the corpus z-scores — is computed
over these bars and presented as a fact *about the composer*.

The consequences are concrete. Bach's measured ornament rate is **0.003 trills
per bar**, which is true of chorales and wildly false of the keyboard music a
brief would be quoting it for. Chopin's measured share of notes faster than a
sixteenth is **0.2%**, because mazurkas are dance pieces and the nocturnes with
their filigree are not in the corpus. Haydn's left-hand texture statistics are
measured from a **cello line**.

### O2. Why the existing caveat missed it

§M5 added a thin-corpus warning keyed on bar count and source count. Bach has
6,795 bars from 410 source movements and Chopin 4,853 from 52 — neither is thin.
**Narrow is a different failure from thin**, and nothing measured it.

`corpus_scope()` now classifies each corpus by genre and flags any corpus where
one genre holds ≥75% of the bars. Seven of eight are flagged. The warning heads
the brief, above the fingerprint, because it qualifies every number below it;
it also qualifies the fingerprint heading itself and the `corpus_divergence`
z-scores in `self_evaluate`, and `composer_coverage_tier` — which reported Bach
as tier A, armed, `needs_reacquire: false` — now carries `dominant_genre`,
`genre_share` and `genre_narrow`.

Getting the classifier right mattered: a first pass read Bach as 54% chorale /
46% unclassified and concluded his corpus was broad. music21's bundled corpora
carry no genre label, only a work id, and both this project ingests are single
genres — `bach/bwv<n>` is the chorales and `opusNNnoN/movementN` is the Haydn
quartets.

### O3. What this does not fix

The honest fix is to broaden the corpora — keyboard Bach, Chopin's nocturnes and
études, Haydn's piano sonatas. That needs network acquisition plus a full corpus
rebuild, and four other sessions were rebuilding corpora concurrently, so
running it would have been reckless rather than bold. The warning tells the
composer what the numbers are worth; it does not make them worth more.

**A test of mine encoded the wrong assumption and had to be corrected**: it
asserted Mozart earns the unqualified "FACTS ABOUT HIM" heading. Nothing in the
current corpus earns it.

## §P — Broadening the corpora, and the three bugs that prevented it (2026-08-26)

§O found that every armed corpus is a single genre and said the honest fix was
to broaden them. This section does that, after finding that the tool for it
could not work.

### P1. The arming tool wrote a format the reader ignores

`_arm_from_bars` wrote its bars inline into `bar_index.json`. But
`_iter_corpus_bars` consults that file only as a **fallback** — sharded
`bars_NN.json` wins whenever it exists, and every composer built by
`build_full_corpus` has shards. So acquiring a composer who was already armed
printed "wrote N bars" and the system went on reading the old shards, never
seeing one new bar. `bar_index.json` is a metadata stub now (schema 3), and
overwriting it with the pre-schema-3 shape corrupted that too.

This is the repo's documented top failure mode — one writer, a different
reader — and it made the whole acquisition path a no-op for exactly the
composers §O says need it.

### P2. The web was unreachable whenever a local corpus existed

`if not paths and use_web:` — web sources were tried only when music21 shipped
*nothing*. music21 ships 433 Bach files and 9 Haydn files, so neither could ever
be broadened by any number of re-runs. Added `--web` (fetch in addition to
local) and `--augment` (merge with what is on disk instead of replacing it).

### P3. The dedupe compared raw source names, and let 947 duplicates in

First run of `bach --web --augment` reported 947 new bars. All 60 "new" sources
were **the chorales already present**, differing only by an absent `bach/`
prefix. Rolled back from backup and normalised the dedupe key by stripping a
leading `<composer>/` — only that prefix, because Haydn holds both `movement4`
and `opus74no1/movement4` and those are different pieces in different keys and
metres. Re-run: 0 new bars, corpus intact.

### P4. What actually broadened

| Composer | Before | After | Now contains |
|---|---|---|---|
| haydn | 1,013 bars, 9 sources, tier B | **3,022 bars, 26 sources, tier A** | Hoboken XVI **piano sonatas**, a Creation aria, sacred works, songs — alongside the quartets |
| chopin | 4,853 bars, 52 sources | **8,013 bars, 99 sources** | ballades, **nocturnes**, waltzes, préludes, études, fantaisie-impromptu |
| bach | 6,795 bars | unchanged | Mutopia's reachable Bach is chorales we already hold |

Neither Haydn nor Chopin is flagged `narrow` any more. Style profiles and
progression models rebuilt.

### P5. Fixing narrowness exposed a second, opposite defect

Broadening Chopin dropped `corpus_scope().narrow` to False — and took the
warning with it — while his measured share of notes faster than a sixteenth
**fell to 0.1%**. The added works are MIDI transcriptions: no ornament marks,
filigree quantised to sixteenths. Measured against the kern mazurkas, the
acquired sources hold 0.05% fast notes versus 0.23%, and their smallest common
value is a sixteenth.

The corpus got broader and the fine-rhythm number got worse, with the caveat
removed. `corpus_fidelity()` now measures this directly — a source recording no
ornament anywhere and nothing under a sixteenth is coarse however it is named:

| Composer | coarse share | flagged |
|---|---|---|
| liszt | 100% | yes |
| bach | 92% | yes |
| chopin | 69% | yes |
| haydn | 34% | yes |
| beethoven | 13% | no |
| mozart | 4% | no |

The warning states what was measured rather than asserting a cause, because for
Bach the answer is the genre (chorales genuinely carry few ornaments) and for
Chopin it is the file format — and the consequence for a composer is identical
either way.

### P6. Four of my own tests asserted a snapshot, not a behaviour

`test_corpus_scope.py` pinned "haydn: string quartets 100%" and "chopin:
mazurkas 100%". Broadening those corpora — an improvement — broke four tests.
They now drive the classifier with synthetic sources and assert the mechanism,
with one live canary that some real corpus is still single-genre.

960 tests pass.
