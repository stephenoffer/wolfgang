# Franz Liszt — Stylistic Evolution

Liszt's creative arc is the most radical in 19th-century music. He begins as a salon dazzler, becomes an orchestral revolutionary, and ends as a proto-modernist writing music that would not sound out of place in 1920. No other Romantic composer changed so completely. The distance from the Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 (1847) to the Bagatelle sans tonalite (1885) is the distance from Beethoven to Schoenberg — and one man traveled it in a single lifetime.

---

## Three-Period Overview

| Period | Years | Nickname | Core Activity | Aesthetic |
|--------|-------|----------|--------------|-----------|
| I | 1835-1847 | Virtuoso Years | Touring performer; transcriptions; etudes | Brilliance, display, Romantic passion |
| II | 1848-1861 | Weimar Master | Conductor; symphonic poems; large forms | Formal innovation, thematic transformation, narrative |
| III | 1862-1886 | Abbe Liszt | Sacred works; late piano miniatures; teaching | Asceticism, experiment, dissolution of tonality |

---

## Period I: Virtuoso Years (1835-1847)

### Characteristics

| Dimension | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Purpose | Piano as spectacle; transcription as creative act; etude as art |
| Texture | Maximum density; three simultaneous layers; full keyboard span |
| Harmony | Chromatic within tonality; virtuosic modulation; dominant chains |
| Melody | Themes from opera, folk, or original — presented in bravura clothing |
| Form | Fantasy, rhapsody, transcription; sectional with cadenzas |
| Dynamic range | pp to fff; extremes for theatrical effect |
| Emotional world | Passion, triumph, nostalgia, showmanship |

### Key Works

| Work | Date | Significance |
|------|------|-------------|
| Transcendental Etudes (final version) | 1851 (begun 1826) | 12 etudes; each a character piece; peak virtuosity |
| Paganini Etudes | 1838/1851 | Piano translations of Paganini violin; La Campanella |
| Hungarian Rhapsodies 1-15 | 1846-1853 | Verbunkos style; lassan-friska form; national identity |
| Reminiscences de Don Juan | 1841 | Mozart opera transcription; the greatest operatic fantasy |
| Annees de Pelerinage I-II | 1835-1849 | Landscape + literature as music; Petrarch Sonnets |

T:Liszt Period I — Bravura Character (Transcendental Etude)

%% Full-keyboard sweep; octaves; maximum brilliance

## Period II: Weimar Master (1848-1861)

### Characteristics

| Dimension | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Purpose | Formal innovation; orchestral composition; conducting; championing others |
| Texture | Orchestral writing; piano textures become more balanced |
| Harmony | Augmented triads as system; whole-tone experiments; tritone axis |
| Melody | Thematic transformation as primary compositional method |
| Form | Symphonic poem (invented); double-function sonata; concerto arch |
| Dynamic range | Full range but rhetorically shaped, not just for effect |
| Emotional world | Heroism, narrative, Faust/Mephistopheles duality, aspiration |

### Key Works

| Work | Date | Significance |
|------|------|-------------|
| Piano Sonata in B minor | 1853 | Single-movement double-function form; greatest piano sonata since Beethoven |
| Les Preludes | 1854 | Symphonic poem No. 3; the model for all tone poems |
| Faust Symphony | 1857 | Three movements: Faust, Gretchen, Mephistopheles; augmented triad as formal principle |
| Dante Symphony | 1856 | Inferno + Purgatorio; tritone and whole-tone as structural intervals |
| Piano Concerto No. 1 | 1855 | Arch form; four sections without break; "triangle concerto" |
| Totentanz | 1859 | Dies Irae variations; piano + orchestra; macabre virtuosity |

### What Changed from Period I

| From (Period I) | To (Period II) | Why |
|-----------------|---------------|-----|
| Transcription/fantasy | Original symphonic works | Conducting opened orchestral thinking |
| Sectional forms | Continuous thematic transformation | Berlioz's idee fixe + Liszt's own invention |
| Piano as solo instrument | Piano + orchestra; pure orchestra | Weimar orchestra available |
| Virtuosity as display | Virtuosity as structure | Maturity; technique serves form |
| Chromatic tonality | Augmented triad system; tritone axis | Harmonic language deepens |

```abc
X:2
T:Liszt Period II — Symphonic Poem Character (Les Preludes)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
%% Thematic transformation: noble statement, full texture
!ff!C3D E3F|G4 c4|B2A2 G2F2|E4 C4|
%% The theme is a hero striding forward; it will become a lover, a soldier, a shepherd
```

## Period III: Abbe Liszt (1862-1886)

### Characteristics

| Dimension | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Purpose | Spiritual expression; experimentation; anticipating the future |
| Texture | Sparse; bare octaves; silence as structural element; monophonic passages |
| Harmony | Atonal passages; whole-tone; augmented triads unresolved; quartal hints |
| Melody | Fragmentary; does not develop; stated and abandoned |
| Form | Miniatures; fragments; no recapitulation; open endings |
| Dynamic range | Predominantly pp-p; fff rare and shocking when it appears |
| Emotional world | Death, mysticism, isolation, the void, religious ecstasy |

### Key Works

| Work | Date | Significance |
|------|------|-------------|
| Via Crucis | 1879 | 14 Stations of the Cross; bare fifths; medieval austerity |
| Nuages gris | 1881 | 50 bars; augmented triads never resolve; ends in ambiguity |
| La lugubre gondola I-II | 1882 | Premonition of Wagner's death; dark, chromatic, desolate |
| Unstern! Sinistre | 1881 | Tritone obsession; whole-tone passages; no key |
| Bagatelle sans tonalite | 1885 | Title says it: "Bagatelle without tonality" — Liszt names the revolution |
| Csardas macabre | 1881-82 | Hungarian dance with death; augmented 2nds over bare texture |

### What Changed from Period II

| From (Period II) | To (Period III) | Why |
|-----------------|----------------|-----|
| Full orchestra | Solo piano; small forces; organ | Retirement from public life; sacred vocation |
| Thematic transformation (complete) | Theme stated once, then silence | The theme has nothing left to prove |
| Chromatic saturation | Thinning; bare intervals; whole-tone | Stripping away what is unnecessary |
| Heroic narrative | Meditation on death and transcendence | Age; grief; religious life |
| Resolution and cadence | No resolution; music stops | Liszt stops believing in harmonic closure |
| 15-30 minute works | 2-5 minute fragments | Everything that can be said in 50 bars |

```abc
X:3
T:Liszt Period III — Nuages gris Character (1881)
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
%% Bare augmented triads; no resolution; the music floats and dies
!ppp![B,EG] z [B,E^G] z|[CE^G] z [C_EG] z|[B,_EG] z z2|
%% This is 1881. Debussy is 19. Schoenberg is 7. Liszt is already there.
```

## Cross-Period Comparison Table

| Aspect | Period I (1835-47) | Period II (1848-61) | Period III (1862-86) |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Main genre | Etude, rhapsody, transcription | Symphonic poem, sonata, concerto | Miniature, sacred work, fragment |
| Average work length | 8-15 min | 15-35 min | 2-6 min |
| Harmonic language | Chromatic tonality | Augmented/whole-tone system | Proto-atonal |
| Texture density | Maximum (orchestral piano) | Balanced (true orchestral) | Minimum (bare, silent) |
| Audience | Concert hall (thousands) | Weimar court + musical elite | Private; future generations |
| Emotional center | Passion, triumph | Narrative, heroism, duality | Death, void, transcendence |
| Formal principle | Sectional + cadenza | Thematic transformation | Fragment; anti-form |
| Influence felt on | Virtuoso tradition | Wagner, Strauss, symphonic poem genre | Debussy, Bartok, Schoenberg |

## Evolution of Specific Elements

### How Dynamics Evolve

| Period | Typical Range | Climax Behavior |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| I | pp-fff, extreme contrasts | ff/fff sustained passages; bravura |
| II | pp-fff, rhetorically shaped | Climax as structural event; Ger+6 -> V -> I fff |
| III | ppp-p predominant | fff rare; when it appears, it is shocking and brief |

### How Endings Evolve

| Period | Typical Ending | Example |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| I | Bravura coda; fff; rapid octaves to final chord | Hungarian Rhapsodies |
| II | Apotheosis or dissolution; programmatic conclusion | Les Preludes (triumph); B minor Sonata (ppp dissolution) |
| III | No cadence; music stops on dissonance or bare interval | Nuages gris (augmented triad); Unstern! (tritone) |
