# Liszt Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `liszt-lh-vocabulary.md`, which says the essential thing: the hand
covers the instrument and the range *moves*. This catalogue is the rest.

The organising fact: **thematic transformation.** One theme returns throughout a
work in completely different characters — a hymn becomes a march becomes a
lament — by changing its rhythm, harmony, register and accompaniment while
keeping its intervals. That is Liszt's replacement for development, and a piece
without it is a set of episodes.

## Thematic devices

1. **Thematic transformation** — the same interval sequence, new character. Slow
   and sustained, then fast and martial, then decorated and lyrical. Change
   everything except the intervals.

2. **The theme in a new register** — the identical material two octaves up or
   down is a different piece of music at the keyboard.

3. **Rhythmic augmentation into a peroration** — the theme in double values,
   fortissimo, in octaves, at the end.

4. **Fragmentation into figuration** — the theme's head becomes the basis of a
   run or a tremolo, so the virtuosity is thematic rather than decorative.

## Textural and pianistic devices

5. **Octave passages** — the melody in octaves in one or both hands, at speed.
   The bravura default.

6. **The "third hand"** — the melody in the middle register with figuration above
   *and* below it, shared between the hands.

7. **Alternating hands on one line** — a single scale or arpeggio split between
   the hands so it can run faster and wider than either could alone.

8. **Orchestral tremolo** — a low tremolo under a sustained chordal melody,
   transcribing a string section.

9. **Cadenza** — an unmeasured or freely-measured flourish, often over a held
   harmony, placed before a return or a final statement.

10. **Repeated-note virtuosity** — the same pitch struck rapidly with alternating
    fingers, a device borrowed from Paganini.

11. **Wide leaps as gesture** — the hand jumping two or three octaves between
    beats, the leap itself being the effect.

12. **Chordal recitative** — a declamatory passage in unison or in bare octaves,
    freely timed, between sections.

## Harmonic devices

13. **Chromatic third relations** — keys and chords a third apart, with no
    functional connection. Liszt uses these more freely than anyone before him.

14. **The augmented triad** — as a colour and as a pivot. Symmetrical, so it
    belongs to no key, and it is his favourite way of dissolving one.

15. **Diminished-seventh sequences** — the same shape sliding by minor thirds,
    obliterating tonality for a passage.

16. **Whole-tone and other symmetrical scales**, especially in the late works,
    used as transitional colour.

17. **Bare open fifths** — for archaism, for the religious works, and for
    emptiness.

18. **Unresolved dissonance at a section's end** — the late works simply stop on
    an unresolved chord.

19. **Modal harmony from Hungarian sources** — the augmented second, the
    "Gypsy" scale, and drone accompaniment in the rhapsodies.

## Structural devices

20. **Double-function form** — one movement that is simultaneously a sonata and a
    four-movement cycle. Ambitious; only relevant for a long piece.

21. **The slow introduction that becomes the main theme** — material introduced
    ambiguously and then revealed.

22. **The apotheosis** — the theme returning at the end transformed into a
    triumphal or hymnic version, at the loudest and widest point of the piece.

23. **Episodic construction with a returning idea** — the sections contrast
    strongly and the theme is what unifies them.

## Dynamics and rhetoric

The range is `ppp` to `fff`, often within a page. Fermatas, general pauses and
free recitative are structural: the music stops because a gesture has ended, not
because a phrase has run out.

## What to avoid

- **A narrow left hand.** The most common failure; see the vocabulary file.
- **A theme that returns unchanged.** Transformation is the method.
- **Four-square phrasing.** Phrases are extended, interrupted, elided.
- **A single dynamic level.** The range is the point.
- **Virtuosity unattached to the theme.** Liszt's figuration is derived; scales
  for their own sake sound like an étude by someone else.
