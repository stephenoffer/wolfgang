# Franz Liszt — Formal Approach

Liszt broke sonata form and reassembled it. The symphonic poem replaced the four-movement symphony with a single continuous arc shaped by thematic transformation. The B minor Sonata compressed sonata-allegro, slow movement, scherzo, and finale into one 30-minute span where themes flow between functions without pausing. This is not formlessness — it is a new kind of form, where the transformation of a theme IS the architecture. Liszt's formal thinking is cinematic: scenes dissolve into each other, and the thread connecting them is the evolving theme.

---

## Primary Formal Models

| Form | Structure | Duration | Key Works |
|------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Symphonic poem (single-movement) | Free sections linked by thematic transformation | 12-25 min | Les Preludes, Mazeppa, Tasso, Orpheus |
| Double-function sonata | One movement containing sonata + slow mvt + scherzo + finale | 25-35 min | B minor Sonata, Dante Sonata |
| Multi-movement symphony | 3-4 movements with shared thematic material | 60-75 min | Faust Symphony, Dante Symphony |
| Rhapsody | Slow introduction (lassan) -> fast dance (friska) | 8-15 min | 19 Hungarian Rhapsodies |
| Concerto (arch form) | 4 sections played without break; themes recur across sections | 18-22 min | Piano Concerto No. 1 (Eb), No. 2 (A) |
| Late miniature | Fragment; no development; statement and silence | 2-6 min | Nuages gris, Bagatelle sans tonalite |

## Thematic Transformation as Form

The master principle: one or two themes generate ALL material through transformation. Sections are defined not by new themes but by new STATES of the same theme.

### Les Preludes — Transformation Map

| Section | Tempo | Theme State | Key | Character |
|---------|-------|------------|-----|-----------|
| Introduction | Andante | Theme as question (fragments only) | C | Mysterious, searching |
| First statement | Andante maestoso | Theme complete, noble | C major | Heroic |
| Lyrical episode | Espressivo | Theme as love song (augmented, legato) | E major | Tender |
| Storm | Allegro tempestoso | Theme fragmented, diminished, turbulent | C minor | Dramatic conflict |
| Pastoral | Allegretto pastorale | Theme as folk song (6/8, woodwinds) | A major | Idyllic |
| Heroic return | Allegro marziale | Theme as march (dotted, brass, ff) | C major | Triumphant |
| Coda | Piu mosso | Theme in apotheosis (full orchestra, fff) | C major | Transcendent |

## The Double-Function Sonata (B minor Sonata Model)

Liszt's greatest formal achievement: one movement that IS four movements simultaneously.

| Time | Sonata-Allegro Function | Multi-Movement Function | Thematic Material |
|------|------------------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| 0:00-1:30 | Slow introduction | — | Descending scale motif; octave hammer motif |
| 1:30-8:00 | Exposition | First movement (Allegro) | Theme A (heroic), Theme B (lyrical — Grandioso) |
| 8:00-14:00 | Development Pt. 1 | Slow movement (Andante) | Theme B transformed into love song; new cantabile |
| 14:00-18:00 | Development Pt. 2 | Scherzo (fugato) | Theme A in fugue; demonic transformation |
| 18:00-25:00 | Recapitulation | Finale | Themes A+B return transformed; Grandioso climax |
| 25:00-30:00 | Coda | Coda | All themes in final states; ends ppp — dissolution |

### Key Relationships in the B minor Sonata

| Section | Key | Relationship to B minor |
|---------|-----|------------------------|
| Main theme | B minor | Tonic |
| Second theme (Grandioso) | D major | Relative major |
| Slow movement | F# major | Dominant (enharmonic) |
| Fugato | Bb minor (= A# minor) | Enharmonic tritone to tonic! |
| Recapitulation climax | B major | Parallel major — triumph |
| Coda | B major -> B (bare octave) | Dissolution to single note |

```abc
X:1
T:Liszt B minor Sonata — Opening Gestures (Formal Seeds)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Bm
%% Three motifs that generate 30 minutes of music
%% Motif 1: descending scale (Lento)
!pp!G2 F2 E2 ^D2|D2 ^C2 C2 B,2|
%% Motif 2: octave hammer (Allegro energico)
!ff!B,4 z4|B,4 z4|
%% Motif 3: ascending leap (the questioning motif)
!f!^D4 E2 F2|G4 A4|
```

## Rhapsody Form (Lassan-Friska)

The Hungarian Rhapsodies follow the verbunkos structure: slow-fast.

| Section | Character | Tempo | Typical Features |
|---------|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| Introduction | Free, improvisatory | Ad libitum / Lento | Cadenza-like flourishes; recitative |
| Lassan | Slow, proud, ornamented | Andante / Adagio | Dotted rhythms; augmented 2nds; rubato |
| Transition | Accelerating | Animato -> Vivace | Trills; scales; building momentum |
| Friska | Fast, wild, virtuosic | Presto / Vivace | Rapid octaves; dance patterns; accelerates to end |
| Coda | Fastest | Prestissimo | Breakneck speed; fff; bravura ending |

```abc
X:2
T:Liszt Rhapsody Form — Lassan to Friska Transition
M:2/4
L:1/16
K:Am
%% Lassan: slow, proud
!f!A4 B2C2|{CD}^D4 E4|
%% Transition: accelerating
F2^G2 A2B2|c2d2 e2f2|
%% Friska: wild dance
!ff!e2c2 A2c2|e2c2 A2c2|e4 a4|
```

## Concerto Arch Form (Piano Concerto No. 1 Model)

| Section | Function | Tempo | Key | Duration |
|---------|----------|-------|-----|----------|
| I | Exposition — heroic theme | Allegro maestoso | Eb major | ~5 min |
| II | Slow episode — theme as nocturne | Quasi adagio | B major (remote!) | ~5 min |
| III | Scherzo — theme as elfin dance | Allegretto vivace | Eb major | ~4 min |
| IV | March-finale — theme triumphant | Allegro marziale | Eb major | ~4 min |
| Coda | All themes combined | Presto | Eb major | ~2 min |

**Note:** The "triangle concerto" — the triangle solo in the scherzo section was considered scandalous. Liszt used it for elfin color, not comic effect.

## Late Works: Anti-Form

After 1870, Liszt's forms become fragmentary and open-ended.

| Feature | Traditional Form | Liszt Late Form |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Ending | Cadence to tonic | Stops on dissonance or silence |
| Development | Theme transformed and returned | Theme stated and abandoned |
| Proportions | Balanced sections | Asymmetric; some sections a few bars |
| Repetition | Recapitulation expected | No return; through-composed fragment |
| Closure | Resolution | The void — music dissolves |

## Formal Proportions Guide

| Form Type | Intro | Exposition/Statement | Development/Middle | Recap/Return | Coda |
|-----------|-------|---------------------|-------------------|-------------|------|
| Symphonic poem | 5-10% | 25-30% | 30-35% | 20-25% | 10-15% |
| B minor Sonata model | 5% | 25% | 35% (slow + fugato) | 25% | 10% |
| Rhapsody | 10-15% (intro) | 30% (lassan) | 10% (transition) | 40% (friska) | 10% (coda) |
| Concerto (4-in-1) | 5% | 25% | 25% | 25% | 20% |
| Late miniature | 0% | 50-70% | 0-30% | 0% | 0-20% |
