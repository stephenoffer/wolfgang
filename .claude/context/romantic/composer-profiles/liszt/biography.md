# Franz Liszt — Biography

The life of a man who lived four lives: child prodigy, rock-star virtuoso, orchestral revolutionary, and mystic priest. Each phase produced a different composer. Liszt did not simply evolve — he shed skins. The showman who dazzled Paris salons in his twenties became the monk who wrote music so spare it anticipated Schoenberg. His generosity was as legendary as his technique: he championed Wagner, Grieg, Borodin, and Smetana when no one else would. To compose "like Liszt" you must know WHICH Liszt.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Role | Significance |
|------|----------|------|-------------|
| 1811 | Raiding, Hungary | Born | Father Adam a musician in Esterhazy service; the boy plays piano at 6 |
| 1822 | Vienna | Student of Czerny | Carl Czerny (Beethoven's pupil) teaches him; Beethoven allegedly kisses his forehead after a concert |
| 1823 | Paris | Child prodigy | Denied entry to Paris Conservatoire (foreigner); studies privately; opera "Don Sanche" at age 13 |
| 1827 | Paris | Crisis | Father dies; Liszt teaches piano to aristocrats; religious crisis; nearly enters priesthood |
| 1831 | Paris | Hears Paganini | Paganini's violin pyrotechnics inspire Liszt to become "the Paganini of the piano" — transcendental technique as goal |
| 1832 | Paris | Meets Chopin & Berlioz | Chopin's intimacy and Berlioz's orchestral vision both leave permanent marks |
| 1835 | Geneva | Elopement with Marie d'Agoult | Countess d'Agoult; they have three children (Cosima later marries Wagner) |
| 1839 | Europe | Touring virtuoso years begin | 8 years of relentless touring across Europe; the most famous performer alive |
| 1842 | Weimar | Appointed Kapellmeister | Begins conducting; premieres Wagner's Lohengrin (1850); premieres Berlioz |
| 1848 | Weimar | Retires from touring | Settles in Weimar; composes symphonic poems, B minor Sonata, Faust Symphony |
| 1858 | Weimar | Loses position | Political opposition; leaves Weimar after son Daniel's death (1859) and daughter Blandine's (1862) |
| 1861 | Rome | Takes minor orders | Becomes Abbe Liszt; composes sacred music; deepens religious engagement |
| 1869 | Weimar/Rome/Budapest | "Vie trifurquee" | Splits year between three cities; teaches masterclasses; composes late experimental works |
| 1881 | Weimar | Falls down stairs | Health declines; cataracts, dropsy; but continues composing radical late works |
| 1886 | Bayreuth | Dies, July 31 | Pneumonia after attending Wagner festival; dies in daughter Cosima's city |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Paganini: Technique as Transcendence

| Paganini Element | Liszt's Piano Translation | Where You Hear It |
|-----------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Left-hand pizzicato | LH leaping bass + melody simultaneously | La Campanella, Hungarian Rhapsodies |
| Rapid arpeggios spanning the instrument | Full-keyboard sweeps in seconds | Transcendental Etudes, Mephisto Waltz |
| Showmanship as art | Theatrical pauses, visual drama | Grand Galop chromatique; concert behavior |
| One instrument = entire orchestra | Piano must produce orchestral textures | Operatic transcriptions; B minor Sonata |

### Berlioz: The Orchestral Imagination

| Berlioz Lesson | How Liszt Applied It | Result |
|---------------|---------------------|--------|
| Idee fixe (recurring theme) | Thematic transformation (one theme, many forms) | Symphonic poems, Faust Symphony |
| Programmatic narrative | Music tells a story without words | Les Preludes, Mazeppa, Hamlet |
| Orchestral color as structure | Timbral changes mark formal sections | Faust Symphony orchestration |
| Unprecedented scale | Extended single-movement forms | B minor Sonata (30 minutes, one movement) |

### Hungary: The Returning Native

| Hungarian Element | Musical Expression | Where You Hear It |
|------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Verbunkos dance | Dotted rhythms, augmented 2nds, czardas slow-fast structure | 19 Hungarian Rhapsodies |
| Gypsy scale | A-B-C-D#-E-F#-G (two augmented 2nds) | Rhapsodies, Hungarian Fantasia |
| Improvised character | Rubato passages, cadenza-like openings | Rhapsody No. 2 "lassan" opening |
| National pride | Large-scale concert works on folk material | Hungarian Coronation Mass |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Parisian prodigy | 1822-1835 | Brilliant, seeking, absorbing influences | Early transcriptions, Apparitions |
| Virtuoso years | 1835-1847 | Peak performing; transcendental technique; operatic transcriptions | Transcendental Etudes, Paganini Etudes, Don Juan Fantasy |
| Weimar master | 1848-1861 | Orchestral thinker; formal innovator; conductor | B minor Sonata, 12 Symphonic Poems, Faust Symphony, Piano Concertos |
| Abbe Liszt | 1861-1886 | Mystical, experimental, stripped-down; anticipates 20th century | Via Crucis, Nuages gris, Bagatelle sans tonalite, late piano pieces |

### What Changes Between Phases

| Aspect | Virtuoso Years | Weimar Master | Late Works |
|--------|---------------|---------------|------------|
| Texture | Maximum density; orchestral piano | Balanced; true orchestral writing | Sparse; silences; bare octaves |
| Harmony | Chromatic within tonality | Extended chromaticism; augmented chains | Atonal passages; whole-tone; tritone axis |
| Form | Fantasy; rhapsody; transcription | Thematic transformation; symphonic poem | Fragments; miniatures; no resolution |
| Dynamics | pp to fff; extremes | Full range; rhetorically shaped | pp predominant; fff rare |
| Emotional world | Triumph, passion, display | Heroism, narrative, Faust/Mephistopheles | Isolation, death, mysticism, void |
