# Felix Mendelssohn — Biography

A life of luminous privilege and relentless productivity, cut short at 38. Born into wealth and culture, Mendelssohn never had to fight for recognition — and this ease became both his gift and his curse: the music flows with a naturalness that later generations mistook for superficiality. It is not superficial. It is the sound of a mind so fluent that effort is invisible.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1809 | Hamburg | Born into the Mendelssohn banking family | Grandfather Moses Mendelssohn was the great Enlightenment philosopher; culture was the family currency |
| 1811 | Berlin | Family moves; converted to Christianity (Bartholdy) | The Jewish-Christian identity tension shadows everything — the need to be "more than good enough" |
| 1816 | Berlin | Studies with Carl Friedrich Zelter | Zelter connects young Felix to Goethe and to the music of J.S. Bach — the two poles of his artistic world |
| 1821 | Weimar | Meets Goethe (age 12) | Goethe declares him more gifted than Mozart at the same age; plays from memory, improvises, astonishes |
| 1825 | Berlin | Composes String Octet Op. 20 (age 16) | The scherzo is already fully Mendelssohnian: elfin, fleet, unprecedented. A masterpiece by a teenager. |
| 1826 | Berlin | Overture to A Midsummer Night's Dream (age 17) | The fairy-scherzo sound crystallized; orchestral magic that no one had heard before |
| 1829 | Berlin | Conducts Bach's St Matthew Passion revival | The Bach Revival: Mendelssohn resurrects a forgotten genius and changes music history |
| 1829–32 | Europe | Grand Tour: England, Scotland, Italy, France | The Hebrides Overture, Italian and Scottish Symphonies born from direct landscape experience |
| 1835 | Leipzig | Becomes conductor of the Gewandhaus Orchestra | Transforms it into Europe's finest; programs Schubert, Schumann, Bach alongside his own works |
| 1843 | Leipzig | Founds the Leipzig Conservatory | With Schumann on faculty; establishes the model for modern music education |
| 1846 | Birmingham | Elijah premiered | His oratorio masterpiece; the English adore him — Mendelssohn becomes Britain's honorary composer |
| 1847 | Leipzig | Sister Fanny dies, May; Felix dies, November 4 | Fanny's death devastates him; a series of strokes follows; dead at 38 |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Privilege and the Aesthetic of Ease

| Aspect | How It Shaped Mendelssohn |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Wealth | Never needed patrons or commissions; composed what he wanted, when he wanted — this freedom is audible in the music's naturalness |
| Education | Studied with the best teachers; met Goethe, Hegel, Humboldt; the music is learned without being academic |
| Travel | The Italian Symphony sounds like Italy because he was there; the Scottish sounds like Scotland; the Hebrides sounds like the sea |
| Social position | Always conducting, organizing, teaching — the music is public-spirited, generous, communicative |

### The Bach Connection

| Dimension | What Bach Gave Mendelssohn |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| Counterpoint | Real fugal writing in a Romantic context — the "Italian" Symphony finale, Elijah choruses, organ works |
| Formal clarity | Bach's architecture as a model for transparency: every voice audible, every line purposeful |
| The Revival itself | Conducting the St Matthew Passion in 1829 was an act of historical consciousness — Mendelssohn understood that music has a past worth honoring |
| Protestant identity | Bach's sacred world gave Mendelssohn (a Jewish convert) a musical language for Christian faith: Elijah, St. Paul, the organ works |

### The Sister — Fanny Mendelssohn Hensel

| Connection | Significance |
|-----------|-------------|
| Musical equal | Fanny was a composer of comparable gifts; her piano works rival Felix's Songs Without Words |
| Intellectual partner | They shared every musical idea; Felix's grief at her death was the grief of losing half his creative mind |
| Gendered constraint | Fanny could not publish or perform publicly (family prohibition); Felix's career was partly built on the freedom she was denied |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Prodigy | 1821–1829 | Astonishing maturity; the elfin and lyrical voices already formed | Octet Op. 20, Midsummer Night's Dream Overture, String Quartets |
| Traveler | 1829–1835 | Landscape impressionism; orchestral color; the overture as tone poem | Hebrides, Italian and Scottish Symphonies, Piano Concerto No. 1 |
| Leipzig master | 1835–1843 | Conductor, educator, institution-builder; mature chamber and vocal works | Songs Without Words (peak sets), String Quartets Op. 44, Organ Sonatas |
| Final years | 1843–1847 | Oratorio, concerto, increasing exhaustion; the Violin Concerto as summit | Elijah, Violin Concerto in E minor, String Quartet Op. 80 (anguished, grief-driven) |
