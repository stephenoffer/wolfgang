# Mendelssohn Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `mendelssohn-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **Classical
forms and phrase rhythm carrying Romantic melody and colour.** He is the most
formally conservative of the Romantics and the most texturally elegant.

## Melodic devices
1. **The song without words** — an unbroken vocal melody over a rippling
   accompaniment, in a clear ternary shape.
2. **Long-breathed lyrical line** with a single arch.
3. **Melody in thirds and sixths** for warmth.
4. **The fairy scherzo idiom** — rapid, light, staccato, high, `pp`, in even
   note values. His invention and unmistakable.
5. **Ornamental turn on an arrival note.**
6. **Melodic sequence rising to a peak.**

## Harmonic devices
7. **Diatonic clarity** — the harmony is simpler than his contemporaries', and
   the effect is transparency.
8. **Chromatic passing motion inside a diatonic frame.**
9. **Modal mixture for a shadow**, used sparingly and therefore effectively.
10. **The plagal, hymn-like cadence** in the sacred and slow movements.
11. **Sequence through closely related keys.**

## Structural devices
12. **Strict ternary (ABA) with a varied return.**
13. **Sonata form, Classically proportioned** — no expanded development, no
    disruptive coda.
14. **Scherzo and trio** — the scherzo fast and light, the trio contrasting in
    texture rather than in tempo.
15. **Cyclic return of a theme at the end of a work.**
16. **The fugato episode** — a short contrapuntal passage inside a homophonic
    movement, from his Bach study.
17. **Overture form for a programmatic single movement.**

## What to avoid
- **Density.** Elegance and transparency are the point.
- **Formal disruption.** He does not interrupt or elide the way Beethoven does.
- **Extreme dynamics.** The range is narrower and the character lighter.
- **Heavy chromaticism.** Diatonic clarity with touches of colour.
