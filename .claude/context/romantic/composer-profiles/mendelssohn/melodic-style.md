# Felix Mendelssohn — Melodic Style

Mendelssohn's melodies sing. They are modeled on the human voice — specifically on Italian bel canto — even when written for violin, cello, or piano. The phrases arch naturally, peak gracefully, and resolve without struggle. This is not simplicity; it is the highest craft of melodic construction, where art conceals art.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Where It Appears |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Bel canto linearity | Long, arching phrases shaped like vocal melodies; conjunct motion with expressive leaps | Slow movements, Songs Without Words, Violin Concerto |
| Singing tone | Even in fast passages, the melody "sings" — connected, legato, breathing at phrase junctions | All lyrical writing |
| 8-bar symmetry | Phrases overwhelmingly in 4+4 or 8+8 bar groups; antecedent-consequent structure | Standard Mendelssohn phrase |
| Peak placement | Melodic climax near the 3/4 point of the phrase — ascent through bars 1-6, peak at bar 6, descent through 7-8 | Consistent across all periods |
| Cadential ornament | Turns, mordents, or short trills at cadence points — never mid-phrase | Cadences of slow movements, Songs Without Words |
| Scalar fluency | Running passages are scalic (stepwise), not arpeggiated; this gives the elfin scherzo its quality | Fast movements, scherzi, finales |

## Phrase Architecture

| Phrase Type | Structure | Character | Example Context |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|----------------|
| Lyrical arch | 4 bars ascent + 4 bars descent, peak on bar 5-6 | The "Song Without Words" shape | Piano pieces, slow movements |
| Antecedent-consequent | 4-bar question (half cadence) + 4-bar answer (full cadence) | Classical balance | Opening themes |
| Sequential extension | 4-bar idea + 4-bar sequence (up a step) + 4-bar cadential | Building intensity | Transition passages |
| Elfin scalar | 2-bar fragments, staccato, descending or circling scales | Weightless, fleet | Scherzi |
| Chorale melody | Stepwise, narrow range, hymn-like rhythm | Protestant solemnity | Oratorio movements, Reformation Symphony |

```abc
X:1
T:Mendelssohn Lyrical Arch — Songs Without Words Character
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:G
!p!B,2 D2 G2 B2|A2 G2 F2 E2|!mf!D2 G2 B2 d2|c2 B2 A4|
!mp!G2 B2 d2 g2|!f!f2 e2 d2 c2|!mp!B2 A2 G2 F2|G8|
%% 8-bar arch: gradual ascent to bar 5-6 peak, gentle descent to tonic
```

## Melodic Intervals

| Interval | Frequency | Mendelssohn's Use |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Steps (2nds) | Very high | The default motion; scalic fluency |
| Minor 3rd | Common | Gentle rise; characteristic of minor-key themes |
| Major 3rd | Common | Bright, affirming; opening leap of many themes |
| Perfect 4th | Moderate | Upward leap for emphasis at phrase starts |
| Perfect 5th | Moderate | Opening gesture of confident themes |
| 6th (major) | Characteristic | The "reaching" interval — climactic leaps in lyrical themes |
| Octave | Rare | Reserved for dramatic moments; never casual |
| 7th or larger | Very rare | Mendelssohn avoids angular intervals; the melody is always approachable |

```abc
X:2
T:Mendelssohn Characteristic 6th Leap — Violin Concerto Character
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Em
!p!B,2 E2 G2 B2|!mf!e2 d2 c2 B2|!f!A2 f2 e2 d2|!mp!e4 B4|
%% The 6th leap (A up to f) at bar 3 is the emotional peak — the "reaching" gesture
```

## The Elfin Melodic Type

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Range | High register: violin 1 above D5, flute, piano RH above C5 |
| Motion | Scalic descent and ascent; circling figures around scale degrees |
| Articulation | All staccato; no sustained notes; everything darts |
| Rhythm | Even subdivisions (sixteenths in 2/4); no dotted rhythms |
| Phrase length | 2-bar fragments, not 8-bar arches |
| Dynamic | pp throughout; the melody whispers |

```abc
X:3
T:Mendelssohn Elfin Melody — Midsummer Night's Dream Scherzo Character
M:2/4
L:1/16
K:E
!pp!efed cBAG|FGAB AGFE|DEFG FEDC|B,CDE DCB,A,|
%% All staccato, all scales, all high register — pure flight
```

## The Chorale Melodic Type

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Range | Middle register; narrow (within an octave) |
| Motion | Almost entirely stepwise; no leaps larger than a 4th |
| Rhythm | Even note values (quarters and halves); hymn-like |
| Articulation | Legato, sustained; no staccato |
| Phrase length | 4-bar hymn phrases |
| Dynamic | mp to mf; solemn, not dramatic |

```abc
X:4
T:Mendelssohn Chorale Melody — Reformation Symphony Character
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:D
!mp!D F A d|c B A G|!mf!F G A B|A2 D2|
%% Stepwise, narrow range, dignified — the Protestant hymn tradition
```

## Ornamental Practice

| Ornament | Placement | Frequency | Character |
|----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Turn | Cadential — penultimate bar | Common | Graceful closure; bel canto inheritance |
| Short trill | On dominant note before final resolution | Moderate | Classical elegance |
| Mordent | On downbeat of arriving note | Occasional | Gentle emphasis |
| Appoggiatura | On strong beats in slow melodies | Moderate | Expressive leaning |
| Gruppetto | Between two notes of a step | Rare | Decorative filling |

## Melody in Different Genres

| Genre | Melodic Character | Signature Feature |
|-------|------------------|-------------------|
| Piano (Songs Without Words) | Vocal line over accompaniment; the piano "sings" | Right hand as soprano voice; left hand as harmonic support |
| Orchestral slow movement | Strings carry the melody; woodwinds echo or extend | Violin 1 or cello as primary singer |
| Scherzo | Elfin scalar fragments; no true melody — texture is the content | Unison strings or flute lead |
| Oratorio | Dramatic recitative + lyrical aria + choral hymn | Three melodic types in alternation |
| Chamber | Melody shared between instruments; dialogue | Imitative entries, conversational trading |
