# Mendelssohn Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Mendelssohn is Classical clarity with Romantic warmth. The left hand is **light,
even and transparent** — never muddy, never thick. Its most characteristic
texture is a rippling broken-chord figure under a singing melody, and its second
is the fleet, staccato scherzo accompaniment nobody else writes as well.

## LH idiom catalog

1. **Rippling broken chords** — `C3e G3e E4e G3e C3e G3e E4e G3e`, even and light,
   in the middle register. The Song Without Words accompaniment.
2. **Light staccato chords** — `[C3,E3]e:stacc ×8` at `pp`. The scherzo idiom:
   fast, dry and even.
3. **Oom-pah, transparent** — bass and chord, but higher and lighter than a
   Classical version.
4. **Arpeggio spanning an octave and a half**, smooth and unhurried.
5. **Sustained chorale chords** — for the hymn-like slow movements and the
   religious works.
6. **Repeated-note pulse** under a sustained melody.
7. **Walking bass in octaves** for the more serious movements.
8. **Contrapuntal bass** — from his Bach revival work; a real second line.
9. **Held pedal with light figuration above it.**
10. **Bare octaves** at a dramatic opening.

## Where the idiom changes
Between sections of a song-form movement, and between the scherzo and trio.

## Breathing
Phrases are clear and regular; the accompaniment thins at each cadence and the
texture is never allowed to become dense.

## What to avoid
- **Muddiness.** Low, thick writing is not this composer.
- **Heavy rubato.** The pulse is steadier than in Chopin or Schumann.
- **Irregular phrasing.** Mendelssohn is Classically square.
