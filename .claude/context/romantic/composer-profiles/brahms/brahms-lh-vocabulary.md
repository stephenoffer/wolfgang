# Brahms Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Brahms's left hand is **thick, low and rhythmically independent**. Its defining
properties are wide spacing (tenths rather than octaves), constant cross-rhythm
against the right hand, and a bass that is genuinely contrapuntal rather than
supporting. Generated "Brahms" is usually too thin and too rhythmically aligned.

## LH idiom catalog

1. **Rolled tenths** — `C2e E3e C2e E3e`. Brahms's hands were large and the tenth
   is his signature interval. Wider and warmer than an octave.

2. **Broken chords in wide spacing** — `F2e C3e Ab3e C4e F3e C3e`. Spanning two
   octaves, filling the middle register that Chopin leaves open.

3. **Cross-rhythm against the melody** — triplets in the left hand under duple in
   the right, or the reverse, sustained for whole phrases. Two against three is
   his normal state, not an effect.

4. **Hemiola at cadences** — the 3/4 regrouped as 3/2 across two bars approaching
   a close. So characteristic it is almost a fingerprint.

5. **Doubled octaves in the bass** — `[C2,C3]h`. Weight and darkness.

6. **Chorale texture in the left hand** — four real voices moving together in the
   lower register, drawn from his study of early music.

7. **Contrapuntal bass** — the left hand imitates or answers the melody rather
   than accompanying it. Brahms is the most contrapuntal Romantic.

8. **Sustained inner pedal** — a held middle voice while the bass and melody move
   around it. Thickens without adding motion.

9. **Arpeggio with the thumb carrying a countermelody** — the top of the LH figure
   forms a second tune.

10. **Syncopated accompaniment** — the chord entering off the beat throughout a
    phrase, so the downbeat has no chord under it.

11. **Bare octaves for a stark statement** — usually at an opening or a return.

## Where the idiom changes
At the arrival of a new theme, and at the point of a developing variation — the
figure evolves gradually rather than being replaced.

## Breathing
Brahms's textures are continuous; the breathing is in the *harmony* arriving at a
point of repose, and in the register dropping. Full silence is rare.

## What to avoid
- **Thin two-part texture.** Brahms fills the middle.
- **Rhythmic alignment between the hands.** The cross-rhythm is the style.
- **Bright, high writing.** The centre of gravity is low.
- **Ornamental filigree.** His decoration is contrapuntal, not decorative.
