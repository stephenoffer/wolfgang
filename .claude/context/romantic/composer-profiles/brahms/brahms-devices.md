# Brahms Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `brahms-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact is **developing
variation**: a motif is never simply repeated, it is altered slightly on every
appearance, so the music evolves continuously rather than restating.

## Rhythmic devices

1. **Hemiola** — 3/4 heard as 3/2 across two bars, especially before a cadence.
   The single most Brahmsian rhythmic gesture.
2. **Two against three** — duple in one hand, triple in the other, sustained.
3. **Syncopation across the barline** — the melody entering off the beat and tied
   over, so the metre is felt rather than heard.
4. **Metric displacement** — the same figure re-entered a beat later than
   expected, holding the displacement for several bars.
5. **Rhythmic augmentation of a motif** within the same passage.

## Melodic and motivic devices

6. **Developing variation** — each restatement of a motif differs: an interval
   widened, a rhythm dotted, a note added. Never a literal repeat.
7. **Motivic saturation** — every voice derived from the same cell, including the
   accompaniment.
8. **Melody built from thirds and sixths** — the intervals themselves are the
   signature sound.
9. **Long-breathed line over a shorter harmonic rhythm.**
10. **The falling third as a motto** — three descending notes, used to open and to
    unify.

## Harmonic devices

11. **Modal mixture throughout** — ♭VI, ♭III and iv in a major key as constant
    colour rather than as an event.
12. **Third-related key areas** rather than dominant ones.
13. **Suspension and appoggiatura on nearly every strong beat** — the harmonic
    surface is dense with prepared dissonance.
14. **Pedal point with chromatic movement above** for a long build.
15. **Plagal inflection at the end** — the final cadence coloured by the
    subdominant.
16. **Chromatic inner-voice motion** while the outer voices hold.

## Structural devices

17. **Sonata form with a developing exposition** — development begins before the
    exposition ends.
18. **Variation form as a whole movement** — a ground bass or a passacaglia.
19. **Cyclic return of a motif across movements.**
20. **The quiet ending** — dissolving rather than concluding loudly.
21. **Archaism** — a chorale, a canon, a passacaglia embedded in Romantic
    harmony.

## What to avoid
- **Literal repetition.** The style is defined by its absence.
- **Thin texture and high register.**
- **Rhythmic squareness.** Something should always be pulling against the metre.
- **Virtuosity for display.** The difficulty is textural, not brilliant.
