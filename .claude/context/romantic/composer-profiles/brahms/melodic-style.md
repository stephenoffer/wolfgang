# Johannes Brahms — Melodic Style

Brahms's melodies are not display pieces — they are arguments. A Brahms theme exists to be developed, combined, inverted, fragmented. Yet the melodies themselves are among the most beautiful in Romantic music: wide-spanning, folk-inflected, often singing from the inner voice rather than the soprano. The paradox: melodies designed for intellectual development that move the heart.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Where It Appears |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Wide compass | Melodies spanning an octave or more; large intervallic leaps (6ths, octaves) within the line | Symphony themes, concerto themes, Requiem vocal lines |
| Developing variation | The theme transforms motivically across the movement — never literal repetition; always growth | All mature works; the organizing principle of Brahms's melodic thought |
| Inner-voice melody | The primary melody placed in tenor or alto register, not soprano | Clarinet Quintet, Symphony 3 mvt 3, Intermezzi |
| Folk-song contour | Simple, stepwise melodies with narrow range — borrowed from German Volkslieder | Wiegenlied, Academic Festival Overture, symphony second themes |
| Parallel-third harmonization | Melody doubled a third below (or sixth above); never left bare in climactic passages | Piano works, orchestral doublings, choral writing |
| Rhythmic displacement | Melody avoids downbeat; enters on beat 2 or 3, or after the bar line | Symphony 1 mvt 1, most first themes |
| Phrase extension | 4-bar phrases extended to 5, 7, or 9 bars by internal repetition or suffix | Everywhere — regular 4+4 phrasing is rare in mature Brahms |

## Melodic Intervals — Signature Shapes

| Interval | Frequency | Expressive Use | Example Context |
|----------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Rising 3rd | Very common | Warmth, gentle aspiration | Opening of Wiegenlied, Symphony 2 main theme |
| Rising 6th | Common | Yearning, expansive emotion | Symphony 3 opening (F-D), Violin Concerto |
| Falling 3rd | Very common | Sighing, resignation, tenderness | Intermezzo Op. 118/2, Clarinet Quintet |
| Octave leap | Moderate | Declaration, arrival, structural weight | Piano Concerto 2 opening, Symphony 1 mvt 4 |
| Falling 5th | Moderate | Cadential, settling, conclusive | Phrase endings throughout |
| Rising 4th (perfect) | Less common | Horn-call, open, pastoral | Hungarian-inflected themes |
| Augmented 2nd | Rare | Hungarian/Romani flavor | Hungarian Dances, Clarinet Quintet finale |

```abc
X:1
T:Brahms Wide-Spanning Melody — Rising 6th opening (Symphony 3 character)
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:F
%% The melody leaps a 6th, then fills it in stepwise — the Brahms arch
!f!F A d|c B A|G2 F|E3|
%% 6th leap (F-D) followed by stepwise descent — tension created, then resolved
```

## Developing Variation — How Brahms Transforms Themes

| Technique | How It Works | Musical Effect |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Intervallic expansion | A rising 3rd becomes a rising 6th; the contour stays, the scale grows | Theme feels larger, more urgent, without losing identity |
| Rhythmic augmentation | Quarter notes become half notes; the theme slows within faster surroundings | Theme gains weight and gravitas |
| Rhythmic diminution | Half notes become quarters or eighths; the theme accelerates | Intensification, compression toward climax |
| Inversion | Rising melody becomes falling melody | Mirror-image: same intervals, opposite emotional direction |
| Fragmentation | Only the first 2–3 notes of the theme used as a motive | Development section building blocks; mosaic construction |
| Melodic reharmonization | Same melody, different harmonic support (major - minor, I - bVI) | The same thought in a different emotional light |
| Metric displacement | Theme shifted by one beat within the bar | Disorientation, rhythmic freshness |

```abc
X:2
T:Developing Variation — Theme + 3 transformations
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:Dm
%% Original theme: falling 3rd, rising step, falling step
!mp!D2 B,2 C2|D4 z2|
%% Augmented (double note values):
D4 B,4|C4 D4|
%% Inverted (intervals reversed):
D2 F2 E2|D4 z2|
%% Fragmented (first 3 notes only, sequenced):
D2 B,2 C2|E2 C2 D2|F2 D2 E2|
```

## Inner-Voice Melody

Brahms places his most expressive melodies in the middle of the texture — the clarinet's chalumeau register, the viola, the cello's tenor range, the piano's inner voice. The soprano carries harmonic figuration while the real song is buried within.

| Register | Instrument | Character | Key Works |
|----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Tenor (C3–G4) | Cello, clarinet, viola, piano LH | Warm, intimate, confessional | Clarinet Quintet, Symphony 3 mvt 3 |
| Alto (F3–C5) | Clarinet, horn, viola | Autumnal, veiled | Clarinet Sonatas, Intermezzo Op. 117/1 |
| Soprano doubled | Violin I + violin II in 3rds | Rich, full, never exposed | Symphony themes in orchestral tutti |

```abc
X:3
T:Brahms Inner-Voice Melody — Alto register (Intermezzo character)
M:3/8
L:1/8
K:Eb
V:1 name="Upper figuration"
V:2 name="Inner melody (the real song)"
%% The melody is in V:2; V:1 provides the harmonic canopy
[V:1] G B e|f e d|e B G|
[V:2] E,2 G,|A,2 B,|G,2 E,|
%% The ear follows the inner voice — the upper part is accompaniment
```

## Folk-Song Influence

| Source | What Brahms Absorbed | Where It Appears |
|--------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Deutsche Volkslieder (49 songs, WoO 33) | Simple stepwise contours, strophic repetition, modal inflections | Second themes, variation themes, choral works |
| Hungarian/Romani music | Augmented 2nds, dotted rhythms, "verbunkos" scale | Hungarian Dances, Clarinet Quintet finale, Piano Quartet Op. 25 finale |
| North German chorale | Hymn-like phrases, plagal endings, modal ambiguity | German Requiem, Symphony 1 finale chorale theme |

## Phrase Structure

| Pattern | Description | Frequency |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| 4+4 (regular) | Balanced antecedent-consequent | Rare in mature works; used deliberately for simplicity |
| 4+5 or 4+6 | Consequent phrase extended by suffix or internal repetition | Very common — the "normal" Brahms phrase |
| 3+3+2 | Asymmetric grouping; avoids the square | Common in 3/4 and 6/8 movements |
| Elided | New phrase begins on the last note/beat of the previous phrase | Frequent; gives continuity without caesura |
| Chain | Phrases overlap; no clear boundary between them | Development sections, late piano works |
