# Chopin Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `chopin-lh-vocabulary.md`. The left-hand file covers the wide,
pedalled accompaniment; this covers what happens above it. The single organising
fact: **Chopin's melody is a singer's line**, modelled on Italian opera, and
almost every device below exists to make a piano sing.

## Melodic devices

1. **Written-out ornamental flourish** — a cadenza-like run of small notes over a
   held or absent accompaniment, notated in real values (often 32nds or a burst
   of irregular groupings). Placed at a phrase's peak or before a return. The
   left hand usually stops.

2. **The ornamented return** — a melody comes back decorated rather than plain.
   This is Chopin's principal variation technique: same notes, more of them,
   arriving in a different order around the same anchors.

3. **Appoggiatura sigh** — a dissonance on the beat resolving down by step,
   slurred: `(Ab5q G5q)`. The core expressive gesture, and it should recur.

4. **Rubato against a steady bass** — the melody written with values that do not
   align evenly with the accompaniment: a triplet against duple, a quintuplet
   over four, dotted rhythms over even ones.

5. **Chromatic passing motion inside a diatonic line** — the melody steps through
   notes outside the key on its way between chord tones. Colour without
   modulation.

6. **Long-breathed line** — a phrase of eight or more bars that does not cadence
   in the middle. The line arches once and lands once.

7. **Melodic leap of a sixth or seventh, filled in on the way back** — the
   operatic gesture: an expressive upward leap, then a stepwise descent from it.

8. **Repeated note with changing harmony beneath** — the melody holds one pitch
   while the chord under it moves, so the same note means something different in
   each bar.

9. **The melody in the tenor** — the tune moves to the left hand's upper register
   with accompaniment above and below it. Used for a middle section or a
   transformed return.

## Harmonic devices

10. **Chromatic voice leading** — inner voices sliding by semitone between chords
    that would otherwise be plain. The harmony is often simple; the *motion*
    between chords is the interest.

11. **Enharmonic pivot** — a diminished seventh or an augmented sixth respelled to
    land in a remote key. Chopin modulates further and faster than any of his
    contemporaries.

12. **Neapolitan and augmented-sixth colour** at moments of pathos, especially in
    the mazurkas and nocturnes.

13. **Modal inflection from Polish folk music** — a raised fourth (Lydian) or a
    flattened seventh in the mazurkas, and drone fifths in the bass. This is what
    separates a mazurka from a waltz.

14. **A dominant that refuses to resolve** — the chord held, decorated, delayed
    over several bars before the tonic finally arrives.

15. **Parallel diminished sevenths descending** — a sequence of the same chord
    shape sliding down by semitone or minor third. Transitional, and it dissolves
    the key.

## Structural and rhythmic devices

16. **Ternary with an ornamented return** — A, contrasting B, then A again with
    the melody decorated. The nocturne and mazurka default.

17. **The coda that dissolves** — the texture thins, the register rises or falls
    away, the dynamic drops to `pp` or `ppp`. Chopin rarely ends loudly.

18. **Mazurka rhythm** — the stress on beat 2 or 3 rather than 1, in 3/4. Written
    as a dotted or accented second beat. Without it a mazurka is a waltz.

19. **Rhythmic dissolution before a return** — the pulse becomes ambiguous through
    irregular groupings, then the return re-establishes it.

20. **Bel canto phrasing marks** — long slurs over whole phrases, not bar by bar.
    A slur that stops at the barline is not this idiom.

## What to avoid

- **A melody in even eighths.** There is nothing for the rubato to be free
  against, and the line stops singing.
- **Short, bar-by-bar slurring.** The phrase is the unit.
- **A plain return.** If A comes back exactly as it was, the variation technique
  is missing.
- **Block chords in the middle register.** The texture collapses into a hymn.
- **No pedal.** These textures are physically impossible without it.
