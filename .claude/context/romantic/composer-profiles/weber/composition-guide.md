# Weber — Composition Guide

## Fingerprints
Any section claiming Weber's style needs ≥3 of these 5 present.

1. **Operatic melody in instrumental clothing** — Themes are singers' lines: an arching phrase with a clear breath point, an appoggiatura leaning into the strong beat, and a cadential turn. Even a piano theme is written as though someone has to breathe in the middle of it.
2. **The diminished seventh as a dramatic hinge** — Weber reaches for a fully-diminished seventh at moments of alarm or transition, often approached by a sudden dynamic drop and resolved into a remote key. It is the sound of the supernatural in *Der Freischütz* and it recurs everywhere in the instrumental music.
3. **Brilliant, wide-spanning keyboard figuration** — Broken chords spanning a tenth or more, rapid scale flourishes, and leaps that assume a large hand. Weber's own reach was exceptional and the writing shows it; the texture is more spread out than Beethoven's for the same harmonic content.
4. **Abrupt dynamic and registral contrast** — A phrase stated forte in the bass answered piano two octaves up, or a sudden subito piano at a structural arrival. The contrasts are theatrical and placed at joins, not smoothed over.
5. **Horn-call and folk-dance topics** — Open fifths and thirds in a horn-fifth pattern for the outdoors, and lilting compound-metre or waltz rhythms for the vernacular. These are quotations of a sound-world, and they arrive marked.

---

## Where he sits

Weber writes with Classical phrase structure and Romantic harmonic colour and orchestral imagination — closer to Beethoven than to Chopin in bar-to-bar construction, but reaching for effects of atmosphere and key colour that belong to the next generation. When in doubt, keep the periodic phrasing and let the harmony and register carry the Romanticism.
