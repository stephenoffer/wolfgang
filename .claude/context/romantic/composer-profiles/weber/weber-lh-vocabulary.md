# Weber Left-Hand Vocabulary

**A caveat first, because the corpus cannot support the usual measurements.**
Weber's armed corpus is 241 bars from a **single clarinet work**. Measuring
right-hand thickness on it returns 0% chords — true of a clarinet, meaningless
for a composer whose piano writing is famously wide and brilliant. The
`voicing_profile` primitive suppresses the number for exactly this reason. What
the corpus *can* say: mostly **2/2 and 6/8**, left hand `block_chord_sparse`
(37%), `block_chord_offbeat` (15%), `pedal_point` (12%), and a harmonic
vocabulary leaning on `V7` and applied dominants (`V7/IV`).

Everything below is craft knowledge rather than corpus measurement, and is
marked as such so nobody mistakes it for a statistic.

The organising fact: **Weber writes for a virtuoso with very large hands.** He
is the bridge from Beethoven to Liszt, and the left hand covers ground.

## The patterns

1. **The wide-spanning broken chord** — a tenth or more between the bass and the
   top of the figure, rolling in even values. The gesture Liszt inherited.
   `C2e G2e C3e E3e G3e E3e C3e G2e`

2. **Rapid repeated chords** — the same chord struck in even eighths or
   sixteenths under a melody, driving and orchestral.

3. **The horn-fifths accompaniment** — parallel thirds and sixths in the left
   hand imitating a horn pair. His signature, and the sound of *Der Freischütz*.
   `[C3,E3]q [B2,D3]q [C3,E3]h`

4. **Offbeat chords over a marked bass** — the waltz and the polonaise
   inheritance, 15% of the armed bars.

5. **Long pedal point under a build** — 12% of bars; used for the operatic
   crescendos.

6. **Octave tremolo in the bass** for storm and supernatural scenes.

## Register

Wider than Beethoven, narrower than Liszt. The left hand routinely leaves the
bass to cross above the right in a solo passage, then returns.

## What to avoid

- **A narrow left hand.** If it never spans a tenth, it is not Weber.
- **Alberti bass.** He has moved past it.
- **Chopin's rubato-driven flow.** Weber's rhythm is theatrical and marked.
- **Treating him as early Classical.** The harmony uses applied dominants and
  chromatic mediants freely.
