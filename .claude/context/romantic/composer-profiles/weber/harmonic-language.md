# Weber — Harmonic Language

Weber's harmony is Classical in its grammar and Romantic in what it is used for.
The functional machinery is intact — he cadences properly and modulates by pivot
— but he reaches for the diminished seventh and the chromatic third relation as
*dramatic* events, and he is the composer who fixes the supernatural in music
through harmony rather than through melody.

The Wolf's Glen scene is the reference point: a diminished seventh sustained and
reinterpreted until the key is genuinely lost.

---

## Cadences

| Cadence | Approach | Where it belongs | Effect |
|---------|----------|------------------|--------|
| Perfect authentic | V7–I, soprano on 1 | Aria and overture closes | Full closure |
| Cadential 6-4 | I64–V7–I | Big structural arrivals | Weight, delay |
| Half cadence | ii6 or IV to V | Antecedent phrases | Question |
| Deceptive | V–vi, or V–bVI | Extending a lyrical phrase | The flat-6 version is his Romantic colour |
| Diminished-seventh interruption | vii°7 held, then reinterpreted | Dramatic scenes; the supernatural | The key is suspended, not changed |
| Horn-call plagal | IV–I with horns in thirds | Forest and hunting music | The German pastoral signature |

---

## Chromatic devices

| Device | Construction | Dramatic use |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| Diminished seventh | Stacked minor thirds | Terror, the uncanny; any of four resolutions |
| Enharmonic reinterpretation | The same dim7 respelled | Sudden, motivated key shift |
| Chromatic mediant | I to bIII, I to bVI | The magical, the distant |
| Augmented sixth | It/Fr/Ger to V | Intensifying a dominant arrival |
| Neapolitan | bII6 before V | Pathos in minor-key arias |
| Modal mixture | Borrowing the parallel minor's flat-6 and flat-3 | Shadow across a major-key phrase |
| Tremolo pedal under shifting harmony | Sustained bass, chords moving above | Atmosphere; the operatic storm |

The diminished seventh is not decoration for him. It is a plot device: held,
respelled, and resolved somewhere the listener did not expect.

---

## Modulation

| From | To | How |
|------|----|-----|
| Tonic | Dominant | Standard pivot; sonata expositions |
| Major | Relative / parallel minor | Direct mixture, often mid-phrase |
| Anywhere | Chromatic mediant | Direct, unprepared, for colour |
| Anywhere | Anywhere | Enharmonic dim7 pivot, for shock |

---

## What this language will not do

- Abandon functional harmony. Underneath the chromaticism the cadences are
  textbook; this is not Wagner.
- Sustain ambiguity for long. The dim7 passages resolve; the uncanny is an
  episode, not a condition.
- Use chromaticism decoratively. Every chromatic event answers to a dramatic
  situation.
