# Weber — Orchestration

Weber is the first orchestrator for whom instrumental colour is *subject matter*.
The clarinet is a character, the horn quartet is a landscape, and the low
clarinets with divided violas in the Wolf's Glen are the sound of the
supernatural. Berlioz learned from him.

---

## The instruments as he uses them

| Instrument | Range (sounding) | His use |
|------------|------------------|---------|
| Clarinet | D3–G6 | His voice. Chalumeau for dread, clarion for lyricism; the concertos are the repertoire |
| Horn | B1–F5 | In fours, in close thirds — the German forest itself |
| Flute | C4–C7 | Brilliance; doubling the melody 8va at climaxes |
| Oboe | Bb3–G6 | Plaintive solo; pastoral |
| Bassoon | Bb1–Eb5 | Comic, and also grotesque in the low register |
| Trumpet | E3–C6 | Fanfare, alarm |
| Trombones | E2–C5 | Solemnity, and the demonic |
| Timpani | C2–C4 | Tremolo and roll for atmosphere, not just rhythm |
| Strings | — | Tremolo as an atmospheric device more than as texture |

---

## Signature combinations

| Combination | Sound | Dramatic use |
|-------------|-------|--------------|
| Four horns in close harmony | Warm, distant, outdoor | Forest, hunt, nostalgia |
| Low clarinets + divided violas | Hollow, unlit | The supernatural |
| Tremolo strings + timpani roll | Suspense | Storm, dread |
| Clarinet solo over sustained strings | Song without words | Lyrical centre of a movement |
| Piccolo + trumpet + full tutti | Brilliant, hard-edged | Climaxes and codas |
| Pizzicato bass under wind chorus | Light, transparent | Pastoral episodes |

---

## Principles

- Colour marks structure. A returning theme returns in a new colour; that is how
  the recapitulation is heard as an event.
- The wind section is treated as a choir with its own identity, not as
  reinforcement for the strings.
- Dynamic range is wide and used dramatically — real pianissimo, real fortissimo,
  and long crescendi over tremolo.
- Doubling is chosen for timbre, not for volume.
