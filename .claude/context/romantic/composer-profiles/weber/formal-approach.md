# Weber — Formal Approach

Weber inherits Classical forms and stretches them toward the theatre. His
concert works are in sonata form and rondo; his operas replace number-opera
convention with continuous scenes; and he invents the concert overture as a
dramatic form in its own right.

---

## Forms

| Form | Where he uses it | What he does to it |
|------|------------------|--------------------|
| Sonata form | Overtures, concerto first movements | Themes are operatic; the second subject is a real melody |
| Slow introduction | Before an overture's allegro | Establishes atmosphere and often quotes the opera to come |
| Rondo | Concerto finales | Fast, brilliant, dance-derived |
| Ternary | Arias, slow movements | Reprise usually ornamented |
| Scene complex | Opera | Recitative, arioso and aria run together without a stop |
| Concert overture | Freistanding | Sonata form built out of the opera's own themes |

The overture-as-preview is his structural invention: the exposition's themes are
the opera's themes, so the form does double duty as drama.

---

## Sonata form, Weber's version

| Section | Content | His deviation |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Slow introduction | Horn chorus, or tremolo atmosphere | Frequently the emotional centre of the piece |
| First subject | Energetic, often arpeggiated | Instrumental, not vocal |
| Transition | Modulating, sequential | Often via chromatic mediant rather than pivot |
| Second subject | A long lyrical melody, clarinet or cello | Genuinely tuneful; a set piece |
| Development | Fragmentation over tremolo | Dramatic rather than contrapuntal |
| Recapitulation | Both subjects in tonic | Second subject often reorchestrated, not merely transposed |
| Coda | Accelerating, brilliant | Extended; the real climax |

---

## Proportions

- Slow introductions run 20–40 bars and set the key by implication, not
  assertion.
- Second-subject groups are long — he will let a melody run its full length
  rather than fragment it early.
- Codas are disproportionately large; the piece ends by accelerating.
