# Weber Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `weber-lh-vocabulary.md`, which carries the corpus caveat: 241 bars
from one clarinet work, so this catalogue is craft knowledge rather than
measurement.

The organising fact: **Weber is a man of the theatre.** Even his instrumental
music is operatic — it has characters, scenery and stage effects. He founded
German Romantic opera and invented much of what the nineteenth century then
treated as standard.

## Dramatic devices

1. **The reminiscence motif** — a theme attached to a character or an idea,
   returning transformed when that idea returns. Wagner's leitmotif in embryo.

2. **The slow, dark introduction** before a fast movement — diminished harmony,
   low register, tremolo, resolving into the main allegro.

3. **The storm scene** — chromatic scales, diminished sevenths, tremolo,
   sforzandi. The Wolf's Glen is the model for a century of them.

4. **The horn call** — an open fifth-and-third fanfare in the natural-horn
   register, standing for the forest. His single most identifiable gesture.

5. **Sudden dynamic and textural contrast** for theatrical effect: full texture
   to bare octaves in one bar.

## Harmonic devices

6. **The diminished seventh as the engine of drama** — used for suspense, for
   modulation, and for the supernatural. He leans on it far harder than
   Beethoven.

7. **Applied dominants everywhere** — `V7/IV` is among the commonest harmonies
   in the armed corpus.

8. **Chromatic mediant shifts** for a change of scene.

9. **Enharmonic reinterpretation** of a diminished seventh to jump keys
   instantly.

10. **Long dominant preparation** before an aria or a theme's return.

## Melodic devices

11. **The wide singing leap** — a sixth or an octave upward into a sustained
    note, vocal in origin.

12. **Arpeggiated melody outlining the triad** — the horn and clarinet
    inheritance, so the tune often moves by chord tones rather than by step.

13. **Dotted rhythms in march and polonaise character.**

14. **The brilliant closing flourish** — a rapid scale or arpeggio to end a
    section, written out.

## Form

15. **The concert piece in linked sections** (Konzertstück) rather than separate
    movements — a form he effectively created.

16. **The dance movement as a character piece**, most famously the waltz treated
    as concert music.

## What to avoid

- **Classical restraint.** The gestures are large and the contrasts are abrupt.
- **Dense counterpoint.** His textures are melody-and-accompaniment, scored
  theatrically.
- **A narrow keyboard span.** See the vocabulary note.
- **Understating the diminished seventh.** It is the harmonic centre of his
  dramatic language, not an occasional colour.
