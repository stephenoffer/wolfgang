# Weber — Melodic Style

An opera composer's melodies, written for instruments. Even a piano theme is
shaped as though a singer has to get through it on one breath.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Artistic Function |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Singer's arch with a breath point | A rising phrase, a clear peak, a falling answer, and an audible place to breathe in the middle | Gives an instrumental line the shape an audience already knows from the stage |
| Appoggiatura on the strong beat | The line leans onto a dissonance above the chord tone and resolves down | The characteristic sigh of the idiom; used at the emotional word of an imagined text |
| Cadential turn | A four-note turn decorating the penultimate note of a phrase | Closes the phrase with a vocal flourish rather than a plain step |
| Horn-fifth topic | Open fifths and thirds moving in parallel, in the horn's rhythm | Instantly evokes the forest, the hunt, the outdoors |
| Wide-spanning arpeggiation | Broken chords covering a tenth or more, assuming a large hand | Fills the harmonic space without thickening the texture |
| Sudden register displacement | The phrase is answered two octaves away | Theatrical contrast placed at a join, not smoothed over |
| Dance lilt | Compound-metre or waltz rhythms with a light second beat | The vernacular voice against the operatic one |

## Where the line sits

Classical periodic phrasing — four-bar units, antecedent and consequent — carrying
Romantic harmonic colour. When in doubt keep the phrase structure square and let
the harmony and the register do the Romanticism.
