# Franz Schubert — Biography

A schoolteacher's son who never held a court position, never traveled far, never lived to see his symphonies performed — and yet produced over a thousand works in thirty-one years, including some of the most profound music in Western civilization. Schubert's life was limitation transformed into abundance.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1797 | Vienna (Lichtental) | Born, son of schoolmaster Franz Theodor | Modest household; music was communal, domestic — the family string quartet began here |
| 1808 | Vienna | Enters Imperial Court Chapel choir (Konvikt) | Studies with Salieri; immersion in Mozart, Haydn; sings treble in the school orchestra |
| 1810–13 | Konvikt | Composes first songs, string quartets, symphony attempts | The prodigious output begins: 7 string quartets before age 16 |
| 1814 | Vienna | "Gretchen am Spinnrade" (D.118) | Age 17: the birth of the Romantic Lied. Piano becomes the emotional narrator |
| 1815 | Vienna | "Erlkönig" (D.328); 144 songs in one year | Miraculous year: the Lied as an art form is invented in real time |
| 1816–18 | Vienna | Leaves school; attempts at opera; first mature symphonies | Financial precarity begins; the teaching career is abandoned |
| 1818 | Zseliz, Hungary | Summer as music tutor to Esterházy family | Only significant journey from Vienna; Hungarian musical elements absorbed |
| 1819–20 | Vienna | "Trout" Quintet, Quartettsatz D.703 | Chamber music innovations; the quintet adds double bass for new sonority |
| 1820–23 | Vienna | Schubertiades become regular gatherings | The intimate concert: friends' living rooms, not concert halls — Schubert's natural habitat |
| 1822 | Vienna | "Unfinished" Symphony No. 8 in B minor | Two movements of devastating originality; then silence — why he stopped remains a mystery |
| 1823 | Vienna | Die schöne Müllerin (D.795); serious illness begins | First complete song cycle; syphilis diagnosis; the shadow of mortality enters |
| 1824 | Vienna | String Quartets D.804 ("Rosamunde") and D.810 ("Death and the Maiden") | Illness deepens the music: D.810's variations on his own death song |
| 1827 | Vienna | Attends Beethoven's funeral as torchbearer | The giant dies; Schubert inherits the symphonic challenge |
| 1827–28 | Vienna | Winterreise (D.911) | The greatest song cycle: 24 songs of despair, wandering, frozen landscape; friends were disturbed |
| 1828 | Vienna | Final year: Symphony No. 9 "Great C Major," Piano Sonatas D.958-960, String Quintet D.956 | Concentrated masterworks in every genre; the final creative explosion |
| 1828 | Vienna | Dies November 19, age 31 | Typhoid fever; buried near Beethoven as he requested |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Poverty and the Domestic Sphere

| Aspect | How It Shaped Schubert |
|--------|----------------------|
| No patron, no position | Music was not a profession — it was existence; compositions given to friends, lost, unpublished |
| The Schubertiade | Intimate gatherings of 15–30 friends; music performed in living rooms, not concert halls |
| Domestic scale | The song, the piano piece, the string quartet — these were natural to the Schubertiade; the symphony was the exception |
| Collaborative art | Poets, painters, singers in the same circle; Schubert set his friends' poetry; music and literature were inseparable |

### Illness and Mortality

| Dimension | Musical Impact |
|-----------|---------------|
| Syphilis (from 1822) | The late music's awareness of death is not abstract — it is lived. "Death and the Maiden" is autobiography |
| Physical decline | Energy concentrated into fewer, larger works after 1825; the late sonatas are expansive, unhurried — as if time itself needed to be stretched |
| Awareness of ending | Winterreise's wanderer knows he will not return. The String Quintet's slow movement achieves a serenity that can only come from acceptance |

### The Beethoven Shadow

| Aspect | Schubert's Response |
|--------|---------------------|
| Symphonic ambition | The "Great C Major" Symphony answers Beethoven not with drama but with melody — the lyrical symphony |
| Sonata form | Schubert does not fight through sonata form; he wanders through it; the key relationships are coloristic, not dramatic |
| The funeral | "Who can do anything after Beethoven?" — and then the final year produced the answer |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Prodigious youth | 1811–1817 | Hundreds of songs, early symphonies; raw melodic gift; Classical frameworks | Erlkönig, Gretchen, Symphonies 1–5, early quartets |
| Search for large form | 1818–1823 | Chamber experiments, opera attempts (mostly failed), first mature works | Trout Quintet, Quartettsatz, Wanderer Fantasy, Die schöne Müllerin |
| Dark maturity | 1824–1826 | Illness transforms the voice; death becomes a subject; harmonic language deepens | "Death and the Maiden" Quartet, "Rosamunde" Quartet, Piano Sonata D.845, Octet |
| Final concentration | 1827–1828 | Every genre reaches its summit simultaneously; the last year is miraculous | Winterreise, Great C Major Symphony, Piano Sonatas D.958-960, String Quintet D.956, Schwanengesang |
