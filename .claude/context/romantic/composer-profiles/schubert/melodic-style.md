# Franz Schubert — Melodic Style

Everything Schubert writes sings. His instrumental themes have the shape of a voice drawing breath, the cadence of a spoken sentence, the instinct for where a word would fall. This is not metaphor — it is method. The six hundred songs trained his melodic imagination so deeply that every string quartet theme, every symphonic melody, every piano sonata subject carries the DNA of the Lied.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Where It Appears |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Vocal contour | Melodies move within a singer's natural range (usually an octave); stepwise motion dominates, leaps are expressive events | All genres — the song origin is inescapable |
| Phrase extension | Phrases do not end at bar 4 or 8 as expected; they find one more inflection, one more bar | Sonata themes, song melodies, slow movements |
| Melodic abundance | Schubert generates themes prolifically — a sonata exposition may contain 3–4 distinct melodies where Beethoven would develop one | Expositions, song cycles, chamber works |
| Syllabic natural rhythm | Melody rhythm follows speech patterns: short-short-long, anacrusis-to-downbeat, breathing pauses | Songs and instrumental works alike |
| The descending phrase | Many Schubert melodies arch upward and then descend stepwise to rest — the shape of a sigh or an exhaled sentence | Slow movements, Lieder, second themes |
| Repetition with color change | The same melody repeated in a different key, mode, or register — not varied, but recolored | Major-minor flip; third-relation restatements |

## Melodic Contour Types

| Contour | Shape | Emotional Character | Example Works |
|---------|-------|--------------------|--------------|
| Rising arch | Stepwise ascent to peak, then stepwise descent | Yearning fulfilled and released | "An die Musik," Symphony No. 8 second theme |
| Descending sigh | Begins at peak, descends through the phrase | Resignation, tenderness, farewell | Winterreise "Gute Nacht," Piano Sonata D.960 opening |
| Hovering | Melody circles around 2–3 notes, barely moving | Obsession, trance, frozen emotion | "Der Leiermann," "Wasserflut" |
| Leap-and-fill | Expressive leap (usually upward 6th or octave), then stepwise descent to fill the gap | Emotional outburst followed by recovery | "Erlkönig" (child's cries), "Gretchen am Spinnrade" climax |
| Strophic return | Melody returns identically but harmony beneath it has changed | Same words, different meaning | Song cycles throughout |

## The Lied as Foundation

| Lied Principle | How It Transfers to Instrumental Music |
|---------------|---------------------------------------|
| Text-painting | Instrumental melodies retain the shape of word-setting; a rising phrase "asks," a falling phrase "answers" |
| Accompaniment as narrator | The piano (or strings) tells the story beneath the melody — the spinning wheel, the brook, the hurdy-gurdy |
| Strophic repetition | Instrumental themes return in varied strophes: same melody, new harmony, new key, new register |
| Dialogue between voice and piano | Instrumental works retain the conversation: melody and accompaniment are two characters |
| Through-composed drama | Some songs (Erlkönig) are through-composed; this feeds the developmental passages of sonatas and quartets |

## Melodic Intervals — Frequency and Meaning

| Interval | Frequency | Expressive Use |
|----------|-----------|---------------|
| Minor 2nd (step) | Very high | The basic melodic motion; most Schubert melodies are predominantly stepwise |
| Major 2nd (step) | Very high | Diatonic stepwise motion; the "walking" melody |
| Minor 3rd | Moderate | Gentle skip; often part of arpeggiated figures |
| Major 3rd | Low-moderate | Sweetness; the major-mode declaration |
| Perfect 4th | Moderate | Anacrusis leap (upbeat to downbeat); "Gute Nacht" opening |
| Perfect 5th | Low | Assertive, horn-like; opening of "Great C Major" Symphony |
| Minor 6th | Low | Yearning — the expressive leap that demands stepwise return |
| Octave | Rare | Dramatic; reserved for climactic moments |


## Ornamentation in Melody

| Ornament | Schubert's Use | Context |
|----------|---------------|---------|
| Appoggiatura | Frequent; the leaning dissonance on strong beats gives vocal melodies their ache | Songs, slow movements |
| Grace note (Vorschlag) | Light, decorative; more common in dance-related themes | Impromptus, Moments Musicaux |
| Turn | Gentle melodic inflection; never virtuosic | Piano sonatas, slow movements |
| Trill | Rare in vocal lines; more common in piano figurations | Impromptus, Wanderer Fantasy |
| Tremolo | Not melodic but atmospheric — the piano tremolo as emotional intensifier | "Erlkönig," "Der Doppelgänger" |

## The Melody-Accompaniment Relationship

| Accompaniment Type | Melodic Behavior | Examples |
|-------------------|-----------------|----------|
| Spinning-wheel sixteenths | Melody moves in longer notes above perpetual motion | "Gretchen am Spinnrade," many songs |
| Walking bass eighths | Melody in quarter/half notes; the bass "walks" beneath | "Gute Nacht," "Das Wandern" |
| Block chords (chorale) | Melody in unison or octaves; accompaniment punctuates | "Der Doppelgänger," slow movements |
| Tremolo | Melody emerges from or floats above tremolo texture | "Erlkönig," "Unfinished" Symphony |
| Alberti/broken chord | Gentle arpeggiation; melody sings freely above | Piano sonatas, Impromptus |
