# Franz Schubert — Formal Approach

Schubert inherited Classical forms and made them sing. Where Beethoven drove sonata form through conflict and resolution, Schubert let it wander — replacing dramatic opposition with lyrical abundance, tonal argument with tonal color, goal-directed motion with exploratory journey. The result is music of "heavenly lengths" (Schumann's phrase): forms that expand not because Schubert couldn't edit, but because the melody always had one more thing to say.

## Core Formal Principles

| Principle | Description | Contrast with Beethoven |
|-----------|-------------|------------------------|
| Lyrical sonata form | Exposition presents multiple melodies rather than one motif developed | Beethoven: 1 theme, many developments. Schubert: 4 themes, each complete |
| Color-based key plan | Second group in mediant (III) or submediant (bVI) rather than dominant (V) | Beethoven: I→V is structural. Schubert: I→III is coloristic |
| The heavenly length | Developments and recapitulations expand beyond Classical proportion through melodic extension | Not "too long" — the form follows the melody's need to breathe |
| Strophic variation | Material returns in the same shape but different harmonic/tonal color | Song form infiltrates instrumental form |
| The wandering development | Development wanders through remote keys via third-relations and chromatic drift | Beethoven fragments and recombines; Schubert journeys |
| Delayed recapitulation | The return of the tonic is not a triumph — it arrives quietly, sometimes unnoticed | Beethoven's recapitulations are events; Schubert's are homecomings |

## Form Types and Schubert's Treatment

### Sonata Form

| Section | Classical Expectation | Schubert's Practice |
|---------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Exposition — 1st group | Single theme, tonic key, decisive | Lyrical theme, often pp; sometimes two themes before transition |
| Transition | Modulatory, driving | May be very brief or nonexistent; the new key simply arrives |
| Exposition — 2nd group | Dominant (V) or relative major | Mediant (III), submediant (bVI), or other third-related key |
| Closing group | Cadential, confirmatory | May introduce yet another new melody |
| Development | Motivic fragmentation, tonal instability | Harmonic wandering; new keys by third-relation; long episodes in remote keys |
| Recapitulation | Tonic return of all exposition material | Quiet return; second group now in tonic but may wander again |
| Coda | Conclusive | Extended; may introduce the divine moment; plagal or fadeaway ending |

```abc
X:1
T:Schubert Sonata Exposition — Three-Theme Model (character sketch)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Bb
%% Theme 1 (tonic, lyrical)
!pp!F2G2 A2B2|c4 d4|
%% Theme 2 (mediant — D major, NOT dominant F)
K:D
!p!^F2A2 d2^c2|d4 A4|
%% Theme 3 (still in D, new character — dance-like)
!mp!d2d2 ^c2B2|A2B2 ^c2d2|
%% Three complete melodies where one would suffice — Schubertian abundance
```

### Lied Forms

| Form | Structure | Schubert's Innovation |
|------|-----------|----------------------|
| Strophic | A-A-A-A | Same music for each verse; the poem's changing words create new meaning over fixed melody |
| Modified strophic | A-A-A'-A | One strophe altered (usually the dramatic one); minimal change, maximum effect |
| Through-composed | A-B-C-D... | Free-flowing: the music follows the text's drama moment by moment |
| Bar form | A-A-B | Two similar strophes, then contrasting material; inherited from Minnesang |
| Ternary (ABA) | A-B-A | Song in three parts; the return of A carries the weight of B's contrast |

### Song Cycle Architecture

| Cycle | Structure | Unifying Devices |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| Die schöne Müllerin (20 songs) | Narrative arc: hope → love → jealousy → despair → death | The brook (piano figuration); key progression from Bb to E to Bb; the wanderer's journey |
| Winterreise (24 songs) | Anti-narrative: circular wandering without resolution | Recurring walking rhythm; D minor orbit; no tonal resolution — the cycle ends unresolved |
| Schwanengesang (14 songs) | Posthumous collection, not Schubert's design | Grouped by poet: Rellstab (7), Heine (6), Seidl (1); tonal contrast between groups |

```abc
X:2
T:Song Cycle Walking Rhythm — Winterreise "Gute Nacht" character
M:2/4
L:1/16
K:Dm
%% The walking bass: steady, mechanical, relentless — the wanderer's footsteps
!p!D2A,2 D2F2|E2A,2 E2G2|F2A,2 F2A2|E2A,2 E2G2|
%% The rhythm never varies; the harmony above it shifts between light and shadow
```

### Variation Form

| Feature | Classical Model | Schubert's Approach |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| Theme character | Often simple, dance-like | The theme is a song — complete, beautiful in itself |
| Variation technique | Melodic decoration, rhythmic diminution | Harmonic recoloring: same melody, different mode/key |
| Major-minor variation | Optional | Essential — the minor variation is the emotional center |
| Final variation | Virtuosic or developmental | Often a return to the theme, simplified — the song comes home |

## "Heavenly Lengths" — Proportional Expansion

| Formal Section | Haydn/Mozart | Beethoven | Schubert |
|---------------|-------------|-----------|----------|
| Exposition | 40–80 bars | 60–120 bars | 80–160 bars (more themes) |
| Development | 30–60 bars | 60–150 bars | 80–200 bars (wandering) |
| Recapitulation | ≈ Exposition | Compressed or expanded | Full restatement, sometimes expanded |
| Coda | 10–30 bars | 30–100 bars | 30–80 bars (divine moment or fadeaway) |
| Total movement | ~200 bars | ~300 bars | ~400+ bars |

## Key-Area Choices in Sonata Expositions

| Tonic | Classical Second Group | Schubert's Second Group | Example |
|-------|----------------------|------------------------|---------|
| C major | G major (V) | E major (III) or Ab major (bVI) | Piano Sonata D.958 |
| Bb major | F major (V) | Gb major (bVI) or D major (III) | Piano Sonata D.960 |
| A minor | C major (III) | C major (III) or F major (bVI) | Piano Sonata D.784 |
| D minor | F major (III) | F major (III) | "Death and the Maiden" Quartet |
| B minor | D major (III) | G major (bVI) | "Unfinished" Symphony |

## Endings and Codas

| Type | Description | Where Used |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Plagal close | IV→I or iv→I replaces V→I at the very end | Chamber works, sonatas |
| Fadeaway | Music diminishes to pp/ppp and dissolves | Late piano sonatas, slow movements |
| Divine-moment coda | The coda contains the transcendent sustained passage | String Quintet slow movement, "Unfinished" Symphony |
| Major-key resolution | Minor-key work ends with tonic major chord or passage | Songs, quartets (tierce de Picardie extended) |
| Circular non-ending | The music returns to its opening — no resolution, only continuation | Winterreise final song ("Der Leiermann") |
