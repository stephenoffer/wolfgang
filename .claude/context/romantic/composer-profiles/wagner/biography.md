# Richard Wagner — Biography

A life spent reshaping the world to fit the art. Wagner did not merely compose operas — he invented a new art form, built a theatre to house it, wrote the philosophy to justify it, and bent an entire century's culture around his will. The music cannot be separated from the man's titanic ambition: every chord carries the weight of a worldview.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1813 | Leipzig | Born | Father dies within a year; stepfather Ludwig Geyer was an actor — the theatre enters Wagner's blood from infancy |
| 1831 | Leipzig | Studies with Thomaskantor Weinlig | Rigorous counterpoint and harmony training; Bach's shadow; Wagner learns to write fugues before he breaks the rules |
| 1834 | Magdeburg | First opera appointment | Early operas (Die Feen, Das Liebesverbot) — Italianate, Weberian; apprentice work |
| 1839 | London/Paris | Flees creditors by sea | The stormy voyage inspires The Flying Dutchman; poverty in Paris hardens him |
| 1842 | Dresden | Rienzi premiered — triumph | Grand opera in the Meyerbeer mold; massive forces, spectacle; Wagner as public figure |
| 1843 | Dresden | Royal Kapellmeister | The Flying Dutchman (1843), Tannhauser (1845), Lohengrin (1848) — the transition from opera to music-drama begins |
| 1849 | Dresden | Joins the May Revolution; arrest warrant | Political exile begins; 13 years banned from Germany; the exile sharpens the theory |
| 1849–52 | Zurich | Writes theoretical works | "The Artwork of the Future," "Opera and Drama" — the Gesamtkunstwerk philosophy codified |
| 1853 | Zurich | Begins Ring cycle composition | Das Rheingold, Die Walkure drafted; the Leitmotif system crystallizes |
| 1857 | Zurich | Breaks off Siegfried Act II | Turns to Tristan und Isolde — the crisis of chromatic harmony |
| 1859 | Venice/Lucerne | Tristan completed | The chord that changed Western music; tonal dissolution as emotional language |
| 1864 | Munich | King Ludwig II rescues Wagner from debt | Royal patronage; Tristan premiered (1865); Die Meistersinger premiered (1868) |
| 1869–74 | Bayreuth | Ring cycle completed and premiered | Bayreuth Festspielhaus built to Wagner's specifications; a theatre for one composer's vision |
| 1882 | Bayreuth | Parsifal premiered | The sacred final work; extreme slowness, ppp textures, communion music |
| 1883 | Venice | Dies, February 13 | Heart failure at Palazzo Vendramin; buried at Wahnfried, Bayreuth |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Revolution and Exile

| Aspect | How It Shaped Wagner |
|--------|---------------------|
| Political idealism | The 1849 revolution was real — Wagner on the barricades with Bakunin; the Ring's critique of power and gold reflects anarchist convictions |
| Exile (1849–1862) | 13 years without hearing his own orchestral music performed; the theoretical works emerge from enforced silence |
| Outsider identity | Wagner as permanent refugee from respectable society; the art must create its own world because the real world rejects the artist |

### The Gesamtkunstwerk Vision

| Element | Wagner's Demand |
|---------|----------------|
| Music + poetry | The composer writes his own libretti — music and words conceived simultaneously, not fitted together |
| Music + staging | Bayreuth: hidden orchestra pit, darkened auditorium, no curtain calls — the audience sees only the drama |
| Music + philosophy | Each opera embodies a philosophical argument: Tristan = Schopenhauer's will, Parsifal = redemption through compassion |
| Total control | Wagner directed, designed sets, chose singers, trained conductors — the first auteur |

### Schopenhauer and the Turn Inward

| Before Schopenhauer (pre-1854) | After Schopenhauer (post-1854) |
|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Art as revolution — change the world | Art as revelation — the world is suffering; music reveals the Will behind appearances |
| Optimistic endings possible | Renunciation as the only true resolution |
| Siegfried as revolutionary hero | Tristan: love and death are the same; Wotan: the god who wills his own end |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Apprentice | 1833–1840 | Italianate, Weberian, conventional | Die Feen, Das Liebesverbot |
| Grand opera | 1840–1845 | Meyerbeer influence, spectacle, but German Romantic roots emerge | Rienzi, Flying Dutchman, Tannhauser |
| Transition | 1845–1852 | Through-composition begins; Leitmotif seeds; theoretical writing | Lohengrin, prose works |
| Music-drama: heroic | 1853–1857 | Ring begun; Leitmotif web; massive orchestra; mythology | Das Rheingold, Die Walkure, Siegfried Acts I–II |
| Music-drama: crisis | 1857–1865 | Chromatic dissolution; Schopenhauer; Tristan chord | Tristan und Isolde |
| Music-drama: synthesis | 1867–1874 | Diatonic and chromatic reconciled; comedy; Ring completed | Die Meistersinger, Siegfried Act III, Gotterdammerung |
| Sacred | 1877–1882 | Extreme refinement; slowest harmonic rhythm; redemption | Parsifal |
