# Richard Wagner — Harmonic Language

Wagner's harmony does not accompany — it *is* the drama. Where Classical harmony confirms, Wagner's harmony yearns. Every chord is a state of desire; every resolution opens a new question. The Tristan chord did not merely introduce a new sonority — it revealed that tonality itself could become an instrument of longing, that the deferral of resolution could sustain an entire evening's emotional architecture.

For shared Romantic harmonic vocabulary (chromatic mediants, augmented 6ths, sequences, voice-leading), see [romantic-harmony.md](../../romantic-harmony.md). This file covers what is distinctly Wagnerian.

## Core Harmonic Character

| Feature | Description | Where It Appears |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Chromatic saturation | Harmony moves by semitone; diatonic progressions are the exception | Tristan throughout, Ring Act III scenes, Parsifal Prelude |
| Deferred resolution | Cadences avoided for 16–32 bars; each "resolution" reveals another dissonance | Tristan Prelude (no tonic for 17 bars), Parsifal transformation music |
| Leitmotif harmonic identity | Each Leitmotif carries a characteristic harmonic context — the Sword motif IS its triad, the Tristan motif IS its half-diminished 7th | Entire Ring cycle, all mature works |
| Enharmonic ambiguity | Chords pivot between keys through enharmonic reinterpretation; a single chord may belong to 3 keys simultaneously | Ring modulations, Tristan Act II love duet |
| Tonal dissolution | Extended passages where no key center is perceptible | Tristan Act III Prelude, Parsifal Good Friday music approach |
| Dominant prolongation | The dominant chord sustained or elaborated for enormous spans before resolution | Ring climaxes, Tristan Liebestod |
| Chromatic sequence | Sequences moving by semitone rather than by diatonic step | Ride of the Valkyries buildup, Siegfried's Rhine Journey |

## The Tristan Chord — Paradigm of Wagnerian Harmony

The Tristan chord (F-B-D#-G#) is a half-diminished 7th. Its power lies not in the chord itself but in its behavior: it resolves by semitone voice-leading to another unstable chord, which resolves to another. The tonic never arrives.

| Element | Content | Function |
|---------|---------|----------|
| The chord | F3-B3-D#4-G#4 | Half-diminished 7th on F; also analyzable as French 6th in A minor |
| First resolution | E3-C4-E4-A4 | V7 of A minor — but A minor never arrives |
| The principle | Each voice moves by semitone | Voice-leading drives harmony, not root motion |
| Emotional effect | Perpetual yearning — desire that intensifies with each "resolution" | The harmonic engine of Tristan und Isolde |

```abc
X:1
T:The Tristan Chord and Its Non-Resolution
M:6/8
L:1/8
K:Am
%% The most famous chord in Western music — and what it does NOT resolve to
[F,B,^D]3-[F,B,^D]3|[E,C=E]3-[E,C=E]3|
w: Tristan chord _ V7/Am (no Am arrives) _
%% The ear expects A minor; Wagner withholds it — the yearning is the music
```

## Chromatic Voice-Leading Techniques

| Technique | Voice Motion | Emotional Effect | Example Work |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Semitone slide (all voices) | Every voice moves by half-step | Shimmering, destabilizing | Tristan Prelude mm.1–3 |
| Chromatic bass descent | Bass: C-B-Bb-A-Ab under shifting upper voices | Sinking, fateful gravity | Gotterdammerung funeral march |
| Contrary chromatic | Bass descends, soprano ascends by semitone | Expanding tension, world opening | Lohengrin Prelude buildup |
| Common-tone pivot | One voice holds while others slide chromatically | Enharmonic reinterpretation | Ring modulations constantly |
| Chromatic inner voice | Middle voices create chromatic lines; outer voices stable | Psychological unease beneath calm surface | Parsifal Grail scene |
| Augmented triad chains | Augmented triads linked by semitone motion | Floating, rootless, otherworldly | Rheingold opening, Parsifal |

```abc
X:2
T:Wagner Chromatic Bass Descent — Funeral March character
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:Cm
%% The bass sinks chromatically; each step darkens the harmony
!ff![CGc]2 [=BFB]2|[_BEb_B]2 [AEbA]2|[_ADAb]2 [GDG]2|
w: Cm _ _ _ _ _
%% Six different harmonies in 3 bars; only the chromatic bass line provides continuity
```

## Harmonic Techniques by Function

### Tension Building (Pre-Climax)

| Device | Mechanism | Duration |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| Dominant pedal with chromatic superstructure | Bass holds the dominant; upper voices pile dissonances | 8–32 bars |
| Sequential chromatic ascent | Sequences rising by semitone, each iteration more dissonant | 4–16 bars |
| Augmented 6th chains | Ger+6 to resolution becomes new Ger+6 to resolution | 4–8 bars |
| Suspension accumulation | 4-3, 7-6, 9-8 suspensions overlapping in multiple voices | 2–8 bars |

### Resolution and Arrival

| Device | Mechanism | When Used |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Deferred tonic | Dominant resolves to vi, IV, or another substitute; tonic withheld | Throughout — Wagner's default |
| The Grand Pause before resolution | Silence or fermata on dominant, then tonic in full orchestra | Act endings, scene climaxes |
| Modal resolution | Major/minor ambiguity resolved by Picardy third | Lohengrin, Meistersinger codas |
| Plagal confirmation after delayed PAC | After the hard-won V-I, additional IV-I settles the key | Meistersinger Act III, Parsifal ending |

### Tonal Dissolution (No Key Center)

| Technique | How It Works |
|-----------|-------------|
| Diminished 7th pivots | dim7 chord enharmonically reinterpreted every 2 bars — no key established |
| Whole-tone passages | Augmented triads in whole-tone motion; no leading tone, no tonic |
| Chromatic omnibus | Contrary chromatic motion in all voices; the key evaporates |
| Leitmotif collision | Two Leitmotifs with incompatible harmonic contexts sounding simultaneously |

```abc
X:3
T:Wagner Tonal Dissolution — Augmented Triad Chains
M:4/4
L:1/2
K:C
%% No key center — the augmented triads float without gravity
[CE^G] [^CE^A]|[DF^A] [^DF=B]|[E^G^B] [^EAc]|
w: C+ C#+ D+ D#+ E+ F+
%% Whole-tone root motion; every chord is augmented; the tonic disappears
```

## Harmonic Rhythm

| Context | Wagner's Rate | Comparison |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Lyrical passage | 1 chord per 2–4 bars | Much slower than Brahms or Beethoven |
| Tristan-style yearning | Chord changes every bar, but each change is a semitone slide | Constant motion, zero arrival |
| Building to climax | Accelerating: 1/2 bars to 1/bar to 2/bar to 1/beat | The wave gathers speed before crashing |
| Parsifal | 1 chord per 4–8 bars | The slowest harmonic rhythm in the orchestral repertoire |
| Meistersinger (diatonic passages) | 1–2 chords per bar | Deliberately archaic — chorale style |

## Leitmotif Harmonic Identity

Each Leitmotif is inseparable from its harmonic context. Changing the harmony changes the meaning.

| Leitmotif | Harmonic Context | Meaning | Reharmonized Meaning |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|---------------------|
| Sword motif (Ring) | C major triad, root position | Heroic clarity, decisive action | Same motif over diminished 7th = the sword's power corrupted |
| Tristan motif | Half-diminished 7th | Unresolved desire | Over dominant 7th = desire approaching fulfillment |
| Valhalla motif | Db major, rich orchestration | Divine grandeur | Same motif in minor = Valhalla's doom |
| Curse motif (Ring) | Diminished 7th, low brass | Fate, corruption | Over pedal tone = the curse embedded in nature |
| Redemption motif | Major, stepwise ascending | Spiritual transcendence | In minor = redemption not yet achieved |

## Key Preferences

| Preference | Keys | Character |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| Most characteristic | Ab major, Db major, Eb major (flat keys) | Rich, dark, orchestrally resonant |
| Heroic/bright | C major, D major | Reserved for Sword motif, Meistersinger Prize Song |
| Chromatic centers | A minor / A major (Tristan) | The Tristan world: yearning and dissolution |
| Sacred | Ab major, Eb major (Parsifal) | Slow, warm, grail-like |
| Avoided | Sharp keys beyond E major | Too bright for Wagner's dark palette |

## Cadences and closure

Wagner's whole technique is the avoidance of cadence — "unendliche Melodie"
means the harmony is not allowed to close until the drama permits it.

| Cadence | Construction | Where it belongs | Effect |
|---------|--------------|------------------|--------|
| Deceptive | V–vi, or V to an unrelated chord | Constantly, for hours | The withheld resolution that drives the form |
| Evaded | The cadence begins; a voice enters and diverts it | Scene joints | Continuity across sections |
| Perfect authentic | V7–I, root position | Only at the end of an act or a drama | Overwhelming, because it has been denied |
| Plagal / redemption | IV–I in the high strings | Transfiguration endings | Benediction |
| Chromatic-mediant close | Tonic approached from bIII or bVI | Arrivals of leitmotif | Distance, the mythic |
| Half-diminished suspension | The Tristan chord resolving to a dominant that then evades | The Tristan idiom | Perpetual yearning |

The single authentic cadence at the end of a five-hour drama is the point: every
evasion before it is what gives that resolution its force.
