# Counterpoint Essentials

## Species Counterpoint (Condensed)

### First Species (1:1 — note against note)
- Use only consonances: P1, P5, P8, m3, M3, m6, M6
- Begin and end on P1, P5, or P8
- Prefer contrary and oblique motion
- No parallel P5s or P8s; no direct (hidden) P5s/P8s to outer voices
- Limit repeated intervals to three consecutive instances max

### Second Species (2:1 — two notes per cantus firmus note)
- Strong beats: consonant. Weak beats: consonant OR passing tone (stepwise dissonance)
- No unisons on strong beats (except first/last measure)
- Avoid parallel P5s/P8s on consecutive strong beats

### Third Species (4:1 — four notes per CF note)
- Beat 1: consonant. Beats 2-4: consonant, passing tone, or neighbor tone
- Cambiata (double neighbor) permitted
- No more than 4 consecutive notes in same direction

### Fourth Species (syncopation / suspensions)
- Tied notes create suspensions: prepare (consonant) -> suspend (dissonant on strong beat) -> resolve (step down)
- Valid suspensions: 7-6, 4-3, 9-8 (upper voice); 2-3 (lower voice)
- Consonant syncopations also allowed (no resolution needed)

### Fifth Species (florid — free combination of all species)
- Mix of all previous species in one line
- Eighth notes only in pairs, on weak beat portions, stepwise

## Motion Types

| Motion type | Definition | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Contrary | Voices move in opposite directions | Preferred; maximizes independence |
| Oblique | One voice moves, the other sustains | Good for pedal points, common tones |
| Similar | Same direction, different intervals | Acceptable; watch for hidden parallels |
| Parallel | Same direction, same interval | Forbidden for P5, P8, P1. OK for 3rds/6ths |

---

## Forbidden Parallels & Related Errors

| Error | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel 5ths | Two voices moving in P5 to P5 | Destroys voice independence |
| Parallel octaves | Two voices moving in P8 to P8 | Destroys voice independence |
| Parallel unisons | Two voices moving in P1 to P1 | Complete loss of independence |
| Direct (hidden) 5ths/8ves | Similar motion into P5/P8 | Allowed only if upper voice steps; avoid in outer voices |
| Consecutive diminished 5ths | d5 to d5 in parallel | Tolerated only in inner voices |

**Note:** Parallel 3rds and 6ths are encouraged (up to ~4 consecutive); they provide smooth consonance while maintaining independence.

## Voice-Leading Principles

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| Resolve 7ths | Chord 7ths resolve stepwise DOWN |
| Leading tone | Resolves UP by half step to tonic (may descend in inner voice to complete chord) |
| Common-tone retention | Keep shared tones in the same voice between chords |
| Nearest position | Move each voice to the nearest available chord tone |
| Avoid large leaps | Prefer stepwise motion; leaps > P4 should resolve by step in opposite direction |
| Tritone resolution | Augmented 4th resolves outward; diminished 5th resolves inward |
| Doubled notes | Double the root (preferred) > 5th > 3rd (never double the leading tone) |

---

## Voice Crossing & Overlap

| Rule | Description |
|---|---|
| No crossing | A lower voice should not move above the note currently in the voice above it (and vice versa) |
| No overlap | A voice should not move to a pitch that crosses where the adjacent voice JUST WAS on the previous beat |
| Spacing limits | S-A and A-T: no more than an octave apart. T-B: up to a 12th (P8 + P5) acceptable |
| Range limits | S: C4-G5, A: F3-D5, T: C3-G4, B: E2-D4 (approximate) |

## Dissonance Treatment

| Type | Approach | Dissonance | Resolution | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Passing tone (PT) | Step | Weak beat, stepwise between consonances | Continues by step | C-**D**-E over C chord |
| Neighbor tone (NT) | Step | Weak beat, returns to starting note | Step back | C-**D**-C over C chord |
| Suspension (SUS) | Tied | Strong beat, prepared by tie | Step down (usually) | Tie G, sounds over Am -> resolves to A (4-3) |
| Retardation | Tied | Strong beat, prepared by tie | Step UP | Like suspension but resolves upward |
| Appoggiatura (APP) | Leap | Strong beat, approached by leap | Step (opposite dir.) | Leap to **F** over C chord -> resolves to E |
| Escape tone (ET) | Step | Weak beat, approached by step | Leap (opposite dir.) | C-**D** (step up) -> B (leap down) |
| Anticipation | — | Weak beat, next chord's tone arrives early | Becomes consonant | Sounds A early before Am chord |
| Pedal tone | Sustain | Bass holds through changing harmonies | Becomes consonant when harmony returns | Tonic or dominant pedal |

## Cadence Types

| Cadence | Chords | Effect | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect Authentic (PAC) | V(7) -> I, both soprano and bass in root position, soprano on tonic | Strongest closure | End of piece, end of section |
| Imperfect Authentic (IAC) | V -> I, but soprano NOT on tonic or chord inverted | Moderate closure | Internal phrase endings |
| Half Cadence (HC) | any -> V | Open, expectant | Mid-phrase pause, antecedent phrases |
| Deceptive (DC) | V(7) -> vi (or other non-tonic) | Surprise continuation | Extending phrases, delaying resolution |
| Plagal (PC) | IV -> I | "Amen" cadence, gentle | Codas, hymn endings, after PAC |
| Phrygian HC | iv6 -> V (in minor) | Distinctive color | Baroque slow movements, transitional |

**Cadential 6/4:** I64 -> V -> I. The I64 is a dominant embellishment (double appoggiatura), NOT a tonic chord.

## SATB Part-Writing Rules (4-Voice)

### Chord Spacing & Doubling

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Doubling priority | Root > 5th > 3rd. NEVER double the leading tone or altered tones |
| Spacing | S-A and A-T: max octave. T-B: max 12th (more flexible) |
| Complete chords | Include all chord members when possible; omit 5th if needed (never root or 3rd) |
| Root-position triads | Double the root |
| First-inversion triads | Double soprano note (or root); avoid doubling bass if it's the leading tone |
| Second-inversion triads | Rare; use only as cadential 6/4, passing 6/4, or pedal 6/4. Double the bass (5th) |
| Seventh chords | All four members present; in root position, may omit 5th and double root |

### Voice-Leading Checklist

1. Resolve the chordal 7th stepwise down
2. Resolve the leading tone up to tonic (inner voice exception: may fall to 5th of I)
3. Keep common tones in the same voice
4. Move remaining voices to nearest chord tone by step or small leap
5. Check for parallel 5ths and 8ves between ALL voice pairs (6 pairs total: S-A, S-T, S-B, A-T, A-B, T-B)
6. Check voice ranges and spacing limits
7. Verify no crossing or overlap
8. Ensure no unresolved dissonances on strong beats

### Common Progressions (Major Key)

| Chord | Typical moves to |
|---|---|
| I | Any chord |
| ii | V, vii° |
| iii | vi, IV |
| IV | V, ii, I (plagal), vii° |
| V | I, vi (deceptive) |
| vi | ii, IV, V |
| vii° | I, iii (rare) |

**Harmonic rhythm:** Chords on strong beats carry more structural weight. Avoid changing harmony on the weakest subdivision only.
