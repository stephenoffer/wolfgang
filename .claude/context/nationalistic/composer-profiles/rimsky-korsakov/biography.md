# Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov — Biography

A naval officer who taught himself to be a composer, then taught himself to be a professor, then taught the next generation everything about orchestration. Rimsky-Korsakov's life is a story of self-invention: from amateur nationalist to Russia's greatest orchestral craftsman, from Mighty Five radical to conservatory pillar. His fairy-tale operas shimmer with an orchestral brilliance that no textbook — including his own — can fully explain.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1844 | Tikhvin, Russia | Born into aristocratic family | Musical household; older brother already a naval officer |
| 1856 | St. Petersburg | Enters Naval Cadet Corps | Military career path; music as amateur pursuit |
| 1861 | St. Petersburg | Meets Balakirev | Joins the Mighty Five; begins composing seriously without formal training |
| 1862–65 | At sea | Naval voyage around the world | Composes Symphony No. 1 at sea; three years away from Russian musical life |
| 1865 | St. Petersburg | Symphony No. 1 premiered | First symphony by a Russian nationalist; Balakirev conducts |
| 1871 | St. Petersburg | Appointed professor at St. Petersburg Conservatory | Teaches composition — while secretly studying harmony and counterpoint for the first time |
| 1873–74 | — | Intensive self-study | Studies fugue, counterpoint, orchestration from textbooks; fills gaps in his education |
| 1878 | — | May Night (opera) premiered | First fairy-tale opera; Ukrainian folk comedy; the genre that defines his career |
| 1881 | — | Mussorgsky dies | Rimsky takes on the task of editing and completing Mussorgsky's works |
| 1882 | — | The Snow Maiden premiered | The definitive fairy-tale opera; nature and magic fused; octatonic scale established |
| 1887 | — | Scheherazade, Russian Easter Overture, Capriccio espagnol | Triple masterpiece year; peak orchestral brilliance |
| 1888 | — | Principles of Orchestration begun | The textbook that taught the 20th century how to orchestrate |
| 1894 | — | Christmas Eve (opera) | Gogol fairy-tale; octatonic "magic" vs. diatonic "real" system mature |
| 1898 | — | Sadko (opera) premiered | Sea-king fairy tale; the orchestra IS the sea; orchestral color as narrative |
| 1902 | — | Kashchei the Immortal | Late chromatic style; approaching Wagner's harmonic intensity |
| 1905 | St. Petersburg | Dismissed from Conservatory | Supported student strikers during 1905 Revolution; later reinstated |
| 1907 | — | The Golden Cockerel (opera) | Final masterpiece; political satire; banned by censors |
| 1908 | Lyubensk | Dies, June 21 (age 64) | Heart disease; The Golden Cockerel premiered posthumously (1909) |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### The Self-Taught Master

| Dimension | What It Meant |
|-----------|---------------|
| No early training | Composed his first symphony without knowing how to harmonize a chorale |
| Naval career | Three years at sea delayed his development but gave him time to think |
| Conservatory appointment (1871) | Hired to teach what he did not yet know; spent years secretly catching up |
| Self-study period (1873–78) | Rigorous counterpoint and harmony study transformed his writing; became more "academic" |
| The paradox | The self-taught amateur became Russia's most important composition teacher |

### The Mighty Five and the Nationalist Mission

| Element | How It Shaped the Music |
|---------|------------------------|
| Balakirev's circle | Orientation toward Russian subjects, folk melody, and rejection of Germanic academic forms |
| Mussorgsky's influence | Raw Russian color; speech-melody; the power of the "unpolished" — which Rimsky later polished |
| Borodin's influence | Exotic harmony; Central Asian color; the orchestral picture |
| The revision project | Completing and editing Mussorgsky, Borodin (Prince Igor) shaped Rimsky's own style — and raised the revision controversy |

### Fairy-Tale Opera — The Defining Genre

| Element | Significance |
|---------|-------------|
| Russian fairy tales (skazki) | Snow Maiden, Sadko, Kashchei, Golden Cockerel — supernatural Russia as operatic subject |
| "Real vs. magic" key system | Diatonic harmony for human/real characters; octatonic/whole-tone for supernatural/magic characters |
| Nature as character | The sea (Sadko), the forest (Snow Maiden), birds (Golden Cockerel) — orchestral nature-painting |
| Orchestral color as narrative | The specific timbre tells the story: flute = bird, tremolo strings = sea, brass = power |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Nationalist amateur | 1861–1871 | Self-taught; Mighty Five member; bold but technically limited | Symphony No. 1, Sadko (tone poem), Antar |
| Self-education | 1871–1878 | Secretly studying counterpoint while teaching at conservatory; style becomes more academic | String quartets, fugue exercises (unpublished) |
| Fairy-tale mastery | 1878–1898 | Mature operatic voice; octatonic language established; orchestral brilliance peaks | May Night, Snow Maiden, Scheherazade, Sadko (opera) |
| Late chromatic | 1898–1908 | More Wagnerian harmony; political engagement; darkening subject matter | Kashchei, The Golden Cockerel |
