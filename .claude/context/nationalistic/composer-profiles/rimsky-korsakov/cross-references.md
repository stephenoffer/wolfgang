# Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov — Cross-References & Influences

## Position in the Tradition

Rimsky-Korsakov stands at the center of Russian music — not at its radical edge (Mussorgsky) or its Western-facing wing (Tchaikovsky), but at the institutional core. He taught the next generation (Stravinsky, Glazunov, Respighi), codified orchestration for the 20th century, and completed/revised the unfinished works of his colleagues. His influence is not merely musical — it is pedagogical. Every orchestration textbook written after 1913 responds to his Principles.

## Who Influenced Rimsky-Korsakov

| Source | What Was Absorbed | Where You Hear It |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Balakirev | Nationalist orientation; informal composition teaching; exotic subjects | Symphony No. 1; Antar; the entire Mighty Five aesthetic |
| Glinka | Russian opera tradition; orchestral color; A Life for the Tsar and Ruslan as models | Fairy-tale operas inherit Ruslan's magic scenes |
| Mussorgsky | Raw Russian color; speech-melody; the power of "crude" harmony | Rimsky absorbed it, then smoothed it — both in his own works and in revisions |
| Borodin | Exotic oriental color; Central Asian melody; orchestral pictures | Scheherazade's exotic color owes debt to Borodin's Polovtsian world |
| Berlioz | Orchestral treatise; programmatic orchestration; the orchestra as storyteller | Rimsky's Principles directly continues Berlioz's Treatise on Instrumentation |
| Liszt | Symphonic poem form; thematic transformation; orchestral brilliance | Symphonic works; the idea of orchestral narrative |
| Wagner | Late influence: continuous operatic texture; chromaticism; leitmotif | Kashchei, Kitezh, Golden Cockerel — the late chromatic period |
| Russian folk song | Modal scales; arched contour; seasonal ritual subject matter | All "real world" scenes in the fairy-tale operas |
| Russian Orthodox liturgy | Chant melody; bell texture; plagal cadences | Russian Easter Overture; church scenes in operas |

## Who Rimsky-Korsakov Influenced

| Successor | What They Took | Where It Led |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| Stravinsky | Octatonic harmony; orchestral color as primary parameter; block scoring | The Firebird is pure Rimsky; Petrushka and Rite extend his octatonic language |
| Glazunov | Orchestral craft; symphonic form; conservatory professionalism | Glazunov's 8 symphonies and ballets; Rimsky's most faithful student |
| Respighi | Orchestral brilliance; color as structure; the Principles as textbook | Pines of Rome, Fountains of Rome — the Italian Rimsky |
| Prokofiev | Orchestral clarity; fairy-tale subject matter; brilliant scoring | Love for Three Oranges (fairy-tale opera); orchestral suites |
| Ravel | Orchestral color; Spanish exoticism; transparency of scoring | Ravel studied Rimsky's scores; Rapsodie espagnole echoes Capriccio |
| Dukas | Orchestral brilliance; the Sorcerer's Apprentice owes to Rimsky's orchestral narrative | Programmatic orchestral clarity |
| Film composers | The entire tradition of narrative orchestration: image-specific instrumentation | Williams, Korngold — the sea sounds like the sea, danger sounds like danger |

## The Stravinsky Connection

The most consequential teacher-student relationship in 20th-century music. Stravinsky studied privately with Rimsky from 1903 until Rimsky's death in 1908.

| Rimsky Technique | Stravinsky's Extension |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| Octatonic scale as system | Expanded into polytonality; octatonic + diatonic superimposition (Petrushka chord) |
| Orchestral color as primary parameter | Radicalized: color IS the composition, not merely the clothing |
| Block scoring (families separate) | Extreme: instruments isolated; no blending; anti-Romantic |
| Fairy-tale narrative | The Firebird (1910): direct continuation of Rimsky's fairy-tale opera world |
| Real vs. magic harmonic duality | Petrushka: puppet world (octatonic) vs. fair world (diatonic) |
| Brilliant orchestral virtuosity | The Rite of Spring: orchestral virtuosity pushed to the breaking point |

## The Mussorgsky Revision Controversy

The defining ethical question of Rimsky's legacy: his re-orchestration and "correction" of Mussorgsky's Boris Godunov, Night on Bald Mountain, and Khovanshchina.

| Dimension | Rimsky's Position | Modern Reassessment |
|-----------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **Harmony** | Parallel 5ths and unresolved dissonances were amateur errors | They were deliberate; the rawness IS Mussorgsky's language |
| **Orchestration** | Mussorgsky's scoring was thin and amateurish | The original scoring has a stark dramatic power that polish removes |
| **Form** | Scenes reordered for better dramatic flow; cuts made for length | Mussorgsky's original structure had its own dramatic logic |
| **Voice-leading** | "Wrong" notes corrected to conservatory standards | The "wrong" notes anticipated Debussy and 20th-century practice |
| **Intent** | Love and admiration; wanted the music to be performed | Good intentions; but the original Mussorgsky was suppressed for decades |
| **Legacy** | Rimsky's versions made Mussorgsky world-famous | 20th-century restorations (Shostakovich, Lamm) recovered the original |
| **The paradox** | Without Rimsky, Boris might have vanished; with Rimsky, the real Boris was hidden | Both statements are true; the controversy has no simple resolution |

The same controversy applies to Borodin's Prince Igor, completed by Rimsky and Glazunov from sketches. How much of Prince Igor is Borodin, and how much is Rimsky's professional imagination?

## Rimsky-Korsakov vs. Tchaikovsky

Two titans of Russian orchestral music — but utterly different answers to the same question: what is Russian music for?

| Dimension | Rimsky-Korsakov | Tchaikovsky |
|-----------|----------------|-------------|
| **Primary genre** | Fairy-tale opera; orchestral suite | Symphony; ballet; concerto |
| **Harmony** | Systematic (octatonic/diatonic duality); modal; exotic | Emotional (chromatic for anguish, diatonic for joy); Western-functional |
| **Melody** | Decorated, instrumental, exotic; serves the picture | Lyrical, singing, emotional; serves the feeling |
| **Orchestration** | Color-specific; each timbre a narrative meaning; transparent | Emotional; doublings for warmth; the orchestra weeps and exults |
| **Emotional mode** | Pictorial, fantastic, brilliant; emotion through imagery | Direct, autobiographical, confessional; emotion through melody |
| **Form** | Suite, fairy-tale opera, showpiece | Symphony, concerto, ballet — Western forms mastered |
| **Subject** | Russian fairy tales; exotic lands; orchestral pictures | Love, fate, death; the inner life |
| **Relationship** | Professional respect; musical disagreements; different camps | Same; they admired each other across an aesthetic divide |
| **Legacy line** | Stravinsky, Respighi, film orchestration | Rachmaninoff, ballet tradition, popular classical |

## The Mighty Five — Rimsky's Position

| Member | Rimsky's Relationship | Musical Exchange |
|--------|----------------------|------------------|
| Balakirev | Teacher/mentor in the 1860s; relationship cooled | Balakirev guided Symphony No. 1; later Rimsky outgrew the teacher |
| Mussorgsky | Close friend; creative opposite; eventual editor | Mussorgsky: raw instinct; Rimsky: polished craft. The revision is the relationship's legacy |
| Borodin | Collegial admiration; occasional collaboration | Rimsky completed Prince Igor; absorbed Borodin's oriental color |
| Cui | The group's critic and publicist; least significant musically | Professional acquaintance; little direct musical influence |
| Balakirev (as leader) | The Five's ideology: Russian subjects, folk melody, anti-academic | Rimsky later became the academy; the Five's radical spirit became institutionalized |

## Comparative Quick Reference

| Trait | Rimsky-Korsakov | Closest Peer | Key Difference |
|-------|----------------|-------------|----------------|
| Octatonic scale | Rimsky (inventor) | Stravinsky (inheritor) | Rimsky: magic/narrative. Stravinsky: structural/harmonic |
| Orchestral color treatise | Rimsky (Principles) | Berlioz (Treatise) | Rimsky: systematic, practical. Berlioz: poetic, visionary |
| Fairy-tale opera | Rimsky (15 operas) | Dvorak (late period) | Rimsky: Russian skazki, octatonic. Dvorak: Czech fairy tales, diatonic |
| Exotic orchestral suite | Rimsky (Scheherazade) | Saint-Saens (Suite algerienne) | Rimsky: narrative frame, solo personality. Saint-Saens: pictorial, less personal |
| Mussorgsky revision | Rimsky (polished) | Shostakovich (re-restored) | Rimsky: smoothed. Shostakovich: recovered the rawness |
| Orchestral showpiece | Rimsky (Capriccio) | Ravel (Rapsodie espagnole) | Rimsky: cadenza chain, soloistic. Ravel: coloristic, atmospheric |
