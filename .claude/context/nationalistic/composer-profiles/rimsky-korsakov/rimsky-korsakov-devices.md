# Rimsky-Korsakov Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `rimsky-korsakov-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **colour
is the subject.** He develops a theme by re-scoring it rather than by
fragmenting it, and his most characteristic passages are harmonically static
while being timbrally in constant motion. Generated "Russian nationalist" tends
to supply Mussorgsky's bluntness; this composer is the opposite — polished,
brilliant, and organised.

## Structural devices

1. **Theme transformed by re-scoring** — the same tune returns in a new
   register, at a new thickness, against new figuration. The development is in
   the dressing.

2. **The narrative solo** — an unaccompanied melodic line, freely rhythmic,
   returning between sections as a storyteller (Scheherazade's violin).

3. **Ostinato under a long melody** — one figure repeated for many bars while
   the tune plays out above it.

4. **Cyclic themes** — a few motifs recurring across every movement, each time
   re-costumed.

5. **The processional build** — a theme stated quietly, then repeated with each
   restatement thicker and higher until the full texture.

## Harmonic devices

6. **The octatonic scale** — alternating tone and semitone, his signature for
   the magical and the supernatural, and largely his invention as a systematic
   device.

7. **Whole-tone passages** for the same purpose.

8. **Chromatic mediant motion** — chords a third apart with no pivot, the
   commonest way his harmony travels.

9. **Static harmony with moving colour** — one chord for eight bars while the
   figuration changes around it. The harmony is not the interest.

10. **Modal folk harmony** — flat sevenths, plagal cadences, and melodies
    harmonised by their own mode rather than by tonic-dominant.

11. **Pedal points, long and low**, under chromatic upper voices.

## Melodic and rhythmic devices

12. **The oriental melisma** — a decorated, sinuous line with augmented seconds,
    turning around itself. Used for every eastern subject.

13. **Dance rhythms** — the tarantella, the trepak, the fandango, driving and
    absolutely square.

14. **Fanfare openings** — a rising arpeggio in dotted rhythm, brass in origin.

15. **Repeated-note figures** in fast even values, imitating the snare or the
    tambourine.

## Figuration

16. **Continuous sixteenth arpeggiation spanning wide intervals** — the harp
    gesture, and his commonest accompaniment at 30% of bars.

17. **Rapid scalar runs** as decoration between phrases, not as melody.

18. **Trills and tremolos as colour**, sustained under a melody rather than
    ornamenting a note.

## What to avoid

- **Mussorgsky's crudeness.** The voice leading here is correct and the scoring
  is polished; he famously edited Mussorgsky to make it so.
- **Thick chromatic counterpoint.** The inner voices are colour, not argument.
- **Developing a theme by fragmenting it.** Re-score it instead.
- **A thin single-line texture.** Sixty-two percent of his right-hand attacks
  carry more than one note.
- **Irregular phrase lengths.** Unlike Mussorgsky, his are square.
