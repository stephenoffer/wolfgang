# Rimsky-Korsakov Left-Hand Vocabulary

Measured over his 352 armed bars: mostly **2/4** (74%), with the left hand
running `broken_chord_wave` (30%), `block_chord_offbeat` (22%),
`block_chord_sparse` (15%) and `alberti` (14%). His right hand is a chord **62%
of the time** — the highest share of any armed composer — and each hand spans
about 12–13 semitones within a bar while the hands sit only 7 apart.

Read those two numbers together: **a thick, close-position middle register with
both hands working the same octaves.** That is a piano reduction of orchestral
scoring, and it is what he is.

The organising fact: **he is an orchestrator writing at the keyboard.** Every
figure is a section of the orchestra. Ask which instrument has each line and the
texture arranges itself.

## The patterns

1. **The broken-chord wave, wide and even** — a rolling arpeggio spanning a
   tenth or more in continuous sixteenths, the harp or the divided strings.
   `E-2s B-2s E-3s G3s B-3s G3s E-3s B-2s`

2. **Offbeat chordal punctuation** — a bass note on the beat, chords on the
   offbeats. The pizzicato-and-winds accompaniment, 22% of his bars.
   `C2e [E-3,G3,C4]e rest_e [E-3,G3,C4]e`

3. **Alberti** — genuinely present at 14%, inherited rather than fashionable,
   and used for the lighter, more diatonic passages.

4. **Repeated block chords in the middle register** — the brass chorale, struck
   evenly, close position.

5. **Held bass with tremolo above** — the string tremolo for the fantastical
   scenes and the sea music.

6. **Bare octave doublings of the melody** in the bass, for a unison statement
   of a theme.

## Colour and register

He keeps to the middle of the keyboard far more than his Romantic
contemporaries, because the middle is where the orchestra lives. The 7-semitone
gap between hands is unusually tight and it is deliberate: this is scoring, not
piano writing. Do not open the hands out to a Chopin spacing.

## What to avoid

- **A bare single-line melody.** At 62% chordal, the tune is nearly always
  doubled — in thirds, sixths or full chords.
- **Chopin's wide left hand.** The bass does not leap two octaves under an
  arpeggio; the register stays gathered.
- **Dense chromatic voice leading.** His harmony is colouristic and often
  static, moving by thirds or by scale, not by chromatic inner motion.
- **Rubato.** The rhythm is dance-derived and steady.
