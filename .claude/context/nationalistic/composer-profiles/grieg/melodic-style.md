# Edvard Grieg — Melodic Style

Grieg's melodies are landscapes. They do not argue or develop — they evoke. A Grieg theme captures one atmosphere in a few bars: the morning mist lifting, the Halling dancer leaping, the fjord in winter stillness. The melodies are short, characterful, and perfect for their purpose. Their construction follows Norwegian folk song principles — pentatonic foundations, the characteristic raised 4th, and dance rhythms that come from the body, not the page.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Where It Appears |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Short, atmospheric phrases | 2-4 bar phrases that capture one mood completely | Lyric Pieces — every piece is a single atmosphere |
| Norwegian raised 4th (D#) | Chromatic passing tone between D and E in A minor context | Throughout — the single most recognizable Grieg melodic fingerprint |
| Pentatonic foundation | Many melodies use only 5 notes: A-C-D-E-G (minor pentatonic) | Folk-song settings, simple themes, atmospheric openings |
| Descending opening gesture | Melodies begin high and fall — the view from the mountain looking down | Lyric Pieces, Piano Concerto, Peer Gynt themes |
| Dance rhythm as melody | Halling (2/4, wide leaps) and Springar (asymmetric 3/4) rhythms shape the melodic contour | Norwegian Dances, Slatter, dance movements |
| Narrow dynamic range | Most melodies live between pp and mp; ff reserved for dance climaxes | The intimate scale — not Romantic declaration |
| One melody per piece | Each Lyric Piece has essentially one melody; no contrasting second theme | The miniature principle: one idea, fully realized |

## The Grieg Motif — Rising Minor, Falling Return

Grieg's most characteristic melodic shape: a rising minor (A-B-C or A-C-D), reaching a peak (often E5), then falling back to the tonic through the pentatonic scale. The raised 4th (D#) may appear during the descent as a chromatic ornament.

| Phase | Motion | Notes (A minor) | Character |
|-------|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| Rise | Stepwise or 3rd | A4 - C5 - D5 | Aspiration, opening |
| Peak | Arrival on 5th or octave | E5 | The view from the top |
| Raised 4th (optional) | Chromatic passing | D#5 (between D and E) | The Norwegian color |
| Descent | Pentatonic steps | E5 - D5 - C5 - A4 | Return, settling, home |

```abc
X:1
T:The Grieg Motif — Rise, Peak, Norwegian Color, Descent
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Am
%% The characteristic shape: up through minor, touch the raised 4th, fall back
!p!A2 c2 d2 e2|^d2 e2 d2 c2|A4 E4|A8|
%% Bar 1: rise to E5. Bar 2: D# appears briefly. Bars 3-4: pentatonic descent home.
```

## Melodic Intervals — Signature Shapes

| Interval | Frequency | Expressive Use | Example Context |
|----------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Minor 3rd (rising) | Very common | The pentatonic step; open, folk | A-C, D-F, E-G — pentatonic framework |
| Perfect 5th (rising) | Common | Wide leap — Halling dancer; horn call | Dance themes, athletic openings |
| Perfect 4th (rising) | Common | Open, pastoral; less dramatic than 5th | Folk-song openings |
| Falling 3rd | Very common | Sighing, settling, folk cadence | Phrase endings throughout |
| Rising semitone (D-D#) | Characteristic | The Norwegian inflection — brief, ornamental | Wherever the raised 4th appears |
| Octave leap | Moderate | Athletic, dance peak | Halling climaxes only |
| Stepwise (conjunct) | Dominant | Foundation of most Grieg melody | Throughout — pentatonic steps |

## Dance Melodies

### Halling (2/4, Athletic, Wide Leaps)

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Meter | 2/4, fast (quarter = 120-160) |
| Defining leap | Rising P4 or P5 on the downbeat — the dancer's jump |
| Articulation | Marcato downbeat, staccato upbeat |
| Register | Wide — leaps cover an octave or more |
| Dynamic | f to ff; the most extroverted Grieg sound |

```abc
X:2
T:Grieg Halling Theme — Athletic Leaps
M:2/4
L:1/8
K:Am
%% The Halling: P5 leap up, staccato descent, marcato beat 1
!f!A,2 E2|D2 C2|A,2 E2|A4|
%% Rising P5 (A-E) = the dancer's leap; falling step = the landing
```

### Springar (Asymmetric 3/4)

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Meter | 3/4 or 3/8, moderate to fast |
| Asymmetry | Beats are NOT equal: often 2+3+2 or 3+2+2 eighth subdivisions |
| Character | Turning, spinning; less athletic than Halling, more flowing |
| Accent pattern | Irregular — not always on beat 1; sometimes on 2 or 3 |

```abc
X:3
T:Grieg Springar — Asymmetric Triple Time
M:3/8
L:1/16
K:Am
%% Springar: the beats are unequal — 2+3+2 subdivision feel
!mf!A2B2 c2d2 e2|d2c2 B2A2 G2|A2E2 A4|
%% The irregular accent gives the turning, spinning quality
```

## Folk-Song Melody Types

| Type | Scale | Range | Phrase Length | Character |
|------|-------|-------|-------------|-----------|
| Pentatonic minor | A-C-D-E-G | Octave | 4 bars | Ancient, simple, open |
| Aeolian (natural minor) | A-B-C-D-E-F-G | Octave+ | 4-8 bars | Norwegian melancholy, noble |
| Dorian | A-B-C-D-E-F#-G | Octave | 4-8 bars | Warmer, less dark; raised 6th brightens |
| With Lydian inflection | Natural minor + D# | Octave | 4-8 bars | The characteristic Grieg folk melody |
| Pentatonic major | G-A-B-D-E | Octave | 4 bars | Bright, pastoral; less common |

```abc
X:4
T:Grieg Pentatonic Folk Melody — Five Notes Only
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:Am
%% Strictly pentatonic: A, C, D, E, G — no B, no F
!p!A C D|E D C|G, A, C|A,3|
%% The simplicity IS the beauty — no chromatic addition, no semitones
```

## Melodic Construction in the Lyric Pieces

| Principle | Description | Frequency |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Phrase = atmosphere | Each 2-4 bar phrase establishes a complete mood | Universal in Lyric Pieces |
| Repetition, not development | The melody repeats (sometimes transposed) rather than being developed motivically | Default approach — Grieg is not a developer |
| ABA melodic arc | A melody, B melody (contrasting), return to A | The standard Lyric Piece shape |
| Descending tendency | Melodies fall more than they rise; ending phrases descend to tonic | Very common — gravity pulls Grieg melodies downward |
| Register = expression | High register (E5-A5) = bright, exposed; Low register (A3-E4) = warm, intimate | Dynamic range controlled by register, not volume |

## Phrase Structure

| Pattern | Description | Frequency |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| 2+2 (short) | Two-bar phrase repeated or varied | Very common in dance movements |
| 4+4 (regular) | Balanced antecedent-consequent; folk-song standard | Common — the default Grieg phrase |
| 4+4+4 (extended) | Three phrases forming a complete thought; the third is cadential | Lyric Pieces — slightly extended folk song |
| 2+2+4 | Short motives leading to a longer concluding phrase | Dance themes |
| Irregular (Springar) | 3+2+3 or 2+3+2 — reflecting the asymmetric dance | Norwegian Dances, Slatter |

## Peer Gynt Themes — Character Melodies

| Theme | Character | Scale | Mood |
|-------|-----------|-------|------|
| Morning Mood | Flute + oboe pastoral | E major pentatonic | Dawn; innocence; the world awakening |
| Ase's Death | Muted strings, descending | B minor, stepwise descent | Grief; the bass descends chromatically |
| Anitra's Dance | Light, oriental color | A minor with augmented 2nd | Exotic; the Middle Eastern episode |
| In the Hall of the Mountain King | Creeping, accelerating | B minor, narrow range, chromatic | Menace; the build from pp to ff |
| Solveig's Song | Folk-song purity | A minor / A major | Devotion; the folk melody as emotional anchor |

```abc
X:5
T:Grieg Morning Mood Character — Pastoral Pentatonic
M:6/8
L:1/8
K:E
%% The flute-and-oboe pastoral: pentatonic, rising, luminous
!p!E2G B2e|d2B G2E|E2G B2e|d3 B3|
%% Pure pentatonic (E-F#-G#-B-C#); the morning quality
```
