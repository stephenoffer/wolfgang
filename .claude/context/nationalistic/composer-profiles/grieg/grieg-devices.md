# Grieg Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `grieg-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **Norwegian folk
colour in short Romantic character pieces.**

## Harmonic devices
1. **The Grieg motif** — a falling second then a falling third, used as a signature.
2. **Modal harmony** — Lydian raised fourth, Mixolydian flat seventh, Aeolian.
3. **Drone fifths** as harmonic foundation.
4. **Chromatic descending inner voice** over a held bass.
5. **Parallel fifths and open sonorities**, deliberately archaic.
6. **Sudden modal shift** — major to minor of the same tonic, folk-style.
7. **Unresolved sevenths and added seconds** as folk colour.
8. **Third-related keys** for section contrast.

## Rhythmic and melodic devices
9. **Springar lilt** — an uneven 3/4 with a lengthened second beat.
10. **Halling rhythm** — a duple dance, angular and accented.
11. **Pentatonic melodic fragments.**
12. **The short repeated melodic cell** — folk-song construction.
13. **Ornamental grace notes** imitating fiddle playing.
14. **Melody in octaves** for a full statement in a short piece.

## Structural devices
15. **Ternary lyric piece** — A, contrasting B, varied A. The default form.
16. **The character piece with a title** naming a scene or mood.
17. **Suite of short movements** rather than a single long argument.
18. **Cyclic motif across a suite.**
19. **The quiet ending** — most of the lyric pieces end softly.
20. **Sharp sectional contrast** rather than transition.

## What to avoid
- **Long development.** The form is small.
- **Functional-only harmony.** The modal colour is the identity.
- **Thick textures.** Grieg is transparent.
