# Edvard Grieg — Cross-References & Influences

## Position in the Tradition

Grieg stands at a crossroads: German Romantic training, Norwegian folk heritage, and an atmospheric harmonic sense that anticipates French Impressionism. He is the proof that nationalism in music is not provincial — his Norwegian miniatures influenced Debussy and Ravel more than many realize. The Lyric Pieces are a bridge between Schumann's character pieces and Debussy's Preludes; the Hardanger fiddle drone anticipates the modal stasis of Impressionist harmony.

---

## Who Influenced Grieg

| Source | What Was Absorbed | Where You Hear It |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Schumann | Lyrical Romanticism, character piece form, piano as expressive medium | Early works, Lyric Pieces concept, rhythmic sensitivity |
| Mendelssohn | Clarity, elegance, lightness of orchestral touch | Morning Mood; Holberg Suite; transparent textures |
| Rikard Nordraak | Norwegian national consciousness as artistic mission; "our music must be Norwegian" | The decision to make folk material central — Nordraak was the catalyst |
| Hardanger fiddle tradition | Open-5th drone, raised 4th, Halling/Springar rhythms, sympathetic resonance | Throughout — the primary folk source |
| Norwegian folk song | Pentatonic and modal melodies, strophic form, narrative ballads | Song settings (Haugtussa), melodic construction everywhere |
| Liszt | Encouragement; pianistic brilliance; the concept of the piano rhapsody | Piano Concerto cadenza; confidence to pursue a national path |
| Chopin | Piano as miniature form; the nocturne as atmospheric piece; rubato | Lyric Pieces: the piano miniature tradition |

## Who Grieg Influenced

| Successor | What They Took | Where It Led |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| Debussy | Chromatic mediant juxtaposition; parallel motion; modal harmony; atmospheric stasis | Debussy acknowledged studying Grieg carefully; the harmonic freshness of the Preludes has Grieg DNA |
| Ravel | Open-5th drones; modal color; atmospheric piano writing | Ravel's folk-influenced works; Jeux d'eau has parallels to Grieg's resonant piano texture |
| Sibelius | Nordic identity in concert music; the precedent that Scandinavian music matters | Sibelius took a different path (symphonic) but Grieg opened the door for Nordic music internationally |
| Nielsen | Danish counterpart; national identity through music | Less direct influence; but Grieg's success validated the Nordic national project |
| Percy Grainger | Folk melody as art music; direct transcription of folk sources | Grainger studied with Grieg; absorbed the folk-to-concert principle directly |
| Bartok | Slatter Op. 72's dissonant folk transcription approach | Bartok's ethnomusicological method parallels Grieg's late Hardanger fiddle work |
| British pastoralists | Folk-based nationalism; atmospheric miniature | Delius (who lived in Norway), Vaughan Williams (indirect) |

## Grieg and Schumann: The Starting Point

Grieg's early music sounds like Schumann, and the Lyric Pieces are the Norwegian continuation of Schumann's character pieces.

| Dimension | Schumann | Grieg |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Character piece** | Scenes from Childhood, Kreisleriana — literary, psychological | Lyric Pieces — atmospheric, landscape-based, simpler |
| **Piano texture** | Dense, thick inner voices, contrapuntal | Open, resonant, drone-based, transparent |
| **Harmonic language** | Chromatic, restless, emotionally complex | Fresh, modal, juxtapositional, emotionally direct |
| **Emotional range** | Bipolar extremes — ecstasy and despair | Narrower — tranquility, melancholy, dance energy |
| **Form** | Fantasy forms, interrupted narratives | ABA almost exclusively; simpler, more predictable |
| **Scale** | Larger character piece collections with programmatic unity | Each Lyric Piece is independent; no narrative arc |

## Grieg and Debussy: The Bridge

Debussy studied Grieg's scores and absorbed techniques that became central to Impressionism.

| Technique | Grieg's Use | Debussy's Extension |
|-----------|------------|-------------------|
| Chromatic mediant juxtaposition | A minor - F# major, side by side | Entire harmonic language built on non-functional chord movement |
| Parallel motion | Open 5ths moving in parallel for folk color | Parallel 9ths, 7ths — systematic parallelism |
| Modal scales | Norwegian folk modes (Dorian, Lydian inflection) | Church modes, whole-tone, pentatonic as system |
| Pedal point under shifting harmony | Tonic drone with chromatic upper voices | Extended pedals as atmospheric foundation |
| Atmospheric miniature | Lyric Pieces: one mood per piece | Preludes: one image per piece |
| Avoidance of German development | Repetition and juxtaposition, not motivic fragmentation | "Development" replaced by color change |

## Grieg and Dvorak: Two Nationalists

Both are nationalistic composers of the same generation who achieved international fame through folk-inspired concert music. Their approaches are fundamentally different.

| Dimension | Grieg | Dvorak |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Scale** | Miniature: Lyric Pieces, songs, suites | Large: symphonies, concertos, opera |
| **Form** | Avoids sonata development; ABA, suite | Embraces Classical sonata form; Brahmsian structure |
| **Melody** | Atmospheric, short, pentatonic folk | Singable, memorable, pentatonic folk — warmer, more generous |
| **Harmony** | Chromatic mediant juxtaposition; atmospheric | bVII modal color; functional, warm |
| **Orchestration** | Chamber-clear, light, minimal brass | Full, warm, wind solos, brass celebration |
| **Emotional temperature** | Cool, intimate, Nordic | Warm, open, Slavic |
| **Folk source** | Norwegian: Halling, Springar, Hardanger fiddle | Czech: Furiant, Dumka, Polka |
| **Development** | Repetition and orchestral variation | Sequential folk-repetition; some Brahmsian development |

---

## Grieg and Sibelius: Nordic Paths Diverge

Both are Nordic composers, but they represent opposite formal philosophies.

| Dimension | Grieg | Sibelius |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| **Scale** | Miniature — the Lyric Piece | Monumental — the Symphony |
| **Form** | ABA, suite, song | Sonata form pushed to organic evolution (Sym 7: one movement) |
| **Harmony** | Fresh, juxtapositional, atmospheric | Stark, pedal-based, slowly evolving |
| **Orchestration** | Chamber-clear, light | Dark, stark, low brass and strings |
| **Landscape** | Norwegian: fjords, mountains, intimate | Finnish: forests, lakes, vast and austere |
| **Development** | None — repetition and contrast | Extreme: motives grow into themes (reverse development) |
| **Temperament** | Intimate, warm, folk-centered | Monumental, severe, nature-centered |

## Grieg and the Hardanger Fiddle: The Folk Source

The Hardanger fiddle (hardingfele) is the primary folk instrument behind Grieg's musical language.

| Fiddle Feature | Grieg's Musical Translation |
|---------------|---------------------------|
| 4-5 sympathetic strings | Sustained resonance; use of piano pedal to create ringing overtones |
| Open-5th tuning | A-E drone bass; the fundamental Grieg piano texture |
| Raised 4th in folk tuning | D# in A context — the "Grieg note" |
| Halling repertoire | 2/4 dance melodies with wide leaps |
| Springar repertoire | Asymmetric 3/4 melodies |
| Ornamental style | Quick grace notes, trills at phrase peaks |
| Slatter (fiddle dances) | Op. 72: direct transcriptions — Grieg's most dissonant, forward-looking work |

## Comparative Quick Reference

| Trait | Grieg | Closest Peer | Key Difference |
|-------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Atmospheric miniature | Grieg | Schumann (Kinderszenen) | Grieg: landscape. Schumann: psychology |
| Chromatic mediant shifts | Grieg | Schubert | Grieg: juxtaposition. Schubert: tonal wandering |
| Open-5th drone bass | Grieg | Dvorak (pedal points) | Grieg: Hardanger fiddle. Dvorak: village band |
| Norwegian folk scales | Grieg | Sibelius (Finnish) | Grieg: intimate. Sibelius: monumental |
| Piano as primary medium | Grieg | Chopin | Grieg: atmospheric. Chopin: virtuosic |
| Non-developmental form | Grieg | Debussy | Grieg: folk precedent. Debussy: aesthetic principle |
| Raised 4th (Lydian) | Grieg | Bartok (folk modes) | Grieg: Norwegian ornament. Bartok: structural element |
