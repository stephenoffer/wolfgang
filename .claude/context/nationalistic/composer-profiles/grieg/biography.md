# Edvard Grieg — Biography

A small man from a small country who made the world hear Norway. Grieg's life is the story of a Nordic musical identity forged in the shadow of German Romanticism — not by rejecting Leipzig's training but by filtering it through the Hardanger fiddle, the fjord, and the mountain air. His music is intimate, atmospheric, and unmistakably Norwegian: the sound of a specific landscape becoming universal.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1843 | Bergen, Norway | Born | Merchant family of Scottish descent; Bergen faces the North Sea — maritime, cosmopolitan, small |
| 1849 | Bergen | Mother begins teaching him piano (age 6) | His first teacher; she was an accomplished pianist; the piano becomes his native instrument |
| 1858 | Bergen | Ole Bull hears young Grieg and insists he go to Leipzig | Bull — Norway's legendary violinist — recognizes talent and opens the door to German training |
| 1858-62 | Leipzig | Studies at Leipzig Conservatory | Mendelssohn/Schumann tradition; learns harmony, counterpoint, Classical form; but the German Romantic mold does not fit |
| 1863 | Copenhagen | Meets Rikard Nordraak | Fellow Norwegian composer; Nordraak converts Grieg to the cause of Norwegian national music; the mission crystallizes |
| 1864 | Copenhagen | Composes Humoresques Op. 6 | First works with conscious Norwegian folk character; the Lydian 4th appears |
| 1866 | Christiania (Oslo) | Nordraak dies (age 23) | Grieg devastated; inherits the national mission as a sacred duty; "I will fight for Norwegian art" |
| 1867 | Christiania | Marries Nina Hagerup | His cousin; soprano; his primary song collaborator; their only child Alexandra dies in infancy (1869) |
| 1868 | Christiania | Piano Concerto in A minor | The work that makes him famous across Europe; Liszt plays it at sight and embraces Grieg |
| 1869 | Rome | Liszt meets Grieg, plays the Piano Concerto | Liszt's endorsement gives Grieg international credibility; "Keep going — you have what it takes" |
| 1874-75 | Norway | Peer Gynt incidental music | Ibsen commissions; Grieg creates orchestral music of lasting fame; "Morning Mood," "In the Hall of the Mountain King" |
| 1876 | Leipzig | Peer Gynt Suite No. 1 published | Concert extraction; becomes his most performed orchestral work |
| 1877-1901 | Bergen/Troldhaugen | Lyric Pieces (10 books, 66 pieces) | The life's work: 24 years of short piano pieces; each one captures one atmosphere |
| 1884 | Bergen | Moves to Troldhaugen | His home by the fjord; the landscape directly feeds the music; composing, walks, nature |
| 1885 | Bergen | Holberg Suite | String orchestra; neo-Baroque forms with Norwegian folk inflection |
| 1898 | Bergen | Organizes first Bergen Music Festival | Establishing Norwegian musical culture institutionally |
| 1906 | Bergen | String Quartet No. 2 (unfinished) | Late, concentrated, more chromatic; the direction his music was heading |
| 1907 | Bergen | Dies, September 4 | Heart failure; 64 years old; state funeral; ashes placed in a mountain cliff by Troldhaugen |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Bergen and the Norwegian Landscape

| Element | How It Shaped Grieg |
|---------|---------------------|
| Fjords | The vast vertical landscape — mountains plunging into water — gives Grieg his sense of open 5ths, wide spacing, sudden contrasts |
| Maritime light | Rapidly shifting light quality; sun through clouds then gone; the harmonic juxtaposition technique |
| Isolation | Bergen's distance from Continental centers; the intimacy of Grieg's forms reflects a culture scaled to small communities |
| Seasons | Long dark winters, brief luminous summers; the contrast between introspective minor and sudden bright major |

### Leipzig Training and Its Limits

| Dimension | What Leipzig Gave | What Grieg Rejected |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|
| Technique | Harmony, counterpoint, Classical form, orchestration basics | Large-scale German symphonic ambition |
| Models | Mendelssohn, Schumann — lyrical Romanticism | Beethoven's developmental drama; Wagnerian dissolution |
| Discipline | Professional craft; the ability to write correctly | Academic formulas that suppressed folk character |
| Limitation | Leipzig could not teach what the Hardanger fiddle teaches | The open 5th, the raised 4th, the drone — these are Norwegian, not German |

### The Hardanger Fiddle Tradition

| Feature | Musical Result |
|---------|---------------|
| Open-5th drone strings | Bass pedal: A2-E3 sustained under changing harmonies |
| Sympathetic resonance | Rich overtone palette; the "ringing" quality of Grieg's piano writing |
| Raised 4th (D# in A) | The characteristic Grieg chromatic inflection — from folk tuning |
| Halling and Springar dances | Rhythmic vocabulary: 2/4 with leaps (Halling), asymmetric 3/4 (Springar) |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Leipzig student | 1858-1863 | German Romantic; Schumann/Mendelssohn model; searching | Student works, early songs |
| National awakening | 1863-1875 | Norwegian identity discovered; folk rhythms and Lydian 4th enter | Piano Concerto, Peer Gynt, early Lyric Pieces, Violin Sonata No. 2 |
| Mature miniaturist | 1875-1900 | The Lyric Pieces decades; Troldhaugen; atmospheric perfection | Lyric Pieces Books 2-10, Holberg Suite, Cello Sonata, songs |
| Late concentration | 1900-1907 | More chromatic, compressed, forward-looking | Slatter Op. 72, late songs, unfinished Quartet No. 2 |
