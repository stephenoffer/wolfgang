# Tchaikovsky Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Tchaikovsky's textures are **orchestral even at the keyboard**: a singing melody
over a full, sonorous accompaniment with an active bass. The defining property is
emotional directness — the accompaniment supports the tune's expression rather
than complicating it.

## LH idiom catalog

1. **Full sustained chords under a melody** — `[C2,G2,C3,E3]h`. Rich, spread wide,
   the string-section sound.
2. **Repeated chords, throbbing** — `[C3,E3,G3]e ×8`, an insistent pulse under an
   intense melody.
3. **Arpeggiated harp-like figure** — even, wide, from the ballet textures.
4. **Waltz oom-pah-pah, warm and full** — bass then two full chords, not the thin
   Chopin version.
5. **Descending chromatic bass** under a sustained melody — his standard
   intensifier.
6. **Octave doubling of a melodic bass line** for weight.
7. **Tremolo** under a sustained chordal melody, transcribing strings.
8. **Pizzicato-style staccato bass** — short, detached, from the ballet.
9. **Counter-melody in the left hand** answering the right.
10. **Pedal point under rising sequences** — the standard build to a climax.
11. **Bare unison octaves** for a fate-motif statement.

## Where the idiom changes
At every emotional turn. Tchaikovsky's textures follow the feeling directly, and
sections contrast strongly.

## Breathing
Long phrases with clear cadences; the texture thins dramatically after a climax,
often to a single line.

## What to avoid
- **Thin texture under a big melody.** The accompaniment should be full.
- **Understatement.** The style is direct.
- **Contrapuntal complexity.** The bass is expressive, not fugal.
