# Modest Mussorgsky — Biography

A radical who never finished his revolution. Mussorgsky heard music that no one else could write — Russian speech turned to song, church bells turned to harmony, peasant life turned to opera — and drank himself to death at 42, leaving masterpieces in fragments for others to complete. His "crudeness" was prophecy: Debussy, Janacek, and Bartok would later arrive at the same truths he found by instinct.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1839 | Karevo, Russia | Born into minor landowner family | Rural childhood; heard peasant songs and folk tales from birth |
| 1849 | St. Petersburg | Enters cadet school | Military career begins; piano lessons as aristocratic polish |
| 1856 | St. Petersburg | Meets Borodin and Balakirev | Balakirev becomes informal composition teacher; the Mighty Five begins to form |
| 1858 | — | Resigns military commission | Decisive break: chooses music over career; no formal conservatory training |
| 1861 | Russia | Emancipation of serfs | Family estates collapse; Mussorgsky faces poverty; deepens identification with common people |
| 1863 | St. Petersburg | Joins civil service | Music becomes part-time pursuit; government clerk by day, composer by night |
| 1868–69 | St. Petersburg | Boris Godunov — first version | Revolutionary: Russian speech-melody, scene-based structure, no love interest, chorus as protagonist |
| 1872 | St. Petersburg | Boris Godunov — revised version (premiered 1874) | Added Polish act (love interest forced by theater); still radical; moderate success |
| 1874 | St. Petersburg | Pictures at an Exhibition (piano) | Written in weeks after death of artist friend Viktor Hartmann; grief as creative fuel |
| 1875–80 | St. Petersburg | Khovanshchina + Sorochintsy Fair | Two operas worked on simultaneously; neither completed; alcoholism worsens |
| 1880 | St. Petersburg | Dismissed from civil service | Alcohol has destroyed his career; friends support him financially |
| 1881 | St. Petersburg | Dies, March 28 (age 42) | Repin's deathbed portrait: bloated, red-nosed, clear-eyed — the face of a visionary destroyed |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### The Mighty Five and the Anti-Academic Stance

| Dimension | What It Meant for Mussorgsky |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| Balakirev's circle | The Five (Balakirev, Cui, Borodin, Rimsky-Korsakov, Mussorgsky) rejected conservatory training and German academic rules |
| Self-teaching | No formal harmony or counterpoint study; learned by analyzing Beethoven, Schumann, Glinka |
| The "crudeness" question | What academics heard as incompetence was often deliberate rejection of smoothness |
| Split with Rimsky | Rimsky-Korsakov entered the conservatory (1871), studied counterpoint, began "correcting" Mussorgsky's scores |

### Russian Realism and the Peasant

| Element | How It Shaped the Music |
|---------|------------------------|
| Emancipation (1861) | Mussorgsky identified with the freed serfs; his operas center common people, not aristocrats |
| Gogol, Pushkin | Literary realism — ugly truth preferred over beautiful fiction; Boris from Pushkin |
| Russian speech | The natural rhythm of spoken Russian became his melodic principle — not Italian vocal convention |
| The chorus as character | In Boris Godunov, the Russian people are the protagonist; the crowd scenes are the opera's heart |

### Alcoholism and the Unfinished Legacy

| Work | State at Death | Completed By |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Boris Godunov | Two versions, both performable | Rimsky-Korsakov (1896 revision, heavily re-orchestrated); Shostakovich (1940 orchestration) |
| Khovanshchina | Mostly composed, not orchestrated | Rimsky-Korsakov (1883, heavily revised); Shostakovich (1959) |
| Sorochintsy Fair | Fragmentary | Various completions (Cui, Shebalin, others) |
| Pictures at an Exhibition | Complete (piano) | Ravel orchestration (1922) became more famous than the original |
| Night on Bald Mountain | Multiple versions, none definitive | Rimsky-Korsakov version (1886) is the standard; original rediscovered in 20th century |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Formation | 1856–1866 | Amateur; Balakirev's student; songs, piano pieces; finding the Russian voice | Early songs, Intermezzo in modo classico |
| Boris period | 1868–1874 | Peak creative intensity; the radical vision fully realized | Boris Godunov (both versions), Pictures at an Exhibition, Songs and Dances of Death, The Nursery |
| Late fragmentary | 1875–1881 | Two operas at once, neither finished; alcoholism; visionary decline | Khovanshchina, Sorochintsy Fair, final songs |
