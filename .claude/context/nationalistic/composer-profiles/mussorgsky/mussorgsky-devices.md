# Mussorgsky Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `mussorgsky-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **he writes
from speech and from pictures, not from a form.** The music follows the shape of
a Russian sentence or the thing being depicted, which is why his phrases are
irregular and his metres change. Generated "Russian Romantic" comes out sounding
like Tchaikovsky — lyrical, four-square, sequenced. Mussorgsky is blunter,
rawer, and far less symmetrical.

## Structural devices

1. **The Promenade principle** — a recurring theme between sections, each time
   in a different key, metre and mood, reflecting what has just been seen.

2. **Irregular and changing metre** — 5/4 and 6/4 alternating, or a bar of 7
   dropped into a march. The metre follows the phrase, not the other way round.

3. **Phrase lengths of five and seven bars** — the tune ends when the sentence
   ends.

4. **Abrupt sectional juxtaposition** — one picture stops, the next starts, with
   no transition.

5. **The cumulative final statement** — the last return is thicker, lower and
   slower than every previous one, in full chords (the Great Gate).

## Harmonic devices

6. **Modal harmony from Russian folk song** — Dorian and Aeolian, a flat seventh
   instead of a leading tone, and cadences that avoid the dominant.

7. **Bare open fifths** — the third omitted entirely, giving the archaic,
   bell-like sound.

8. **Whole-tone colouring** for the supernatural and grotesque pictures.

9. **Parallel triads moving in blocks** — the whole chord shifted, parallel
   fifths and all, which the conservatory forbade and he used deliberately.

10. **Static harmony over a long pedal** with the melody carrying all the
    motion.

11. **The bell effect** — a low octave struck against a mid-register chord,
    repeated with the harmony changing above it, imitating Russian church bells.
    Both hands wide apart; the 16-semitone gap in his own bars is this.

## Melodic devices

12. **Speech rhythm** — the tune follows the stresses of a Russian phrase, so
    repeated notes and irregular groupings dominate. He described this as his
    method.

13. **Narrow-range recitative** — the melody circles a few notes, declaimed
    rather than sung.

14. **Repeated notes as emphasis**, often the same pitch four or five times.

15. **The sudden leap of a seventh or an octave** at a moment of shock, into an
    otherwise stepwise line.

## Colour

16. **The extreme registers used bare** — the very bottom and the very top, with
    nothing between.

17. **Abrupt dynamic contrast** — `ff` beside `pp` with no hairpin.

## What to avoid

- **Tchaikovsky's lyricism.** Long singing lines over flowing accompaniment are
  the wrong composer.
- **Sequences.** He restates; he does not sequence.
- **Four-square phrasing.**
- **Functional V-I cadences with a leading tone.** Use the modal seventh.
- **Smoothing the voice leading.** The parallels are intentional.
- **Ornaments.** Trills and turns belong to a politer idiom.
