# Modest Mussorgsky — Orchestration

Mussorgsky's orchestration is the most contested legacy in Russian music. His piano writing — Pictures at an Exhibition — is a masterpiece of keyboard color, using the piano as a one-person orchestra. His orchestral scores (Boris Godunov, Night on Bald Mountain) are raw, sometimes unpolished, sometimes brilliant — and were systematically "corrected" by Rimsky-Korsakov, who smoothed away exactly the qualities that made them distinctive. The 20th century rediscovered the originals and found that Mussorgsky's roughness was often more powerful than Rimsky's polish.

## The Orchestration Controversy

| Version | Character | What Was Changed |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| Mussorgsky original (Boris, Night) | Raw, sometimes sparse, sometimes overwhelming; doublings unusual; balance unconventional | — |
| Rimsky-Korsakov revision | Smooth, brilliant, well-balanced, professional | Voice-leading "corrected," harmony polished, orchestration thickened and standardized |
| Shostakovich re-orchestration (Boris) | Faithful to Mussorgsky's harmony; modern orchestral clarity | Restored "wrong" notes Rimsky removed; better balance without changing intent |
| Ravel orchestration (Pictures) | Brilliant French orchestral color applied to Russian piano music | A new masterpiece, but French in spirit; not what Mussorgsky "would have" done |

## Piano as Orchestra — Pictures at an Exhibition

Mussorgsky's greatest orchestral achievement is a piano work. Each "picture" uses the piano to evoke specific timbres.

| Movement | Piano Technique | Orchestral Evocation |
|----------|----------------|---------------------|
| Promenade | Thick chords, changing meters | Brass choir; the visitor's confident stride |
| Gnomus | Low register clusters, irregular accents | Grotesque woodwind + low brass |
| The Old Castle | Sustained bass drone, singing middle voice | Bassoon or saxophone solo over string pedal |
| Tuileries | Light staccato, high register | Flutes, clarinets, light strings |
| Bydlo | Heavy fortissimo bass, plodding rhythm | Tuba melody; full brass; timpani |
| Ballet of Unhatched Chicks | Rapid staccato, trills, grace notes | Piccolo, flute, staccato strings |
| Samuel Goldenberg and Schmuyle | Massive unison octaves vs. chattering repeated notes | Full brass unison vs. muted trumpet or oboe |
| Catacombs | Low sustained chords, pp | Low brass chorale; organ-like |
| Baba Yaga | Violent accents, irregular meter, whole-tone fragments | Full orchestra, percussion, demonic energy |
| Great Gate of Kiev | Bell chords, hymn melody, massive climax | Full orchestra + bells; brass chorale; fff |

```abc
X:1
T:Mussorgsky — Great Gate of Kiev Piano Texture (bell imitation)
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:Eb
%% The piano becomes bells + brass choir
!ff![E,B,E]2 [E,B,E]2|[A,E,A]2 [E,B,E]2|[A,E,A]2 [B,F,B]2|[E,B,E]4|
%% Open 5ths in bass; no 3rd until the hymn melody enters above
```

## Boris Godunov — Original Orchestral Approach

| Scene | Orchestral Character | Mussorgsky's Method |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Coronation | Massive bell texture; brass sustaining open 5ths | Bells literally notated; brass doubles vocal harmony |
| Boris monologue | Sparse; voice nearly unaccompanied; bass clauses punctuate | The emptiness IS the orchestration — Boris is alone |
| Clock hallucination | String tremolo, chromatic clusters, whole-tone fragments | Unconventional doublings create disorientation |
| Crowd scenes | Full orchestra in block chords; choir is the primary instrument | Orchestral punctuation, not accompaniment |
| Death scene | Gradual thinning; instruments drop out; final bars nearly bare | Orchestral depletion mirrors Boris's dying |

## Instrument Usage

| Instrument | Mussorgsky's Approach | vs. Rimsky's Revision |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Strings | Block chords, unison passages, tremolo for atmosphere; not virtuosic | Rimsky added divisi, smoother part-writing |
| Woodwinds | Solo moments for color; clarinet and bassoon for darkness | Rimsky added doublings, smoother transitions |
| Brass | Massive in ceremonial scenes; sustained open 5ths; raw power | Rimsky smoothed the balance; added inner voice motion |
| Percussion | Timpani + actual bells (Boris coronation) | Rimsky standardized percussion usage |
| Harp | Rarely used | Rimsky added harp for color |
| Voice/Choir | THE primary instrument in operas; orchestra serves the voice | Voice-leading in choir "corrected" by Rimsky |

## Texture Types

| Texture | Description | Where Used |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Bell texture | Open 5th bass drone + slow chord movement + folk melody above | Coronation, Great Gate of Kiev |
| Bare declamation | Voice with minimal accompaniment (single bass notes, silence) | Boris monologues, Songs and Dances of Death |
| Block orchestral | Full orchestra in homophonic block chords, parallel motion | Crowd scenes, ceremonial passages |
| Atmospheric tremolo | String tremolo + isolated wind notes; creating unease | Supernatural scenes, hallucinations |
| Piano-orchestral | Piano writing that implies specific orchestral colors | All of Pictures at an Exhibition |
| Choral mass | Choir as primary sound; orchestra supports, does not lead | Boris crowd scenes, Khovanshchina |

```abc
X:2
T:Mussorgsky — Bare Declamation Texture (Boris monologue character)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Dm
V:1 name="Voice (Boris)"
V:2 name="Bass (low strings)" clef=bass
%% Almost nothing accompanies the Tsar — the emptiness is the point
[V:1] !mf!D2 D2 EF D2|A,4 z4|
[V:2] D,8|z8|
%% One bass note, then silence; the voice carries everything
```

## Dynamic Palette

| Dynamic | Context | Orchestral Realization |
|---------|---------|----------------------|
| ppp | Death, disappearance, the supernatural fading | Single instrument, dying away |
| pp | Prayer, distance, memory | Solo wind or muted strings |
| p | Narrative, recitation | Voice + sparse accompaniment |
| mf | Folk song, walking (promenade) | Moderate ensemble, warm |
| f | Proclamation, crowd energy | Full brass, choir |
| ff | Bell pealing, coronation approach | Full orchestra, open 5ths |
| fff | Great Gate, Boris coronation climax | Everything, including actual bells |

## Composing "Mussorgsky Orchestration" for Instruments

| Principle | Realization |
|-----------|-------------|
| Rawness over polish | Prefer unusual doublings; don't smooth out the texture |
| Silence as color | Leave gaps; not every beat needs sound |
| Bell bass | Open 5th in lowest register; sustain through texture changes |
| Block motion | Move entire sections in parallel, not independent voice-leading |
| Extreme dynamic range | pp to fff within a movement; the contrast is the drama |
| Piano thinking | Even in orchestral writing, think in terms of keyboard attack and resonance |
