# Mussorgsky Left-Hand Vocabulary

Measured over his 458 armed bars: **every one of them in 2/2**, and the left
hand is `block_chord_sparse` (50%) and `block_chord_offbeat` (28%) — four bars
in five are struck chords, not figuration. Both hands span about **12 semitones
within a bar** and sit 16 apart. His right hand is a chord 45% of the time.

Note the corpus is *Pictures at an Exhibition* and near relations, so it is one
work's habits; the caveat matters less than usual because those habits are the
ones everyone means by "Mussorgsky".

The organising fact: **the piano is struck, not stroked.** Where a Chopin left
hand flows, this one lands. Generated "Romantic piano" almost always supplies
arpeggios; supplying them here is the single surest way to lose him.

## The patterns

1. **Bare doubled octaves** — the bass line in octaves, no chord, marching.
   The Promenade and the Great Gate open this way.
   `[G2,G3]q [F2,F3]q [E-2,E-3]h`

2. **Block chords on the beat, thick and low** — five and six notes struck
   together in the bass register. Not spread, not rolled.
   `[C2,G2,C3,E-3,G3]h [C2,G2,C3,E-3,G3]h`

3. **Off-beat chordal stabs** — a rest on the beat and the chord after it,
   giving the lurching gait of *Bydło* and *Gnomus*. 28% of his bars.
   `rest_e [C3,E-3,G3]e rest_e [C3,E-3,G3]e`

4. **Tremolo or repeated chord under a melody** — a chord repeated in even
   values for tension, usually growing.

5. **The pedal bass with chords above** — a low held note while the harmony
   moves over it. Used for the ominous pictures.

6. **Sudden silence** — the hand stops dead for a beat. He uses rests as
   punctuation, and they are often the loudest moment.

## Register and dynamics

He writes lower than his contemporaries and uses the bottom octave without
apology. Dynamics are extreme and change abruptly — `ff` to `pp` between one bar
and the next, with no hairpin joining them. Terraced, not graded.

## What to avoid

- **Arpeggios and broken-chord waves.** Almost absent. If the left hand is
  flowing, it is not this composer.
- **Alberti bass.** Wrong century and wrong temperament.
- **Graded crescendos through a phrase.** He changes level in steps.
- **Politely voiced chords.** The spacing is often crude on purpose — open
  fifths, doubled thirds, whatever lands hardest.
- **Rubato.** The rhythm is square and the squareness is the character.
