# Bedrich Smetana — Biography

The father of Czech music. Smetana did not merely compose in a Czech style — he created it. Before him, Bohemia had folk songs and dance; after him, it had a musical identity that could stand beside Germany, Russia, and France. His deafness, like Beethoven's, sealed his art with autobiographical intensity, and Má vlast (My Homeland) became not just music but a national monument in sound.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1824 | Litomysl, Bohemia | Born March 2; father a brewer and amateur musician | Musical household; early talent; piano prodigy from age 6 |
| 1843–47 | Prague | Studies music; works as piano teacher to nobility | Classical training; exposure to Prague concert life; Liszt and Schumann admiration |
| 1848 | Prague | Revolution; Smetana joins the barricades | Political awakening; Czech nationalism becomes personal mission |
| 1848 | Prague | Opens a music school with Liszt's support | Liszt's encouragement and financial help — the Lisztian influence begins |
| 1856–61 | Gothenburg, Sweden | Conductor of Gothenburg Philharmonic Society | Escape from Habsburg repression; exposure to Scandinavian culture; writes symphonic poems in Lisztian style |
| 1861 | Prague | Returns to Prague; Habsburg rule loosening | The national opera project begins; Smetana positions himself as the Czech musical leader |
| 1863 | Prague | Opens Provisional Theatre as conductor | The venue for Czech-language opera; Smetana's platform for national art |
| 1866 | Prague | The Bartered Bride premiered | The Czech comic opera; polka rhythm as art music; international success |
| 1868 | Prague | Dalibor premiered | Serious Czech opera; Wagnerian influence debated; Czech heroic drama |
| 1872 | Prague | Libuse composed (premiered 1881) | The national opera — written for the opening of the National Theatre; Libuse = Czech founding legend |
| 1874 | Prague | Goes completely deaf (October) | Like Beethoven: total deafness at 50; continues composing from inner hearing |
| 1874–79 | Prague | Má vlast composed (6 tone poems) | The national cycle: Vysehrad, Vltava, Sarka, From Bohemian Fields and Forests, Tabor, Blanik |
| 1876 | Prague | String Quartet No. 1 "From My Life" | Autobiographical; final movement's high E = the ringing in his ears before deafness |
| 1882–83 | Prague | String Quartet No. 2 | "The whirlpool of music in the head of one who has lost his hearing" — fragmented, disturbing |
| 1883 | Prague | Mental deterioration begins | Syphilis-related dementia; hospitalized |
| 1884 | Prague | Dies, May 12 | In an asylum; buried with national honors; a hero's funeral |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Czech National Awakening (Narodni obrozeni)

| Aspect | How It Shaped Smetana |
|--------|----------------------|
| Language revival | Czech language recovering from 200 years of German suppression; Smetana composed the first major Czech-language operas |
| Political context | Habsburg rule; 1848 revolution; gradual autonomy; the need for national cultural institutions |
| National Theatre | Built by public subscription ("The nation to itself"); Smetana wrote Libuse for its opening in 1881 |
| Folk culture | Czech polka, furiant, dumka — dance forms AS national identity |
| Heroic legends | Libuse, Dalibor, the Hussite warriors — Czech legends as opera subjects |

### The Liszt Connection

| Element | What Liszt Gave Smetana | How Smetana Transformed It |
|---------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Symphonic poem | The idea of program music as a serious art form | Smetana created the first national symphonic poem cycle (Má vlast) |
| Pianistic style | Virtuoso technique, thematic transformation | Czech dance rhythm replaces Hungarian rhapsody |
| Financial support | Liszt helped fund Smetana's early career | Smetana's debt = artistic freedom to pursue Czech music |
| Formal freedom | The tone poem's flexible narrative structure | Adapted to Czech landscape and legend |

### Deafness and Autobiography

| Aspect | Before Deafness (pre-1874) | After Deafness (1874–1884) |
|--------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Subject matter | National legends, opera, patriotic | Personal, autobiographical, introspective |
| Form | Opera, tone poem (public genres) | String quartet, piano (private genres) |
| Emotional register | Heroic, pastoral, communal | Desperate, fragmented, confessional |
| Key work | The Bartered Bride (communal joy) | String Quartet No. 1 (personal suffering) |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Early Romantic | 1843–1856 | Pianistic, Lisztian, Schumann-influenced, searching for style | Piano works, early chamber music |
| Gothenburg exile | 1856–1861 | Symphonic poems in Liszt's manner; Scandinavian exposure | Richard III, Wallenstein's Camp, Hakon Jarl |
| National opera | 1862–1874 | Czech-language opera creation; polka and furiant as art forms | The Bartered Bride, Dalibor, Libuse |
| Deaf masterpieces | 1874–1883 | Autobiographical intensity; national tone poem cycle; private chamber works | Má vlast, String Quartets 1–2, Czech Dances |
