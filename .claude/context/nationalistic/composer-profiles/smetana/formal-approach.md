# Bedrich Smetana — Formal Approach

Smetana's forms are Classical vessels filled with Czech content. His training in Germanic tradition gave him sonata form, variation, and the Lisztian symphonic poem; his national mission gave him Czech dance, legend, and landscape to pour into them. The result: clear, well-proportioned structures that never feel academic because the content is alive with folk energy and programmatic narrative.

## Core Formal Character

| Feature | Description | Where It Appears |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Symphonic poem cycle | Multiple tone poems linked by shared themes and narrative arc | Ma vlast — the paradigm; 6 poems forming a national epic |
| Clear Classical logic | Identifiable themes, development, recapitulation even in programmatic works | Throughout — Smetana's Germanic training shows |
| Program as form-generator | The narrative shapes the structure; river = continuous flow; battle = ABA conflict | Ma vlast: each poem's form follows its subject |
| Opera dramaturgy | Scene-based structure; recitative/aria/ensemble; dramatic pacing | The Bartered Bride, Dalibor, Libuse |
| Dance as formal section | Polka and furiant episodes are structural units, not decorations | Opera dance scenes; Ma vlast folk sections |
| Thematic transformation | Liszt's principle: one theme transforms to represent different narrative aspects | Vysehrad theme returns transformed in Blanik |

## Symphonic Poem Form — The Ma vlast Model

### The Cycle as Architecture

| Poem | Subject | Formal Type | Key |
|------|---------|-------------|-----|
| 1. Vysehrad | The ancient castle; Czech legend | Arch form: A-B-A' (past-glory, ruin, prophecy) | Bb major |
| 2. Vltava | The river Moldau from source to sea | Rondo/journey: A-B-A-C-A-D-A (river + episodes) | E minor to E major |
| 3. Sarka | Amazon warrior legend | Dramatic narrative: seduction-betrayal-battle | D minor |
| 4. From Bohemian Fields and Forests | Czech countryside | Pastoral ABA with fugue | G minor to G major |
| 5. Tabor | Hussite warriors | Based on Hussite chorale; continuous development | D minor |
| 6. Blanik | Hussite warriors sleeping in a mountain | Continues Tabor; Vysehrad theme returns for apotheosis | D minor to D major |

### Cyclic Unity in Ma vlast

| Unifying Element | Where It Appears | Function |
|-----------------|-----------------|----------|
| Vysehrad theme (harp) | Poem 1 (main), Poem 2 (river passes castle), Poem 6 (final apotheosis) | National identity — the castle is the nation |
| Hussite chorale | Poem 5 (main), Poem 6 (continuation) | Historical continuity — the warrior tradition |
| E minor tonality | Poem 2 (Vltava), echoed elsewhere | The river binds the landscape |
| D minor/major arc | Poems 5–6 (D minor to D major) | Darkness to light — the nation's triumph |

```abc
X:1
T:Smetana — Vysehrad Theme (the cyclic motto of Ma vlast)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Bb
V:1 name="Harp"
%% The bard begins — the opening of the entire cycle
!mf!B,2 D2 F2 B2|d2 B2 F2 D2|B,4 F,4|B,,8|
%% Broad arpeggiation; the castle rises from the harp's strings
```

## Individual Poem Forms

### Vltava — Journey Form (Rondo-like)

| Section | Content | Key | Bars (approx.) |
|---------|---------|-----|----------------|
| A: Two springs | Flute arpeggiation, then violin; two streams merge | E minor | 1–40 |
| B: Forest hunt | Horn calls, dotted rhythm | Unrelated | 41–80 |
| A: River continues | Vltava theme returns, fuller | E minor | 81–120 |
| C: Village wedding | Polka! 2/4, bright, dance | G major | 121–160 |
| A: River resumes | Night falls; muted strings | E minor | 161–200 |
| D: St. John's Rapids | Furious string figuration; the rapids | E minor, turbulent | 201–240 |
| A: Broad river | Full theme, majestic, E major | E major | 241–280 |
| Coda: Vysehrad | Castle theme in brass; river passes into distance | Bb major to E major | 281–end |

### The Bartered Bride — Opera Dramaturgy

| Element | Smetana's Treatment | Character |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| Overture | Sonata form; virtuosic fugato; establishes Czech dance energy | The fastest, most joyful overture in Czech music |
| Act structure | 3 acts; comic plot with serious undercurrents | Classical proportion; no Wagnerian through-composition |
| Dance numbers | Polka, furiant, comedians' dance — structural pillars within the drama | Dance IS the drama, not interruption of it |
| Recitative | Czech-language; speech-rhythm sensitive; not Italianate | Prosodic melody — Czech declamation |
| Ensembles | Duets, trios, choruses — Classical opera ensemble tradition | Social music — the community participates |

## String Quartet Form — Autobiographical

### String Quartet No. 1 "From My Life"

| Movement | Subject | Form | Key |
|----------|---------|------|-----|
| 1. Allegro vivo | "The love of art in my youth; longing for something inexpressible" | Sonata form | E minor |
| 2. Allegro moderato | "The quasi-polka reminds me of joyful days of youth; dances" | Polka (ABA) | A minor |
| 3. Largo sostenuto | "The bliss of my first love" | Ternary (ABA') | Bb major |
| 4. Vivace | "Discovery of national music; joy — then the catastrophe of deafness" | Rondo, interrupted | E minor |

**The sustained high E** in the finale's coda = the ringing in Smetana's ears: the moment hearing died, rendered as music.

```abc
X:2
T:Smetana — String Quartet No.1 Polka Movement (character)
M:2/4
L:1/16
K:Am
V:1 name="Violin I"
%% Autobiographical polka — youth remembered through dance
!mf!A3B c2d2|e3d c2B2|A3G F2E2|A4 z4|
%% The polka rhythm = youth; minor key = memory, not present joy
```

## Formal Devices

| Device | Description | Effect |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Thematic return across movements/poems | Vysehrad theme returns in Blanik; opera themes recur | Large-scale unity; cyclic coherence |
| Programmatic form-shaping | The river's journey determines Vltava's rondo; the battle determines Sarka's arc | Form serves narrative, not the reverse |
| Dance as structural section | Polka or furiant provides a complete formal unit within a larger structure | Czech identity embedded in the architecture |
| Hussite chorale as cantus firmus | The chorale melody underpins entire sections (Tabor, Blanik) | Historical depth; the past alive in the present |
| Picardy tierce resolution | Minor-key poem ending in major (Vltava: E minor to E major) | Hope; the Czech narrative arc from suffering to triumph |
| Harp framing | Harp opens and closes Ma vlast | The bard begins and completes the tale |

## Movement/Section Types

| Type | Character | Examples |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Programmatic allegro | Narrative-driven fast movement; episodic | Vltava, Sarka |
| Dance episode | Polka or furiant as a self-contained section | Wedding in Vltava; Bartered Bride dances |
| Chorale-based continuous development | Hussite hymn as foundation for ongoing variation | Tabor — the chorale generates everything |
| Pastoral | Slow, sustained, landscape-descriptive | From Bohemian Fields and Forests opening |
| Heroic apotheosis | Culmination; all themes combined; major key | Blanik finale; Libuse prophecy scene |
