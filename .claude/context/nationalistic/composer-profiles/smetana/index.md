# Bedřich Smetana — Index

**Era:** Nationalistic | **Nationality:** Czech | **1824–1884**

**Key Works:** Má vlast (My Homeland — 6 tone poems, incl. Vltava), The Bartered Bride (opera), Dalibor (opera), Libuše (opera), String Quartet No.1 "From My Life," String Quartet No.2, Piano Trio in G minor, Czech Dances (piano)

**Style Summary:**
- Father of Czech music: Smetana created a specifically Czech musical identity, drawing on Czech dance (polka, furiant), landscape (the Vltava river, Bohemian forests), and national legend (Libuše, Dalibor, Šárka).
- Tone poem narrative: Má vlast is the paradigm — music that represents specific Czech landscapes and legends. The Vltava begins as two small streams, joins them, flows through the countryside, passes a castle, and reaches the sea.
- Czech dance idiom: the Polka (2/4, dotted rhythm, strong downbeat, cheerful-vigorous) and the Furiant (3/4 with 2/4 hemiola) are structural material, not decoration.
- Clear Classical formal logic despite nationalist content: Smetana studied with Liszt's influence but built in Germanic Classical forms. The "Czech" quality is in the themes and dance idioms.
- Went deaf (like Beethoven) in 1874: wrote his most personal work (String Quartet No.1) afterward.
