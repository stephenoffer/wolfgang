# Bedrich Smetana — Cross-References & Influences

## Position in the Tradition

Smetana is a founder. He did not inherit a Czech musical tradition — he created one. Where Dvorak refined and internationalized Czech music, and Janacek radicalized it, Smetana built the foundation from nothing: Czech opera, Czech symphonic poem, Czech dance as art music. His relationship to Czech music is what Glinka is to Russian music — the source.

## Who Influenced Smetana

| Source | What Was Absorbed | Where You Hear It |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Liszt | Symphonic poem form; thematic transformation; virtuoso piano style; personal encouragement | Gothenburg symphonic poems (Richard III, etc.); Ma vlast formal model |
| Schumann | Lyrical Romanticism; intimate piano writing; formal imagination | Early piano works; the Piano Trio; song-like themes |
| Beethoven | Late-period autobiography; deafness as spiritual trial; sonata form as argument | String Quartet No. 1 "From My Life" — conscious Beethoven parallel |
| Wagner | Continuous orchestral development; leitmotif principle (debated) | Dalibor — critics called it "Czech Wagner"; Smetana denied it |
| Czech folk music | Polka, furiant, dumka rhythms; diatonic melody; modal inflections | The Bartered Bride onward — folk rhythm as structural material |
| Czech legends (Kalevala parallel) | Libuse, Dalibor, Vysehrad, Hussite tradition | Ma vlast; the opera subjects; national mythology |

## Smetana vs. Dvorak: The Generational Tension

The most important comparison in Czech music. Smetana created Czech art music; Dvorak made it international. They respected but did not entirely understand each other.

| Dimension | Smetana | Dvorak |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Role** | Founder — created Czech art music from nothing | Inheritor — refined and exported Czech music worldwide |
| **Folk material** | Often quotes real folk tunes; polka/furiant as structural material | Creates folk-LIKE themes; rarely quotes directly |
| **Harmonic language** | Clear, tonal, Lisztian inheritance; simpler | Richer chromatic palette; Brahmsian warmth + folk color |
| **Orchestration** | Programmatic; clear foreground-background; operatic clarity | More colorful; better orchestrator technically; Brahms-influenced density |
| **Form** | Tone poem cycle; opera; Lisztian model | Symphony; concerto; chamber music; Brahmsian model |
| **Primary genre** | Opera and tone poem | Symphony and chamber music |
| **International reception** | Known mainly through Ma vlast and Bartered Bride | Known worldwide; the "international Czech" |
| **Emotional range** | Heroic/pastoral binary; national pride dominant | Wider: nostalgia, homesickness, humor, warmth, darkness |
| **Cultural politics** | Fought for Czech music against German establishment | Benefited from Smetana's victories; less combative |
| **Relationship** | Smetana was conductor; Dvorak played viola in his orchestra | Generational respect; occasional artistic friction |

## Smetana and Liszt: Master and Student

| Aspect | Liszt | Smetana |
|--------|-------|---------|
| Symphonic poem | Invented the form; subjects from literature, mythology, philosophy | Adapted it for national subjects: Czech landscape, legend, history |
| Thematic transformation | Theme transforms to represent different aspects of the subject | Vysehrad theme transforms through Ma vlast — same technique, Czech content |
| Piano writing | Transcendental virtuosity; piano as orchestra | More modest; salon-to-art-music; Czech dance rhythm |
| Cultural role | Hungarian identity in cosmopolitan package | Czech identity in specifically local package |
| Support | Liszt supported Smetana financially and artistically | Smetana acknowledged the debt; adapted, did not imitate |

## Smetana and Wagner: The Disputed Connection

Smetana's opera Dalibor (1868) was attacked by Czech critics as "Wagnerian" — through-composed, leitmotif-like themes, serious dramatic subject. Smetana denied the influence, but the comparison persists.

| Dimension | Wagner | Smetana |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Opera form | Through-composed; no numbers | Number opera with some continuous passages |
| Leitmotif | Systematic; every character/concept has a motive | Some recurring themes; not a system |
| Orchestra | Orchestra IS the drama; voice secondary | Voice primary; orchestra supports |
| Harmony | Chromatic dissolution of tonality | Tonal, clear; no Tristan chromaticism |
| Scale | 4-hour music dramas | 2–3 hour operas; Classical proportion |
| Smetana's own view | "I am no Wagnerian" — the Czech voice is its own thing | Independent; influenced by but not imitating |

## Smetana and Glinka: Parallel Founders

| Dimension | Glinka (Russian music) | Smetana (Czech music) |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Role | Created Russian art music from folk sources | Created Czech art music from folk sources |
| Key work | A Life for the Tsar (opera) | The Bartered Bride (opera) |
| National opera | Russian language; Russian subjects | Czech language; Czech subjects |
| Folk integration | Russian folk melody in orchestral texture | Czech dance rhythm in operatic structure |
| Legacy | All Russian composers follow | All Czech composers follow |
| International fame | Moderate; overshadowed by Tchaikovsky | Moderate; overshadowed by Dvorak |

|-------|---------|-------------|----------------|
