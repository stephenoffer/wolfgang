# Bedrich Smetana — Stylistic Evolution

Smetana's career divides into four clear phases, each shaped by external circumstances as much as internal development. The exile in Sweden, the return to Prague, the deafness — each event redirected the music. The trajectory is from Lisztian imitation through national self-invention to harrowing autobiography.

## Period Overview

| Period | Years | Character | Defining Works |
|--------|-------|-----------|---------------|
| Early Romantic | 1843–1856 | Pianistic, Schumann/Liszt influence, searching | Piano works, Piano Trio in G minor |
| Gothenburg Exile | 1856–1861 | Lisztian symphonic poems; Scandinavian experience | Richard III, Wallenstein's Camp, Hakon Jarl |
| National Opera | 1862–1874 | Czech opera creation; polka/furiant as art form | The Bartered Bride, Dalibor, Libuse, Two Widows |
| Deaf Masterpieces | 1874–1883 | Ma vlast cycle; autobiographical chamber works | Ma vlast, String Quartets 1–2, Czech Dances |

## Detailed Period Comparison

### I. Early Romantic (1843–1856)

| Aspect | Character | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Melody | Schumann-influenced; lyrical, pianistic | Piano works — song-like themes |
| Harmony | Romantic functional; Schumann's warmth | Standard Romantic progressions; no folk inflection yet |
| Orchestration | Primarily piano; limited orchestral experience | Piano Trio: chamber, intimate |
| Form | Classical forms: sonata, variation | Piano Sonata, Trio — Germanic models |
| National character | Absent or minimal | The Czech voice has not yet emerged |
| Influences | Schumann, Liszt, early Romanticism | Clear stylistic dependence |

### II. Gothenburg Exile (1856–1861)

| Aspect | Character | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Melody | Lisztian thematic transformation; broader, more dramatic | Richard III: dark, theatrical |
| Harmony | Lisztian; enharmonic modulations; more chromatic | Symphonic poems — Liszt's harmonic language |
| Orchestration | First major orchestral works; learning from Liszt's scoring | More colorful than later Czech works |
| Form | Lisztian symphonic poem: single-movement, program-driven | Richard III, Wallenstein's Camp, Hakon Jarl |
| National character | Non-Czech subjects (Shakespeare, Schiller, Norse) | Exile = distance from Czech material |
| Influences | Liszt dominant; Berlioz orchestral color | Direct Lisztian imitation |

### III. National Opera (1862–1874)

| Aspect | Character | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Melody | Czech folk-like themes; polka rhythm; singable vocal lines | Bartered Bride: the Czech melodic voice invented |
| Harmony | Simplified from Liszt; clear I-IV-V-I; polka bass; modal touches | Czech dance harmony replaces Lisztian chromaticism |
| Orchestration | Opera orchestra; clear foreground-background; dance scoring | Lighter, more transparent than Lisztian period |
| Form | Opera: aria, ensemble, dance number; Classical proportions | German opera structure with Czech content |
| National character | FULL — this is where Czech art music is born | Every work declares Czech identity |
| Influences | Liszt (formal model), but content is entirely Czech | The Lisztian vessel filled with Czech wine |

### IV. Deaf Masterpieces (1874–1883)

| Aspect | Character | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Melody | Two modes: folk-national (Ma vlast) and fragmented-autobiographical (quartets) | Vltava = serene folk; Quartet 2 = mental disintegration |
| Harmony | Tonal but with more modal depth; Hussite chorale modal harmonization | Tabor, Blanik: modal minor with chorale foundation |
| Orchestration | Peak orchestral mastery in Ma vlast; chamber intimacy in quartets | Ma vlast = Smetana's orchestral summit |
| Form | Tone poem cycle (Ma vlast); autobiographical sonata (quartets) | The two poles of his late work |
| National character | Strongest in Ma vlast; most personal in quartets | National epic AND private confession |
| Influences | Beethoven's deafness as spiritual precedent | "My life" quartet = Heiligenstadt Testament in music |

## What Changes Between Periods

| Dimension | Early (1843–56) | Gothenburg (1856–61) | National Opera (1862–74) | Deaf (1874–83) |
|-----------|----------------|---------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| Primary genre | Piano | Symphonic poem | Opera | Tone poem + quartet |
| Melodic source | German Romantic | Lisztian dramatic | Czech folk/dance | National + autobiographical |
| Harmonic language | Romantic functional | Lisztian chromatic | Simplified; folk-tonal | Modal depth; chorale |
| Orchestral style | N/A (piano) | Lisztian richness | Opera clarity | Mastery (Ma vlast) |
| Czech identity | Absent | Absent | Fully present | Peak expression |
| Emotional register | Romantic yearning | Dramatic, theatrical | Communal joy, national pride | National epic + personal tragedy |
| Formal model | Schumann | Liszt | Classical opera | Liszt + personal program |

## The Deafness Divide

| Before Deafness (pre-1874) | After Deafness (1874–1883) |
|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Public genres: opera for the nation | Private + public: quartets AND Ma vlast |
| Communal joy dominant | Joy AND anguish; national AND personal |
| Music heard externally | Music heard only internally |
| The Bartered Bride: society celebrating | String Quartet No. 1: an individual suffering |
| Clear, confident forms | Forms that fragment (Quartet No. 2) |

## The Legacy Question

| What Smetana Created | What He Left for Others |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| Czech art music (ex nihilo) | Dvorak inherited and internationalized it |
| Czech opera tradition | Janacek radicalized it |
| Czech tone poem (Ma vlast) | No successor matched this achievement |
| Polka/furiant as art-music forms | Dvorak used them extensively |
| Musical autobiography in Czech | Janacek's late quartets continue this |
