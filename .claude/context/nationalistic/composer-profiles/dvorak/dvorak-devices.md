# Dvořák Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `dvorak-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **folk material in
Classical forms**, with the folk element in the melody's shape and the rhythm's
identity rather than in quotation.

## Melodic devices
1. **Pentatonic melody** — five notes, no semitones. The most recognisable
   Dvořák sound and the basis of the American works.
2. **The flattened seventh** in a major-key tune — modal, folk-derived.
3. **Melody built on a repeated short phrase** — the folk-song manner.
4. **Long lyrical line over a simple accompaniment.**
5. **Melody in thirds and sixths** for warmth.
6. **The rising fourth as a melodic signature.**

## Rhythmic devices
7. **Furiant** — duple accents across triple metre, hemiola as a dance.
8. **Dumka** — slow lament alternating with fast dance, within one movement.
9. **Polka and skočná rhythms** — bright, square, duple.
10. **Syncopation as folk energy** rather than as metrical disruption.
11. **The Scotch snap** — short-long on the beat.

## Harmonic devices
12. **Modal inflection** — Mixolydian and Dorian colouring in a tonal frame.
13. **Third-related key relationships**, Schubertian in feel.
14. **Drone fifths** under a modal melody.
15. **Plagal cadences** for folk-like closure.
16. **Modal mixture** for warmth rather than for shadow.

## Structural devices
17. **Classical forms** — sonata, rondo, variation — carrying folk material.
18. **Slavonic dance as a movement type.**
19. **Cyclic return** of a theme across movements.
20. **The lyrical slow movement as the emotional centre.**
21. **Full-orchestral thinking**, even in chamber and keyboard textures.

## What to avoid
- **Literal folk quotation.** The idiom is absorbed, not cited.
- **Chromatic complexity.**
- **Losing the dance identity.** Each movement should be recognisably a
  something.
