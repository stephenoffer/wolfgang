# Dvořák Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Dvořák writes **warm, full, folk-inflected textures** with a strong rhythmic
identity. The left hand is generous — full chords, active bass lines, and dance
rhythms that come from actual dances.

## LH idiom catalog
1. **Full warm chords under a melody** — `[C2,G2,C3,E3]h`, spread and sonorous.
2. **Dumka rhythm** — alternating slow lament and fast dance, with the
   accompaniment changing character completely between them.
3. **Furiant cross-rhythm** — duple accents across a 3/4 bar, two bars at a time.
4. **Polka oom-pah** — bass and chord, bright and square.
5. **Drone fifths** — `[C2,G2]w` under a modal melody, from folk practice.
6. **Repeated chords, throbbing**, under an intense lyrical melody.
7. **Counter-melody in the bass** answering the tune — Dvořák's basses sing.
8. **Arpeggiated accompaniment, wide and warm.**
9. **Pizzicato-style detached bass** for the lighter dances.
10. **Octave doubling of a folk melody** for a full statement.
11. **Syncopated off-beat chords** for dance energy.

## Where the idiom changes
Between the contrasting sections of a dumka, and between dance types.

## Breathing
Clear four- and eight-bar phrases with real cadences, in the folk manner.

## What to avoid
- **Thin texture.** Dvořák is generous.
- **Harmonic complexity.** The vocabulary is diatonic with modal colour.
- **Losing the dance.** The rhythmic identity is the character.
