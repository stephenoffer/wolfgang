# Antonin Dvorak — Formal Approach

Dvorak inherits Classical form from Brahms but fills it with folk content. His sonata movements have proper expositions, developments, and recapitulations — but the themes are folk songs, the scherzo is a furiant, and the slow movement is a dumka. The architecture is Viennese; the furnishing is Bohemian. This is not a compromise — it is a synthesis that produces forms more immediately accessible than Brahms's while remaining structurally serious.

## Core Formal Philosophy

| Principle | Description | Contrast with Brahms |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Classical forms with folk content | Sonata form, ABA, rondo — but themes are folk-like, dance-derived | Brahms: themes designed for development. Dvorak: themes designed to sing |
| Sectional clarity | Clear boundaries between themes, sections, movements | Brahms: elided phrases, blurred boundaries. Dvorak: clear phrase endings |
| Czech dance forms as movements | Furiant replaces scherzo; dumka replaces slow movement | Brahms: intermezzo. Dvorak: national dance |
| Melodic repetition over motivic development | Themes repeat with orchestral variation rather than fragmentation | Brahms: developing variation. Dvorak: orchestral variation |
| Emotional directness | Joy is joyful, sadness is sad — no ambiguity, no irony | Brahms: emotional complexity. Dvorak: emotional clarity |

## Form Types and Dvorak's Treatment

### Sonata Form

| Element | Classical Norm | Dvorak's Treatment |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| First theme | In tonic, on downbeat | Often folk-like, rhythmic, minor; clear and memorable |
| Transition | Modulates to V or III | Can be abrupt; Dvorak sometimes simply arrives at the new key |
| Second theme | Contrasting character | Lyrical, pentatonic, singing — maximum contrast with first theme |
| Exposition repeat | Standard | Dvorak keeps it in symphonies 7 and 8; omits in 9 |
| Development | Modulatory, fragmentary | Sequential repetition at higher pitch levels; less fragmentation than Brahms |
| Recapitulation | First theme in tonic | Often reorchestrated with added warmth; sometimes shortened |
| Coda | Closing gesture | Extended and triumphant; plagal cadences; bVII-I endings |

```abc
X:1
T:Dvorak Sonata Form — Theme 1 (rhythmic) vs Theme 2 (lyrical)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Em
%% Theme 1: rhythmic, minor, angular — symphonic energy
!f!E2 G2 B2 e2|d2 B2 A2 G2|
%% Theme 2: lyrical, major, pentatonic — folk-song singing
K:G
!p!G4 B4|d4 e4|d2 B2 G4|
%% Maximum contrast — the Dvorak two-theme principle
```

### Dumka Form (Dvorak's Distinctive Contribution)

A complete slow-fast-slow movement type borrowed from Ukrainian/Czech folk tradition.

| Section | Tempo | Mode | Character | Duration |
|---------|-------|------|-----------|----------|
| A | Lento/Andante | Minor | Melancholy, lyrical, sustained | 30-40% |
| B | Vivace/Presto | Major | Dance, energetic, staccato | 20-30% |
| A' | Lento | Minor | Return, varied, deeper | 20-30% |
| Coda | Variable | Variable | May combine both characters | 10-20% |

**The dumka principle:** mourning and celebration are not opposites — they are two faces of the same emotional reality. The contrast between slow and fast sections is not a formal device but a statement about human experience.

### Furiant (Replaces Scherzo)

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Tempo | Presto or Vivace (quarter = 126-160) |
| Meter | 3/4 with persistent hemiola (implied 2/4) |
| Form | ABA (trio form) — like a Classical scherzo |
| Trio | Contrasting: slower, more lyrical, often in relative major |
| Character | Rhythmic energy, stamping dance, accented downbeats |

```abc
X:2
T:Dvorak Furiant — ABA Structure Outline
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:D
%% A section: fast, accented, hemiola
!ff!D2F2A2|d4A2|d2c2B2|A2G2F2|
%% Trio: lyrical contrast
K:Bm
!p!B,4 D4|F4 E4|D4 B,4|
%% A return: da capo — exact or with added dynamics
```

### Variation Form

| Variation Type | Dvorak's Approach | Key Works |
|----------------|------------------|-----------|
| Orchestral variation | Same theme, different instruments/registers | Symphonic Variations Op. 78 |
| Character variation | Each variation has a distinct character (march, waltz, nocturne) | Piano Quintet slow movement |
| Folk-strophic | Theme repeated with minimal change — folk song practice | Song arrangements, simpler chamber works |

### Symphonic Poem (Late Period)

| Element | Dvorak's Treatment |
|---------|-------------------|
| Program | Czech fairy tales (Water Goblin, Noon Witch, Golden Spinning Wheel) |
| Form | Narrative-driven; loose sonata or rondo with programmatic deviations |
| Orchestration | More colorful than symphonies; programmatic effects (water, storms) |
| Melody | Leitmotif-like themes for characters, less folk-like |

## Multi-Movement Architecture

| Symphony | Mvt 1 | Mvt 2 | Mvt 3 | Mvt 4 |
|----------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| No. 7 (D minor) | Tragic sonata (Brahmsian weight) | Lyrical slow | Scherzo (furiant character) | Triumphant sonata-rondo |
| No. 8 (G major) | Pastoral sonata | Lyrical slow (Adagio) | Graceful waltz-scherzo | Theme and variations |
| No. 9 (E minor) | Sonata (American themes) | Largo (cor anglais) | Scherzo (folk dance) | Fiery sonata (cyclic return) |

**Note:** Symphony 9 is cyclic — themes from earlier movements return in the finale. This Lisztian technique is unusual for Dvorak and reflects the American period's synthetic ambition.

## Proportional Balance

| Section | Typical Proportion | Note |
|---------|-------------------|------|
| Exposition | 30-35% | Clear, singable themes; generous space for each |
| Development | 20-25% | Less intense than Brahms; sequential, not fragmentary |
| Recapitulation | 25-30% | Fuller than exposition — added orchestral warmth |
| Coda | 10-15% | Triumphant or warm; bVII-I cadence chain |

## How Form Serves Emotion

| Emotional Arc | Formal Realization | Example |
|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| Joy - celebration | Major-key sonata with triumphant coda | Symphony 8 |
| Struggle - triumph | Minor opening to major coda | Symphony 9 |
| Melancholy - dance - melancholy | Dumka form | Piano Quintet Op. 81 mvt 2, "Dumky" Trio |
| Folk narrative | Symphonic poem; program drives form | Water Goblin, Noon Witch |
| Homesickness - acceptance | Lyrical slow movement framed by energetic outer movements | New World Symphony |
