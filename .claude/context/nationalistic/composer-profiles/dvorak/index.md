# Antonín Dvořák — Index

**Era:** Nationalistic | **Nationality:** Czech | **1841–1904**

**Key Works:** Symphony No.9 "From the New World," Cello Concerto Op.104, String Quartet Op.96 "American," Slavonic Dances (Opp.46/72), Piano Quintet Op.81, String Quartets Op.51/61, Stabat Mater, Requiem

**Style Summary:**
- The tuneful Nationalist: Dvořák's greatest gift is melody — simple, singable, folk-like, as if it always existed. His themes sound discovered, not composed.
- Pentatonic/hexatonic folk melody: C-D-E-G-A, no complex leaps, clear question-answer structure. The New World's Largo theme is the paradigm.
- Czech dance forms as structural material: Furiant (fast, hemiola between 3/4 and 2/4), Dumka (alternating slow melancholy and fast dance — mourning and celebration in one breath).
- Flat-VII chord (Mixolydian) as characteristic color: Bb major in C major context gives a specifically Bohemian, folk-modal quality.
- American synthesis (late Dvořák): African-American spiritual and Native American pentatonic idioms absorbed during his New York period (1892–1895).
