# Ennio Morricone — Cross-References

## Morricone vs. Other Film Composers

### Morricone vs. John Williams

| Dimension | Morricone | Williams |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Training | Rome Conservatory + avant-garde (Petrassi, Gruppo) | Juilliard + Hollywood studio system |
| Orchestration | Eclectic: whistle, harmonica, jaw harp, voice | Traditional symphony orchestra |
| Melody | Simple, folk-derived, singable by anyone | Complex, Leitmotif-based, developmental |
| Harmony | Modal/folk + occasional avant-garde eruption | Full post-Romantic tonal vocabulary |
| Silence | Central structural element | Rarely used structurally |
| Texture | Sparse, open spaces; countermelody layers | Dense, layered orchestral |
| Countermelody | 2–3 independent singable layers simultaneously | One melody + countermelody + accompaniment |
| Scale | Varies: intimate solo to moderate orchestra | Consistently full symphonic |
| Cultural root | Italian folk + Rome avant-garde | Germanic Romantic + Hollywood tradition |
| Prolificacy | 500+ scores | ~150 scores |
| Scoring process | Often composed before filming | Always composed after filming |

### Morricone vs. Hans Zimmer

| Dimension | Morricone | Zimmer |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Instruments | Acoustic eclectic (harmonica, whistle, guitar) | Electronic-orchestral hybrid |
| Melody | Singable, folk-simple | Motif-based, rhythmic identity |
| Silence | Half the score | Rare (electronic pad always present) |
| Texture | Sparse, countermelody layers | Dense, layered walls of sound |
| Harmony | Modal folk + avant-garde touches | Drone + 2–4 chords |
| Dynamic range | pppp to f (emphasis on quiet) | pp to fff (emphasis on loud) |
| Process | Solo composer, manuscript | Team-based, DAW |
| Influence on Zimmer | Zimmer cites Morricone as primary influence | *True Romance* = Morricone homage |

### Morricone vs. Bernard Herrmann

| Dimension | Morricone | Herrmann |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Melody | Simple, folk-derived, long phrases | Short motifs, obsessive repetition |
| Orchestration | Eclectic solos over sparse background | Unusual orchestral combos (strings-only, etc.) |
| Silence | Structural, vast | Rare; tension is continuous |
| Emotional tone | Wide range: joy, violence, nostalgia, beauty | Predominantly dark, anxious |
| Cultural root | Italian | American/Wagnerian |

## Italian Avant-Garde Roots

### Gruppo di Improvvisazione Nuova Consonanza

| Member | Instrument/Role | Connection to Morricone's Film Work |
|--------|----------------|-------------------------------------|
| Franco Evangelisti | Piano, electronics | Cluster techniques in horror scores |
| Egisto Macchi | Percussion | Found-sound percussion in Westerns |
| Morricone | Trumpet, voice | Extended techniques; voice as instrument |
| Roland Kayn | Electronics | Electronic textures (rare in Morricone) |

| Gruppo Technique | Film Score Application |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| Free improvisation | Unpredictable dramatic scoring |
| Extended techniques (prepared piano, etc.) | Unusual timbres as solo voices |
| Noise as music | Whip cracks, chains, natural sounds as score |
| No fixed pitch | Atonal eruptions within tonal scores |
| Collective texture | Multiple independent layers (countermelody architecture) |

## The Leone Partnership — Unique in Film History

| Comparison | Normal Film Scoring | Leone-Morricone Process |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| When music composed | After filming/editing | BEFORE filming |
| Music on set | Not played | Played during filming |
| Actors and music | Never hear the score | Perform TO the score |
| Edit to music | Music fits the edit | Edit fits the music |
| Silence | Director decides | Morricone composes the silences |
| Result | Music serves image | Music and image are co-equal |

## Influence on Later Film Scoring

| Domain | Morricone's Influence |
|--------|---------------------|
| Spaghetti Western genre | Created the entire sonic language |
| Indie/art film scoring | Sparse, eclectic instrumentation model |
| Tarantino's films | Morricone tracks in *Kill Bill*, *Inglourious Basterds*; original score for *Hateful Eight* |
| World cinema | Showed that film music need not sound "Hollywood" |
| Hans Zimmer | Cites Morricone as primary influence; eclectic timbres, emotional directness |
| Radiohead/Jonny Greenwood | Greenwood's film scores (*There Will Be Blood*) echo Morricone's avant-garde + tonal mix |
| Andrea Morricone | Son; continues the family scoring tradition |

### Direct Descendants / Influenced

| Composer | Morricone's Influence |
|----------|---------------------|
| Zimmer | Eclectic timbres; voice as instrument; emotional directness |
| Greenwood | Avant-garde + tonal mixing; string-dominated scoring |
| Desplat | European melodic sensibility; restraint |
| Sakamoto | Minimalist + avant-garde mixture in film |
| Indie film scoring generally | The idea that film music can be sparse, eccentric, non-Hollywood |

## Morricone in Context of Film Scoring History

| Era | Scoring Approach | Morricone's Position |
|-----|-----------------|---------------------|
| Golden Age (1930–60) | Romantic orchestra (Steiner, Korngold) | Training in Rome; outside Hollywood |
| Modernist (1960–75) | Jazz, experimental (Herrmann, Mancini) | Creates Spaghetti Western language |
| Williams Era (1977–90) | Neo-Romantic symphony | International art-film master |
| Hybrid (1990–present) | Electronic-orchestral | Elder statesman; concert career |

## Comparative Silence Usage

| Composer | Silence in Scores |
|----------|------------------|
| Morricone | Central: half the Western scores are silence |
| Pärt | Central: structural silences between phrases |
| Williams | Rare: occasionally for shock |
| Zimmer | Rare: electronic pad fills most space |
| Herrmann | Moderate: builds tension through absence |
