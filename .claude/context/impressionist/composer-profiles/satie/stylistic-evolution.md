# Erik Satie — Stylistic Evolution

Satie's evolution is not the typical early-middle-late narrative of growing mastery. It is stranger: an untrained mystic who writes revolutionary music at 21, goes silent for a decade, returns to school at 39, and emerges as the patron saint of the avant-garde. Each period is a different Satie — and they do not progress linearly.

## Period Overview

| Period | Years | Age | Character | Key Works |
|--------|-------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| Mystic minimalist | 1886-1895 | 20-29 | Modal, static, Rosicrucian, visionary | Gymnopedies, Gnossiennes, Ogives, Sonneries |
| Silent years | 1895-1905 | 29-39 | Near-total silence; cabaret songs; poverty | Few published works |
| Schola Cantorum | 1905-1908 | 39-42 | Student; counterpoint and fugue study | En Habit de Cheval, academic exercises |
| Sardonic humorist | 1908-1917 | 42-51 | Ironic titles, miniature forms, deliberate absurdity | Sports et Divertissements, Embryons Desseches |
| Avant-garde elder | 1917-1925 | 51-59 | Ballet, Dada, collaboration, conceptual art | Parade, Socrate, Relache, Furniture Music |

## Period 1: Mystic Minimalist (1886-1895)

The founding works. Written before Satie had any formal training — and they are more radical than anything being composed in Paris at the time.

| Feature | Early Satie | Contrast with Contemporaries |
|---------|------------|------------------------------|
| Harmony | Static, modal, parallel chords, unresolved 9ths | Franck, Saint-Saens: functional chromatic harmony |
| Melody | Bare, stepwise, modal, no ornament | Late Romantic: ornamented, wide-ranging |
| Form | Repetition, no development, brevity | Sonata form, symphonic argument |
| Texture | Melody + simple accompaniment; 2 layers | Thick Romantic textures; 4-6 layers |
| Dynamics | pp-p exclusively | Full dynamic range |
| Aesthetic | Mysticism, Rosicrucian ritual, medieval revival | Concert hall drama, emotional display |

```abc
X:1
T:Early Satie — Ogives Character (Gregorian austerity)
M:4/4
L:1/2
K:D phrygian
%% Block chords moving in parallel — medieval organum modernized
[D,F,A,D] [E,G,B,E]|[F,A,C,F] [E,G,B,E]|[D,F,A,D]2|
%% No melody, no accompaniment — just chords in parallel motion
```

### What the Gymnopedies Invented

| Innovation | 1887 Context | Later Impact |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Parallel chord motion | Forbidden by academic harmony | Debussy adopts it by 1893 |
| Unresolved 9th chords | Dissonance must resolve (Conservatoire rule) | Becomes Impressionist default |
| Repetition without development | Every Conservatoire form demands development | Anticipates minimalism by 80 years |
| Modal melody in art music | Church modes = "primitive" in 1887 | Debussy, Ravel, 20th century composers reclaim modes |

## Period 2: Silent Years (1895-1905)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Output | Minimal — a handful of cabaret songs, some unpublished sketches |
| Life | Poverty in Arcueil; daily walks to Paris; isolation |
| Significance | The silence is part of the story: Satie's refusal to produce is itself an aesthetic act |
| Self-awareness | He recognized his lack of orchestration skill; this drove the Schola decision |

## Period 3: Schola Cantorum (1905-1908)

| Study | What He Gained | How He Used It |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| Counterpoint | Ability to write fugue and canon | En Habit de Cheval: a fugue. Then he mostly abandoned counterpoint |
| Orchestration | Technical competence for larger forces | Parade, Socrate — orchestral works become possible |
| Form | Understanding of Classical structure | Understanding deepened his rejection — now it was informed refusal |
| Chorale harmonization | Hymn-style voice-leading | Parade's chorale prelude; Socrate's austerity |

## Period 4: Sardonic Humorist (1908-1917)

The title IS the piece. Satie transforms irony from an attitude into a compositional method.

| Work | Title | Musical Content | The Joke |
|------|-------|----------------|----------|
| Trois Morceaux en forme de poire | "Three Pieces in the Shape of a Pear" | Seven pieces (not three), conventional, pretty | Debussy told him his music lacked form; he replied with pieces "in the shape of a pear" |
| Embryons Desseches | "Desiccated Embryos" | Parodies of Chopin and Beethoven | Funeral marches for sea creatures |
| Veritables Preludes Flasques | "Genuine Flabby Preludes (for a Dog)" | Simple, charming miniatures | The grandeur of the title vs. the modesty of the music |
| Sports et Divertissements | 21 miniature portraits of sports/leisure | Each 30 seconds, deft and witty | Prose commentary between staves is equal to the music |

```abc
X:2
T:Sardonic Satie — Embryons Desseches Character
M:2/4
L:1/16
K:C
%% Chopin Funeral March parodied as sea-creature elegy
!p!E2E2 E2E2|D2D2 C4|B,2B,2 B,2B,2|C8|
%% Deadpan delivery of absurd material — the humor is in the straight face
```

### Compositional Techniques of the Humorous Period

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| Musical quotation (distorted) | Known melodies presented wrong | Funeral March in Embryons |
| Title-music contradiction | Grand title + simple music (or vice versa) | Flabby Preludes |
| Prose in the score | Text between staves: "like a nightingale with toothache" | Sports et Divertissements |
| Extreme brevity | A "piece" may be 8 bars | Individual Sports movements |
| Silence as material | Long rests are part of the composition | Throughout this period |

## Period 5: Avant-Garde Elder (1917-1925)

Satie becomes the spiritual father of Parisian modernism. Les Six gather around him; Cocteau, Picasso, Picabia collaborate with him.

| Work | Year | Collaborators | Innovation |
|------|------|--------------|-----------|
| Parade | 1917 | Cocteau (libretto), Picasso (sets), Massine (choreography) | Non-musical sounds in orchestra; cubist collage form |
| Socrate | 1918 | None (Plato's text) | Anti-expression: vocal delivery without emotion; white-note harmony |
| Furniture Music | 1920 | None | Music designed to be ignored — ambient sound concept |
| Relache | 1924 | Picabia (concept), Rene Clair (film) | Dadaist ballet; film interlude during intermission |

```abc
X:3
T:Late Satie — Socrate Character (white-note austerity)
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
%% No accidentals. No dynamics beyond p. No expression. Pure tone.
!p!E D C D|E F E D|C D E C|D2 C2|
%% The death of Socrates, told at the same volume as a description of trees
```

## Evolution Comparison Table

| Parameter | Mystic (1886-95) | Humorist (1908-17) | Avant-garde (1917-25) |
|-----------|------------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Harmony | Modal, static, parallel | Same + ironic quotation | White-note austerity OR cubist collision |
| Melody | Bare, stepwise, Dorian/Phrygian | Same + parodic distortion | Either stripped further (Socrate) or fragmented (Parade) |
| Texture | Melody + accompaniment | Same, more varied | Ranges from extreme simplicity to orchestral collage |
| Form | Repetition, ABA, brevity | Set of miniatures, title-as-form | Conceptual: the idea IS the form |
| Aesthetic | Mystical, Rosicrucian, earnest | Ironic, sardonic, deliberate | Anti-art, Dadaist, philosophical |
| Forces | Piano solo | Piano solo | Orchestra, voice, found sounds, film |
