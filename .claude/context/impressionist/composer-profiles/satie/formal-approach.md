# Erik Satie — Formal Approach

Satie rejected every formal convention of the 19th century. No sonata form, no development sections, no thematic transformation, no dramatic arcs. His forms are built on repetition, juxtaposition, and the radical idea that music need not "go" anywhere. A Satie piece is not a journey — it is a place.

## Core Formal Principles

| Principle | Description | Anti-principle (what Satie rejects) |
|-----------|-------------|--------------------------------------|
| Repetition as form | The piece IS its repetition; no development needed | Development-based form (sonata, fugue) |
| Stasis over direction | The music occupies a state, not a trajectory | Goal-directed form (build to climax) |
| Brevity | Many pieces are 1-3 minutes; nothing is extended | Extended forms (symphony, concerto) |
| Juxtaposition | Sections placed next to each other without transition | Transition and connection between sections |
| Whimsy as structure | The title, the instructions, the absurdity — these ARE formal elements | Formal logic, thematic argument |
| Conceptual form | The idea behind the piece matters more than its notes | Purely musical form |

## Form Types in Satie

| Form | Structure | Example Works |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| ABA with literal repeat | A (8 bars) - B (8 bars) - A (literal) | Gymnopedies |
| Through-composed miniature | No repeats; a single gesture | Sports et Divertissements (each piece) |
| Loop (infinite repeat) | Short phrase repeated 840 times | Vexations |
| Set of pieces | 3, 6, or more short pieces grouped by concept | Gnossiennes (6), Embryons Desseches (3) |
| Cubist collage | Sections from different styles juxtaposed without logic | Parade |
| Strophic song | Same music, different text stanzas | Socrate (modified strophic) |
| Furniture loop | 4-8 bars repeated for ambient duration | Musique d'ameublement |

## The Gymnopedie Form

The three Gymnopedies share an identical formal template — the most characteristic Satie structure.

| Section | Bars | Content | Harmonic Activity |
|---------|------|---------|-------------------|
| A | 1-8 | Two-chord oscillation + melody | Static: two chords alternating |
| B | 9-16 | Slightly varied melody, same harmony | Minimal change — perhaps one new chord |
| A' | 17-24 | Return of A, minor melodic variation | Same two chords |
| Coda | 25-28 | Final statement, no resolution | Stops on the non-tonic chord |

```abc
X:1
T:Gymnopedie Formal Skeleton
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:D
%% A section: 2-chord oscillation
!pp![G,B,E]2 [G,B,E]|[F,A,D]2 [F,A,D]|[G,B,E]2 [G,B,E]|[F,A,D]2 [F,A,D]|
%% B section: same chords, melody shifts slightly
[G,B,E]2 [G,B,E]|[F,A,D]2 [F,A,D]|
%% A' section: literal return. The form is circular, not progressive.
```

## Vexations — The Conceptual Extreme

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Duration | ~840 repetitions of a ~2-minute phrase |
| Total time | ~18-24 hours in performance |
| Form | A single page repeated; the performer decides when 840 is reached |
| Harmonic content | A bass theme using all 12 chromatic notes + two harmonizations |
| Formal meaning | Repetition as endurance; the form IS the concept; music as ritual, not art |
| First complete performance | 1963, organized by John Cage — 12 pianists in relay |

## Sports et Divertissements — The Miniature Set

21 pieces, each 30 seconds to 2 minutes. No development within any piece; the form is the set itself.

| Piece Type | Typical Length | Formal Principle |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Descriptive vignette | 8-16 bars | Single gesture: a wave, a race, a flirtation |
| Text-and-music | Throughout | Satie's handwritten text between staves is part of the piece |
| No repeat | Most pieces play once through | Brevity is the form — no extension needed |
| Silence between pieces | Essential | The pauses are structural |

## Socrate — The Anti-Opera

| Movement | Text Source | Form | Duration |
|----------|-----------|------|----------|
| Portrait de Socrate | Plato, Symposium | Modified strophic — same music, different text | ~8 min |
| Bords de l'Ilissus | Plato, Phaedrus | Gentle through-composition, minimal contrast | ~6 min |
| Mort de Socrate | Plato, Phaedo | Sustained austerity; the music thins toward silence | ~12 min |

The formal principle of Socrate: the music does not dramatize the text. The death of Socrates is delivered at the same dynamic, the same tempo, the same register as the description of a riverbank. Anti-drama as form.

```abc
X:2
T:Socrate — Formal Austerity (Mort de Socrate character)
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
%% The music continues at the same level while the text describes death
!pp!C D E D|C B, C D|C C C C|C4|
%% No crescendo for the death scene. The form refuses drama.
```

## Parade — Cubist Form

| Section | Content | Relation to Previous |
|---------|---------|---------------------|
| Prelude | Chorale-like, solemn | Sets up expectation of traditional form |
| Chinese Conjurer | Pentatonic melody, sparse | Contradicts the prelude — non sequitur |
| American Girl | Ragtime rhythms, typewriter | Style juxtaposition — not development |
| Acrobats | Waltz fragments, mechanical | Another disjunct section |
| Finale | All elements collide | Not synthesis but collision — cubist superimposition |

## Repetition vs. Development: The Core Distinction

| Parameter | Satie (Repetition) | Romantic/Classical (Development) |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------------------------|
| Theme treatment | Stated, then restated identically | Stated, then fragmented, varied, recombined |
| Harmonic journey | None — stays in place | Modulation, remote keys, return |
| Climax | None — energy level constant | Built toward, arrived at, resolved |
| Ending | Cessation (the music stops) | Resolution (the music arrives) |
| Time experience | Circular or static | Linear and directional |
| Listener's role | Be present in the sound | Follow the argument |

## Proportions

| Work Type | Typical Duration | Number of Ideas |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Gymnopedie | 3-4 minutes | 1-2 melodic phrases |
| Gnossienne | 2-4 minutes | 1 melodic phrase, free duration |
| Humorous miniature | 30 sec - 2 min | 1 gesture |
| Socrate movement | 6-12 minutes | Sustained single texture |
| Parade section | 3-5 minutes | Collage of fragments |
| Vexations | 18-24 hours | 1 phrase, 840 times |
