# Satie Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `satie-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **Satie removes
rather than adds.** Every device below is a subtraction from what the period
would otherwise supply — no development, no modulation scheme, no climax, no
counterpoint, no rubato. Generated "impressionist" writing errs toward Debussy's
richness; Satie's power is in what is missing, and a full texture is the single
surest way to get him wrong.

## Structural devices

1. **Strophic repetition without development** — the same music three times,
   with tiny changes or none. There is no development section because there is
   no argument.

2. **Sectional blocks that simply stop** — a section ends and another begins
   with no transition and no modulation joining them.

3. **The three-piece set** — Gymnopédies, Gnossiennes: variations on one mood
   rather than one theme, each piece nearly the same as the last.

4. **No climax.** The dynamic level barely moves. The piece has no highest
   point, which is why it feels suspended rather than shaped.

5. **Absence of a double bar's worth of preparation** — no retransition, no
   dominant build. Arrivals are not prepared.

## Harmonic devices

6. **Parallel seventh and ninth chords** — the whole chord moving in parallel,
   which the period forbade. The Gymnopédies open on a major seventh.

7. **Unresolved sevenths** — a seventh chord that simply moves to another
   seventh chord. Nothing resolves in the textbook sense.

8. **Modal rather than functional harmony** — Dorian and Lydian inflections, a
   flat seventh instead of a leading tone, cadences on degrees that are not the
   dominant.

9. **Chromatic mediants** — a shift from a chord to one a third away with no
   pivot. The commonest way his harmony moves.

10. **Static harmony under a moving line** — one chord held for four or eight
    bars while the melody wanders. See `pedal_point` in the vocabulary.

11. **Bare fourths and fifths** — open intervals with the third omitted, giving
    the medieval colour of the Gnossiennes.

## Melodic devices

12. **Long even note values** — the melody in half and quarter notes, unhurried,
    with no ornament.

13. **Small range** — a fifth or a sixth for a whole phrase. The tune does not
    reach.

14. **Repetition of a short cell** — three or four notes turned over several
    times before moving on.

15. **Phrase lengths that are not four bars** — five, seven, eleven. He does not
    square his phrases.

## Notation

16. **Prose directions instead of Italian** — *"Lent et douloureux"*, and the
    famous absurd ones. Written above the staff as text expressions.

17. **No barlines, no key signature, no time signature** in some pieces. Where
    the engine requires them, keep the accidentals local and the metre simple.

## What to avoid

- **Development.** There is none. Restate instead.
- **A thick middle register.** The texture is bass and top, with air between.
- **Functional cadences.** V-I with a leading tone is the wrong language.
- **Hairpins and crescendos.** The level is flat.
- **Ornaments.** He does not write them.
- **Counterpoint.** No imitation, no independent inner voices.
