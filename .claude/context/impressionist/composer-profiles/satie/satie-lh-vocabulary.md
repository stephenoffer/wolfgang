# Satie Left-Hand Vocabulary

Measured over his 913 armed bars: **3/4 in half of them**, and the left hand is
`block_chord_sparse` (29%), `pedal_point` (18%), `walking_bass` (17%) and — the
number that names the style — **silence in 16%**. One bar in six has no left
hand at all. His right hand is a chord 22% of the time, but the two hands sit
**21 semitones apart**, the widest gap of any armed composer. That gap is the
sound: a bare bass, a long way below, and nothing in the middle.

The organising fact: **Satie writes the same accompaniment for pages and asks
you to notice the harmony changing over it.** Repetition is not laziness here;
it is the form. Do not vary the left hand to keep it interesting.

## The patterns

1. **The Gymnopédie bass-and-chord** — a single low bass note on beat 1, a
   mid-register chord on beat 2, nothing on beat 3. In 3/4 forever.
   `G2h. // rest_q [B3,D4,F#4]q rest_q`
   The rest on three is the whole gesture. Filling it destroys the piece.

2. **Bare octaves in the low register** — the bass alone, doubled at the octave,
   under a slow melody. Used for the Gnossiennes' opening.
   `[D2,D3]w`

3. **The unchanging ostinato** — one figure repeated exactly while the right
   hand moves through unrelated harmony. Change the ostinato only at a section.

4. **Pedal point under everything** — a held bass for eight or sixteen bars
   while the upper chords shift over it. 18% of his bars.

5. **Slow walking bass in even quarters** — the cabaret inheritance, used in the
   more popular pieces. Even, unhurried, never syncopated.

6. **Silence** — the left hand stops for a bar or a phrase. 16% of his bars. In
   any other composer this is a gap; here it is a texture, and it is why the
   returning bass has weight.

## Register and spacing

Bass mostly C2–G2. The chord above it sits around C4, leaving the octave and a
half between them empty. **Do not close that gap** by adding an inner voice —
the emptiness is the sound, and filling it turns Satie into salon music.

## What to avoid

- **Arpeggiating the left-hand chord.** These are blocks, struck together.
- **Varying the figure to relieve monotony.** The monotony is the piece.
- **Crescendos through the accompaniment.** The dynamic is flat and quiet;
  Satie marks the mood in words, not hairpins.
- **A busy inner voice.** There is no inner voice.
- **Rubato in the left hand.** It keeps absolutely steady time.
