# Erik Satie — Biography

The strangest figure in French music. A Montmartre bohemian who lived in a tiny room in Arcueil, walked 10 km to Paris daily, owned twelve identical grey velvet suits, and wrote music that dismantled every assumption of the 19th century. Not a rebel with a program — a quiet provocateur who simply refused to participate in the Romantic project.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1866 | Honfleur | Born | Norman coast; Protestant father, Catholic mother; the outsider identity begins at birth |
| 1879 | Paris | Enters Conservatoire | Expelled twice for laziness and incompetence; the institution rejected him and he rejected it back |
| 1884 | Paris | First published piece (Allegro) | Conventional student work; nothing yet of the mature voice |
| 1886 | Montmartre | Settles in Montmartre | The artistic bohemia: Toulouse-Lautrec, Verlaine, Alphonse Allais — cabaret, absurdism, poverty |
| 1887 | Paris | Gymnopédies (3) | The founding act: static harmony, modal melody, no development. Everything that follows is contained here |
| 1888 | Paris | Gnossiennes (6) | No bar lines. No time signature. Performance directions as prose: "wonder about yourself" |
| 1890 | Paris | Chat Noir cabaret pianist | Plays nightly at the famous shadow-theatre cabaret; meets Debussy; bohemian life at its peak |
| 1891 | Paris | Joins Rosicrucian Order | Composes for Josephin Peladan's Rose+Croix salons; the mystic period: Sonneries, Ogives |
| 1893 | Paris | Founds "Metropolitan Church of Art" | Sole member. Issues papal bulls to himself. The humor is already structural |
| 1898 | Arcueil | Moves to Arcueil suburb | A single room, no running water. Walks to Paris daily. Lives here until death — 27 years of deliberate poverty |
| 1905 | Paris | Enrolls at Schola Cantorum (age 39) | Studies counterpoint and orchestration with d'Indy and Roussel. Three years of rigorous study. The late craftsman emerges |
| 1911 | Paris | Ravel champions his early works | Public concerts of Gymnopedies/Gnossiennes; Satie is "discovered" — 24 years after composing them |
| 1912 | Paris | Humorous piano pieces begin | Sports et Divertissements, Embryons Desseches, Veritables Preludes Flasques — the sardonic titles arrive |
| 1917 | Paris | Parade (ballet with Cocteau/Picasso) | Typewriters, sirens, pistol shots in the orchestra. Cubism meets music. Scandal at premiere |
| 1918 | Paris | Socrate (symphonic drama) | The opposite of Parade: extreme simplicity, white-note austerity, Plato's text delivered without emotion |
| 1920 | Paris | Furniture music (Musique d'ameublement) | Music designed NOT to be listened to — sonic wallpaper. The audience kept sitting down to listen; Satie shouted at them to talk |
| 1924 | Paris | Relache (ballet with Picabia) + Cinema (film score) | Final Dadaist provocations; Francis Picabia's anti-ballet; Rene Clair's film interlude |
| 1925 | Paris | Dies July 1, cirrhosis | Room opened for the first time: piles of umbrellas, letters never opened, two pianos stacked on top of each other |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Montmartre and the Cabaret

| Aspect | How It Shaped Satie |
|--------|---------------------|
| Cabaret aesthetic | Music as atmosphere, not concert-hall drama; brevity, wit, understatement |
| Visual art connections | Toulouse-Lautrec, later Picasso — simplicity of line, flatness, anti-perspective |
| Literary absurdism | Alphonse Allais, later Cocteau — the humor of contradiction, deadpan irony |
| Economic precarity | Poverty as artistic choice; the music's spareness mirrors the life's austerity |

### The Schola Cantorum Period (1905–1908)

| What He Studied | What It Gave Him |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Counterpoint (d'Indy) | The ability to write complex textures — which he then chose not to use |
| Orchestration (Roussel) | Parade, Socrate, Relache — late works that required orchestral craft |
| Fugue and chorale | En Habit de Cheval (1911) — fugues written to prove he could, then abandoned |
| Medieval modes | Deepened the modal vocabulary already present in Gymnopedies |

### The Anti-Wagnerian Stance

Satie's entire aesthetic is a negation of Wagner: where Wagner is long, Satie is brief; where Wagner develops, Satie repeats; where Wagner is emotionally overwhelming, Satie is cool and detached.

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Mystic minimalist | 1886–1895 | Modal, static, Rosicrucian, stripped bare | Gymnopedies, Gnossiennes, Ogives, Sonneries de la Rose+Croix |
| Silent years | 1895–1905 | Almost no composition; poverty; artistic isolation | Only a few cabaret songs |
| Schola Cantorum student | 1905–1908 | Late formal study; acquiring craft | Fugues, chorales — student exercises |
| Sardonic humorist | 1908–1917 | Humorous titles, miniature forms, anti-Romantic irony | Sports et Divertissements, Embryons Desseches, Avant-dernieres Pensees |
| Avant-garde elder | 1917–1925 | Ballet, Dadaism, furniture music, collaboration with visual artists | Parade, Socrate, Musique d'ameublement, Relache, Cinema |
