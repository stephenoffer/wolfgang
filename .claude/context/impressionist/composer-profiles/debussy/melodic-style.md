# Claude Debussy — Melodic Style

Debussy's melody is not a "tune" in the Classical sense — it is an arabesque, a curve of sound that traces a shape in the air and dissolves. Where Mozart's melody is vocal and periodic (4+4 bars, question and answer), Debussy's melody is fluid, asymmetric, and often impossible to separate from its harmonic context. The melody does not sit "on top of" the harmony — it emerges from it, like a shape forming in mist.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Artistic Function |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Arabesque contour | Sinuous, curving lines that rise and fall without predictable symmetry | The ear follows a shape, not a structure — visual, not architectural |
| Modal/pentatonic basis | Melodies built from scales without leading tones (pentatonic, Dorian, Lydian) | No gravitational pull — the melody floats without tonal destination |
| Fluid phrase boundaries | Phrases overlap, elide, or dissolve — no clear "period" structure | Continuous flow; the listener cannot predict where phrases begin/end |
| Registral extremes | Melody in the extreme high register (C5-C7 on piano, high flute/violin) | Distance, luminosity — the melody is heard "from above" |
| Ornamental integration | Grace notes, turns, and arabesques are the melody, not decoration on it | Ornamentation IS melodic content; remove it and the melody vanishes |
| Gamelan layering | Multiple melodic layers at different speeds, pentatonic, overlapping | Debussy heard this in 1889 and never forgot it |
| Speech-rhythm prosody | Vocal melodies follow natural French speech patterns, not metric verse | Pelléas: the voice barely rises above recitative |

## Melodic Construction Principles

### The Arabesque vs. the Period

| Classical Period (Mozart) | Debussy Arabesque |
|--------------------------|-------------------|
| 4 + 4 bars, antecedent + consequent | 5, 7, or 11 bars — asymmetric, unpredictable |
| Clear melodic peak at 3/4 of phrase | Peak may occur anywhere — or nowhere (the melody simply curves) |
| Ends with cadence (PAC or HC) | Ends by dissolving, fading, or overlapping with the next phrase |
| Melody distinct from harmony | Melody emerges from harmonic texture — inseparable |
| Steps and leaps with clear direction | Chromatic inflections, whole-tone turns, modal ambiguity |

### Intervallic Vocabulary

| Interval | Frequency | Expressive Meaning | Context |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| Whole step (M2) | Very high | Default motion — smooth, floating | Whole-tone passages, modal scales |
| Minor 3rd | High | Pentatonic warmth, folk character | Pentatonic melodies (C-D-E-G-A) |
| Major 3rd | Moderate | Open, bright | Arpeggio-based lines, opening gestures |
| Perfect 4th | Moderate | Quartal, open, gamelan-like | Pagodes, bell-like figures |
| Augmented 4th / Tritone | Low-moderate | Whole-tone world, mystery | Transitions, dream-passages |
| Perfect 5th | Low | Open, archaic, hollow | La cathédrale engloutie — medieval parallel 5ths |
| Minor 2nd (semitone) | Context-dependent | Chromatic inflection, sigh | Grace notes, expressive passing tones |
| Octave leap | Rare | Registral shift — entering a new space | Opening of L'isle joyeuse |

```abc
X:1
T:Debussy — Arabesque Melody (Première Arabesque character)
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:E
%% Sinuous, curving — the melody traces a shape in the air
E2F2 G2A2 B2c2 d2e2 | f2e2 d2c2 B2A2 G2F2 | E4 z4 G2A2 B2c2 |
%% No period structure. The curve rises, falls, and begins again from a different point.
```

## Pentatonic Melody

Debussy's most characteristic melodic surfaces are pentatonic — five notes, no semitones, no leading tone, no pull.

| Pentatonic Type | Notes (from C) | Character | Example Work |
|----------------|----------------|-----------|-------------|
| Major pentatonic | C-D-E-G-A | Open, pastoral, Eastern | La fille aux cheveux de lin |
| Minor pentatonic | C-Eb-F-G-Bb | Darker, folk-adjacent | Canope, certain Préludes |
| Pentatonic on Gb | Gb-Ab-Bb-Db-Eb | Black-key warmth | Pagodes (primarily on black keys) |
| Shifted pentatonic | Any 5-note anhemitonic set | Varies by context | Throughout the Préludes |

```abc
X:2
T:Debussy — Pentatonic Melody (La fille aux cheveux de lin character)
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:Gb
%% Almost entirely pentatonic on Gb — Gb Ab Bb Db Eb
G2 A2 B2 | d2 e2 d2 | B2 A2 G2 | G6 |
%% No semitones in the melody. Pure pentatonic warmth. The harmony below can be rich without disturbing the melody's simplicity.
```

## Whole-Tone Melody

Melodies built entirely from the whole-tone scale create directionless, floating lines — the ear cannot predict the next note because every interval is the same size.

| Property | Effect on Melody |
|----------|-----------------|
| All intervals equal (M2) | No sense of "up" being different from "down" |
| No leading tone | No pull toward resolution |
| No perfect 5th | Cannot outline a triad — rootless quality |
| Augmented intervals available | Tritone leaps feel natural within the scale |

## Gamelan-Influenced Layering

After the 1889 Exposition, Debussy translated gamelan principles into Western notation:

| Gamelan Principle | Debussy's Translation |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Core melody (balungan) at slow speed | Bass or inner voice: long notes, pentatonic |
| Elaborating melody at double speed | Upper voice: same pentatonic set, faster rhythm |
| Punctuating gong at phrase boundaries | Low bass notes marking structural points |
| Metallic resonance and overtone halo | Sustained pedal creating overtone wash |

```abc
X:3
T:Debussy — Gamelan Layering (Pagodes character)
M:2/4
L:1/16
K:Bb
V:1 clef=treble name="Upper elaboration"
V:2 clef=bass name="Core melody"
%% Two pentatonic layers at different speeds
[V:1] B2c2 d2f2 | d2c2 B2G2 |
[V:2] B,4 D4 | F,4 B,,4 |
%% Upper voice: fast pentatonic figuration. Lower voice: slow pentatonic foundation. Gamelan translated to piano.
```

## Vocal Melody (Pelléas, Songs)

Debussy's vocal writing follows French speech rhythm, not periodic musical meter. The voice moves in small intervals, rarely leaping, rarely rising above mezzo-forte. The orchestra carries the emotional weight; the voice is intimate, almost whispered.

| Vocal Principle | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| Speech rhythm over meter | Syllables fall where French prosody demands, not on downbeats |
| Narrow range | Most vocal phrases within a 5th or 6th |
| No arias | Pelléas has no set pieces — continuous speech-song |
| Orchestra as subconscious | The orchestra expresses what the character cannot say |
| Understatement | Emotional peaks are quieter, not louder |

## Anti-Patterns in Debussy Melody

| Wrong | Why | Instead |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Regular 4+4 bar phrases | Sounds Classical, not Impressionist | Asymmetric: 5, 7, 11 bars |
| Melody in middle register (C4 range) | Sounds Romantic, earthbound | High register: C5-C7 range |
| Strong metric accent on beat 1 | Sounds dance-like, periodic | Floating rhythm; melody crosses barlines |
| Dominant-tonic melodic cadence (ti-do) | Sounds functional, goal-directed | Modal or pentatonic ending; or fadeout |
| Melody separate from accompaniment | Sounds Classical — melody "over" harmony | Melody emerges from harmonic texture |
