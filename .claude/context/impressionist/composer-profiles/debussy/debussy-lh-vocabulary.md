# Debussy Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Debussy's left hand creates **resonance**, not harmony in the functional sense.
Its properties are wide spacing, heavy pedal, and figures that blur rather than
articulate. Generated "Debussy" usually keeps a functional bass line, which
destroys the effect entirely.

## LH idiom catalog

1. **Wide, low, sustained fifths and octaves** — `[C1,G1]w`, pedalled, as a
   resonant floor rather than a bass line.
2. **Parallel chords moving in blocks** — the whole chord shape sliding by step or
   third, `[C3,E3,G3]q [D3,F3,A3]q`. Planing; the defining Impressionist texture.
3. **Rippling wide arpeggios** spanning two or more octaves, pedalled into a wash.
4. **Ostinato figure repeated unchanged** under changing upper harmony.
5. **Pentatonic figuration** — black-key patterns, no leading tone.
6. **Whole-tone arpeggio** — symmetrical, keyless, floating.
7. **Bare fifths in the deep bass** for archaism and openness.
8. **Chord with an added second or sixth** — `[C3,D3,G3,A3]` — resonance rather
   than function.
9. **The silence between gestures** — Debussy's textures are episodic and the gaps
   are part of them.
10. **Tremolo, quiet and blurred**, under a sustained upper line.
11. **Cross-hand arpeggio covering four octaves** as a single sweeping gesture.

## Where the idiom changes
Debussy's pieces are sequences of *images*; the texture changes when the image
does, which can be every four bars.

## Breathing
Silence and fermatas between gestures. The music frequently stops entirely.

## What to avoid
- **A functional bass line.** Root-position progressions with a walking bass
  destroy the idiom.
- **Regular metrical accompaniment.** The pulse should be veiled.
- **Dry texture.** Pedal is not optional; it is the instrument.
- **Leading tones and resolutions.** Avoiding them is the point.
