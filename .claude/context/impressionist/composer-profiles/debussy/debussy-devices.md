# Debussy Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `debussy-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **colour replaces
function.** A chord is chosen for how it sounds, not for where it leads, and the
usual machinery of tension and resolution is deliberately disabled.

## Harmonic devices
1. **Planing** — a chord shape moved in parallel, all voices in the same
   direction by the same interval. Parallel fifths and ninths are the point, not
   an error.
2. **Whole-tone scale** — symmetrical, no leading tone, no tonic. Used for
   suspension and unreality.
3. **Pentatonic collections** — five notes, no semitones, no tendency.
4. **Modes** — Dorian, Lydian, Mixolydian, chosen for colour.
5. **Added-note chords** — sixths, seconds and ninths added to triads for
   resonance.
6. **Unresolved dominant sevenths** used as stable sonorities.
7. **Bitonal or ambiguous bass** — the bass and the upper harmony implying
   different centres.
8. **Chord streams over a pedal** — the harmony moving while the bass does not.
9. **The tritone as a structural interval** rather than a dissonance to resolve.
10. **Avoiding the leading tone** almost entirely.

## Textural and rhythmic devices
11. **Veiled pulse** — the metre deliberately obscured by ties, irregular
    groupings and rests on strong beats.
12. **Irregular groupings** — 5, 7 and 11 against the beat.
13. **Register as colour** — the same material moved octaves for a different
    quality.
14. **Extreme dynamic range at the quiet end** — `pppp` markings, and most of the
    music below `mf`.
15. **Silence as material** — gaps between gestures rather than continuous flow.
16. **Layered ostinati** — two or three repeating figures at different lengths.

## Structural devices
17. **Episodic, image-based form** — sections defined by texture and colour rather
    than by key.
18. **Arch shape** — building to one climax and receding.
19. **A title that names an image**, with the music depicting it directly.
20. **The gesture that does not return** — material used once and abandoned.
21. **Ending by evaporation** — thinning to nothing rather than cadencing.

## What to avoid
- **Functional progressions.** ii-V-I is the opposite of this style.
- **A strong, regular pulse.**
- **Dry, unpedalled texture.**
- **Developing a theme.** Debussy juxtaposes; he does not argue.
- **Loud, sustained climaxes.** The dynamic centre is low.
