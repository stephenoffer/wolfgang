# Maurice Ravel — Melodic Style

Ravel's melody sings. Unlike Debussy's arabesques, which emerge from harmonic texture and dissolve back into it, Ravel's melodies are clear, shapely, and always audible — you can hum them. But they are not simple: a Ravel melody may be modal (Phrygian, Dorian), Spanish-inflected, jazz-tinged, or mechanically repeated until the repetition itself becomes hypnotic. The melody always knows where it is going, even when the harmony beneath it is iridescent.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Artistic Function |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Clarity above all | Melody always audible, never lost in texture | The listener always knows the tune — even in complex orchestration |
| Spanish/Basque inflection | Phrygian lowered 2nd, augmented 2nds, dance rhythms | Iberian warmth heard even in "abstract" works |
| Modal basis | Dorian, Phrygian, Mixolydian preferred over chromatic | Archaic warmth without chromatic complication |
| Regular phrase structure | 4+4, 8+8 periods more common than in Debussy | Classical balance — Ravel respects periodicity |
| Repetition as principle | Same melody repeated exactly, many times (Boléro = 18 repetitions) | The melody is a given; orchestral color is the variable |
| Ornamental precision | Every grace note, trill, and turn is exact and notated | Nothing left to performer interpretation — Ravel controls everything |
| Wide dynamic range in melody | Melody can be pp (Pavane) or ff (Boléro climax) | The melody's character changes with its dynamic/timbral context |

## Melodic Construction Principles

### The Period vs. The Arabesque

| Ravel (Period) | Debussy (Arabesque) |
|---------------|---------------------|
| 4+4 or 8+8 bar phrases, clear | 5, 7, 11 bars — asymmetric, unpredictable |
| Melody ends with identifiable cadence | Melody dissolves or overlaps |
| Melody sits above harmony, distinct | Melody emerges from harmonic texture |
| Can be extracted and hummed | Often inseparable from its texture |
| Clear melodic peak and resolution | Peak may be anywhere; resolution optional |

### Intervallic Vocabulary

| Interval | Frequency | Expressive Meaning | Context |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| Step (M2) | Very high | Default motion — singing, natural | All melodic contexts |
| Minor 2nd | Moderate | Phrygian color, chromatic inflection | Spanish passages; grace notes |
| Minor 3rd | High | Warm, folk-like | Pentatonic passages, Basque themes |
| Major 3rd | Moderate | Bright, open | Theme openings, clarinet/horn melodies |
| Perfect 4th | Moderate | Open, modal | Quartal themes; Boléro opening |
| Augmented 2nd | Low-moderate | "Gypsy"/Spanish exotic color | Tzigane, Rapsodie espagnole |
| Perfect 5th | Low | Archaic, open | Pastoral themes; Ma Mère l'Oye |
| Octave | Rare | Dramatic, declamatory | Climactic moments; concerto solo entries |

```abc
X:1
T:Ravel — Spanish Melody (Rapsodie espagnole character)
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:Am
%% Phrygian inflection: the Bb (lowered 2nd) gives the Spanish color
A2B2c2 | d4 _B2 | A2^G2A2 | E6 |
%% The _B in bar 2 = Phrygian. The ^G in bar 3 = raised leading tone. Both together = Spanish mode.
```

## Boléro Melody — Repetition as Art

The Boléro melody repeats 18 times without variation. The melody itself never changes — only the orchestration does. This is Ravel's most radical melodic statement: the melody is fixed; color is variable.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Key | C major/minor (alternating A and B themes) |
| Length | Each theme statement = 16 bars |
| Pitch variation | Zero — the melody is identical each time |
| Rhythmic variation | Zero — the rhythm is identical each time |
| What changes | Instrumentation, dynamics, doublings, countermelodies |
| Total duration | ~15 minutes of the same two melodies |

```abc
X:2
T:Ravel — Boléro Theme A (Opening character)
M:3/4
L:1/16
K:C
%% The melody that repeats 18 times — clear, modal, singable
c4d4e4 | f4e4d4 | c4B4c4 | d8 z4 |
%% Simple. Diatonic. Perfectly periodic. The genius is in what surrounds it.
```

## Basque/Folk Melody

Ravel's mother sang Basque songs to him as a child. These melodies — modal, repetitive, pentatonic — surface throughout his work.

| Folk Trait | Ravel's Translation | Example |
|-----------|---------------------|---------|
| Pentatonic contour | 5-note melodies without semitones | Ma Mère l'Oye: Pavane de la Belle |
| Repetitive structure | Short phrases repeated with variation | Piano Trio, mvt 1 (Basque theme) |
| Modal scales | Dorian, Mixolydian without chromatic alteration | Chansons madécasses |
| Dance rhythm integration | Melody and rhythm inseparable | Boléro, Rapsodie espagnole |
| Narrow range | Melody stays within an octave | Many slow-movement themes |

## Orchestral Melody

When Ravel writes for orchestra, the melody is almost always given to a single solo instrument — not a section, not a doubling. The soloist sings; the orchestra colors.

| Instrument | Melodic Character | Example Work |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------|
| Flute | Bright, pastoral, high | Ma Mère l'Oye |
| Clarinet | Warm, vocal, intimate | Boléro (early passes) |
| Oboe | Plaintive, nasal, expressive | Piano Concerto in G, slow mvt |
| English horn | Melancholy distance | Daphnis et Chloé, dawn scene |
| Bassoon | Dark, sardonic, low | Boléro (opening solo — extreme high register) |
| Horn | Noble, warm, hunting calls | Pavane orchestral version |
| Solo violin | Brilliant, passionate, virtuosic | Tzigane |
| Solo piano | Clear, percussive, crystalline | Both piano concertos |

```abc
X:3
T:Ravel — Orchestral Solo Melody (Daphnis et Chloé, dawn)
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:A
V:Fl name="Solo Flute"
V:Str name="Strings (background)"
%% The flute sings alone; strings provide color beneath
[V:Fl] A2B2c2 | e4 d2 | c2B2A2 | A6 |
[V:Str] [A,CE]6 | [A,CE]6 | [A,CE]6 | [A,CE]6 |
%% One melody, one color. The clarity is absolute.
```

## Vocal Melody

Ravel's vocal writing respects French prosody but shapes it into clear melodic contour — more "sung" than Debussy's speech-song.

| Vocal Principle | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| Follows French accent | Stressed syllables on strong beats, but not rigidly |
| Wider range than Debussy | Vocal lines can span an octave or more |
| Clear melodic shape | You can identify the tune without the words |
| Sparse accompaniment | Voice always prominent — accompaniment supports, never competes |

## Anti-Patterns in Ravel Melody

| Wrong | Why | Instead |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Melody lost in texture | Ravel's melody is always audible | Solo instrument or clear top voice |
| Asymmetric phrases throughout | Sounds Debussy, not Ravel | Regular periods: 4+4, 8+8 |
| Melody that varies with each repetition | Un-Ravelian — repetition IS the technique | Exact repetition; vary the orchestration |
| Heavy chromatic inflection | Too Wagnerian/late-Romantic | Modal (Phrygian, Dorian, pentatonic) |
| Melody without dance rhythm | Ravel's melodies often have dance DNA | Bolero, habanera, waltz rhythms underneath |
