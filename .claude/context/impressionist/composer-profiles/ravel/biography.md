# Maurice Ravel — Biography

A Swiss-Basque watchmaker's son who became the supreme orchestral craftsman of the 20th century. Ravel's music has the precision of a Swiss mechanism and the warmth of Basque folk song — a paradox that defines his art. He was a miniaturist who could fill a concert hall, a perfectionist who spent years polishing a single piece, and a man who hid profound emotion behind a mask of ironic detachment. The mask was the art.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Role | Significance |
|------|----------|------|-------------|
| 1875 | Ciboure (Basque Country) | Born | Father Swiss-French engineer; mother Basque. Moved to Paris at 3 months |
| 1889 | Paris Conservatoire | Student (age 14) | Studied piano with Bériot; later composition with Fauré and Gédalge |
| 1893-95 | Paris | Early compositions | Menuet antique, early songs; Fauré's influence dominant |
| 1898 | Paris | Emerging voice | Pavane pour une infante défunte; Sites auriculaires with Viñes |
| 1901 | Paris | Prix de Rome: 2nd place | Cantata Myrrha; beginning of the scandal |
| 1903 | Paris | String Quartet | Debussy comparison begins; masterful chamber work at age 28 |
| 1905 | Paris | Prix de Rome scandal | Rejected at preliminary round despite being the outstanding candidate; public outcry forced Dubois to resign as Conservatoire director |
| 1905-08 | Paris | Mature masterworks | Miroirs, Sonatine, Rapsodie espagnole, Gaspard de la Nuit |
| 1909-12 | Paris | Orchestral maturity | Daphnis et Chloé (Diaghilev commission), Ma Mère l'Oye |
| 1914-17 | Front lines | Ambulance driver | Volunteered despite small stature (rejected from infantry); served at Verdun; mother's death 1917 devastated him |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Basque-Swiss Heritage

Ravel's dual heritage is audible in every bar: the mechanical precision of Swiss engineering and the warm, modal folk music of the Basque Country.

| Heritage | Musical Manifestation | Example Works |
|----------|----------------------|--------------|
| Swiss precision | Clockwork rhythmic regularity, exact repetition, meticulous craft | Boléro, L'heure espagnole (clock shop setting) |
| Basque folk melody | Modal scales, Phrygian inflection, dance rhythms | Rapsodie espagnole, Piano Trio (Basque themes) |
| Mother's Basque songs | Lullabies, folk melodies heard in childhood | Ma Mère l'Oye, Basque-inflected themes throughout |
| Father's engineering | Structural clarity, architectural form | Concerto in G — Classical form with jazz harmony |

### Fauré and the Conservatoire

| What Fauré Taught | How Ravel Absorbed It |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Elegant modal harmony | Extended into jazz-inflected added-note chords |
| Melodic refinement | Crystalline precision in every melodic line |
| Formal proportion | Classical balance maintained even in most advanced works |
| Restraint, anti-Romantic poise | Ironic detachment that masks emotional depth |

```abc
K:G
%% Elegant, modal, restrained — Fauré's student, but the harmony is already richer
B2d2 e2d2 | c2B2 A4 | G2A2 B2G2 | E6 z2 |
%% Regular phrasing, clear melody, archaic quality — the "antique" is the Fauré inheritance.
```

### The Prix de Rome Scandal (1905)

Ravel competed for the Prix de Rome five times. In 1905, having already composed Jeux d'eau, the String Quartet, and Pavane pour une infante défunte, he was eliminated in the preliminary round by a jury stacked with conservatives. The public outcry was immense — the "Affaire Ravel" made front-page news and forced the director's resignation.

| Effect on Ravel | Musical Consequence |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Confirmed outsider status | Composed without institutional obligation |
| Public recognition of his talent | Early celebrity; commissions followed |
| Deepened anti-establishment stance | Refused Légion d'honneur (1920); never joined academies |
| Liberated from academic style | Free to pursue Spanish, jazz, and modernist influences |

### World War I

Ravel served as an ambulance driver near Verdun — too small for infantry, too determined to stay home. The war changed him. His mother's death in 1917 broke something permanently.

| Before WWI | After WWI |
|-----------|----------|
| Elegant, playful, sometimes ironic | Darker, more complex, occasionally savage |
| Rich orchestral textures | Sometimes stripped, chamber-scale |
| Humor and charm | La Valse: waltz as disintegration; grotesque behind the elegant |
| Emotional distance as aesthetic | Emotional distance as survival mechanism |

```abc
X:2
T:Ravel — Post-War Darkness (La Valse character)
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:D
%% The waltz becomes sinister — elegance disintegrating
D2F2A2 | d4 ^c2 | d2c2B2 | _A4 z2 |
%% The _A in bar 4 — the waltz world cracking. Ravel's post-war shadow.
```

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Student/Early | 1893-1901 | Fauré influence; emerging personal voice; antique elegance | Pavane, Menuet antique, Jeux d'eau |
| First Maturity | 1901-08 | Full command; virtuoso piano writing; Spanish color | String Quartet, Miroirs, Gaspard de la Nuit, Rapsodie espagnole |
| Orchestral Peak | 1909-14 | Supreme orchestrator; ballet; fairy tale | Daphnis et Chloé, Ma Mère l'Oye, Valses nobles et sentimentales |
| War and Aftermath | 1914-22 | Darkness enters; La Valse; Tombeau de Couperin (memorial) | La Valse, Le Tombeau de Couperin, Sonata for Violin and Cello |
| Late Mastery | 1924-32 | Jazz influence; concertos; Boléro; maximum craft | Boléro, Piano Concerto in G, Left Hand Concerto, Tzigane |
