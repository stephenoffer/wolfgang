# Ravel Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Ravel shares Debussy's harmonic vocabulary and almost none of his temperament.
Where Debussy blurs, Ravel is **precise**: the rhythms are exact, the textures are
cleanly voiced, and the pulse is firm. He is a Classicist writing Impressionist
harmony.

## LH idiom catalog

1. **Precise ostinato** — a figure repeated exactly, in strict time, for pages.
   The *Boléro* principle applies everywhere in his music.
2. **Wide arpeggios, cleanly voiced** — every note audible rather than washed
   together.
3. **Parallel chords in strict rhythm** — planing, but rhythmically exact.
4. **Dance-form accompaniment** — waltz, forlane, rigaudon, pavane. The bass keeps
   the dance.
5. **Added-note chords in close position** — `[C3,D3,F3,G3]`, crisp rather than
   resonant.
6. **Repeated notes as a mechanism** — clockwork, music-box, machine textures.
7. **Bare fifths in the bass** for archaism, especially in the neoclassical works.
8. **Spanish idiom** — habanera rhythm, guitar-like strummed chords, drone fifths.
9. **Independent contrapuntal bass** — cleaner and more classical than Debussy's.
10. **Extreme register separation** — very low and very high with a deliberate gap
    in the middle.
11. **Cascading arpeggio across the full keyboard** as a virtuoso gesture.

## Where the idiom changes
At structural boundaries, which are clear and Classical. Ravel's forms are tidy.

## Breathing
Clean phrase endings with real cadences. Less silence than Debussy and more
articulation.

## What to avoid
- **Rhythmic vagueness.** Ravel's pulse is exact.
- **Muddy pedalling.** The voicing should stay audible.
- **Formal looseness.** The forms are Classical even when the harmony is not.
