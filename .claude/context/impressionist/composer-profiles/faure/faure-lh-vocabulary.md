# Fauré Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Fauré is **restrained, flowing and harmonically subtle**. The left hand is a
smooth, continuous flow of gentle figuration — never dramatic, never percussive,
and almost always in the middle register. The subtlety is in the harmony, not the
texture.

## LH idiom catalog
1. **Flowing broken chords, unbroken** — `C3e G3e E4e G3e` continuously, even and
   quiet, for whole sections.
2. **Gently syncopated accompaniment** — the chord entering just off the beat.
3. **Arpeggio spanning an octave and a half**, smooth and unhurried.
4. **Sustained chords with a moving inner voice** — the harmony shifting
   underneath a still surface.
5. **Repeated chords, soft**, under a vocal line.
6. **Contrapuntal inner voice** — Fauré's textures are more linear than they
   sound.
7. **Walking bass in slow harmonic rhythm.**
8. **Held pedal note with the harmony changing above it.**
9. **Wide spacing without weight** — open, but never heavy.
10. **The barcarolle lilt** — a rocking 6/8 accompaniment.

## Where the idiom changes
Rarely. Fauré maintains a texture for long stretches and lets the harmony provide
the movement — closer to Schubert than to Debussy in that respect.

## Breathing
Long phrases with elided cadences; the music flows continuously and rarely stops.

## What to avoid
- **Drama.** The dynamic range is narrow and mostly quiet.
- **Percussive attack.** Everything is legato.
- **Textural extremes.** The middle register is home.
