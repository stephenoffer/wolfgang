# Gabriel Faure — Formal Approach

Faure works within Classical forms — sonata, song form, variation — but transforms them through his harmonic language. Where Beethoven's sonata form is driven by tonal conflict and resolution, Faure's is driven by continuous modulation and avoided cadences. The forms are recognizable but softened: boundaries blur, sections overlap, recapitulations arrive in unexpected keys. The music flows rather than argues.

## Core Formal Principles

| Principle | Description | Effect |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Elided phrase boundaries | Phrases overlap — the end of one is the beginning of the next | Continuous flow; no stopping points |
| Modulating recapitulation | Second theme returns in a key other than the tonic | The "resolution" is not fully resolved — the journey continues |
| Plagal codas | Codas close with IV-I, not V-I | Settling rather than arriving |
| Developmental flow | Development is not a dramatic confrontation but a continuous modulation | Gentle, wandering, not combative |
| Cyclic unity | Themes from one movement/song return in later ones | La Bonne Chanson; Piano Quintet No.2 |
| Late compression | Late works shorten and strip forms to essentials | String Quartet Op.121: no padding, no display |

## Form Types in Faure

| Form | Faure's Usage | Key Examples |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| Sonata form | Modified — continuous modulation replaces tonal drama | Violin Sonata Op.13, Piano Quartets |
| Song form (ABA/ABA') | The melodie art; strophic or through-composed | ~100 melodies |
| Song cycle | Songs linked by recurring themes | La Bonne Chanson Op.61, L'Horizon chimerique Op.118 |
| Ternary (ABA) | Simple, elegant, often with varied return | Nocturnes, Barcarolles |
| Rondo-like | Recurring refrain with episodes, but boundaries softened | Some Barcarolles |
| Theme and variations | Rare but significant | Piano trio (variation movement) |
| Fugal | Academic training evident, used sparingly | Requiem "Offertoire" fugal passages |
| Free/through-composed | Late works: form follows harmonic logic alone | Late Nocturnes, String Quartet |

## Sonata Form in Faure

| Section | Classical Practice | Faure's Transformation |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Exposition (1st theme) | Tonic key, strong profile | Tonic key, but melody begins gently — no declarative opening |
| Transition | Modulation to dominant or relative major | Continuous modulation through multiple keys; no clear transition zone |
| Exposition (2nd theme) | Dominant or relative major | May arrive in mediant, submediant, or other third-related key |
| Development | Fragmentation, modulation, tension-building | Continuous, gentle modulation; themes combined but not fragmented |
| Recapitulation | Both themes in tonic | First theme in tonic, but second theme may remain in a non-tonic key |
| Coda | Tonic confirmation, often dramatic | Plagal cadence, diminuendo, the music settles rather than triumphs |

```abc
X:1
T:Faure — Sonata Form Character (Violin Sonata Op.13 opening)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:A
%% The first theme: warm, singing, in A major — but already leaning toward modulation
!p!A2c2 e2d2|c2B2 A2G2|F2A2 c2e2|d4 c4|
%% By bar 4, the harmony has already shifted — the tonic is present but not anchored
```

## Song Form — The Melodie

Faure's melodies are the heart of his formal art. The form is deceptively simple: the structure serves the poetry.

| Structure | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| Strophic | Same music for each stanza; rare in mature works | Early songs only |
| Modified strophic | Same melodic shape, different harmony each stanza | "Lydia" Op.4, "Clair de lune" Op.46 |
| Through-composed | Each stanza has new music following the text | Most mature melodies |
| ABA with varied return | A section returns with harmonic or melodic changes | Many Verlaine settings |
| Cyclic song form | Themes from earlier songs return in later songs | La Bonne Chanson Op.61 |

### La Bonne Chanson — Cyclic Structure

| Theme | First Appearance | Returns In |
|-------|-----------------|-----------|
| Theme 1 ("Puisque l'aube grandit") | Song 1 | Songs 5, 9 |
| Theme 2 ("La lune blanche") | Song 3 | Song 9 |
| Theme 3 ("J'allais par des chemins") | Song 4 | Song 9 |
| Theme 4 (love theme) | Song 6 | Songs 7, 9 |
| Theme 5 (joy) | Song 8 | Song 9 |
| Song 9 ("L'hiver a cesse") | Combines all 5 themes | — |

## The Requiem — Formal Architecture

| Movement | Duration | Form | Emotional Arc |
|----------|----------|------|---------------|
| Introït et Kyrie | ~7 min | ABA (Introït / Kyrie / Introït return) | Gentle grief, not anguish |
| Offertoire | ~8 min | Through-composed with fugal passages | The most complex movement; light and shadow |
| Sanctus | ~3 min | Simple ABA; soprano solo | Luminous peace |
| Pie Jesu | ~4 min | Strophic; solo soprano (or boy treble) | The emotional center: simplicity as profundity |
| Agnus Dei | ~6 min | Rondo-like (refrain + episodes) | Calm acceptance |
| Libera me | ~5 min | Through-composed; the one dramatic movement | Brief intensity; the only ff in the Requiem |
| In Paradisum | ~4 min | Through-composed; dissolves into silence | Pure consolation; the music evaporates into D major |

```abc
X:2
T:Faure — Requiem "In Paradisum" Character (dissolution into light)
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:D
%% The final movement: simple, luminous, floating toward silence
!pp!D2F2A2|d4 c2|B2A2G2|F6|
%% The music thins, rises, and disappears. No cadence. Only light.
```

## Nocturnes and Barcarolles — Piano Form

| Form Element | Nocturnes | Barcarolles |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Structure | ABA or ABAB'A | Ternary or rondo-like |
| A section | Lyrical, arpeggiated, flowing | Rocking 6/8 rhythm, singing melody |
| B section | More agitated, modulatory | Contrasting episode, often darker |
| Return of A | Varied — new harmony, new key, or abbreviated | Often with added intensity |
| Coda | Diminuendo to pp; the music fades | Same — the ending is always soft |
| Duration | 4-8 minutes (early shorter, late longer) | 4-7 minutes |

## Late Works — Formal Compression

| Work | Duration | Form | What's Stripped Away |
|------|----------|------|---------------------|
| Cello Sonata No.1 Op.109 | ~18 min | 3 movements, compressed | Transitions shortened; development sections lean |
| Cello Sonata No.2 Op.117 | ~17 min | 3 movements, austere | Hardly any repetition; through-composed quality |
| Piano Trio Op.120 | ~22 min | 3 movements | Each movement concentrated; no filler |
| String Quartet Op.121 | ~25 min | 3 movements | Bare texture; no accompaniment layer; every note essential |

## Proportional Tendencies

| Section | Proportion of Movement | Faure Signature |
|---------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Exposition | 35-40% | Standard, but with continuous modulation within |
| Development | 20-25% | Shorter than Classical norm; less combative |
| Recapitulation | 25-30% | Often compressed or harmonically varied |
| Coda | 10-15% | Plagal, diminuendo, settling |
