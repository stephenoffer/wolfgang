# Fauré Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `faure-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **harmonic subtlety
under a calm surface.** The modulations are constant and almost imperceptible;
the texture barely changes while the tonality drifts a long way.

## Harmonic devices
1. **Constant, gentle modulation** — moving through several keys in a phrase
   without any of them being announced.
2. **Modal writing** — Lydian and Aeolian inflections inside a tonal frame.
3. **Seventh and ninth chords as stable sonorities**, not as tensions.
4. **Chromatic mediant shifts** made smooth by common tones.
5. **The unresolved appoggiatura** left as colour.
6. **Enharmonic reinterpretation** used quietly rather than dramatically.
7. **Avoided cadences** — the expected resolution sidestepped repeatedly so the
   phrase extends.
8. **Plagal colour** at closes.

## Melodic devices
9. **The long, calm vocal line** — Fauré is a song composer above all.
10. **Stepwise melody with a narrow range.**
11. **Melodic sequence rising gently** without a dramatic peak.
12. **The line that begins mid-phrase**, as though already in progress.

## Structural and rhythmic devices
13. **Elided phrase endings** — one phrase begins before the last has closed.
14. **Continuous flow** — very few full stops.
15. **Gentle syncopation** as a constant, not as an effect.
16. **Barcarolle and nocturne forms.**
17. **Ternary with a subtly varied return.**
18. **The quiet ending**, almost always.
19. **Restrained climax** — the peak of a Fauré piece may be `mf`.

## What to avoid
- **Drama and dynamic extremes.**
- **Abrupt modulation.** The shifts should be almost unnoticed.
- **Strong cadential punctuation.** The style flows.
- **Debussy's colouristic effects.** Fauré is linear and classical in method.
