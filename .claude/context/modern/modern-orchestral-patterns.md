# Modern Orchestral Patterns

Orchestral patterns for the 20th-century modern period (c. 1900-1970). These patterns appear in works by Bartok, Stravinsky, Shostakovich, Prokofiev, and their contemporaries. Each entry includes a name, reference, ABC notation, usage context, and variation suggestions.

---

## Bartok String Effects

### MP1. Snap Pizzicato (Bartok Pizzicato)
**Ref:** Bartok String Quartet No. 4, IV; Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta
```abc
X:800
T:Modern Snap Pizzicato
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Cm
V:Vn1 name="Violin I (snap pizz.)" clef=treble
!ff! C2 z2 _E2 z2 | G2 z2 C2 z2 |
V:Vn2 name="Violin II (snap pizz.)" clef=treble
!ff! z2 _E2 z2 G2 | z2 C2 z2 _E2 |
V:Vla name="Viola (snap pizz.)" clef=alto
!ff! _E,2 z2 G,2 z2 | C2 z2 _E2 z2 |
V:Vc name="Cello (snap pizz.)" clef=bass
!ff! C,2 z2 G,,2 z2 | _E,2 z2 C,2 z2 |
```
**Context:** Percussive, aggressive passages; the string snaps back against the fingerboard, producing a sharp crack — the string section becomes a drum battery.
**Variation:** Alternate snap pizz. with arco for textural shock; use single snap notes as accents within arco passages.

### MP2. Col Legno Battuto
**Ref:** Bartok Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta, III; Holst The Planets, "Mars"
```abc
X:801
T:Modern Col Legno Battuto
M:7/8
L:1/8
K:C
V:Vn1 name="Violin I (col legno)" clef=treble
!p! C D E z C D E | z C D E C D z |
V:Vn2 name="Violin II (col legno)" clef=treble
!p! z C D E z C D | E z C D E C D |
V:Vla name="Viola (col legno)" clef=alto
!p! E z C D E z C | D E z C D E z |
```
**Context:** Eerie, skeletal textures; striking with the wood of the bow produces a dry, clicking sound — ghostly rhythm without pitch warmth.
**Variation:** Combine with normal bowing in lower strings; use in rhythmic canon; alternate with pizzicato.

### MP3. String Glissandi
**Ref:** Bartok Concerto for Orchestra, IV; Penderecki Threnody
```abc
X:802
T:Modern String Glissandi
M:4/4
L:1/2
K:C
V:Vn1 name="Violin I (gliss.)" clef=treble
C !gliss! c | c !gliss! C |
V:Vn2 name="Violin II (gliss.)" clef=treble
E !gliss! e | e !gliss! E |
V:Vla name="Viola (gliss.)" clef=alto
G, !gliss! G | G !gliss! G, |
V:Vc name="Cello (gliss.)" clef=bass
C, !gliss! C | C !gliss! C, |
```
**Context:** Swooping gestures, comic effects, portamento expressiveness; slides between notes create vocal, almost human quality.
**Variation:** Stagger the glissandi across parts; use contrary-motion glissandi (half up, half down); combine with tremolo.

## Stravinsky Layered Ostinati

### MP4. Rhythmic Cells in Different Meters
**Ref:** Stravinsky The Rite of Spring, "Augurs of Spring"; Petrushka
```abc
X:803
T:Modern Polymetric Ostinati
M:2/4
L:1/8
K:Eb
V:Vn1 name="Strings (accented chords)" clef=treble
!ff! z [_EG_Bc] z [_EG_Bc] | [_EG_Bc] z z [_EG_Bc] | z [_EG_Bc] [_EG_Bc] z | [_EG_Bc] z z [_EG_Bc] |
V:Hn name="Horn (3-note cell)" clef=treble
!f! _E2 _E _E | z _E2 _E | _E z _E2 | _E _E z _E |
V:Bsn name="Bassoon (2-note cell)" clef=bass
!f! _E, _B, _E, _B, | _E, _B, _E, _B, | _E, _B, _E, _B, | _E, _B, _E, _B, |
```
**Context:** Primitive, driving rhythmic passages; different instruments repeat cells of different lengths, creating ever-shifting accent patterns.
**Variation:** Add or subtract notes from cells during the passage; change instruments on cells; sudden tutti unison breaks the polymetry.

### MP5. Octatonic Layering
**Ref:** Stravinsky Petrushka; Firebird
```abc
X:804
T:Modern Octatonic Layering
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:C
V:Fl name="Flute (octatonic run)" clef=treble
!mf! C4D4_E4F4 | _G4_A4A4B4 | c4d4_e4f4 | _g8 z8 |
V:Cl name="Clarinet (sustained octatonic chord)" clef=treble
!mf! [C_EF_A]8 [C_EF_A]8 | [C_EF_A]8 [C_EF_A]8 | [D_G_AB]8 [D_G_AB]8 | [C_EF_A]8 z8 |
V:Vc name="Cello (octatonic bass)" clef=bass
!mf! C,4 _E,4 _G,4 A,4 | C,4 _E,4 _G,4 A,4 | D,4 F,4 _A,4 B,4 | C,8 z8 |
```
**Context:** Exotic, ambiguous tonality; the octatonic (diminished) scale provides rich harmonic color that is neither major nor minor.
**Variation:** Alternate between the three octatonic collections; use octatonic over diatonic pedal for bitonal tension.

## Shostakovich Patterns

### MP6. Sardonic March (Brass)
**Ref:** Shostakovich Symphony No. 5, II; Symphony No. 7, "Invasion"
```abc
X:805
T:Modern Sardonic March
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Dm
V:Tpt name="Trumpet (sardonic melody)" clef=treble
!mf! D2D2 F2F2 | A2A2 d2d2 | ^c2d2 A2F2 | D4 z4 |
V:Sn name="Snare Drum"
!p! D,2D,2D,2D,2 | D,2D,2D,2D,2 | D,2D,2D,2D,2 | D,4 z4 |
V:Vc name="Cello (mechanical)" clef=bass
!p! D,2 z2 A,,2 z2 | D,2 z2 A,,2 z2 | D,2 z2 A,,2 z2 | D,4 z4 |
```
**Context:** Grotesque scherzo movements, mock-military passages; repetitive, mechanical rhythm with distorted melody conveys irony and menace.
**Variation:** Add gradual crescendo from ppp to fff (Bolero-like invasion buildup); distort melody further with each repetition.

### MP7. Sudden Dynamic Extremes (ff/pp)
**Ref:** Shostakovich Symphony No. 10, II; Symphony No. 5, I
```abc
X:806
T:Modern Sudden Dynamic Extremes
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Cm
V:Vn1 name="Violin I" clef=treble
!fff! [CG]4 [CG]4 | !ppp! G,2A,2B,2C2 | D2E2F2G2 | !fff! [CG]4 z4 |
V:Tbn name="Trombone" clef=bass
!fff! C,4 G,,4 | !ppp! z8 | z8 | !fff! C,4 z4 |
V:Timp name="Timpani"
!fff! C,4 G,,4 | !ppp! z8 | z8 | !fff! C,4 z4 |
```
**Context:** Terror, psychological shock; the violent alternation between extremes creates an atmosphere of unpredictability and fear.
**Variation:** Use fff single chord followed by extended ppp solo; add silence between extremes for maximum disorientation.

## Prokofiev Motor Rhythms

### MP8. Driving Repeated Notes
**Ref:** Prokofiev Symphony No. 5, IV; Piano Concerto No. 3
```abc
X:807
T:Modern Motor Rhythm
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:C
V:Vn1 name="Violin I" clef=treble
!f! C4C4C4C4 | D4D4D4D4 | E4E4E4E4 | C4C4C4C4 |
V:Vn2 name="Violin II" clef=treble
!f! G,4G,4G,4G,4 | A,4A,4A,4A,4 | B,4B,4B,4B,4 | G,4G,4G,4G,4 |
V:Vc name="Cello" clef=bass
!f! C,4 E,4 G,4 C4 | D,4 F,4 A,4 D4 | E,4 G,4 B,4 E4 | C,4 G,,4 C,4 z4 |
```
**Context:** Toccata-like finales, moto perpetuo passages; relentless mechanical energy with angular harmonic shifts.
**Variation:** Shift the repeated pitch by semitone each bar; add accents on irregular beats; combine with lyrical counter-melody.

### MP9. Angular Melody
**Ref:** Prokofiev Symphony No. 1 "Classical," I; Romeo and Juliet
```abc
X:808
T:Modern Angular Melody
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
V:Ob name="Oboe (angular melody)" clef=treble
!mf! C2 ^G2 E2 _B2 | ^F2 D2 A2 _E2 | c2 G2 _D2 A,2 | C4 z4 |
V:Bsn name="Bassoon (bass)" clef=bass
!mf! C,4 E,4 | _B,,4 D,4 | _A,,4 F,,4 | C,4 z4 |
```
**Context:** Themes with wide leaps, chromatic angularity; the melody feels athletic, witty, slightly off-balance.
**Variation:** Invert the intervals; augment rhythmically for slower statement; fragment for development.

## Percussion as Primary Voice

### MP10. Percussion Ensemble Passage
**Ref:** Bartok Sonata for Two Pianos and Percussion; Stravinsky Les Noces
```abc
X:809
T:Modern Percussion as Primary Voice
M:5/8
L:1/8
K:C
V:Xyl name="Xylophone" clef=treble
!f! C E G z C | E z G C z |
V:Sn name="Snare Drum"
z D, z D, z | D, z z D, D, |
V:BD name="Bass Drum"
C,, z z z C,, | z z C,, z z |
V:Tym name="Timpani"
C, z G,, z C, | z G,, z C, z |
```
**Context:** Rhythmic interludes, primitive/ritualistic passages; percussion carries the structural argument, not just color.
**Variation:** Add pitched percussion (vibraphone, marimba) for melodic content; layer multiple rhythmic cells.

## Polyrhythmic Layering

### MP11. Cross-Rhythm Texture (3 against 4)
**Ref:** Bartok Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta, I; Carter String Quartets
```abc
X:810
T:Modern Polyrhythmic Layering (3 vs 4)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
V:Vn1 name="Violin I (4 groups)" clef=treble
!mf! C2 E2 G2 c2 | C2 E2 G2 c2 |
V:Vla name="Viola (3 groups)" clef=alto
!mf! C2E2 G2C2 E2G2 | C2E2 G2C2 E2G2 |
V:Vc name="Cello (sustained)" clef=bass
!mf! C,8 | C,8 |
```
**Context:** Metric complexity, development sections; the listener perceives shifting accents as the patterns phase against each other.
**Variation:** Add a 5-group layer in winds; gradually align all layers for climactic unison; use cross-rhythms between pitched and unpitched instruments.

## Tone Clusters and Sound Masses

### MP12. Tone Cluster (String Divisi)
**Ref:** Penderecki Threnody; Ligeti Atmospheres
```abc
X:811
T:Modern Tone Cluster
M:4/4
L:1/1
K:C
V:Vn1a name="Vn I div. a" clef=treble
!ppp! C4 |
V:Vn1b name="Vn I div. b" clef=treble
!ppp! ^C4 |
V:Vn2a name="Vn II div. a" clef=treble
!ppp! D4 |
V:Vn2b name="Vn II div. b" clef=treble
!ppp! ^D4 |
V:Vlaa name="Vla div. a" clef=alto
!ppp! E4 |
V:Vlab name="Vla div. b" clef=alto
!ppp! F4 |
V:Vc name="Cello" clef=bass
!ppp! ^F,4 |
```
**Context:** Sound mass, static texture, sonic landscapes; individual pitches dissolve into a band of sound — color without melody or harmony in the traditional sense.
**Variation:** Slowly expand the cluster outward (both directions); contract to unison; add tremolo for extra density; crescendo from ppp to fff.

### MP13. Extended Techniques Catalog
**Summary table of modern extended techniques with ABC shorthand:**

| Technique | Instrument | ABC Marking | Effect |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Snap pizzicato | Strings | `!snap!` | Sharp crack, percussive |
| Col legno battuto | Strings | `!collegno!` | Dry clicking, ghostly |
| Col legno tratto | Strings | `!cltratto!` | Scratchy, thin bowing |
| Sul ponticello | Strings | `!sulpont!` | Glassy, overtone-rich |
| Sul tasto | Strings | `!sultasto!` | Flautando, ethereal |
| Harmonics (natural) | Strings | `!harm!` | Pure, whistling overtone |
| Mute (con sordino) | Strings/Brass | `!mute!` | Veiled, distant |
| Flutter-tongue | Winds/Brass | `!flutter!` | Buzzing, tremolo-like |
| Multiphonics | Winds | `!multi!` | Two+ pitches simultaneously |
| Key clicks | Winds | `!keyclick!` | Percussive, pitched |
| Stopped horn | Horn | `!stopped!` | Nasal, metallic |
| Rim shot | Percussion | `!rimshot!` | Sharp crack |
| Prepared piano | Piano | `!prep!` | Altered timbre per note |

**Context:** Modern scores regularly combine multiple extended techniques; the table above provides the ABC vocabulary for indicating these effects.
