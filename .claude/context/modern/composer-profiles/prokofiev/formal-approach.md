# Sergei Prokofiev — Formal Approach

Prokofiev is a Classicist. Unlike Stravinsky, who shatters Classical forms into blocks, or Shostakovich, who distorts them into testimony, Prokofiev genuinely inhabits sonata form, rondo, and variation — with 20th-century harmonic language filling traditional containers. The Classical Symphony (No. 1) is the manifesto: Haydn's architecture, Prokofiev's vocabulary. His ballet scores add a narrative-dramatic dimension where form follows story, but even there the internal structure of each number is remarkably Classical.

## Core Formal Philosophy

| Principle | Description | Contrast with Contemporaries |
|-----------|-------------|------------------------------|
| Classical container, modern content | Sonata, rondo, ternary forms used genuinely (not ironically) | Stravinsky: Classical forms used ironically |
| Clear thematic contrast | First theme vs second theme clearly differentiated in character | Shostakovich: themes often derived from same material |
| Functional recapitulation | Themes return in tonic; recapitulation is structural, not decorative | Stravinsky: literal return or no return |
| Short development | Development sections are compact; not Beethovenian working-out | Shostakovich: development as existential crisis |
| Ballet narrative form | Story determines the sequence of numbers; each number is a closed form | Prokofiev's unique contribution to formal design |
| Cadential clarity | Sections end with clear cadences (even if "wrong-note") | Not ambiguous endings; arrivals are definite |

## Form Types

### Sonata-Allegro (Primary Form)

| Element | Prokofiev's Treatment |
|---------|----------------------|
| Exposition: 1st theme | Motoric, angular, in tonic; strong rhythmic profile |
| Exposition: 2nd theme | Lyrical, singing, in contrasting key; genuine beauty |
| Exposition: closing | Short; often motoric, staccato; decisive |
| Development | Compact; themes fragmented, keys shift rapidly; motoric energy |
| Recapitulation | Both themes in tonic; 2nd theme may be reharmonized with "wrong" chords |
| Coda | Either motoric hammering (ff) or lyrical fadeaway (pp) |

```abc
X:1
T:Sonata Form — Two Contrasting Themes
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:C
%% Theme 1: motoric, angular, staccato
!ff!c2c2 B2c2 d2^d2 e4|c2c2 _B2A2 G2^G2 A4|
%% Theme 2: lyrical, stepwise, legato
K:G
!p!B,2D2 G2A2 B4 A2G2|F2E2 D2E2 D8|
%% Contrast: motoric (ff, staccato, chromatic) vs lyrical (p, legato, diatonic)
```

### Rondo

| Element | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| Refrain (A) | Bright, memorable, usually march-like or dance-like |
| Episodes (B, C) | Contrasting character; one lyrical, one grotesque |
| Returns | Refrain returns literally or with slight reharmonization |
| Coda | Extended refrain; accelerando to brilliant close |

### Ternary (ABA)

| Section | Character |
|---------|-----------|
| A | Established character: motoric, march, or grotesque |
| B | Contrasting: if A is fast, B is lyrical (and vice versa) |
| A' | Return; may be abbreviated or intensified |

### Theme and Variations

| Feature | Prokofiev's Approach |
|---------|---------------------|
| Theme | Simple, Classical, clearly stated |
| Variations | Each variation changes texture and character — not just figuration |
| Character variation | One variation motoric, one lyrical, one grotesque, one march |
| Final variation | Often returns to original simplicity or explodes into coda |

## Ballet Form — Narrative Architecture

Prokofiev's three great ballets (*Romeo and Juliet*, *Cinderella*, *The Stone Flower*) are his formal innovation. Each act is a sequence of numbers, each number a closed musical form, but the sequence follows dramatic narrative.

| Structural Unit | Musical Form | Narrative Function |
|----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Character introduction | Short ternary (ABA) | Establishes person + musical identity |
| Pas de deux (love duet) | Extended song form (AABA) | Lyrical climax; slow, intimate |
| Dance of crowd/ensemble | Rondo or strophic | Energy, spectacle, variety |
| Dramatic confrontation | Through-composed or fragmented sonata | Tension, conflict |
| Death/transformation | Slow passacaglia or ground bass | Tragedy; relentless repetition |
| Finale | Recapitulation of themes from act | Closure; thematic recall |

```abc
X:2
T:Ballet Number — Dance Character (Rondo Refrain)
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:D
%% Bright, dance-like refrain — Classical ballet form
!f!D2 F2 A2|d4 c2|B2 A2 G2|F4 E2|D2 F2 A2|d4 ^d2|e2 d2 c2|d6|
%% 8-bar period, clear key, dance rhythm — then episodes contrast
```

## Opera Form

| Opera | Structure | Formal Innovation |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| *Love for Three Oranges* | Prologue + 4 acts; numbered scenes | Commedia dell'arte framework; grotesque |
| *The Fiery Angel* | 5 acts; through-composed scenes | Expressionistic; leitmotif system |
| *War and Peace* | 2 parts (Peace + War); 13 scenes | Epic scope; alternating intimate/public |
| *Betrothal in a Monastery* | 4 acts; ensemble comedy | Mozartean: arias, ensembles, finales |

## Multi-Movement Architecture

| Work | Movements | Key Scheme | Character Arc |
|------|-----------|------------|--------------|
| Classical Symphony | Allegro - Larghetto - Gavotta - Finale | D-A-D-D | Joy - Grace - Dance - Brilliance |
| Symphony No. 5 | Andante - Allegro marcato - Adagio - Allegro giocoso | Bb throughout | Epic - Motoric - Deep - Exuberant |
| Piano Concerto No. 3 | Andante-Allegro - Tema con var. - Allegro ma non troppo | C-E-C | Lyric/motoric - Variation - Brilliant close |
| Piano Sonata No. 7 | Allegro inquieto - Andante caloroso - Precipitato | Bb-E-Bb | Anxiety - Warmth - Violence |

## Proportional Balance

| Section | Typical Proportion | Prokofiev's Treatment |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Exposition | 30–40% of movement | Standard Classical proportions |
| Development | 15–25% | Shorter than Beethoven; compact, energetic |
| Recapitulation | 25–35% | May be compressed; coda extends |
| Coda | 5–15% | Sometimes substantial; motoric or lyrical |

## How Form Serves Expression

| Formal Strategy | Emotional Effect |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Classical sonata | Order, clarity, wit — the Enlightenment through a 20th-century lens |
| Lyrical second theme in sonata | A moment of genuine beauty within aggressive framework |
| Ballet narrative | Story-driven emotion; each number has a dramatic purpose |
| Compact development | Restless energy without Romantic excess |
| Wrong-note recapitulation | The home key returns — but something has changed |
