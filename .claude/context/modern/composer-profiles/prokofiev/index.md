# Sergei Prokofiev — Index

**Era:** Modern | **Nationality:** Russian/Soviet | **1891–1953**

**Key Works:** Romeo and Juliet (ballet), Peter and the Wolf, Classical Symphony (No.1), Symphonies 5–7, Piano Concertos 1–5, Violin Concertos 1–2, Piano Sonatas 6–8 (War Sonatas), Lieutenant Kijé Suite, Love for Three Oranges (opera), War and Peace (opera)

**Style Summary:**
- Tonal but "wrong": Prokofiev writes in keys with identifiable melodies and cadences, but spikes every phrase with a deliberately unexpected harmony — a D major chord where C major is expected.
- Motoric, percussive energy: his fast movements are mechanical, relentless, rhythmically precise. The Scythian Suite, the Toccata. No sentiment; kinetic energy.
- Genuine lyricism alongside the grotesque: Romeo and Juliet's love theme, the Piano Concerto No.3 slow movement. The tenderness is real — not ironic.
- Grotesque character: leering, slightly manic music that grins too wide. March from Love for Three Oranges. Cheerful but wrong.
- Neo-Classical clarity: formal transparency, identifiable themes, tonal centers — even when the harmony bites.
