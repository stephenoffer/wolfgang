# Sergei Prokofiev — Cross-References

Prokofiev exists at a unique intersection: trained in the Russian Romantic tradition (Tchaikovsky, Rimsky-Korsakov), competitive with Stravinsky's modernism, parallel to Shostakovich's Soviet experience, and rooted in Classical formalism that neither rival shared as deeply. Understanding these relationships clarifies what is distinctly Prokofiev.

## Prokofiev vs Stravinsky

Two Russians in Paris, both commissioned by Diaghilev, both labeled "Neo-Classical" — yet fundamentally different.

| Dimension | Prokofiev | Stravinsky |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Melody | Real tunes; singing lines; memorable themes | Cells, fragments, objects; rarely "tunes" |
| Harmony | Tonal with wrong notes; key always felt | Polytonal or pandiatonic; key often absent |
| Form | Genuinely Classical: sonata, rondo, variation | Classical form as ironic container; block juxtaposition |
| Rhythm | Regular pulse with motor drive; 4/4, 2/4 | Changing meter; additive rhythm; asymmetric |
| Expression | Ranges from sardonic to genuinely tender | Impersonal; anti-expression in all periods |
| Orchestration | Classical orchestra used athletically | Stratified layers; winds dominant; anti-blend |
| Development | Classical motivic development (compressed) | Repetition and juxtaposition; no development |
| Neo-Classicism | Genuine inhabitant of Classical forms | Ironic commentator on Classical forms |

### The Diaghilev Connection

| Aspect | Prokofiev's Relationship | Stravinsky's Relationship |
|--------|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| Duration | 1914–1929 (Diaghilev's death) | 1909–1929 |
| Number of ballets | 4 (*Chout*, *Le pas d'acier*, *L'enfant prodigue*, *Sur le Borysthene*) | 7+ (Firebird through Apollo) |
| Creative dominance | Prokofiev: junior partner; commissions adapted to Diaghilev's taste | Stravinsky: equal partner; defined the Ballets Russes sound |
| Post-Diaghilev ballet | Prokofiev's BEST ballets come after (*Romeo*, *Cinderella*) | Stravinsky continued with Balanchine |

## Prokofiev vs Shostakovich

Two Soviet composers under the same political terror — yet their musical responses could not be more different.

| Dimension | Prokofiev | Shostakovich |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Irony | Sardonic, grotesque, grinning — humor that bites | Dark, bitter, political — irony as coded resistance |
| Lyricism | Genuine, unguarded tenderness in slow music | Aching, painful; beauty inseparable from suffering |
| Wrong notes | A sly joke — the note grins at you | A political message — the note is the crack in the mask |
| Orchestration | Classical clarity; lean, athletic | Chamber within orchestra; extreme registers |
| Form | Classical sonata genuinely inhabited | Sonata distorted into testimony; form as autobiography |
| Rhythm | Motoric drive: mechanical, precise, forward | Obsessive ostinato: mechanical, oppressive, trapped |
| Major key | Bright, energetic, sometimes sardonic | Always suspicious; forced cheerfulness = terror |
| Slow movements | Tender; cantabile; beautiful | Anguished; dolente; the music speaks of suffering |
| Soviet experience | Returned voluntarily (1936); initially thrived | Born Soviet; survived within; never left |
| Zhdanov decree | Denounced alongside Shostakovich; less publicly humiliated | Primary target; forced public confession |

## Tchaikovsky Inheritance

Prokofiev inherited more from Tchaikovsky than he would have admitted. The lyrical gift, the ballet instinct, the emotional directness.

| What Prokofiev Inherited | What He Changed |
|-------------------------|-----------------|
| Long, singing melodies | Added angular leaps and "wrong" intervals |
| Ballet as primary dramatic form | Modernized harmony and orchestration |
| Emotional directness in slow music | Surrounded tenderness with irony and motoric energy |
| Russian character | Filtered through 20th-century harmonic language |
| Orchestral brilliance | Made it leaner, more percussive, less lush |

| Specific Connections | Tchaikovsky | Prokofiev |
|--------------------|-------------|-----------|
| Ballet love duet | *Swan Lake* Adagio: lush, string-dominated | *Romeo* love theme: equally beautiful, leaner scoring |
| March | *Nutcracker* March: charming, elegant | *Three Oranges* March: charming but grotesque |
| Symphonic slow movement | Symphony No. 6, Andante: despair | Symphony No. 5, Adagio: aching lyricism |
| Fairy tale | *Sleeping Beauty*: magical, ornate | *Cinderella*: magical with wrong notes |

## The Soviet Context

| Composer | Response to Soviet Pressure |
|----------|----------------------------|
| Prokofiev | Attempted compliance; simplified language; maintained quality where possible |
| Shostakovich | Coded resistance; irony; "double meaning" in every work |
| Myaskovsky | Quiet conformity; 27 symphonies within acceptable bounds |
| Khachaturian | Nationalist color; folk elements satisfied ideology |
| Kabalevsky | Full compliance; produced ideologically correct music |

## Influence Chains

### Who Influenced Prokofiev

| Influence | What Prokofiev Took |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Tchaikovsky | Lyrical melody, ballet instinct, emotional directness |
| Rimsky-Korsakov | Orchestral brilliance, Russian color |
| Haydn | Classical formal clarity (Classical Symphony) |
| Liszt | Piano virtuosity, percussive technique |
| Ravel | Orchestral transparency, wit |
| Mussorgsky | Russian speech rhythms, grotesque character |

### Who Prokofiev Influenced

| Composer/Style | What They Took from Prokofiev |
|---------------|-------------------------------|
| Shostakovich (early) | Motoric drive, grotesque marches; then diverged sharply |
| Kabalevsky | Simplified Neo-Classical style; accessible lyricism |
| Soviet film composers | Patriotic orchestral writing; *Nevsky* as template |
| Khachaturian | Motoric energy, dance rhythm (though more folk-derived) |
| John Williams | Prokofiev's fairy-tale orchestration influenced *E.T.*, *Harry Potter* |
| Prokofiev's own late style | The simplification demanded by Soviet pressure anticipated some minimalist clarity |

## Quick Comparison Table

| Feature | Prokofiev | Stravinsky | Shostakovich | Bartok |
|---------|-----------|------------|-------------|--------|
| Tonality | Always tonal (with wrong notes) | Polytonal or serial | Tonal/modal with extreme chromaticism | Folk-tonal with dissonant overlay |
| Melody | Real tunes; singing | Cells, fragments | Long, aching lines; DSCH motif | Folk-derived; angular |
| Rhythm | Motoric, regular pulse | Changing meter, additive | Ostinato, obsessive repetition | Folk rhythm, asymmetric |
| Orchestration | Classical, lean, athletic | Stratified, wind-dominant | Chamber-in-orchestra, extreme registers | Coloristic, percussion-heavy |
| Form | Genuine Classical | Ironic Classical / block form | Distorted Classical / testimony | Arch form, night music |
| Expression | Sardonic + tender | Impersonal | Dark irony + anguish | Folk energy + night mystery |
