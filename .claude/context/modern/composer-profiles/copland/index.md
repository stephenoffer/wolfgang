# Aaron Copland — Index

**Era:** Modern | **Nationality:** American | **1900–1990**

**Key Works:** Appalachian Spring (ballet), Billy the Kid (ballet), Rodeo (ballet), Fanfare for the Common Man, Symphony No.3, Piano Variations, Piano Sonata, Clarinet Concerto, Lincoln Portrait, Twelve Poems of Emily Dickinson

**Style Summary:**
- The American sound: Copland invented what "American classical music" sounds like — open 5ths and 4ths in the bass, wide spacing, folk and hymn melody, vast open space.
- Pandiatonicism: all seven notes of a diatonic scale simultaneously, no note more important than any other. Bright, "all-white-key" tonal texture without functional harmony.
- American folk and hymn melody: Simple Gifts, Western cowboy tunes, American folk idiom used as direct thematic material or as the model for composed folk-like tunes.
- Sudden silence and wide dynamic range: complete silence (2–4 bars) as structural events representing open American landscape.
- Jazz-influenced rhythm: off-beat accents, 3+3+2 groupings in 4/4 (the hoedown rhythm), dance energy without jazz harmony.
