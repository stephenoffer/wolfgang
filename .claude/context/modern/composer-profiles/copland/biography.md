# Aaron Copland — Biography

The sound of America. Copland did not find a pre-existing American classical music tradition — he invented one. A Brooklyn-born son of Lithuanian Jewish immigrants, trained in Paris by Boulanger, he returned to forge a style that became synonymous with the American landscape: open fifths, wide spacing, folk melody, and silence as vast as the prairie. No other 20th-century composer so completely defined a national sound.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1900 | Brooklyn, NY | Born (Nov 14) | Parents: Harris and Sarah Copland, Lithuanian Jewish immigrants; music not a family profession |
| 1917 | Brooklyn | Begins harmony lessons with Rubin Goldmark | Conservative training: Romantic harmony, sonata form; Copland quickly outgrows it |
| 1921 | Fontainebleau, France | Studies with Nadia Boulanger | The decisive encounter: Boulanger teaches clarity, structure, neo-classical rigor; Copland is her first American student |
| 1921–24 | Paris | Composition studies | Absorbs Stravinsky, Les Six, jazz; the Paris modernist milieu |
| 1924 | New York | Returns to America | Organ Symphony premiered (Damrosch: "If a young man can write like that at 23...") |
| 1925–29 | New York | Jazz-influenced period | Music for the Theatre, Piano Concerto: jazz rhythms, blues harmony, urban energy |
| 1930–35 | New York | Abstract/austere period | Piano Variations, Short Symphony, Statements: compressed, dissonant, uncompromising |
| 1936 | Mexico | El Salon Mexico | The populist turn begins: folk material, accessible harmony, rhythmic energy |
| 1938 | New York | Billy the Kid (ballet) | First cowboy ballet; Western folk tunes; the American landscape sound crystallizes |
| 1942 | New York | Fanfare for the Common Man | 3 minutes that define American brass writing; commissioned for wartime |
| 1942 | New York | Rodeo (ballet) — Hoedown | The 3+3+2 rhythm; de Mille choreography; popular success |
| 1944 | Washington | Appalachian Spring (ballet) | Pulitzer Prize; Simple Gifts; the pastoral American masterpiece; Martha Graham |
| 1946 | New York | Symphony No. 3 | Incorporates Fanfare; the large-scale American symphony |
| 1948 | Tanglewood | Film scores: The Red Pony, The Heiress | Academy Award for The Heiress; film scoring as serious composition |
| 1950 | New York | Twelve Poems of Emily Dickinson | Art song masterpiece; intimate, chromatic, lyrical — a different Copland |
| 1957 | Tanglewood | Piano Fantasy | Serial techniques incorporated; the late turn toward abstraction |
| 1962 | Washington | Connotations for Orchestra | Twelve-tone; premiered at Lincoln Center opening; audience bewildered |
| 1970 | Peekskill, NY | Essentially stops composing | Alzheimer's disease gradually takes hold |
| 1990 | North Tarrytown, NY | Dies (Dec 2) | Buried at Cortlandt Manor; 90 years old |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Nadia Boulanger (1921–1924)

| What Boulanger Taught | How Copland Transformed It |
|-----------------------|---------------------------|
| Clarity of texture — every voice audible | The open-spaced American sound: bass, silence, melody |
| Neo-classical structure: form before feeling | Ballet structures: narrative clarity, scene-painting |
| Stravinsky as model: rhythm, block form | Block contrasts: pastoral silence vs. hoedown energy |
| Respect for craft over inspiration | Copland's lifelong commitment to technique as foundation |

### Jazz and American Vernacular (1925–1930)

| Element Absorbed | Compositional Use |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Blues 3rds (major/minor ambiguity) | Melodic color in slow movements |
| Syncopation (off-beat accents) | The hoedown 3+3+2 rhythm |
| Urban energy, dance rhythm | Fast movements: Rodeo, El Salon Mexico |
| Simplicity of popular melody | Folk-like themes composed (not quoted) from scratch |

### The American Landscape (1936–1946)

| What It Represents | Musical Result |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Open prairie, vast sky | Wide spacing: bass in C1, melody in C5, nothing between |
| Pioneer simplicity | Hymn-tune melody: Simple Gifts, folk-like themes |
| Democratic optimism | Major-mode brightness; no irony, no darkness |
| Silence of open space | Multi-bar rests as structural events |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Key Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Paris training | 1921–24 | Boulanger discipline, modernist absorption | Organ Symphony |
| Jazz period | 1925–30 | Urban, syncopated, blues-influenced | Music for the Theatre, Piano Concerto |
| Abstract/austere | 1930–36 | Compressed, dissonant, uncompromising | Piano Variations, Short Symphony, Statements |
| Populist Americana | 1936–49 | Open 5ths, folk melody, vast space, ballet | Billy the Kid, Rodeo, Appalachian Spring, Fanfare, Symphony No. 3 |
| Late serial | 1957–62 | Twelve-tone techniques, denser harmony | Piano Fantasy, Connotations, Inscape |
| Silence | 1970–90 | Alzheimer's; no new works | — |
