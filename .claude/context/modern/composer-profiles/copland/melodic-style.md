# Aaron Copland — Melodic Style

Copland's melodies are American hymns — simple, wide-spaced, modal, and direct. They do not arch and soar like Romantic melody; they step plainly, pause on open intervals, and breathe the silence around them. The folk sources (Shaker hymns, cowboy songs, Appalachian tunes) are sometimes quoted directly, sometimes composed fresh in their image. Either way, the melody carries a sense of landscape, openness, and unaffected sincerity.

## Core Melodic Character by Period

| Feature | Jazz (1925–30) | Abstract (1930–36) | Populist (1936–49) | Late Serial (1957–62) |
|---------|---------------|--------------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Source | Jazz/blues idiom | Composed; no folk source | Folk song, hymn tune | Tone row |
| Range | Moderate (octave+) | Wide; disjunct | Narrow to moderate (5th–octave) | Wide; row-determined |
| Contour | Bluesy bends, syncopated | Angular, compressed | Stepwise with open-interval leaps | Angular, serial |
| Phrase length | Regular (4+4) | Irregular, compressed | Hymn-regular (4+4, 8+8) | Row-length |
| Expression | Urban, rhythmic | Austere, intellectual | Simply, gently, earnestly | Abstract |

## The American Melody (Populist Period)

| Technique | Description | Where It Appears |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Stepwise motion with 4th/5th leaps | Mostly steps (C-D-E), punctuated by a leap of a 4th or 5th | Appalachian Spring themes |
| Pentatonic basis | Melody from C-D-E-G-A (no F, no B) | Billy the Kid folk scenes |
| Hymn rhythm | Quarter notes and half notes; no complex subdivision | Simple Gifts variations |
| Open-interval opening | Melody begins with a 4th or 5th leap upward | Fanfare for the Common Man (C-G) |
| Modal inflection | Mixolydian (b7) or Dorian (b3, b7) coloring | Western folk character |
| No ornament | Melody is plain; no trills, turns, or grace notes | All populist works |

```abc
X:1
T:American Melody — Hymn-Like Simplicity (Appalachian Spring character)
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:G
%% Simple melody: stepwise + one 4th leap; quarter and half notes only
!p!G A B d|e2 d2|B A G B|A2 G2|
%% No ornament, no chromatic alteration, no syncopation — pure and plain
```

```abc
X:2
T:Pentatonic Folk Melody (Billy the Kid character)
M:2/4
L:1/8
K:D
%% Pentatonic: D-E-F#-A-B only; cowboy-song contour
!mf!D2 F2|A2 B2|A2 F2|D4|E2 F2|A2 B2|A F E2|D4|
%% No C#, no G — pure pentatonic; range of a 6th; folk simplicity
```

## Simple Gifts and the Shaker Melody

The most famous Copland melody is a direct quotation: the Shaker hymn "Simple Gifts" (1848), used as the theme for variations in Appalachian Spring. Its qualities define Copland's melodic ideal.

| Quality | In Simple Gifts | As Copland's Ideal |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| Range | One octave (A4–A5) | Melodies rarely exceed an octave |
| Motion | Stepwise with small leaps | Steps + occasional 4th/5th |
| Rhythm | Even quarter notes, dotted halves | Hymn rhythm: simple, steady |
| Mode | Major (with Mixolydian hint) | Diatonic, modal |
| Character | Earnest, sincere, dance-like | All populist melodies share this |

## Melodic Intervals — Signature Shapes

| Interval | Copland's Use | Character |
|----------|--------------|-----------|
| Unison | Melody begins on repeated note | Plainchant simplicity |
| Major 2nd | Primary melodic motion | Stepwise hymn movement |
| Major 3rd | Gentle lift | Warmth without drama |
| Perfect 4th | Opening leap upward | The Fanfare interval — heroic, open |
| Perfect 5th | Opening leap or cadential | The American interval — space, sky |
| Octave | Rare; reserved for Fanfare climax | Monumental, not lyrical |
| Minor 7th | Jazz period only | Blues inflection |

```abc
X:3
T:Signature Opening Intervals — 4ths and 5ths
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
%% Fanfare: opening 5th leap
!ff!C G c G|
%% Pastoral: opening 4th leap
!p!C F E D|C4|
%% Both intervals define the Copland melodic landscape
```

## Jazz-Period Melody (1925–1930)

| Technique | Description | Where It Appears |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Blue note bends | Melody touches b3 and b7 in major context | Music for the Theatre |
| Syncopated rhythm | Off-beat accents, tied notes across bar lines | Piano Concerto |
| Trumpet-like calls | Short, brassy, fanfare-derived motifs | Orchestral jazz works |
| Walking melodic bass | Stepwise quarter-note melody in low register | Music for the Theatre |

```abc
X:4
T:Jazz-Period Melody — Syncopated and Bluesy
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
%% Blue notes (Eb, Bb) against C major context; syncopated rhythm
!mf!C2 _E2 G2 c2|_B2 G2 _E2 C2|C _E G _B c2 z2|
%% The melody swings; blue 3rds and 7ths color the line
```

## Abstract-Period Melody (1930–1936)

| Technique | Description | Where It Appears |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Wide leaps | Major 7ths, minor 9ths — angular | Piano Variations |
| Compressed cells | Short 3–4 note motifs, repeated and transposed | Short Symphony |
| No folk reference | Deliberately non-tonal, non-folk | Statements |
| Rhythmic irregularity | Asymmetric groupings, no dance rhythm | Piano Variations |

## Phrase Structure

| Pattern | Period | Description |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| 4+4 (hymn) | Populist | Regular, symmetrical, congregational |
| 8+8 (folk song) | Populist | Longer folk-song phrases, balanced |
| 2+2+3 (jazz) | Jazz | Syncopated, asymmetric |
| Irregular, compressed | Abstract | 3+5, 2+3+2 — no regularity |
| Row-determined | Serial | Phrase = row statement |

## Melodic Rhythm

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Hymn rhythm default | Quarter notes and half notes in pastoral melody |
| Dotted rhythm for folk dance | Dotted quarter + eighth in hoedown contexts |
| Syncopation for energy | Off-beat eighth notes in fast movements |
| Long notes for landscape | Whole notes and tied notes for vast, still passages |
| No rubato implied | Melody is metronomic; expression from simplicity, not tempo change |
