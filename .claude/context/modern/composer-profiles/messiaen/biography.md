# Olivier Messiaen — Biography

The mystic and the ornithologist. Messiaen is the only major composer whose music is simultaneously an act of Catholic worship, a scientific transcription of birdsong, and an exploration of Indian and Greek rhythmic systems. Born in Avignon, trained at the Paris Conservatoire, imprisoned in a German POW camp where he composed the Quartet for the End of Time, he spent his life as organist of La Trinite in Paris and professor at the Conservatoire where he taught Boulez, Stockhausen, and Xenakis. His music sounds like nothing else.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1908 | Avignon, France | Born (Dec 10) | Mother: poet Cecile Sauvage; father: English teacher/Shakespeare translator |
| 1919 | Paris | Enters Paris Conservatoire (age 11) | Studies with Dukas (composition) and Dupre (organ); prodigious talent |
| 1929 | Paris | Graduates with 5 first prizes | Organ, fugue, counterpoint, accompaniment, composition |
| 1930 | Paris | Appointed organist of La Trinite | Position held for 61 years (until death); the organ as his primary instrument |
| 1931 | Paris | Les offrandes oubliees (first orchestral work) | Already characteristic: mystic, slow, luminous |
| 1936 | Paris | Founds "La Jeune France" group | With Jolivet, Daniel-Lesur, Baudrier; against neo-classicism; for human expression |
| 1940 | Gorlitz, Germany | Captured as POW (Stalag VIII-A) | Composes Quatuor pour la fin du temps for the instruments available in camp |
| 1941 | Gorlitz | Quartet for the End of Time premiered | Performed for 5,000 prisoners in freezing cold; 4 players; the Book of Revelation |
| 1941 | Paris | Released; appointed professor at Conservatoire | Begins teaching harmony that will shape the postwar avant-garde |
| 1944 | Paris | Vingt regards sur l'Enfant-Jesus | 2-hour piano cycle; 20 movements; the theological piano masterpiece |
| 1944 | Paris | Technique de mon langage musical published | Self-analysis: modes, rhythms, birdsong, color — the theoretical manifesto |
| 1948 | Paris | Turangalila-Symphonie completed | 10 movements; massive orchestra + Ondes Martenot + piano; love and divine joy |
| 1949–52 | Paris | Experiments with total serialism | Mode de valeurs et d'intensites; influenced Boulez and Stockhausen directly |
| 1953–58 | France (field) | Intensive birdsong transcription | Catalogue d'oiseaux (13 piano pieces depicting specific birds in landscapes) |
| 1956–58 | Paris | Catalogue d'oiseaux | Birds as primary melodic source; landscape painting through sound |
| 1960 | Paris | Chronochromie | Orchestral; 18 real birdsongs simultaneously in the "Epode" |
| 1962–64 | Paris | Couleurs de la Cite celeste | Stained-glass music; Revelation; colors as structure |
| 1971–74 | Utah, France | Des canyons aux etoiles | Inspired by Utah landscapes; birds + geology + theology |
| 1975–83 | Paris | Saint Francois d'Assise (opera) | 8 years; 4+ hours; his only opera; St. Francis, birds, divine grace |
| 1984 | Paris | Livre du Saint Sacrement (organ) | 18 movements; the final organ cycle; theological summation |
| 1992 | Clichy, France | Dies (April 27) | 83 years old; buried at La Trinite |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Catholic Faith

| Aspect | Musical Manifestation |
|--------|---------------------|
| The Incarnation (Christ as infant) | Vingt regards: 20 contemplations of the divine child |
| The Resurrection | Quartet for the End of Time: Mvt 8, "Louange a l'Immortalite de Jesus" |
| The Book of Revelation | Quartet: apocalyptic imagery; angel who announces the end of time |
| Saints and mystical experience | Saint Francois d'Assise: divine grace received through nature |
| The Mass and liturgy | Organ works: meditations on the Blessed Sacrament |
| Transcendence as aesthetic goal | Slow, sustained, luminous passages: the listener is meant to experience the divine |

### Birdsong

| Phase | Activity | Musical Result |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| Early (1930s–40s) | Stylized bird-like figures | Decorative ornaments in orchestral works |
| Intensive (1953–58) | Scientific field transcription, notebook in hand | Catalogue d'oiseaux: each piece = one bird in its habitat |
| Mature (1960s–80s) | Birdsong as primary melodic material | Chronochromie, Des canyons: 10–20 birds simultaneously |
| Identification | Species-specific calls, rhythms, pitches | Blackbird, song thrush, garden warbler, golden oriole |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Key Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Early mystic | 1929–39 | Catholic meditation; organ; modes | L'Ascension, La Nativite, Les corps glorieux |
| Wartime/aftermath | 1940–48 | Revelation theology; piano; vast love-symphony | Quartet for the End of Time, Vingt regards, Turangalila |
| Serial experiment | 1949–52 | Total serialism; parameter organization | Mode de valeurs, Livre d'orgue |
| Birdsong | 1953–64 | Scientific transcription; bird as melodic source | Reveil des oiseaux, Catalogue d'oiseaux, Chronochromie |
| Late synthesis | 1965–92 | All elements combined: birds + theology + color + landscape | Des canyons aux etoiles, Saint Francois d'Assise |
