# Arnold Schoenberg — Index

**Era:** Modern | **Nationality:** Austrian (later American) | **1874–1951**

**Key Works:**
- Late Romantic: Verklärte Nacht, Gurrelieder, Pelleas und Melisande
- Atonal: Three Piano Pieces Op.11, Five Orchestral Pieces Op.16, Pierrot Lunaire, Erwartung
- Twelve-Tone: Piano Suite Op.25, Violin Concerto Op.36, Piano Concerto Op.42, String Quartet No.4

**Style Summary (THREE COMPLETELY DIFFERENT PERIODS — specify):**
- **Late Romantic (1899–1908):** Post-Wagnerian chromaticism at the breaking point; Brahmsian thematic development; still tonal, barely.
- **Atonal/Expressionist (1908–1923):** Free atonality, no tonal center, no resolution. Sprechstimme. Short compressed forms. Extreme psychological states.
- **Twelve-Tone (1923–1951):** Dodecaphonic rows governing all material. Row in four forms (P/R/I/RI) × 12 transpositions. Neo-Classical formal containers re-inhabited with serial method.

**Why Schoenberg matters:** He proved that Western tonality was not inevitable — that music could be organized on other principles. Whether you agree with his solution, his question changed everything.
