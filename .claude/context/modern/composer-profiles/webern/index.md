# Anton Webern — Index

**Era:** Modern | **Nationality:** Austrian | **1883–1945**

**Key Works:** Symphony Op.21, Five Pieces for Orchestra Op.10, Six Bagatelles for String Quartet Op.9, String Quartet Op.28, Piano Variations Op.27, Concerto Op.24, Cantatas Op.29/31

**Style Summary:**
- Maximum compression: Webern's entire output fits in a few hours. His pieces are extraordinarily short (Symphony Op.21: under 10 minutes). Every note carries enormous weight.
- Pointillism (Klangfarbenmelodie): melody fragmented across instruments — one note in flute, one in harp, one in violin. The melodic line is assembled by the listener from isolated timbral events.
- Silence as primary structural material: rests between notes are as long as the notes themselves. The silence resonates.
- Perfect symmetry: works are often palindromes — second half is the retrograde of the first. Structural perfection as aesthetic value.
- Strict serial application: rows are themselves often symmetrical; the serial method is applied more rigorously than in Schoenberg or Berg.
