# Bartók Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `bartok-lh-vocabulary.md`, which carries the corpus caveat: 16
armed bars, so this is craft knowledge rather than measurement.

The organising fact: **peasant music plus rigorous structure.** He collected
thousands of folk melodies in the field and built modernist architecture out of
their intervals and rhythms. Generated "modern" writing tends to be atonal and
formless; Bartók is neither — it is centred, and it is tightly organised.

## Melodic and modal devices

1. **Folk-derived modality** — Lydian, Dorian, Phrygian, and the "acoustic
   scale" (Lydian with a flat seventh). Not major or minor.

2. **The Phrygian second** — a flat second above the centre, the sharpest folk
   inflection he uses.

3. **Narrow-range melodies** — a fourth or a fifth for a whole tune, circling
   its centre, the shape of a collected peasant song.

4. **Parlando-rubato melody** — free, speech-derived, unmetred, against a still
   accompaniment.

5. **Tempo giusto** — the opposite: strict, dance-driven, motoric.

## Structural devices

6. **Arch form** — ABCBA, the movement mirroring itself around a centre. His
   signature large-scale plan.

7. **Mirror inversion and retrograde** of a subject, worked strictly.

8. **Fugal exposition on a chromatic subject** — entries at the fifth,
   accumulating.

9. **Golden-section placement of the climax** — the high point placed roughly
   61.8% of the way through. Whether he calculated it or not, the effect is that
   the climax is late.

10. **Night music** — a slow movement of isolated sounds, birdcalls and
    dissonant specks against silence.

## Harmonic devices

11. **Polymodal chromaticism** — two modes on the same centre sounding at once,
    producing chromatic aggregate from purely modal parts.

12. **The axis system** — tonic, subdominant and dominant functions each shared
    by four keys a minor third apart.

13. **Quartal harmony** — chords built in fourths rather than thirds.

14. **The tonal centre asserted by insistence** — repetition and register, not
    by a cadence.

15. **Unresolved seconds and sevenths as consonance.**

## Rhythmic devices

16. **Bulgarian rhythm** — 5/8, 7/8, 9/8 grouped in unequal beats (2+2+3), fast
    and danced.

17. **Changing metre bar to bar**, following the phrase.

18. **Ostinato** driving a whole section unchanged.

19. **Accents displaced against the barline**, marked explicitly.

## Instrumental devices

20. **The percussive attack** — the piano struck, marked `pesante` or with
    accents, dry.

21. **Snap pizzicato**, glissando, col legno and sul ponticello in the string
    writing — effects treated as normal vocabulary.

## What to avoid

- **Atonality.** There is always a centre.
- **Romantic phrasing and rubato** over the motoric passages.
- **Resolving dissonance conventionally.**
- **Regular four-bar phrases in a fast movement.**
- **Thirds-based functional harmony.**
