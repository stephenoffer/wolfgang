# Igor Stravinsky — Stylistic Evolution

Stravinsky's creative arc is not an evolution — it is a series of deliberate reinventions. Most composers develop; Stravinsky replaced. The Russian primitivist became the Neo-Classical ironist, who became the serial ritualist. Each period is a complete aesthetic, internally consistent, with its own harmony, melody, orchestration, and formal logic. Understanding which Stravinsky is being requested is not a nuance — it is the fundamental decision.

## Period Overview

| Period | Years | Age | Character | Scale |
|--------|-------|-----|-----------|-------|
| Russian primitivist | 1908–1920 | 26–38 | Block form, polytonality, folk cells, pagan energy, ballet | Large: full orchestra, ballet, staged works |
| Neo-Classical | 1920–1951 | 38–69 | Baroque/Classical forms, tonal but displaced, dry, ironic | Variable: chamber to orchestral; wind-dominant |
| Transitional | 1951–1954 | 69–72 | Gradual adoption of serial elements within tonal framework | Small: cantata, septet |
| Serial | 1954–1966 | 72–84 | Twelve-tone rows, sparse texture, ritualistic austerity | Variable: ballet to oratorio to miniature |

## Detailed Period Comparison

### What Changes

| Dimension | Russian (1908–20) | Neo-Classical (1920–51) | Serial (1954–66) |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| **Melody** | Folk cells, narrow range, pentatonic, circular | Diatonic scales, Baroque gesture, displaced accent | Row statements, angular, pointillistic |
| **Harmony** | Polytonality, octatonic, unresolved clusters | Added-note triads, wrong-note diatonic, pandiatonic | Row-determined; no tonal center |
| **Rhythm** | Changing meter, additive, asymmetric, primal | Regular meter with displaced accent, motoric | Free; row determines rhythm loosely |
| **Orchestration** | Massive; stratified layers; percussion dominant | Chamber; winds primary; dry, no vibrato | Sparse; pointillistic; single timbres |
| **Form** | Block juxtaposition, ritual tableaux | Fugue, passacaglia, ritornello, sonata | Row rotation, canonic, liturgical |
| **Texture** | Dense layered ostinati | Transparent counterpoint | Sparse; silence between events |
| **Expression** | Impersonal, ritualistic, primal | Ironic, witty, detached | Austere, liturgical, transcendent |
| **Dynamic range** | ppp to fff; extreme contrasts | mp to f; moderate, controlled | ppp to mf; quiet dominates |

### Russian Period (1908–1920): Revolution

The Diaghilev ballets. Each commission pushed Stravinsky further from Rimsky's Romantic orchestral world toward a new language of rhythm, block form, and polytonality.

| Work | What It Shows |
|------|---------------|
| Firebird (1910) | Rimsky's orchestral brilliance + emerging rhythmic energy; still Romantic |
| Petrushka (1911) | Bitonality (Petrushka chord), block form, piano as orchestral percussion |
| The Rite of Spring (1913) | Everything new: changing meter, block form, polytonal harmony, primal rhythm |
| The Soldier's Tale (1918) | Swiss exile economy: 7 players; mixed genres (tango, waltz, ragtime) |
| Les Noces (1923) | Four pianos + percussion + voices; the last Russian work; folk abstraction |

### Neo-Classical Period (1920–1951): The Long Reinvention

Thirty years of working within Classical forms. Not pastiche — Stravinsky fills old containers with modern rhythmic language and ironic harmonic displacement.

| Work | What It Shows |
|------|---------------|
| Pulcinella (1920) | Pergolesi reworked; the first Neo-Classical act; beauty + distortion |
| Octet for winds (1923) | Pure wind chamber music; fugue, variation; the aesthetic manifesto |
| Oedipus Rex (1927) | Opera-oratorio; Latin text; monumental stasis; Verdi through Stravinsky |
| Symphony of Psalms (1930) | No violins; choral + wind; the sacred masterpiece of Neo-Classicism |
| Symphony in C (1940) | Most "Classical" of the symphonies; Haydn-scale; transparent |
| The Rake's Progress (1951) | Full-length Mozart-model opera; the summit and farewell of Neo-Classicism |

```abc
X:2
T:Neo-Classical — Ironic Baroque Gesture (Pulcinella character)
M:3/8
L:1/16
K:G
%% 18th-century melody, metrically correct — but the harmony will be "wrong"
!mf!B2AG BAGF|G2AB c2B2|A2GF G4|
%% Classical in contour; the displacement comes in the harmonic support
```

### Serial Period (1954–1966): The Final Transformation

After Schoenberg's death (1951), Stravinsky — influenced by Robert Craft's enthusiasm for Webern — adopted serial technique. But Stravinsky's serialism is ritualistic and austere, not expressionistic.

| Work | What It Shows |
|------|---------------|
| Canticum Sacrum (1955) | Sacred text; partial serialism; the transition point |
| Agon (1957) | Ballet: some movements tonal, some serial; the hybrid masterpiece |
| Threni (1958) | First fully serial large work; austere, monumental |
| Movements for Piano and Orchestra (1959) | Pure serial; Webern-influenced pointillism; most abstract work |
| Requiem Canticles (1966) | Final major work; serial, spare, luminous; bells toll at the end |

```abc
X:3
T:Serial Period — Sparse Ritualistic Texture (Threni character)
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
%% Isolated notes, wide intervals, silence as structure
!pp!C z z _E|z ^F z z|A z _B z|z z D z|
%% Each note surrounded by silence — the serial Stravinsky
```

## Transition Markers

| Transition | What Triggers It | What Changes |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Russian - Neo-Classical | Pulcinella commission (1919); Swiss exile economy; rejection of Russian nationalist identity | Everything: form, harmony, texture, orchestration, scale |
| Neo-Classical - Serial | Schoenberg's death (1951); Craft introduces Webern; Stravinsky hears row technique as compatible with his block thinking | Pitch organization becomes serial; texture thins; expression becomes more austere |

## The Constant Across All Periods

| Element | Present in All Three Periods |
|---------|------------------------------|
| Octatonic scale | Harmonic resource from Firebird through Requiem Canticles |
| Rhythmic primacy | Rhythm is composed before pitch in all periods |
| Anti-development | Material does not grow or transform organically in any period |
| Clarity of texture | Every line audible; no Romantic blending |
| Impersonality | Music does not express the composer's emotions in any period |
| Block thinking | Even serial works are organized in discrete sections |

## Guide for Composition: "Which Stravinsky?"

| If the request says... | Use period... | Key traits to emphasize |
|----------------------|--------------|------------------------|
| "Rite of Spring," "primitive," "ballet" | Russian | Block form, changing meter, polytonality, ostinato, ff |
| "Neo-Classical," "Baroque," "Octet," "Psalms" | Neo-Classical | Baroque container, wrong-note harmony, dry wind texture, ironic |
| "Serial," "Agon," "Threni," "late Stravinsky" | Serial | Row technique, sparse texture, silence, ritual austerity |
| "Stravinsky" (no period specified) | Russian (default) | The "standard" Stravinsky: block form, rhythmic energy, polytonality |
