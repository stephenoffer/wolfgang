# Igor Stravinsky — Orchestration

Stravinsky's orchestration is the opposite of Romantic blending. Every instrument is heard as itself — exposed, specific, irreplaceable. In the Russian period, the orchestra is massive but stratified into independent layers. In the Neo-Classical period, the orchestra shrinks to chamber proportions with wind primacy. In the serial period, textures become pointillistic — single notes placed in silence. What connects all periods: clarity, anti-blend, and the conviction that each timbre is a structural identity.

## Core Orchestral Character by Period

| Feature | Russian (1910–20) | Neo-Classical (1920–51) | Serial (1953–66) |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| Forces | Massive (quadruple winds, large percussion) | Chamber-sized (winds primary, strings reduced) | Variable; often small and sparse |
| Texture | Stratified layers, each independent | Dry, transparent, no vibrato | Pointillistic; individual notes, silence between |
| String role | Rhythmic, percussive (col legno, ponticello) | Reduced; accompanimental; dry tone | Single notes, wide leaps, harmonics |
| Wind role | Primary melodic carriers; brilliant solos | Dominant; the Stravinsky instrument family | Isolated tones; coloristic punctuation |
| Brass role | Fanfares, rhythmic punctuation, power | Horn calls, chorale-like passages | Sparse, ritualistic |
| Percussion | Huge battery; rhythmic driving force | Minimal; timpani, occasional snare | Bells, chimes; ritualistic color |
| Piano | Percussive orchestral instrument (Petrushka) | Concertante or absent | Percussive, dry, pointillistic |

## The Rite of Spring Orchestra (Russian Period)

| Section | Forces | Role |
|---------|--------|------|
| Woodwinds | Picc, 3 Fl, Alto Fl, 4 Ob, Cor Anglais, Eb Cl, 3 Cl, Bass Cl, 4 Bsn, Contrabsn | Melodic cells; layered ostinati; the primary voices |
| Brass | 8 Hn, Tpt in D, 4 Tpt in C, 3 Tbn, 2 Tuba | Rhythmic hammering; fanfares; power chords |
| Percussion | 5 Timp, Bass drum, Tam-tam, Triangle, Guiro, Cymbals | Rhythmic anchor; primal pulse |
| Strings | Full complement, often divided | Rhythmic ostinato (Augurs chord), tremolo, col legno |

### The "Augurs of Spring" Chord

The most famous orchestral attack in 20th-century music: strings play a polytonal chord (Eb7 + Fb major) with irregular accents reinforced by 8 horns.

```abc
X:1
T:Augurs of Spring — Repeated Polytonal Chord (reduction)
M:2/4
L:1/8
K:C
%% Strings hammer a dissonant chord; accents irregular
!sfz![_E_G_Bc_e]2 [_E_G_Bc_e]2|[_E_G_Bc_e] !sfz![_E_G_Bc_e] [_E_G_Bc_e]2|
%% The chord never changes — only the accents shift
```

## Neo-Classical Orchestration

| Principle | Description | Where It Appears |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Wind primacy | Winds carry melody; strings accompany or are absent | Octet (winds only), Symphony of Psalms (no violins) |
| No vibrato | Dry, straight tone; anti-Romantic | All Neo-Classical orchestral works |
| Staccato default | Detached articulation; legato is the exception | Octet, Dumbarton Oaks |
| Anti-doubling | Each instrument has its own line; no blended doublings | The opposite of Brahms's doubled 3rds |
| Piano as orchestra member | The piano plays as part of the ensemble, not as soloist | Petrushka, Symphony in Three Movements |
| Transparency | Every line audible; no thick textures | Chamber scoring even in large orchestra |

```abc
X:2
T:Neo-Classical Wind Texture — Octet Character
M:2/4
L:1/16
K:C
V:1 name="Flute"
V:2 name="Clarinet"
V:3 name="Bassoon" clef=bass
%% Three independent lines — each audible, no blending
[V:1] !mf!c2BA G2FE|D2EF G4|
[V:2] E2DC B,2A,G,|F,2G,A, B,4|
[V:3] C,2D,E, F,2G,A,|B,,2A,,G,, C,4|
%% Each voice moves independently — counterpoint, not harmony
```

## Instrument Roles — Detailed

### Woodwinds (Stravinsky's Primary Voice)

| Instrument | Stravinsky's Use | Signature Moments |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Bassoon (high register) | Melody in extreme high range; exposed, vulnerable | Rite of Spring opening solo (C#5–D5) |
| Clarinet | Agile runs, folk-character solos | Petrushka, The Soldier's Tale |
| Oboe | Piercing, nasal; melodic in Neo-Classical works | Pulcinella, Symphony of Psalms |
| Flute | High doublings, trills; rarely warm | Firebird, Agon |
| Cor Anglais | Dark, melancholy solo color | Rite of Spring, Symphony in Three Movements |

### Strings

| Technique | Description | Period |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Rhythmic ostinato | Repeated chords with irregular accents | Russian (Augurs chord) |
| Col legno | Struck with wood of bow; dry, percussive | Russian, Serial |
| Ponticello | Glassy, metallic tone near bridge | Russian, Serial |
| Pizzicato accents | Sharp, percussive plucked attacks | All periods |
| Divisi clusters | Divided strings, each on adjacent pitch | Russian (Rite climaxes) |
| No vibrato | Straight tone; historically informed | Neo-Classical |

### Brass

| Instrument | Stravinsky's Use | Character |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Horn | Fanfare calls, held pedals, rhythmic punctuation | Not warm (unlike Brahms); bright, declarative |
| Trumpet | Fanfare, melodic in high register, rhythmic attacks | Brilliant, exposed, never muted for sentiment |
| Trombone | Chorale passages, rhythmic bass, glissandi (Rite) | Power and weight, not warmth |
| Tuba | Bass foundation, rhythmic anchor | Rare melodic use |

### Percussion and Piano

| Role | Description | Period |
|------|-------------|--------|
| Timpani as melodic instrument | Tuned to specific pitches, thematic | All periods |
| Piano as percussion | Dry, hammered, no pedal; rhythmic chords | Petrushka, Symphony in Three Movements |
| Bells/chimes | Ritualistic, liturgical color | Serial period (Requiem Canticles) |
| Xylophone | Dry, bright rhythmic accent | Les Noces, Agon |

## Texture Types

| Texture | Description | Period |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| Stratified layers | 3–4 independent ostinato layers, each in its own key/meter | Russian |
| Antiphonal blocks | Groups alternate without overlap | Russian, Neo-Classical |
| Chamber transparency | 2–3 voices, each fully exposed | Neo-Classical |
| Tutti rhythmic unison | Entire orchestra on same rhythm, different pitches | Russian (climaxes) |
| Pointillistic | Single notes in isolation, surrounded by silence | Serial |
| Chorale homophony | Block chords, hymn-like, wind-dominated | Neo-Classical (Symphony of Psalms) |

```abc
X:3
T:Stratified Layers — Two Independent Ostinati
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
V:1 name="Oboe (layer 1 — C major)"
V:2 name="Clarinet (layer 2 — F# major)" 
%% Two layers, two keys, two rhythms — simultaneously
[V:1] C2E2 G2C2|C2E2 G2C2|
[V:2] ^F2^A2 ^c2^F2|^F^A^c^F ^F2^A2|
%% Neither layer acknowledges the other — the collision is the point
```

## Dynamic Approach

| Dynamic Level | Stravinsky Character | Orchestral Realization |
|--------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| ppp | Nocturnal, mysterious (rare) | Solo instrument, muted strings |
| pp | Transparent, clear | Chamber reduction; 1–2 instruments |
| p | The default working dynamic | Winds exposed; strings pizzicato or tacet |
| mf | Active, clear | Full winds, light strings |
| f | Energetic, rhythmic | All instruments; rhythmic unison |
| ff | Massive, primal | Full orchestra; percussion dominant |
| sfz / accent | Structural punctuation | Placed on "wrong" beats; the Stravinsky accent |
