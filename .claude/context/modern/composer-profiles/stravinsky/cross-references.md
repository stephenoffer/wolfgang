# Igor Stravinsky — Cross-References & Influences

## Position in the Tradition

Stravinsky is the 20th century's most protean composer. Where Schoenberg pursued a single radical path (atonality to dodecaphony), Stravinsky reinvented himself three times — and each reinvention reshaped the musical landscape. The Russian period liberated rhythm; the Neo-Classical period reclaimed form; the serial period proved that even an octogenarian could change completely. His influence is universal: every film score that uses ostinato and block cutting, every minimalist who repeats a pattern, every neo-tonalist who quotes the past with irony — all owe something to Stravinsky.

## Who Influenced Stravinsky

| Source | What Was Absorbed | Where You Hear It |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Rimsky-Korsakov | Orchestral brilliance, octatonic scale, Russian folk modality | Firebird (directly), octatonic harmony in all periods |
| Russian folk music | Narrow-range cells, pentatonic melody, asymmetric phrase | Petrushka, Rite of Spring, Les Noces |
| Debussy | Orchestral color as structure, whole-tone harmony, non-functional chord motion | Firebird harmonic shifts, Symphony of Psalms static harmony |
| Tchaikovsky | Ballet form, orchestral clarity, formal elegance | Firebird, Pulcinella, The Fairy's Kiss (explicit homage) |
| Pergolesi / Baroque | Ritornello form, counterpoint, motivic sequence | Pulcinella, Octet, Dumbarton Oaks |
| Jazz / American popular music | Ragtime rhythm, blues inflection, syncopation | Ragtime (1918), Ebony Concerto, The Soldier's Tale |
| Webern | Sparse texture, pointillism, short forms, row technique | Movements, Requiem Canticles, all serial works |

|-----------|---------------|-------------|

## Stravinsky vs. Schoenberg: The 20th Century's Central Argument

The defining musical opposition of the 20th century — not personal enemies, but philosophical opposites. Both responded to the crisis of tonality; their answers were irreconcilable.

| Dimension | Stravinsky | Schoenberg |
|-----------|------------|------------|
| **Diagnosis** | Tonality exhausted by Romanticism; find new uses for old materials | Tonality exhausted; discover new organizing principles |
| **Solution** | Return to pre-Romantic forms with modern content (Neo-Classical), then adopt serialism on his own terms | Emancipation of dissonance; twelve-tone method |
| **Rhythm** | Primary element; composed first; changing meter, additive | Secondary; serves pitch structure; more regular |
| **Form** | Block juxtaposition; Baroque containers; ritual | Through-composed; motivic development; dramatic |
| **Expression** | Anti-expressive; impersonal; music as object | Intensely expressive; music as psychological truth |
| **Texture** | Clear, stratified, transparent | Dense, contrapuntal, saturated |
| **Orchestration** | Each timbre separate; anti-blend; dry | Klangfarbenmelodie; timbral melody; blended |
| **Legacy** | Neo-Classicism, minimalism, postmodernism | Serialism, total serialism, academic modernism |
| **Irony** | Stravinsky adopted serialism after Schoenberg's death | Schoenberg never adopted Neo-Classicism |

## Rimsky-Korsakov's Inheritance

| What Stravinsky Inherited | How He Transformed It |
|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Orchestral brilliance (Rimsky = the supreme colorist) | Color becomes identity — each timbre IS a musical idea, not decoration |
| Octatonic scale (Rimsky used it for fairy-tale chromaticism) | Octatonic becomes structural — generates harmony across all three periods |
| Clear orchestral layers (Rimsky's transparent scoring) | Layers become INDEPENDENT — each with its own key, meter, rhythm |
| Folk melody as source (Rimsky collected and arranged) | Folk cells are ABSTRACTED — broken into intervals, rhythmicized, decontextualized |
| Programmatic narrative (Rimsky's operas and orchestral poems) | Narrative REJECTED — music as ritual or object, never story |

## Debussy's Influence

| What Stravinsky Took from Debussy | How It Appears |
|----------------------------------|---------------|
| Non-functional chord succession | Chords chosen for color, not resolution; Firebird's shifting harmonies |
| Orchestral color as primary structural element | Each timbre = a structural identity; color is not decoration |
| Whole-tone scale as resource | Transitional passages in Firebird, atmospheric moments in Petrushka |
| Static harmony (harmony that doesn't "go" anywhere) | Ostinato harmony: a chord or progression that repeats without functional motion |
| Rejection of German developmental logic | Both Debussy and Stravinsky reject Beethovenian development — but for different reasons |

## Stravinsky vs. Bartok

| Dimension | Stravinsky | Bartok |
|-----------|------------|--------|
| Folk material | Russian folk; treated as raw material for block composition | Hungarian/Romanian folk; treated as scientific data AND emotional source |
| Ethnomusicology | Not systematic; folk is stylistic inspiration | Systematic collector (10,000+ recordings); folk is DNA |
| Asymmetric rhythm | Changing meter bar-by-bar; meter follows accent | Bulgarian rhythms (5/8, 7/8); meter as fixed pattern |
| Harmony | Polytonality, pandiatonicism | Axis system, chromatic compression, night music |
| Form | Block juxtaposition, no development | Arch form (ABCBA), palindromes, developing variation |
| Piano writing | Percussive; piano as orchestral member | Percussive; piano as PRIMARY solo instrument |
| Expression | Impersonal, anti-Romantic | Personal, deeply felt (despite modern language) |

## Universal Impact

| Domain | Stravinsky's Contribution |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Ballet | Elevated ballet music to the highest art; every post-Rite ballet composer responds to Stravinsky |
| Rhythm | Liberated rhythm from regular meter; changed how the 20th century thinks about time |
| Orchestration | Proved that clarity and independence of line > Romantic blending |
| Neo-Classicism | Single-handedly created the movement; showed that the past can be used without pastiche |
| Postmodernism | The ironic quotation, the stylistic mask, the refusal of single identity — all start here |
| Film music | Block scoring, ostinato, sudden textural change — the grammar of film scoring |

## Comparative Quick Reference

| Trait | Stravinsky | Closest Peer | Key Difference |
|-------|------------|-------------|----------------|
| Block form | Stravinsky | Debussy (juxtaposition) | Stravinsky: hard cut. Debussy: dissolve |
| Ostinato | Stravinsky | Ravel (Bolero) | Stravinsky: accumulation. Ravel: orchestral variation |
| Changing meter | Stravinsky | Bartok | Stravinsky: every bar changes. Bartok: fixed asymmetric pattern |
| Neo-Classical irony | Stravinsky | Prokofiev | Stravinsky: formal irony. Prokofiev: melodic irony ("wrong notes") |
| Serial technique | Stravinsky | Webern | Stravinsky: ritualistic, austere. Webern: crystalline, concentrated |
| Rhythmic primacy | Stravinsky | Bartok | Stravinsky: rhythm as block identity. Bartok: rhythm as folk pattern |
