# Igor Stravinsky — Biography

Three revolutions in one lifetime. Stravinsky did not evolve — he reinvented himself. The Russian primitivist who wrote the Rite of Spring became the Neo-Classical ironist of the Octet, who became the serialist of Requiem Canticles. Each transformation was total, deliberate, and irreversible. No other composer of the 20th century changed so completely while remaining so unmistakably himself.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1882 | Oranienbaum, Russia | Born | Father was a bass-baritone at the Mariinsky Theatre; music was household air |
| 1902 | St Petersburg | Meets Rimsky-Korsakov | The only real teacher; studies orchestration privately — never attends conservatory formally |
| 1905–08 | St Petersburg | Studies with Rimsky-Korsakov | Learns brilliant orchestration, Russian folk modality, the craft of the colorist |
| 1908 | St Petersburg | Rimsky-Korsakov dies | Loses his mentor; Fireworks (Op. 4) catches Diaghilev's attention |
| 1910 | Paris | Firebird premiered (Ballets Russes) | Instant fame at 28; Diaghilev becomes patron, impresario, catalyst |
| 1911 | Paris | Petrushka premiered | Bitonality (C + F# chord); the puppet's two keys = the soul's duality |
| 1913 | Paris | The Rite of Spring — riot at premiere | The 20th century begins in music; rhythm as primary element; block form |
| 1914–18 | Switzerland | War exile | Small forces, economy: The Soldier's Tale (7 players), Renard, Les Noces |
| 1920 | Paris | Pulcinella premiered | The Neo-Classical turn begins: Pergolesi reworked through Stravinsky's lens |
| 1923 | Paris | Octet for winds | Pure Neo-Classicism: Baroque container, modern rhythmic content, dry sound |
| 1930 | Paris | Symphony of Psalms | Sacred Neo-Classicism; the choral masterpiece; Latin text, archaic harmony |
| 1940 | USA | Moves to America | Hollywood, Harvard lectures (Poetics of Music), becomes US citizen (1945) |
| 1948 | New York | Meets Robert Craft | Craft becomes assistant, conductor, intellectual partner; facilitates serial turn |
| 1951 | Venice | The Rake's Progress premiered | Last Neo-Classical work; full-length opera; Mozart container, Stravinsky content |
| 1952–54 | Los Angeles | Studies Webern's music (via Craft) | The serial conversion begins: Cantata, Septet show transitional technique |
| 1957 | Los Angeles | Agon completed | Ballet mixing tonal and serial movements; the pivotal transitional work |
| 1958 | Venice | Threni premiered | First fully serial large work; austere, ritualistic, monumental |
| 1966 | Los Angeles | Requiem Canticles | Final major work; serial, spare, luminous — his own requiem |
| 1971 | New York | Dies, April 6 | Buried in Venice, near Diaghilev's grave on San Michele island |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### Rimsky-Korsakov's Orchestration

| What Rimsky Taught | How Stravinsky Transformed It |
|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| Brilliant orchestral color (Scheherazade) | Color as structure, not decoration; each timbre = a musical identity |
| Russian folk scales (octatonic, whole-tone) | Octatonic scale becomes Stravinsky's harmonic DNA across all periods |
| Clear orchestral layers | Layers become independent blocks; each layer has its own key, meter, rhythm |
| Wind writing as specialty | Winds dominate Stravinsky's palette; strings are secondary in most works |

### Diaghilev and the Ballet

| Dimension | What Diaghilev Provided |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Commission structure | Firebird, Petrushka, Rite — each commission pushed Stravinsky further |
| Visual thinking | Music conceived as physical gesture; rhythm as bodily movement |
| Parisian audience | The most sophisticated listeners in Europe; expectation of shock |
| Collaboration culture | Stravinsky worked with Nijinsky, Balanchine, Picasso, Cocteau |

### The Swiss Exile (1914–1918)

| Constraint | Creative Result |
|-----------|----------------|
| No large orchestra available | The Soldier's Tale: 7 players. Economy becomes aesthetic |
| Russian folk sources studied in isolation | Les Noces: the last Russian work; folk material pushed to abstraction |
| Distance from Paris spectacle | Introspection; the seeds of Neo-Classicism |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Defining Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Russian primitivist | 1908–1920 | Block form, ostinato, polytonality, pagan energy, ballet | Firebird, Petrushka, Rite of Spring, Les Noces, The Soldier's Tale |
| Neo-Classical | 1920–1951 | Baroque/Classical forms, tonal but displaced, dry orchestration, ironic | Pulcinella, Octet, Symphony of Psalms, Dumbarton Oaks, The Rake's Progress |
| Serial | 1952–1966 | Twelve-tone rows, sparse texture, ritualistic austerity, Webern influence | Canticum Sacrum, Agon, Threni, Movements, Requiem Canticles |
| Late silence | 1967–1971 | Health decline; no major new works | — |
