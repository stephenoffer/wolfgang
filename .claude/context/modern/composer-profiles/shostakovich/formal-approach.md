# Dmitri Shostakovich — Formal Approach

Shostakovich works within Classical forms — sonata, rondo, passacaglia, fugue — but distorts them into vehicles for testimony and autobiography. His symphonies are not abstract music; they are statements about life under totalitarianism. His quartets are not chamber entertainment; they are private diaries. The form serves the expressive content, and the expressive content is inseparable from the political context. A Shostakovich sonata-allegro does not simply develop themes; it enacts a drama of oppression and resistance.

## Core Formal Philosophy

| Principle | Description | Contrast with Contemporaries |
|-----------|-------------|------------------------------|
| Symphony as testimony | The symphony is a public statement about the human condition | Prokofiev: symphony as Classical architecture |
| Quartet as diary | String quartets are private, autobiographical, death-haunted | Bartok: quartet as formal experiment |
| Passacaglia as obsession | The repeating bass = the inescapable reality (political or existential) | Baroque: passacaglia as variation form |
| Fugue as intellectual rigor | Fugues are disciplined, Bach-influenced, architecturally precise | Stravinsky: fugue as ironic container |
| Satirical movement | Scherzo or march that parodies official culture | Prokofiev: scherzo as grotesque humor |
| Ambiguous ending | Finales that "resolve" into forced major or dissolve into silence | Beethoven: triumph; Mahler: resignation or transcendence |

## Form Types

### Sonata-Allegro — The Testimony Form

| Element | Shostakovich's Treatment |
|---------|-------------------------|
| Exposition: 1st theme | Often long, slow, monologue-like; sets emotional landscape |
| Exposition: 2nd theme | Contrasting but derived from same material; not "beautiful" contrast |
| Development | The crisis: themes fragmented, chromaticism increases, ostinato builds |
| Recapitulation | Themes return changed — not simply transposed; something has happened |
| Coda | Either forced triumph (ff major) or dissolution (ppp, single notes) |

```abc
X:1
T:Sonata-Allegro — Shostakovich Opening Theme Character
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Em
%% Long, slow, monologue-like — the Symphony No. 10 opening
!pp!E,2 G,2 B,2 E2|D2 C2 B,2 A,2|G,2 A,2 B,2 C2|D4 z4|
%% The theme does not "begin" — it emerges from silence
%% 16+ bars before the second theme arrives; Shostakovich takes his time
```

### Passacaglia — The Obsession Form

| Element | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| Bass theme | 8–12 bars; chromatic descent; minor key; stated alone |
| Variation 1–2 | Sparse upper voices; pp; chamber texture |
| Variation 3–4 | Increasing density; new countermelodies; growing dynamic |
| Variation 5–6 | Full orchestra; ff; maximum dissonance; climax |
| Final variation | Either dissolution (ppp) or brutal close (fff) |

| Work | Passacaglia Location | Bass Character |
|------|---------------------|----------------|
| Symphony No. 8, mvt 4 | Entire movement | Chromatic descending; C minor; relentless |
| Violin Concerto No. 1, mvt 3 | Entire movement | Modal; D minor; 17 variations |
| Piano Trio No. 2, mvt 4 | Finale over dance rhythm | Jewish dance + passacaglia = lament through dance |

### Fugue — The Intellectual Form

| Element | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| Subject | Chromatic, angular, Bach-influenced; intellectually rigorous |
| Answer | Tonal or real; strict contrapuntal procedure |
| Episodes | Sequences, inversions; increasing chromaticism |
| Stretto | Entries pile up at climax; voices compress |
| Final statement | Subject in augmentation or full orchestral unison |

```abc
X:2
T:Fugue Subject — 24 Preludes and Fugues Character
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Am
%% Angular, chromatic, Bach-derived — intellectual rigor
!mf!A,2 C2 E2 D2|^C2 D2 F2 E2|D2 C2 B,2 A,2|^G,4 A,4|
%% The subject is angular but tonal; the chromaticism comes in the answer
```

### The Satirical Scherzo/March

| Element | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| Character | Major key; staccato; fast; xylophone + piccolo |
| Meaning | NOT genuine cheerfulness — coded satire of official culture |
| Structure | Rondo or ternary; regular phrase lengths; march rhythm |
| The "wrong" element | One chromatic intrusion per phrase; one unexpected silence |
| Trio | Contrasting: either lyrical (genuine) or even more grotesque |

## Multi-Movement Architecture — Symphonies

| Symphony | Movements | Key Scheme | Narrative Arc |
|----------|-----------|------------|--------------|
| No. 5 | Moderato - Allegretto - Largo - Allegro non troppo | D-A-F#-D | Struggle - Satire - Grief - "Triumph" (forced?) |
| No. 7 | Allegretto - Moderato - Adagio - Allegro non troppo | C-F#-Db-C | War - Memory - Grief - Victory |
| No. 8 | Adagio - Allegretto - Allegro non troppo - Largo - Allegretto | C-C-C-G-C | Tragedy - March - March - Passacaglia - Ambiguous |
| No. 10 | Moderato - Allegro - Allegretto - Andante-Allegro | E-E-E-E | Meditation - Brutality (Stalin?) - DSCH - Resolution |
| No. 14 | 11 movements | Various | 11 poems about death; no resolution |
| No. 15 | Allegretto - Adagio - Allegretto - Adagio-Allegretto | A-various-A | Quotations (Rossini, Wagner); enigmatic farewell |

## Multi-Movement Architecture — String Quartets

| Quartet | Movements | Character |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| No. 8 | 5 movements (Largo-Allegro molto-Allegretto-Largo-Largo) | DSCH throughout; autobiographical |
| No. 11 | 7 short movements played attacca | Concentrated; no movement longer than 4 minutes |
| No. 13 | Single movement | Viola monologue with tapping sounds |
| No. 15 | 6 Adagios played attacca | All slow; death meditation; 40 minutes of Adagio |

## Proportional Balance

| Section | Shostakovich's Treatment |
|---------|--------------------------|
| First movement | Often the longest; 15–25 minutes in major symphonies |
| Scherzo | Compact (5–8 minutes); can precede or follow slow movement |
| Slow movement | Long; 10–20 minutes; the emotional core |
| Finale | Variable: forced-march triumph, ambiguous dissolution, or enigmatic silence |

## How Form Serves Expression

| Formal Strategy | Emotional Effect |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Extended opening monologue | The listener enters a world of private grief before public drama begins |
| Passacaglia as climactic movement | Inescapability; the repeated bass = the system that cannot be escaped |
| Forced-major finale | "Triumph" that the listener questions — is this genuine or coerced? |
| Fugue as order within chaos | Intellectual discipline as resistance against emotional dissolution |
| Satirical scherzo between slow movements | Comic relief that is not comic — the satire is more disturbing than the tragedy |
| Dissolving coda (ppp, single notes) | Annihilation; the music doesn't end — it ceases to exist |
