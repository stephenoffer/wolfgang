# Monteverdi — Melodic Style

The line follows the words. Where Palestrina's melody is governed by what a
voice can sing smoothly, Monteverdi's is governed by what the text is saying,
and he will accept an ugly interval to get it.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Artistic Function |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Declamatory repetition | Several notes on one pitch following the natural stresses of the line | The music delivers the words at speaking speed before it sings them |
| Sudden melisma on one word | An otherwise syllabic line opens into a run at a single charged word | The listener's attention is thrown exactly where the poet wanted it |
| Expressive leap | A seventh, a diminished fourth, an unprepared leap where the sense turns | Discomfort as meaning, not as error |
| Chromatic inflection at the turn | A raised or lowered degree at the moment of feeling, often against another voice | Colour applied to a word, never to a passage |
| Broken line | A rest interrupts a word or phrase — the musical sigh, gasp, or hesitation | The body's response to the text made audible |
| Narrow then suddenly wide | The line sits in a small compass and then breaks out of it | The range itself becomes an expressive event |
| Cadential formula deferred | The expected cadence is evaded so the sense can continue | Keeps the text moving through the music's punctuation |

## Reading this against Palestrina

Everything here is a *departure* from a norm Monteverdi has completely in hand.
Write the smooth idiom first and break it at the word that demands it — once,
deliberately, and audibly. Breaking it everywhere is not the *seconda pratica*;
it is just rough writing.
