# Monteverdi Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `monteverdi-lh-vocabulary.md`, which says the essential thing:
decide first whether you are writing the early madrigal texture (five equal
voices, Palestrina's rules bent) or the late concertato one (solo voice over
continuo). They share almost nothing.

The organising fact across both: **the text governs.** Harmony, rhythm,
dissonance and texture all bend to a word. Monteverdi said so himself, and it is
the thing to write into the notes rather than to admire from outside.

## Word-painting devices

1. **Rising line for rising text** — *ascende*, *cielo*, *alto*. Literal and
   deliberate.

2. **Falling chromatic line for grief** — *dolore*, *morte*, *pianto*. Usually a
   descending fourth by semitones.

3. **The written sigh** — a rest inside a word, then a falling second. *Sospiro*
   painted by an actual gap.

4. **Fast imitative scattering for flight** — *fugge*, *vola*: the voices chase
   each other in close entries.

5. **Stillness for stillness** — a held chord, all voices, no motion, for
   *silenzio* or *dorme*.

6. **Silence before the crucial word** — everything stops, then the word arrives
   alone. The most Monteverdian gesture there is and it costs one rest.

7. **Sudden homophony for a line that must be understood** — all voices in the
   rhythm of speech, then back to polyphony.

## Harmonic devices — the *seconda pratica*

8. **Unprepared dissonance** — a seventh or ninth entered without preparation
   because the text demands it. Forbidden in the older practice; the entire point
   of the newer one.

9. **Cross relation** — the same pitch class natural in one voice and altered in
   another, close together. Harsh and intentional.

10. **The descending tetrachord ground** — `A3 G3 F3 E3` repeated as an ostinato
    under changing upper material. The lament bass; the *Lamento della Ninfa* is
    built entirely on one, and it is the most reusable device in his vocabulary.

11. **Slow harmonic rhythm under recitative** — one chord for a bar or two while
    the voice declaims freely above it.

12. **Chromatic bass descent** — semitone by semitone, for grief.

13. **The unresolved suspension** — a dissonance resolving onto another dissonance
    rather than a consonance.

14. **Modal harmony with expressive ficta** — the raised leading tone used for
    intensity rather than only at cadences.

## Textural and rhythmic devices

15. **Stile concitato** — the "agitated style": rapid repeated notes on one pitch,
    `C4s ×16`, for warfare and passion. His own invention and unmistakable.

16. **Speech rhythm** — the vocal line following the accentuation of the words,
    not the barline. Long notes on stressed syllables, short runs on unstressed.

17. **Echo** — a phrase repeated immediately, quieter, often by a different voice
    or at a distance.

18. **Concertato contrast** — a small group answered by a large one, or voices
    answered by instruments. The Baroque's founding textural idea.

19. **The ritornello** — a short instrumental refrain between vocal sections.

20. **Ornamental division on a long note** — the voice decorating a held pitch
    with rapid written-out figuration.

## Structural devices

21. **Strophic variation** — the same bass and harmonic plan, new melody in each
    verse. Between strict strophic and through-composed.

22. **Recitative — aria alternation** — declamation for narrative, lyrical
    measured writing for reflection. The structural invention that makes opera
    possible.

23. **A phrase length set by the line of text**, not by four-bar symmetry.

## What to avoid

- **Mixing the two practices in one section.** Pick the texture and hold it.
- **Metrical evenness in a declamatory passage.** It should follow speech.
- **Fast harmonic rhythm under recitative.**
- **Instrumental figuration in the voices.** Even the late works are vocal in
  conception.
- **Regular four-bar phrasing.** Text lines are not four bars long.
- **Dissonance without a textual reason.** In this style the reason is the
  justification; a harsh chord for its own sake is a later aesthetic.
