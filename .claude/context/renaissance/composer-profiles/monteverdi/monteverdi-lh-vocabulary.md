# Monteverdi Voice & Bass Vocabulary

Monteverdi stands on the hinge. The early madrigals are Palestrina's world with
the rules bent for the sake of a word; the late works have a figured bass, a
declaiming solo voice, and dissonances Palestrina would have forbidden. Which one
you are writing has to be decided before a note is written, because the two
textures share almost nothing.

The organising principle across both is that **the text governs everything**.
Harmony, rhythm, dissonance and texture all bend to a word. That is the
*seconda pratica*, stated by Monteverdi himself, and it is the thing to write
into the notes.

Shorthand reminder: `//` for two voices in one hand, numbered inner voices
beyond. Both textures need real independent lines.

## Idiom catalog

1. **Point of imitation** — as in Palestrina, and the default in the earlier
   madrigal texture: a motif passed between voices at staggered entries.

2. **The declaiming solo over a slow bass** — one voice in speech rhythm, the
   bass holding whole notes underneath: `C3w` under a freely-moving upper line.
   The recitative texture, and Monteverdi's invention as a musical form.

3. **Basso continuo in slow harmonic rhythm** — `G2w`, `C3w`, one chord per bar
   or per two bars. It supports; it does not compete.

4. **Descending tetrachord ground** — `A3h G3h F3h E3h` repeated as an ostinato
   under changing upper material. The lament bass, and the *Lamento della Ninfa*
   is built entirely on one. The most reusable device in his vocabulary.

5. **Stile concitato** — the "agitated style": rapid repeated notes on one pitch,
   `C4s ×16`, representing warfare and passion. Monteverdi's own invention and
   unmistakably his.

6. **Unprepared dissonance for a word** — a seventh or a ninth entered without
   preparation because the text says *cruda*, *dolore*, *morte*. Forbidden in the
   *prima pratica* and the whole point of the second.

7. **Suspension chains** — as in Palestrina, but longer and more insistent, and
   allowed to resolve onto another dissonance.

8. **Homophonic declamation** — all voices together in the rhythm of the words,
   for a line of text that must be understood. Alternates with polyphony.

9. **Voice pairs answering** — two upper voices against two lower, trading a
   phrase. The concertato texture.

10. **Chromatic descent in the bass** — semitone by semitone, `A2q Ab2q G2q
    Gb2q`. Grief. Later than Palestrina and earlier than everyone else who used
    it.

11. **Echo** — a phrase repeated immediately, quieter, often by a different
    voice or at a distance. Written as a literal repeat.

12. **Silence for a word** — everything stops before a crucial word. The most
    Monteverdian gesture there is, and it costs one rest.

## Choosing the texture

- **Madrigal (early)**: five equal voices, imitative, no independent bass,
  dissonance prepared. Palestrina's rules with word-painting on top.
- **Concertato / operatic (late)**: solo voice or small group over a continuo
  bass, declamatory rhythm, free dissonance. Two real strata, not five equal
  ones.

Mixing them produces something that is neither. Pick, and hold it for a section
at least.

## Word painting, concretely

- **Rising text** — the line ascends. *Ascende*, *cielo*, *alto*.
- **Falling text** — the line descends, often chromatically. *Cade*, *morte*.
- **"Fugge"** (flees) — a fast imitative point, voices chasing.
- **"Sospiro"** (sigh) — a rest inside the word, then a falling second.
- **Stillness** — a held chord, all voices, no motion.

These are not decorations; in this repertoire they are the reason for the notes.

## Breathing

Staggered in the polyphonic texture, and rhetorical in the declamatory one — the
solo voice stops where a speaker would stop, and the bass holds through it.

## What to avoid

- **Even, metrical rhythm in a declamatory passage.** It should follow speech, not
  the barline.
- **Fast harmonic rhythm under a recitative.** One chord for two bars is normal.
- **Instrumental figuration.** Even the late works are vocal in conception.
- **Regular four-bar phrases.** Phrase lengths follow the line of text, and text
  lines are not four bars long.
- **Treating the bass as a tune.** In the late texture it is a foundation; in the
  early one it is a voice among equals. Never an accompaniment figure.
