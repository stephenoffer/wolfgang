# Monteverdi — Harmonic Language

Monteverdi is the hinge. He wrote in Palestrina's language and then broke it on
purpose, and defended the breakage in print: in the *seconda pratica* the words
are the mistress of the harmony, not its servant. So his harmony has to be read
in two layers — the old rules, and the specific places he violates them because
the text demands it.

Writing him as if the rules simply do not apply produces incoherence. Writing him
as if they do produces Palestrina. The style *is* the tension.

---

## Cadences

| Cadence | Frame | Where it belongs | Note |
|---------|-------|------------------|------|
| Clausula vera | 6→8 by contrary step | Structural closes, as in the old style | Inherited intact |
| Suspension cadence (7–6, 4–3) | Prepared, struck strong, resolved down | The default full close | Inherited intact |
| Phrygian | Bass falls a semitone | E-final sections; laments | Very frequent — it is his grief cadence |
| Evaded / interrupted | Frame forms, bass moves elsewhere | Mid-text, where the words are not finished | Used far more freely than Palestrina |
| Dominant-with-seventh close | The seventh taken freely, not prepared | Dramatic arrivals in the later books | The rule-break he was attacked for |
| Plagal extension | Subfinal to final after the close | Final Amen; end of a lament | Inherited |

## The licences — his actual signature

| Device | What it breaks | What it is for |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Unprepared dissonance | Dissonance must be prepared | A word of pain lands on the dissonance directly |
| Seventh taken by leap | Dissonance approached by step | Shock at a specific syllable |
| Cross-relation | The false relation is forbidden | Bitterness, contradiction in the text |
| Chromatic inflection | Ficta only at cadences | Weeping, sighing, the *durezze* of the lament |
| Stile concitato | No repeated-note agitation exists | Warfare, anger — rapid repeated notes on one chord |
| Descending tetrachord ostinato | Nothing was built on a repeating bass | The lament: a bass falling a fourth, over and over |

The descending tetrachord is worth singling out. A bass stepping down a fourth
(often chromatically) repeating beneath a free upper line is the single most
recognisable Monteverdi structure, and later becomes the whole Baroque
convention of the lament.

## Modal and tonal practice

He is writing in modes that are visibly turning into keys.

| Feature | Old practice | Monteverdi's usage |
|---------|--------------|--------------------|
| Final | Modal final, one per piece | Sections cadence on several degrees; the final asserts itself as a tonic |
| Leading tone | Ficta, cadence only | Frequent and structural; drives toward the final |
| B-flat | Lydian softening | A signature-like habit; effectively key signatures emerge |
| Triads | Consequence of lines | Increasingly chosen as sonorities in their own right |
| Bass | A voice among voices | Increasingly a *foundation* — the basso continuo is arriving |

---

## What this language will not do

- Fully functional tonality. There is no circle-of-fifths chain and no modulation
  scheme; the harmony still moves by step and by third as often as by fifth.
- Decorative chromaticism. Every accidental outside a cadence must be answerable
  to a word. Chromaticism for colour alone is Gesualdo's habit, not his.
- Smooth voice-leading through the dramatic moments. The whole point of the
  licence is that it is heard as a breach.
