# Palestrina — Scoring

There is no orchestration here in any modern sense: the medium is unaccompanied
voices, and "scoring" means choosing how many, in what ranges, and when they
sing. Every constraint below is a constraint of breath and of the human throat,
which is why the music sounds the way it does.

---

## The voices

| Voice | Clef in the sources | Comfortable range | Role in the texture |
|-------|---------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Cantus / Superius | Treble, C1 | C4–F5 | Carries the subject at its clearest; the ear's line |
| Altus | Alto, C3 | F3–C5 | Filler and inner imitation; the hardest to write well |
| Tenor | Tenor, C4 | C3–F4 | Often holds the cantus firmus; the structural voice |
| Bassus | Bass, F4 | F2–C4 | Supplies the cadential frame's lower note; moves least |
| Quintus | Varies | Doubles whichever part it shadows | The extra voice in five-part writing; not a fixed range |

Ranges are narrow on purpose — a ninth or a tenth per voice across a whole
piece. Writing a voice across two octaves is not Palestrina, whatever the notes.

---

## Textures and when they are used

| Scoring | Effect | Where it belongs |
|---------|--------|------------------|
| Full (4–6 voices) | Weight, resonance | Points of arrival; final sections |
| Reduced trio | Transparency; every line audible | Interior contrast; before a full close |
| Paired duets (high vs low) | Antiphony without a second choir | Answering text phrases |
| Homorhythmic block | Words suddenly intelligible | A phrase of text that must be *heard* — "et incarnatus est" |
| Double choir (cori spezzati) | Spatial antiphony | Ceremonial works only; Venetian more than Roman |

The homorhythmic block is the strongest device in the style precisely because
it is rare: after twenty breves of staggered entries, all voices agreeing on a
syllable stops time.

---

## What the medium forbids

- Dynamics and tempo marks. Neither notated nor conceived; shape comes from
  register, density and entry.
- Instrumental figuration. Every line must be singable at sight, on breath, by
  a person reading one part with no score.
- Wide leaps and awkward intervals. Nothing larger than a sixth except the
  octave; no augmented or diminished melodic intervals at all.
- Continuous full texture. If no voice ever rests, the imitation is inaudible
  and the singers cannot breathe.
