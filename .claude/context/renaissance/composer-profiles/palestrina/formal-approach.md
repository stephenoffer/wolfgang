# Palestrina — Formal Approach

Form in Palestrina is not a container the music is poured into. It is the text.
A motet has as many sections as its text has clauses, each section is a point of
imitation on a subject derived from the words of that clause, and the piece ends
when the words do. There is no sonata, no rondo, no ternary reprise — asking
where the "B section" is mistakes the genre.

What replaces those forms is the **point of imitation**, and the architecture of
a whole work is the chain of them.

---

## The point of imitation — the unit of construction

| Stage | What happens | Typical length |
|-------|--------------|----------------|
| Entry | One voice states the subject alone, or against a thinning texture | 2–4 breves |
| Answer | A second voice enters at the fifth or octave; the first continues freely | 2–4 breves |
| Accumulation | Remaining voices enter in turn; the texture fills | 4–8 breves |
| Drive | All voices active; subject fragments, free counterpoint takes over | 2–6 breves |
| Cadence | A clausula vera forms, usually evaded so the next point can begin | 1–2 breves |

Successive points **overlap**. The cadence of one is the entry of the next in
another voice, which is why the music never stops until the final close. A
section that begins only after the previous one has fully cadenced sounds like a
sequence of exercises.

---

## Whole-work shapes

| Genre | Shape | What governs it |
|-------|-------|-----------------|
| Motet | Chain of points, one per text clause | The text; no reprise unless the words repeat |
| Mass movement (Kyrie) | Ternary by liturgy — Kyrie / Christe / Kyrie | The liturgy, not musical contrast; the second Kyrie is new music |
| Gloria, Credo | Long chains, more syllabic, faster surface | Text length forces declamation over melisma |
| Sanctus / Agnus | Fewer, longer points; often reduced voices then full | Ceremonial weight |
| Parody / imitation Mass | Points borrowed from a model and reworked across movements | The model supplies subjects, not structure |

**Texture is the structural device.** Contrast comes from voices dropping out
and re-entering — a trio passage inside a five-voice work marks a new section as
clearly as a key change marks one in Haydn. Reduced-voice sections before a full
close are the standard way of shaping an Agnus Dei.

---

## Proportions

- Sections run 8–20 breves; nothing is symmetrical and nothing should be.
- Phrase lengths are set by the text line, so they are uneven by construction.
  Four-square phrasing is the commonest way this idiom is faked.
- The final section is usually the longest and the fullest, often with the tempo
  of harmonic events slowing and a plagal extension after the last cadence.
