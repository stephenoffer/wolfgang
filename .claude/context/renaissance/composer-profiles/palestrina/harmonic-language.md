# Palestrina — Harmonic Language

Palestrina has no harmony in the later sense. Nothing progresses toward a goal;
chords are what four or five independent lines happen to spell at a given
moment. The vertical sonority is a *consequence*, and writing it the other way
round — choosing a chord and finding voices for it — produces something that
sounds like a hymn, not like Palestrina.

What governs the sound is therefore not a chord vocabulary but three habits:
the modal framework, the treatment of dissonance, and where a line is allowed
to rest.

---

## Cadences

The cadence is the only moment where the vertical is planned. Every one of them
is built from a two-voice frame — the *clausula vera* — and everything else is
decoration around it.

| Cadence | Two-voice frame | Where it belongs | Ficta needed |
|---------|-----------------|------------------|--------------|
| Clausula vera | 6→8, the two voices stepping out by contrary motion | Any structural close | Raise the lower leading note in Dorian/Mixolydian |
| Suspension cadence (7–6, 4–3) | Dissonance prepared, struck on the strong beat, resolved down by step | The main cadence of any section | As above |
| Phrygian | Bass falls a semitone, upper rises a tone | Mode 3/4 closes; also mid-piece on E | None — the semitone is diatonic |
| Plagal | Subfinal to final, no leading tone | Final Amen; after a full close | None |
| Evaded cadence | The frame forms, then a voice moves away or enters anew | Interior joints; keeps the music running | As for the frame |
| Under-third (Landini) | The frame decorated by dipping to the note below | Archaic colour, sparing | As for the frame |

The evaded cadence does most of the structural work: in a long motet the music
must not stop at every point of imitation, so the frame is formed and then
sidestepped by an entry in another voice.

## Dissonance

Every dissonance is one of exactly four things. If a note is dissonant and is
none of these, it is wrong.

| Type | Approach | Departure | Metric position |
|------|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Passing | Step | Step, same direction | Weak |
| Neighbour | Step | Step, back where it came from | Weak |
| Suspension | Held over from a consonance | Step DOWN | Strong |
| Cambiata | Step down | Leap of a third down, then step up | Weak |

The suspension is the expressive event of the style — the one place a
dissonance is allowed to land on a strong beat, and the reason his cadences
have any tension at all. There is no unprepared dissonance, no dissonance
resolving upward, and no dissonance left by leap.

## Modal practice

| Mode | Final | Reciting tone | Character in use |
|------|-------|---------------|------------------|
| Dorian (1/2) | D | A (F in plagal) | The default; grave, flexible |
| Phrygian (3/4) | E | C (A in plagal) | The semitone above the final colours everything |
| Lydian (5/6) | F | C (A in plagal) | Almost always with B-flat, i.e. heard as F major-ish |
| Mixolydian (7/8) | G | D (C in plagal) | Bright; needs ficta F-sharp only at cadences |
| Aeolian (9/10) | A | E (C in plagal) | Late addition; the nearest thing to minor |
| Ionian (11/12) | C | G (E in plagal) | Late addition; the nearest thing to major |

Musica ficta raises the leading note **at cadences only**. A raised seventh
anywhere else is an anachronism, and a B-flat in Lydian is not ficta but
standard practice.

---

## What this language will not do

- Functional progression. There is no ii–V–I, no circle of fifths, no dominant
  preparation. A cadence is a contrapuntal event, not a harmonic one.
- Seventh chords as entities. A seventh is a suspension in progress.
- Chromaticism. Outside cadential ficta, accidentals belong to Gesualdo, not
  to Palestrina.
- Parallel fifths or octaves. Here the prohibition really is near-absolute:
  independence of line is the whole point of the texture.
- Modulation. The music moves between reciting tones and cadence degrees within
  one mode; it does not change key.
