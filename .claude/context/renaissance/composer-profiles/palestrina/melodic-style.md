# Palestrina — Melodic Style

Every line is written for a specific human voice to sing, at sight, with breath.
That constraint produces the whole style: nothing is unsingable, so nothing is
angular, so the surface is smooth in a way no instrumental idiom is.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Artistic Function |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Singable at sight | Every interval is one a chorister can pitch from the previous note; no augmented or diminished intervals melodically | The line can be *performed*, which is the constraint the whole idiom is built on |
| Leap recovery | A leap is answered by stepwise motion in the opposite direction, filling the gap it opened | Keeps the line balanced and the tessitura stable; the single most recognisable habit of the style |
| Predominantly conjunct | Steps far outnumber leaps; leaps beyond a sixth are rare and the octave is the limit | Smoothness is the default, so a leap becomes an event |
| Single arched climax | The line rises to one high note, touches it once, and descends | Gives a phrase of otherwise even motion an audible shape |
| Narrow compass | A phrase usually spans a ninth or less; a whole voice part rarely exceeds a twelfth | Keeps every note in the comfortable part of the voice |
| Rhythmic evenness with syncopation | Mostly equal note values, displaced by suspensions and ties | The tension comes from where notes *land*, not from how long they are |
| Text-governed length | The phrase is as long as its line of text, and breathes at its punctuation | Musical and verbal sense coincide |

## What breaks the illusion

- Repeating the climax pitch within a phrase — the arch collapses into a plateau.
- Two leaps in the same direction, or a leap not recovered.
- A melisma with no textual reason. Melismas belong on the accented syllable of an important word.
- Instrumental figuration of any kind: broken chords, scale runs for their own sake, anything requiring a keyboard.
