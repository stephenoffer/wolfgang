# Rachmaninoff Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Rachmaninoff's left hand is **enormous**. Wide-spaced chords, deep bass octaves,
and figures spanning two octaves are the norm — he had famously large hands and
wrote for them. The texture is full, dark and continuously resonant.

## LH idiom catalog
1. **Wide-spaced arpeggio spanning two octaves** — `C1e G1e Eb2e G2e C3e Eb3e`,
   pedalled into a wash.
2. **Deep bass octaves** — `[C1,C2]h`, the bottom of the instrument used
   constantly.
3. **Full chords in both hands** — six to eight notes sounding, the "orchestral"
   piano texture.
4. **Repeated chords, throbbing**, under a long melody.
5. **Chromatic inner-voice descent** over a held bass — his standard intensifier.
6. **Counter-melody in the tenor**, played by the left hand's thumb.
7. **The bell texture** — low octaves struck and pedalled, tolling.
8. **Rippling figuration under a sustained melody**, wide and even.
9. **Rolled chords too wide to strike** — written with an arpeggio sign.
10. **Bare octaves for a Dies Irae statement.**
11. **Tremolo, low and dark**, orchestral in intent.

## Where the idiom changes
At each stage of a long build. Rachmaninoff's climaxes are approached over many
bars with the texture thickening continuously.

## Breathing
Long phrases, and the collapse after a climax is where the texture opens — often
to a single line.

## What to avoid
- **Narrow spacing.** The width is the sound.
- **A high centre of gravity.** The music lives low.
- **Short phrases.** The lines are long.
- **Dry texture.** Pedal throughout.
