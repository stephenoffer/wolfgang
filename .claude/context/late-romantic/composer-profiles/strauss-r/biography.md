# Richard Strauss — Biography

The last of the great Romantics, composing as if the 20th century were merely a weather report he could ignore. Strauss lived through Wagner, Mahler, Schoenberg, two World Wars, and the rise of jazz — and ended his career writing music that sounded like the most beautiful sunset the 19th century never quite had. His art is the art of a man who mastered everything young and then spent sixty years deciding what mattered.

## Life in Brief

| Year | Location | Event | Musical Significance |
|------|----------|-------|---------------------|
| 1864 | Munich | Born; father Franz Strauss was principal horn of the Munich Court Orchestra | Grew up hearing the finest orchestral playing in Europe; the horn is his totem instrument from birth |
| 1874 | Munich | First composition at age 6 | Prodigy, but not a flashy one — thorough, Brahmsian training from his conservative father |
| 1882 | Munich | Symphony No. 1 performed | Competent, Mendelssohnian; the young Strauss writes like his father's hero, Brahms |
| 1885 | Meiningen | Appointed assistant to Hans von Bulow | Bulow transforms him: introduces Liszt's tone poems and Wagner's scores; Strauss converts from Brahmsian to New German School |
| 1886 | Munich | Aus Italien — first orchestral work with programmatic content | The turn: Brahms's forms give way to Liszt's programs; the tone poet is born |
| 1888 | Munich | Don Juan premiered — instant fame at age 24 | The work that changed orchestral music: virtuosic scoring, erotic energy, narrative thrust; Strauss arrives fully formed |
| 1889–98 | Weimar/Munich | The great tone poems | Tod und Verklarung, Till Eulenspiegel, Also sprach Zarathustra, Don Quixote, Ein Heldenleben — each a masterpiece of orchestral storytelling |
| 1905 | Dresden | Salome premiered | One-act opera: biblical horror, polytonal clashes, scandalous nudity; Wagner's harmonic world pushed to the edge of atonality |
| 1909 | Dresden | Elektra premiered | The most dissonant work Strauss ever wrote; bitonality, screaming brass; then — he retreats |
| 1911 | Dresden | Der Rosenkavalier premiered | The great turn: from Elektra's violence to Viennese waltz nostalgia; Mozart reimagined through Wagnerian orchestration; his most popular opera |
| 1912–42 | Various | Collaboration with Hugo von Hofmannsthal (d. 1929), then others | Ariadne auf Naxos, Die Frau ohne Schatten, Arabella, Capriccio — each a new formal experiment within tonal lyricism |
| 1933–45 | Germany | Nazi period: appointed president of Reichsmusikkammer, then dismissed | Morally compromised: cooperated to protect his Jewish daughter-in-law; wrote little of significance; the silence of complicity |
| 1945 | Garmisch | Metamorphosen for 23 solo strings | Elegy for the destruction of German culture; polyphonic, Mozartean, heartbroken — the first work of the Indian Summer |
| 1948 | Switzerland | Four Last Songs | The farewell: soprano voice floats above warm orchestra; Eichendorff and Hesse poetry; death as gentle sleep |
| 1949 | Garmisch | Dies, September 8, age 85 | "Dying is just as I composed it in Tod und Verklarung" — his last words |

## The Forces That Shaped the Music

### The Father's Horn

| Aspect | Influence |
|--------|-----------|
| Franz Strauss | Principal horn, Munich opera; played under Wagner (loathed him personally, loved the horn parts); conservative, anti-Wagner |
| Musical result | Richard heard the orchestra from inside the horn section; brass writing is instinctive, not learned; the soaring horn theme IS Strauss |
| The rebellion | Converted to Wagner against his father's wishes; the tone poems are both homage to Wagner and defiance of Brahmsian father |

### Von Bulow and the Conversion

| Before Meiningen (1885) | After Meiningen (1885) |
|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Brahmsian forms: symphony, sonata | Lisztian forms: tone poem, character piece |
| Conservative harmony: functional, diatonic | Wagnerian harmony: chromatic, extended, luxuriant |
| Abstract music: no programs | Programmatic music: every piece tells a story |
| The father's world | The world the father feared |

## Career Phases

| Phase | Period | Character | Key Works |
|-------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Conservative apprentice | 1876–1885 | Brahmsian; formal; Munich establishment | Symphony in F minor, Cello Sonata, Wind Serenade |
| Tone poem master | 1886–1898 | Orchestral narrative; virtuosic scoring; fame | Don Juan, Tod und Verklarung, Till, Zarathustra, Don Quixote, Heldenleben |
| Operatic radical | 1901–1909 | One-act shock operas; pushing tonality to the limit | Feuersnot, Salome, Elektra |
| Lyric opera master | 1911–1942 | Tonal, warm, witty; Hofmannsthal collaboration; Viennese nostalgia | Rosenkavalier, Ariadne, Frau ohne Schatten, Arabella, Capriccio |
| Indian Summer | 1943–1948 | Autumnal; elegiac; crystalline orchestration; farewell | Metamorphosen, Oboe Concerto, Four Last Songs |
