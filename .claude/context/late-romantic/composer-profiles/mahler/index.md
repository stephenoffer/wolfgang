# Gustav Mahler — Index

**Era:** Late Romantic | **Nationality:** Austrian | **1860–1911**

**Key Works:** Symphonies 1–9 (+ unfinished 10), Das Lied von der Erde, Songs of a Wayfarer, Kindertotenlieder, Des Knaben Wunderhorn songs

**Style Summary:**
- The symphonic maximalist: Mahler's symphonies require massive forces (100+ players, soloists, chorus) but his most personal moments are chamber-quiet — a solo oboe, a solo horn in the distance.
- Chamber-within-orchestra: the defining Mahler move — suddenly reduce from full orchestra to 3–4 solo instruments mid-phrase. The texture collapses to reveal something intimate.
- Folk/march/dance intrusion: a waltz interrupts tragedy; a march becomes grotesque; a Ländler appears in the middle of a symphonic development. High and low culture collide.
- Tonal dissolution: Mahler's key relationships wander far from the home key. The final movement may not return to the key of the first movement. Nothing is secure.
- The postlude as real climax: the last pages of a Mahler movement — after the main drama is over — often contain the movement's most extraordinary music.
