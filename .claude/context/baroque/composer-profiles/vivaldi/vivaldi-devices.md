# Vivaldi Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `vivaldi-lh-vocabulary.md`. The organising fact: **ritornello form**
— a memorable tutti passage returning in different keys, alternating with solo
episodes. Almost everything else follows from it.

## Structural devices
1. **Ritornello** — a fixed tutti passage, usually built from two or three short
   modules, returning in the tonic, the dominant, the relative minor, and the
   tonic again. Later returns can use only part of it.
2. **Solo episode** — virtuosic figuration over a thinner bass, modulating.
3. **Fast–slow–fast** in three movements.
4. **The slow movement as an aria** — a long singing line over a simple repeated
   accompaniment, often in the relative minor.
5. **Programmatic depiction** — birds, storm, sleep, ice. Vivaldi invented much of
   the vocabulary; the effects are literal and unembarrassed.

## Rhythmic and textural devices
6. **Driving motoric rhythm** — continuous even notes as the default surface.
7. **Repeated-note tutti** — the whole ensemble hammering one chord rhythmically.
8. **Echo dynamics** — a phrase repeated immediately, softer.
9. **Unison opening** — everything on one line for a bold start.
10. **Solo against sustained strings** — the accompaniment holds while the soloist
    moves.
11. **String tremolo** for storm and agitation.

## Melodic and harmonic devices
12. **Sequence** — the principal method of continuation, usually falling by step
    or by fifth, three or four times.
13. **Circle of fifths** under a sequence.
14. **The memorable ritornello head** — a short, rhythmically striking figure that
    is instantly recognisable on its return.
15. **Slow harmonic rhythm** — one chord for bars at a time under fast figuration.
16. **Neapolitan sixth and diminished seventh** at moments of drama.
17. **Picardy third** to end a minor movement.
18. **Arpeggiated solo figuration** across the instrument's range.
19. **The written-out slow-movement ornamentation.**

## What to avoid
- **Bach's counterpoint.** Vivaldi's textures are homophonic and driven.
- **Fast harmonic rhythm.**
- **Varying a sequence.** Exact transposition.
- **Understatement in the programmatic passages.** The effects are literal.
