# Vivaldi Bass Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Vivaldi's bass is a **motor**. Where Corelli's walks and Bach's argues, Vivaldi's
drives: relentless repeated notes, insistent rhythmic figures, and long stretches
of one harmony. The style's energy comes from rhythmic drive and sequence, not
from contrapuntal interest.

## Bass idiom catalog

1. **Driving repeated eighths** — `D2e ×8` on one pitch for a whole bar or several.
   The ritornello engine.
2. **Walking quarters, plain** — `D2q A2q D3q A2q`, root and fifth.
3. **The pounding downbeat** — `D2q rest_q D2q rest_q`. Space in the bass; the
   upper parts fill it.
4. **Sequential descent by step** under a sequential upper line — the two together
   are most of the style.
5. **Long pedal on one note** for eight or more bars under a solo episode.
6. **Unison with the upper strings** — everything on one line, the concerto's
   opening gesture.
7. **Silence under the solo** — the continuo drops out entirely so the soloist is
   alone, then re-enters at the cadence.
8. **Off-beat bass** — the bass entering on beat 2, leaving the downbeat to the
   upper parts.
9. **Repeated-note crescendo** — the same figure, accumulating instruments.
10. **Simple alternating root-fifth** for a slow movement, whole notes.
11. **Pizzicato-style detached quarters** under a lyrical slow-movement melody.

## Where the idiom changes
Between ritornello and episode, every time. Full bass in the tutti; thin or absent
under the soloist. That alternation *is* the form.

## Breathing
At every ritornello boundary. The bass rests under solo entries.

## What to avoid
- **Contrapuntal busyness.** The bass drives; it does not argue.
- **Fast harmonic rhythm.** One chord can last four bars over a driving figure.
- **Corelli's regularity of suspension.** Vivaldi is about momentum.
