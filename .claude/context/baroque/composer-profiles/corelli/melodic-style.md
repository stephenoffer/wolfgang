# Corelli — Melodic Style

Two violins of equal standing, over a bass that is a third melody. The lines are
instrumental but restrained — this is violin writing before virtuosity became
the point.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Artistic Function |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Sequential continuation | A one- or two-bar figure restated a step lower, two or three times | How a phrase travels from its opening to its cadence; the engine of the style |
| Suspension-driven line | The upper voice hangs over the bar and resolves down by step, repeatedly | Dissonance becomes the melody's forward motion rather than an incident in it |
| Paired thirds and sixths | The two violins run in parallel for long stretches, then cross | A single sweetened line that periodically separates into two |
| Conjunct with cadential leaps | Mostly stepwise; leaps concentrated at phrase openings and cadential approaches | Keeps the sequence smooth and marks the joints |
| Modest compass | Rarely above the third position; no extended range display | The interest is the interaction of the parts, not the reach of one |
| Even rhythmic surface | Steady quavers or crotchets, changing value at cadences | The harmonic rhythm carries the drama; the surface stays level |
| Written-out ornament at cadences | Trills and appoggiaturas notated into the cadential formula | The cadence is the ornamental moment, not the phrase interior |

## Fast and slow

Slow movements are sustained and suspension-rich, the line long and the bass
walking beneath. Fast movements are sequential and motoric. A movement rarely
changes character partway; the contrast belongs between movements.
