# Corelli — Scoring

Corelli writes for strings and continuo and nothing else. The sound is defined
by what a small string body plus a chordal bass can do, and by the fact that the
bass line is always present.

---

## The instruments

| Instrument | Range (sounding) | Role |
|------------|------------------|------|
| Violin I | G3–D6 | First of the pair; leads the suspension chains |
| Violin II | G3–D6 | Equal partner, not accompaniment — the two cross and exchange |
| Viola | C3–D5 | Ripieno filler; often absent in trio textures |
| Cello | C2–E5 | The bass line, played not realised |
| Violone | E1–G3 | Doubles the bass an octave below in tutti |
| Harpsichord / organ | continuo | Realises the figures above the bass |
| Archlute / theorbo | continuo | Alternative or additional realisation |

The upper limit of the violin writing sits around D6; Corelli almost never goes
above the third position's reach, which is part of why the sound is so even.

---

## Textures

| Texture | Scoring | Where |
|---------|---------|-------|
| Trio | 2 violins + bass + continuo | The default; the whole sonata repertoire |
| Tutti ripieno | Full strings doubling the trio | Concerto grosso statements |
| Solo over continuo | One violin + bass | Slow movement decoration |
| Unison passagework | All upper strings together | Emphatic cadential drive |
| Walking bass alone | Cello + continuo, upper voices silent | Transitions; setting up an entry |

The two violins in parallel thirds and sixths, or exchanging a suspension chain,
is the characteristic Corelli sound. Writing violin II as an accompaniment part
loses the style entirely.

---

## What this scoring will not do

- Wind instruments. Not in the sonatas or the concerti grossi.
- Extended technique, high positions, or double stops as a main texture.
- A texture without a bass. The continuo runs continuously; a passage with no
  bass is not this idiom.
- Dynamic gradation. Contrast is terraced — concertino against ripieno — not
  crescendo.
