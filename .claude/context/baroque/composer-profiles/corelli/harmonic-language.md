# Corelli — Harmonic Language

Corelli is where functional tonality becomes fully explicit. Earlier composers
arrive at dominant-tonic motion; Corelli builds whole movements out of it. The
sequence of descending fifths — the chain that later theorists simply call "the
Corelli sequence" — is his structural engine, and the suspension chain that runs
above it is his surface.

Writing him means committing to the bass. Nothing here floats.

---

## Cadences

| Cadence | Approach | Where it belongs | Voice-leading |
|---------|----------|------------------|---------------|
| Perfect authentic (PAC) | V7–I, root position, soprano on 1 | End of every movement and most sections | 7th resolves down, leading tone up |
| Cadential 6-4 | I64–V7–I | Structural closes; the standard preparation | The 6-4 is a dominant, not a tonic |
| Half cadence | Any to V | Mid-movement articulation | Frequently with a 4–3 suspension |
| Phrygian half cadence | iv6–V in minor | End of a slow movement, leading attacca to the next | Bass falls a semitone |
| Deceptive | V–vi | Extends a phrase already promising to close | Delays the real cadence |
| Trill cadence | Cadential trill on 2 over V | The soloist's close in a slow movement | The trill is obligatory, not optional |

The Phrygian half cadence closing a slow movement is a signature: the movement
does not resolve, it hands over.

---

## The sequences

| Sequence | Pattern | What it does |
|----------|---------|--------------|
| Descending fifths | vi–ii–V–I, or the full circle | The main engine; drives most modulations |
| 7–6 suspension chain | Suspensions resolving down over a stepwise bass | The characteristic surface of a slow movement |
| 4–3 chain over descending fifths | Suspension on every strong beat | Trio sonata slow movements almost entirely |
| Ascending 5-6 | Bass rises stepwise, alternating 5 and 6 | Building sequences; less common than the falling chain |
| Pachelbel / descending tetrachord | Bass steps down from 1 to 5 | Ground-bass movements |

## Modulation

| From | To | How |
|------|----|-----|
| Major | Dominant | Pivot on ii of the new key; secondary dominant V/V |
| Major | Relative minor | Pivot on vi; raise its leading tone |
| Minor | Relative major | Pivot on III; the commonest minor-key departure |
| Minor | Minor dominant | Rare and dark; kept for the middle of a movement |

Modulations are prepared, not abrupt, and almost always arrive through a
descending-fifth chain rather than a single pivot chord.

---

## What this language will not do

- Chromatic third relations. The harmony moves by fifth and by step.
- Unprepared dissonance. Every seventh and every suspension is prepared.
- Ambiguous tonality. The key is asserted at the opening and confirmed at every
  cadence; a Corelli movement is never in doubt about where it is.
- Enharmonic reinterpretation. That is a later device entirely.
