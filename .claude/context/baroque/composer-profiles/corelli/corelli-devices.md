# Corelli Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `corelli-lh-vocabulary.md`, which says the essential thing: the
style's defining quality is its **regularity**. The harmony moves at a steady
rate, the sequences are exact, and the suspensions arrive on schedule.

The organising fact: Corelli standardised the Baroque's vocabulary, and writing in
his style means using that vocabulary *plainly*. Generated "Baroque" usually
over-complicates him.

## Contrapuntal devices

1. **The suspension chain** — the two violins suspending in alternation while the
   bass walks down by step, 7-6 or 4-3, for four to eight bars. The single most
   Corellian texture, and the sound the whole style is remembered for.

2. **Imitation between the two violins** — one states, the other answers a bar
   later at the unison or the octave. Constant in the fast movements.

3. **Voice crossing between the violins** — the second rising above the first for
   a bar or two. Gives the trio texture its interweaving quality.

4. **Fugal exposition in a fast movement** — subject in violin 1, answer in violin
   2, then the bass. The church sonata's second movement.

5. **Parallel thirds and sixths between the violins** — for a lyrical passage or
   the approach to a cadence.

## Harmonic devices

6. **Circle of fifths** — longer than anyone before him, six or eight chords,
   with a sequence in the upper parts. His signature progression.

7. **The 4-3 cadence** — the upper voice suspends a fourth over the bass's
   penultimate note and resolves down to the third. Every phrase ends this way,
   and that consistency is the period's punctuation, not monotony.

8. **The Corelli clash** — the resolution of a suspension sounding briefly against
   the note it resolves to, in another voice. Named after him.

9. **Secondary dominants inside a sequence** — each step of a circle tonicised.
   Systematic rather than expressive.

10. **The pedal on the dominant** before a final cadence, four to eight bars.

11. **Picardy third** to end a minor-key movement.

12. **Even harmonic rhythm** — one chord per beat or per bar, held for a whole
    section. Irregular harmonic rhythm sounds later than this style.

## Structural devices

13. **Exact sequence** — a one- or two-bar unit transposed without variation,
    three or four times, usually falling by step or by fifth. Do not vary it; the
    exactness is the idiom.

14. **Slow–fast–slow–fast** — the church sonata's four movements: suspensions over
    a walking bass, fugal, a shorter slow movement often in the relative key, and
    a dance-like finale in triple time.

15. **The dance suite alternative** — allemande, corrente, sarabande, giga, each
    in binary form with repeats.

16. **Ground bass** — a descending tetrachord repeated with varied upper material,
    for a slow movement or a chaconne finale.

17. **Ritornello contrast in the concerto grosso** — the full group (*concerto
    grosso*) answering the soloists (*concertino*), with the same material.

18. **Regular four- and eight-bar phrases** — Corelli is squarer than almost
    anyone, and the regularity is the frame the suspensions hang on.

## Ornamentation

19. **The written skeleton, performer-ornamented** — slow movement lines are
    intentionally plain and were decorated in performance. If writing the
    ornamentation out, use passing notes, cadential trills and turns around long
    notes.

20. **The cadential trill** — on the penultimate note of every significant
    cadence, resolving to the final.

21. **Terraced echo** — a phrase repeated softer. The whole dynamic vocabulary.

## What to avoid

- **Contrapuntal density.** Three voices, clearly laid out. Bach's intricacy is
  two generations later.
- **Irregular phrase lengths.**
- **Remote modulation.** Diatonic with secondary dominants inside sequences; the
  keys visited are the closely related ones.
- **Varying a sequence.** Exact transposition is the style.
- **Keyboard figuration.** This is string writing — no Alberti, no broken-chord
  accompaniment patterns.
- **Hairpins.** Terraced dynamics only.
