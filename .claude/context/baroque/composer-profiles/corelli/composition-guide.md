# Corelli — Composition Guide

## Fingerprints
Any section claiming Corelli's style needs ≥3 of these 5 present.

1. **Sequence as the engine of continuation** — A one- or two-bar figure is restated a step lower (occasionally a step higher) two or three times, usually over a circle-of-fifths bass. This is how a Corelli phrase gets from its opening to its cadence, and it is the most recognisable thing about the style. Four or more repetitions become mechanical; two or three is the norm.
2. **The suspension chain** — Consecutive 7–6 or 4–3 suspensions between the two upper parts, each resolution preparing the next dissonance. Often welded onto the sequence above, so harmony and dissonance drive forward together.
3. **A walking bass that is a real line** — The bass moves in steady crotchets or quavers with its own contour and its own cadences, not as chord roots. Under the sequences it walks by fifths; under the cadences it steps.
4. **Two equal upper voices crossing and exchanging** — The trio-sonata texture: the violins are peers, trade the leading part, cross freely, and move in parallel thirds and sixths for long stretches. Neither is an accompaniment to the other.
5. **Clear, unhurried cadential punctuation** — Phrases end in unambiguous perfect or half cadences with a 4–3 suspension in the penultimate chord, and the harmonic rhythm broadens as they approach. The form is audible because the cadences are.

---

## Texture and scale

Corelli's writing is spare by later Baroque standards: two melodic lines and a bass, with the harmony implied by the figured bass rather than filled in. Resist thickening it. The interest is in the interaction of three lines, not in the number of notes.

Slow movements are sustained and suspension-rich; fast movements are sequential and driven by the walking bass. A movement rarely changes character partway — the contrast is between movements.
