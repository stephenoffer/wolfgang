# Corelli — Formal Approach

Corelli standardised two genres so thoroughly that everyone after him inherits
his plan: the sonata da chiesa and the sonata da camera, plus the concerto grosso
that grows out of them.

---

## The two sonata plans

| Genre | Movement plan | Character |
|-------|---------------|-----------|
| Sonata da chiesa | Slow – Fast (fugal) – Slow – Fast | Church; abstract, contrapuntal |
| Sonata da camera | Prelude then dance movements | Chamber; Allemanda, Corrente, Sarabanda, Giga |
| Concerto grosso | Same four-movement frame, concertino against ripieno | The alternation IS the form |

Note that "sonata" here is a multi-movement plan, not the single-movement sonata
form of the Classical period — there is no exposition, no development, no
recapitulation.

---

## Inside a movement

| Movement type | Shape | Articulation |
|---------------|-------|--------------|
| Opening slow | Continuous suspension chain over a walking bass | Cadences on V then home |
| Fugal allegro | Exposition of subject in each voice, episodes on sequences | Entries mark the sections |
| Second slow | Often a binary aria, or a Phrygian-cadence link | May not close in the tonic |
| Final fast | Binary dance form, both halves repeated | Double bar in the middle |

**Binary form** governs every dance movement: first half moves tonic to dominant
(or to the relative major in minor), second half works back. Both halves repeat.
That two-part shape, not ternary, is the Baroque default.

---

## Concerto grosso alternation

| Block | Scoring | Function |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Ripieno | Full strings + continuo | Statement, weight, the tutti frame |
| Concertino | Two violins + cello + continuo | Elaboration, sequence, dialogue |
| Overlap | Concertino continues under an entering ripieno | Keeps the seam from showing |

Sections run 4–16 bars and are rarely symmetrical. The alternation is the
structural rhythm of the movement.
