# Bach Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `bach-lh-vocabulary.md`, which says the essential thing: Bach has no
accompaniment, only lines. This catalogue is what those lines *do*.

The organising fact: **a Bach piece is one idea worked out.** A subject, a motif,
a figure — stated, imitated, inverted, sequenced, and carried through every key
the piece visits. A piece with several unrelated ideas is not in this style
however Baroque its harmony.

## Contrapuntal devices

1. **Imitation at the octave or fifth** — a voice states the subject, another
   follows one or two bars later. The basic Baroque texture, and the thing most
   often missing from generated writing.

2. **Inversion** — the same shape upside down: every rising interval falls by the
   same amount. Used for the answer, for an episode, or for a later entry.

3. **Augmentation and diminution** — the subject in double or half values.
   Augmentation at a final entry is a standard climax device.

4. **Stretto** — entries overlapping more closely than the subject's length, so
   the voices pile up. Reserved for the end of a fugue; it is the intensification.

5. **Countersubject** — a fixed second line that accompanies the subject every
   time it appears. Invertible: it must work above *or* below.

6. **Sequence** — a one- or two-bar unit transposed exactly, three or four times,
   usually falling by step or by fifth. The engine of every episode.

7. **Pedal point** — the bass locks on the dominant while the upper voices work
   above it, for four to sixteen bars before a final cadence.

8. **Voice-count change as punctuation** — four voices thin to two for an episode
   and return for the next entry. This is Baroque dynamics.

## Melodic and harmonic devices

9. **Suspension chain** — a held note becomes dissonant when the bass moves, falls
   by step, is held again. Chains of four or six are ordinary. Baroque expression
   lives here; a texture with no suspensions is bland however correct.

10. **Circle of fifths** — the bass falling a fifth and rising a fourth for six or
    eight chords. Combined with a sequence in the upper voices it will fill any
    episode.

11. **Chromatic fourth (the lament)** — a descending line covering a fourth by
    semitones: `A3 Ab3 G3 Gb3 F3 E3`. Used for grief, and often as a ground bass.

12. **Compound melody** — a single line implying two voices by alternating
    registers: `G3s D4s F3s D4s E3s D4s`. One hand, two audible parts. This is
    how a solo violin or a keyboard line implies harmony.

13. **Written-out ornamentation** — the melodic line decorated in real note values
    rather than with symbols, especially in slow movements.

14. **Neapolitan sixth and the diminished seventh** at moments of intensity,
    usually approaching a cadence in a minor key.

15. **Picardy third** — a minor-key piece ending on a major tonic chord. The
    period default for a final cadence.

16. **Modulation through the relative and dominant keys**, returning through the
    subdominant. Bach's tonal plan is systematic, not exploratory.

## Structural devices

17. **Ritornello** — a fixed passage returning in different keys between episodes.
    The concerto and many arias are built on it.

18. **Binary form with a repeat** — two halves, the first modulating to the
    dominant, the second returning. The dance-suite default.

19. **The final entry in augmentation over a pedal** — the standard fugal
    peroration: subject slowed to double values, bass locked on the dominant.

20. **Written-out cadenza before the final cadence** — a free flourish, often
    over a held chord, in the preludes and concertos.

21. **Terraced dynamics** — the level changes at a structural point and holds. Not
    hairpins, which are a later notation.

## What to avoid

- **A melody with accompaniment.** The single commonest failure.
- **Ideas that do not come back.** Everything derives from the subject.
- **Homophony throughout.** Correct only for a chorale.
- **Hairpins and crescendos.** Anachronistic notation.
- **No suspensions.** The expressive vocabulary is missing.
- **Alberti bass.** A Classical invention, a generation later.
