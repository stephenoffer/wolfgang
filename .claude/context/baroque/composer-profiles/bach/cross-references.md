# Bach — Cross-References & Influences

## Position in the Tradition

Bach stands at the terminus of the Baroque polyphonic tradition — the final synthesis of German, Italian, and French styles into a single contrapuntal language. He was not an innovator of forms (he invented nothing) but the perfector who absorbed every technique available and pushed each to its logical extreme. His music looks backward (Renaissance polyphony, Buxtehude, Pachelbel) while inadvertently creating the harmonic vocabulary that Haydn and Mozart would inherit.

## Who Influenced Bach

| Source | What Was Absorbed | Where You Hear It |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Buxtehude | Free organ fantasy, dramatic toccata style | Toccata & Fugue in D minor BWV 565 |
| Vivaldi | Ritornello form, driving rhythm, concerto structure | Brandenburg Concertos; transcriptions BWV 972–987 |
| Corelli | Trio sonata texture, walking bass, sequential harmony | Sonatas for violin & continuo |
| Frescobaldi | Chromatic ricercar, toccata figuration | Chromatic Fantasy BWV 903 |
| Pachelbel | Chorale-based organ writing, clear diatonic harmony | Organ chorales, early cantatas |
| Couperin/French clavecinistes | Suite dance forms, ornament vocabulary, style galant | French Suites, English Suites, Overture BWV 831 |
| Palestrina (via Fux) | Strict voice-leading, species counterpoint | Art of Fugue, Mass in B minor stile antico |
| Lully | French overture (slow–fast), dotted rhythms | Orchestral Suite openings BWV 1066–1069 |

## Comparative Table: Baroque Masters

| Dimension | Bach | Handel | Vivaldi |
|-----------|------|--------|---------|
| **Primary texture** | Polyphonic — 3–4 independent voices | Homophonic with polyphonic buildup | Melody + bass; thin inner parts |
| **Melody source** | Generated by counterpoint; Fortspinnung (spinning forward) | Self-contained theme; stated in 4 bars, singable | Violin-derived figuration; arpeggiated, sequential |
| **Harmonic complexity** | Dense chromaticism; secondary dominants, dim7, aug6 | Direct functional; chromatic only for drama | Simple I-IV-V; pedal points, sudden minor shifts |
| **Bass line role** | Second melody — equally important, stepwise, active | Harmonic foundation — supports melody, less independent | Pedal or walking pattern — rhythmic anchor, not melodic |
| **Rhythm** | Continuous motion; no rests until structural arrivals | Dramatic contrast; silence as gesture (General Pause) | Driving ostinato; relentless eighth-note pulse |
| **Form** | Fugue, invention, ritornello, binary suite | Da capo aria, French overture, concerto grosso | Ritornello concerto (solo vs. tutti alternation) |
| **Orchestration** | Functional — instruments serve the counterpoint | Festive — brass and drums for grandeur, choral mass | Idiomatic — violin writing shapes everything |
| **Purpose** | Sacred/intellectual: music as theological architecture | Public/dramatic: music as theater for mass audiences | Virtuosic/pedagogical: music as brilliant display |
| **Sequence use** | Developmental engine — modulates through key areas | Builds dramatic momentum toward climax | Structural filler — extends episodes between ritornellos |
| **Cadential gesture** | Trill on penultimate note; bass V→I | Hemiola regrouping; grand unison arrival | Strong tutti punctuation; clear V→I with full orchestra |

## Key Contrasts

### Bach vs. Handel: Counterpoint as Architecture vs. Drama

Bach builds counterpoint as the *substance* of music — every voice equally important, the structure existing in the conversation between lines. Handel uses counterpoint as a *device* — fugal entries build tension toward a homophonic climax where all voices unite.

**Bach — fugue entry as permanent texture (WTC I, C minor):**
```abc
X:1
T:Bach Fugal Texture
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:Cm
V:1
V:2 clef=bass
[V:1] G2AB c2d2 e2d2 c2B2|A2B2 c2A2 F2G2 A2F2|
[V:2] z16|G,2A,B, C2D2 E2D2 C2B,2|
```
Voice 2 enters with the same subject — both remain independent throughout. The counterpoint IS the music.

**Handel — fugal entry building to homophonic climax (Messiah, "And He shall purify"):**
```abc
X:2
T:Handel Fugue-to-Homophony
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:G
V:1
V:2
[V:1] B2B2 A2G2|d4 d2z2|[B2d2][B2d2] [A2c2][G2B2]|[G4B4d4] z4|
[V:2] z8|G2G2 F2E2|G,4 G,4|G,4 z4|
```
The independent entries converge into unison rhythm — the arrival IS the point.

### Bach vs. Vivaldi: Internal Complexity vs. External Brilliance

Bach absorbed Vivaldi's ritornello form but filled it with counterpoint. Where Vivaldi's inner parts double or sustain, Bach gives every part melodic independence.

**Vivaldi — solo over static accompaniment:**
```abc
X:3
T:Vivaldi Solo Episode
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:Am
V:1 name="Solo"
V:2 name="Ripieno"
[V:1] A2c2e2a2 c'2a2e2c2|B2d2f2b2 d'2b2f2d2|
[V:2] A,4A,4 A,4A,4|E,4E,4 E,4E,4|
```
Accompaniment repeats one note per bar — all attention on the soloist.

**Bach — same concerto texture, but bass is a countermelody:**
```abc
X:4
T:Bach Concerto Texture
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:Am
V:1 name="Solo"
V:2 name="Bass" clef=bass
[V:1] A2c2e2a2 c'2a2e2c2|B2d2^g2b2 d'2b2e2d2|
[V:2] A,2B,2C2D2 E2F2G2A2|^G,2A,2B,2C2 D2E2F2^G2|
```
Bass traces its own ascending line — a second melody, not accompaniment.
