# Bach Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Bach has no accompaniment. That single fact is what generated "Bach" gets wrong:
it writes a tune and puts something underneath it. In Bach the lower part is
*another line* — it has its own melodic shape, its own rhythm, and it is as
interesting as the upper part. If you could not play the left hand alone and have
it sound like music, it is not this style.

Shorthand reminder: each hand's bar sums to the meter. Two-voice-per-hand writing
uses `//`; three and four voices use the numbered inner voices. Bach needs them.

## LH idiom catalog (adapt pitches to the harmony)

1. **Walking bass** — steady quarters or eighths stepping and leaping through the
   harmony with a shape of its own: `C2q D2q E2q F2q` / `C2e G2e E2e C2e G1e B1e
   C2e D2e`. The default in the chorale-derived and continuo textures. It should
   move mostly by step with an occasional leap of a fourth or fifth to a new
   harmony.

2. **Imitation of the right hand** — the left hand states the subject a bar or two
   after the right, usually at the octave or the fifth below. This is the single
   most characteristic Baroque texture and it is nearly absent from generated
   writing. If the piece has a subject, the bass must state it.

3. **Running sixteenths in one voice** — a continuous line, the same rhythmic
   value throughout, changing direction and register as the harmony moves:
   `C2s D2s E2s F2s G2s A2s B2s C3s …`. The invention and prelude engine. Its
   *continuity* is the point; a rest inside it is an event.

4. **Broken-chord figuration** — `C2s G2s E3s G2s C2s G2s E3s G2s`. The C major
   prelude shape. Note it is written as one unbroken stream, not as bass-plus-
   chord: the harmony is *implied* by the figure rather than stated under it.

5. **Pedal point** — the bass holds one note, usually the dominant, for four to
   sixteen bars while everything above changes: `G1w` repeated, or `G1w~` tied.
   Bach's standard preparation for a final cadence.

6. **Suspension chain in an inner voice** — a held note becomes a dissonance when
   the bass moves, then falls by step, then is held again. Written with ties:
   `F3h~ F3q E3q`. This is where Baroque expression lives, and a texture with no
   suspensions sounds bland however correct the chords are.

7. **Two independent voices in the left hand** — a held tenor over a moving bass,
   written with `//`: `C2h G2h // E3q F3q E3q D3q`. Real three- and four-voice
   writing needs this; a single-stream left hand cannot do it.

8. **Sequence** — the same figure transposed down a fifth or up a fourth, two to
   four times. `C2e E2e G2e C3e` then `F1e A1e C2e F2e` then `B1e D2e F2e B2e`.
   Bach's principal method of continuation, and it should be *exact* transposition,
   not an approximation.

9. **Alberti** — does not exist here. It is a Classical invention. Using it makes
   the music mid-eighteenth-century at the earliest.

10. **Chordal, all voices together** — reserved for chorale texture and for
    cadential arrivals: `[C2,C3,E3,G3]q`. Four real voices moving in the same
    rhythm, each singable.

11. **The bass rests while an inner voice takes over** — a texture change without
    a change of idiom. Bach thins from four voices to two and back constantly.

## Voice count as form

Bach's textures are described by *how many voices are sounding*, and the number
changes as an event. A fugue's exposition adds one voice per entry; an episode
drops to two; the final entry is often the fullest. Plan the voice count as an
arc and the form takes care of itself.

## Where the idiom changes

- **At an episode.** Fewer voices, sequential, usually derived from the subject's
  tail.
- **At a new entry.** The subject arrives in a new voice, and the others become
  countersubject or free.
- **At the final pedal.** The bass locks onto the dominant and the upper voices
  intensify above it.

## Breathing

Baroque breathing is not silence — it is a cadence with a change of voice count
immediately after. A texture that has been four-voice for sixteen bars and drops
to two is a paragraph break. Rests happen inside individual voices constantly
(that is how imitation works) but total silence is rare and dramatic.

## What to avoid

- **Melody-plus-accompaniment.** The commonest failure. If the lower part has no
  shape of its own, this is not Bach.
- **A bass that only outlines the chord.** Real Bach basses have contour: they
  rise and fall, they have peaks, they sequence.
- **Homophony everywhere.** If every voice moves in the same rhythm for a whole
  piece, it is a chorale — a legitimate form, but only one of them.
- **No suspensions.** A Baroque texture with no prepared dissonance is missing its
  expressive vocabulary entirely.
- **Dynamics and hairpins.** Bach did not write them. Terraced changes at
  structural points are the period's equivalent, and a page covered in crescendos
  is a nineteenth-century edition, not the music.
