# J.S. Bach — Formal Approach

Bach does not merely use forms — he inhabits them so completely that the form becomes inseparable from the musical thought. His fugues are not "pieces in fugue form"; they are acts of contrapuntal thinking that happen to crystallize as fugues. Every form Bach touches becomes a vehicle for systematic exploration of a single musical idea.

---

## Forms and Mastery

| Form | Mastery | Key Works | Bach's Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fugue | Definitive | WTC I & II, Art of Fugue, organ fugues | Exhaustive exploration of a single subject through every contrapuntal device |
| Suite | Masterful | French Suites, English Suites, Cello Suites | Dance movements elevated to concert art; character preserved but deepened |
| Chorale prelude | Definitive | Orgelbuchlein, Leipzig Chorales, Schubler | Hymn tune as cantus firmus with independent contrapuntal commentary |
| Concerto | Excellent | Brandenburgs, Violin Concertos | Ritornello form with motivic integration between tutti and solo |
| Cantata | Definitive | ~200 church cantatas | Multi-movement theological argument: chorus-recit-aria-chorale arc |
| Passion | Definitive | St. Matthew, St. John | Large-scale dramatic narrative with turba choruses, reflective arias |
| Mass | Masterful | B minor Mass | Encyclopedic synthesis of every choral technique |
| Invention | Unique | 2-part Inventions, 3-part Sinfonias | Pedagogical form that is simultaneously great art |
| Variation | Masterful | Goldberg Variations, chorale partitas | Theme as harmonic skeleton; each variation a new character piece |
| Sonata | Excellent | Solo Violin Sonatas, Flute Sonatas | Sonata da chiesa structure with fugal movements |
| Toccata | Masterful | Toccata & Fugue D minor, Toccata C major | Virtuosic freedom paired with strict fugue |

## Fugue: How Bach Inhabits It

The fugue is Bach's supreme form — not a template but a process of thinking. Each fugue is unique in how it deploys the universal techniques.

### Fugue Architecture

| Section | Function | Bach's Typical Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Exposition | All voices enter with subject + answer | Subject in tonic, answer at the fifth (tonal or real); countersubject established |
| Counter-exposition | Optional restatement in all voices | Used when the subject benefits from further establishment |
| Episode | Free development between subject entries | Built from subject fragments, sequences, invertible counterpoint |
| Middle entries | Subject in related keys | Relative major/minor, dominant, subdominant — systematically explored |
| Stretto | Subject overlaps with itself | Reserved for climactic passages; tightens the imitative distance |
| Final entry | Subject in home key | Often combined with augmentation, pedal point, or all subjects simultaneously |

### Contrapuntal Techniques Within Fugue

| Technique | When Bach Deploys It | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Invertible counterpoint | Episodes and double expositions | Voices swap registers — same material, new perspective |
| Stretto | Climax and final section | Compression creates intensity and inevitability |
| Augmentation | Late entries, often in bass | Broadens the subject, adds weight and grandeur |
| Diminution | Developmental passages | Quickens pace, adds energy |
| Mirror inversion | When subject shape permits | Subject upside-down — intellectual delight, fresh sound |
| Pedal point | Final bars, over tonic or dominant | Grounds the harmony while free voices spin above |
| Double/triple fugue | Grand movements (B minor Mass, Art of Fugue) | Multiple subjects combined — the ultimate demonstration of unity |

### ABC Example — Fugue Exposition (3 voices, C minor)

Subject enters in soprano, then alto at the fifth, then bass at the octave. The countersubject appears against each answer.

```abc
X:1
T:Bach Fugue Exposition — C minor (3 voices)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Cm
V:1 name="S"
V:2 name="A"
V:3 name="B" clef=bass
%% Bar 1-2: Soprano states subject alone
[V:1] c2 dc _e2 dc|B2 c2 G4|
[V:2] z8|z8|
[V:3] z8|z8|
%% Bar 3-4: Alto enters with answer at fifth; soprano has countersubject
[V:1] _e2 fg _a2 gf|e2 f2 c4|
[V:2] G2 AG _B2 AG|F2 G2 _E4|
[V:3] z8|z8|
%% Bar 5-6: Bass enters with subject at octave; upper voices continue freely
[V:1] g2 _af g2 fe|d2 _e2 c4|
[V:2] _e2 dc _e2 fg|_a2 g2 g4|
[V:3] C2 DC _E2 DC|B,2 C2 G,4|
```

## Suite: How Bach Inhabits It

Each suite movement is a character piece in binary form. Bach preserves the dance's rhythmic identity while elevating it with contrapuntal thinking and harmonic richness.

### Suite Movement Order and Character

| Movement | Meter | Tempo | Character | Bach's Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prelude | Various | Varies | Free, improvisatory | Only in English Suites and Partitas; often the largest movement |
| Allemande | 4/4 | Moderate | Flowing, serious | Continuous sixteenths, polyphonic texture, rich ornamentation |
| Courante | 3/4 or 3/2 | Fast | Running, lively | French (3/2, cross-rhythms) or Italian (3/4, running notes) |
| Sarabande | 3/4 | Slow | Noble, weighted | Emphasis on beat 2; most harmonically rich movement |
| Galanterie | Various | Varies | Lighter character | Minuet, Bourree, Gavotte, Passepied — one or two inserted here |
| Gigue | 6/8 or 12/8 | Fast | Energetic, fugal | Often opens with fugal imitation; compound meter drive |

### Binary Form in Bach's Dances

| Section | Key Motion | Content |
|---|---|---|
| A (first half) | Tonic to dominant (or relative major) | Opening material, establishes character |
| B (second half) | Dominant to exploration to return to tonic | Development of A material, often inverted; longer than A |

The B section is where Bach transforms: the opening idea appears in new keys, inverted, fragmented. The return to the home key feels earned, not automatic.

## Concerto: Ritornello Form

| Section | Function | Bach's Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Ritornello (tutti) | States main theme in full | Strong, rhythmically driving, modular — can be fragmented later |
| Solo episode | Virtuosic passage, develops motifs | Draws from ritornello material; sequences through new keys |
| Partial ritornello | Tutti returns with fragments | Shorter, in new key, often just the head motif |
| Final ritornello | Full return in home key | Complete statement, sometimes extended with coda |

Bach's ritornello themes are modular: they contain 3–4 segments that can appear independently. The Brandenburg Concertos demonstrate every possible concertino/ripieno combination.

## Cantata and Passion: Sacred Drama

### Cantata Movement Plan

| Position | Type | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening chorus | Grandest movement; chorale fantasia or fugal chorus |
| 2–3 | Recitative + Aria | Individual reflection on the Gospel text |
| 4 | Central chorus or duet | Dramatic turning point (optional) |
| 5–6 | Recitative + Aria | Further reflection, often contrasting affect |
| Final | Chorale | Congregational hymn; simple 4-part harmonization |

### Passion-Specific Elements

| Element | Function | Bach's Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Evangelist (tenor) | Narrates Gospel text | Secco recitative — urgent, speech-like |
| Jesus (bass) | Speaks Christ's words | Accompagnato recitative — sustained strings create a halo of sound |
| Turba chorus | Crowd scenes | Short, dramatic, rhythmically unified |
| Reflective aria | Emotional response to events | Da capo form; obbligato instrument mirrors the singer's emotion |
| Chorale | Congregational hymn in narrative | Familiar melody, rich harmonization; the listener's own voice |
| Opening/closing chorus | Frame the work | Monumental, multi-layered — St. Matthew opening is double chorus with chorale |

## ABC Example — Suite Gigue Opening (Fugal, D minor)

A gigue opening with fugal imitation — the subject enters in the treble, then the bass imitates at the fifth.

```abc
X:2
T:Bach Gigue Opening — Fugal Imitation in D minor
M:6/8
L:1/8
K:Dm
V:1
V:2 clef=bass
%% Treble states the gigue subject
[V:1] D2A AGF|E2^C D2F|A2d d^cB|A3 z3|
[V:2] z6|z6|z6|z6|
%% Bass enters with answer at the fifth; treble spins forward
[V:1] f2e dcB|A2G FED|^C2D E2F|G3 A3|
[V:2] A,2E, E,D,C,|B,,2^G,, A,,2D,|F,2A, AGF|E3 z3|
```

The gigue subject (D–A–A–G–F) leaps up a fifth then falls stepwise — a defining gesture that the bass imitates four bars later, transposed to A. The treble continues with new material derived from the subject's intervals.

## Variation Form: Goldberg Model

| Principle | How Bach Applies It |
|---|---|
| Bass line as skeleton | The aria's 32-bar bass line (not the melody) generates all 30 variations |
| Character variety | Each variation is a distinct genre: French overture, fughetta, aria, toccata |
| Canon series | Every third variation is a canon at increasing intervals (unison through ninth) |
| Architectural arc | Opens gently, builds through virtuosity, culminates in quodlibet, returns to aria |
| Unity in diversity | Wildly different surfaces share the same harmonic foundation |

## General Formal Principles

| Principle | How Bach Embodies It |
|---|---|
| Organic unity | Every element derives from the opening material |
| Systematic exploration | All contrapuntal possibilities of a subject are methodically exhausted |
| Proportion | Sections relate in balanced (often golden-ratio) proportions |
| Cadential hierarchy | Half cadences for mid-phrase, authentic cadences for section ends, deceptive cadences to extend |
| Tonal architecture | Key areas are structurally planned — modulation serves formal function, not just color |
| Elision | Phrase endings overlap with new beginnings — forward momentum maintained |
