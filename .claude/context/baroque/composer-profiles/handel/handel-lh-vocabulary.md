# Handel Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Handel's bass is a **continuo** bass. It is the harmonic floor of a public,
gestural music, and its job is to be clear, strong and unhurried — not to be
clever. Where Bach's lower parts are lines in a contrapuntal argument, Handel's
are a foundation that lets the upper voices declaim. Generated "Handel" usually
sounds like thin Bach; the difference is that Handel is *simpler and grander*.

Shorthand reminder: each hand's bar sums to the meter. Two voices in a hand use
`//`.

## LH idiom catalog (adapt pitches to the harmony)

1. **Steady continuo quarters** — `D2q A2q D3q A2q`. Root and fifth alternating,
   one chord per bar or per half bar. The default, and it should sound
   unhurried.

2. **Walking eighths** — `D2e E2e F#2e G2e A2e B2e C#3e D3e`. Drives an aria or
   a fast movement; mostly stepwise with an occasional leap to the next harmony.

3. **Repeated notes on one pitch** — `D2e ×8`. Insistent, and used under a
   sequence where the harmony above is doing the moving.

4. **Dotted-rhythm bass (the French overture)** — `D2dq D2e A2dq A2e`. Stately
   and ceremonial. Paired with a dotted upper part; this is the single most
   recognisable Handel gesture and it belongs at openings.

5. **Descending tetrachord / ground bass** — `D2h C2h Bb1h A1h`, repeated as an
   ostinato under changing upper material. The lament bass. Handel builds whole
   movements on one.

6. **Octave leaps between root and its octave** — `D2q D3q D2q D3q`. Solid and
   plain; gives weight without motion.

7. **Sustained pedal on the dominant** — `A1w` held for four or eight bars while
   the upper parts intensify. Handel's standard preparation for a final cadence
   and for a chorus entry.

8. **Sequential descent by fifths** — the bass falling a fourth and rising a
   second: `D2q G1q E2q A1q F#2q B1q`. The circle-of-fifths engine, and Handel
   uses it plainly and often.

9. **Unison with the upper parts** — everything on the same line, in octaves, for
   a declamatory opening or a sudden fortissimo. Bare and public.

10. **Silence under a solo entry** — the bass drops out entirely for a phrase so
    a voice or a solo instrument can be heard alone, then re-enters at the
    cadence. Common in the arias and almost never generated.

11. **Broken octaves for agitation** — `D2e D3e D2e D3e`. The storm and rage
    idiom; heavier and slower than a Classical tremolo.

## The Handelian shape

- **Terraced dynamics.** No hairpins. The level changes at a structural point and
  stays. A whole section is `f`, the next is `p`.
- **Repeated sequential blocks.** A four-bar idea stated, then repeated a step or
  a third lower, then a third time. Handel builds by repetition and sequence far
  more than by development.
- **The grand pause.** Everything stops before the final cadence of a chorus.
- **Long dominant preparation.** Ten bars over a pedal is not excessive.

## Where the idiom changes

- **Between the ritornello and the solo.** Full bass in the tutti; thinner, or
  absent, under the voice.
- **At a new key area.** Usually to a different bass idiom entirely.
- **At the final cadence.** To sustained notes, or to silence before it.

## Breathing

Handel's bass rests at every structural join, and it rests under solo entries.
Silence in the bass is how the texture opens up; a continuo that never stops
makes an aria sound like a machine.

## What to avoid

- **Contrapuntal busyness.** If the bass is as intricate as the top, this is
  Bach's texture, not Handel's.
- **Hairpins and crescendos.** Anachronistic. Terrace instead.
- **Constant harmonic motion.** Handel prolongs; a single harmony can hold for
  two or four bars while the upper parts move.
- **Alberti.** Later than this style by a generation.
