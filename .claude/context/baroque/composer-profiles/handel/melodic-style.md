# G.F. Handel — Melodic Style

Handel's melodies are public speeches set to music. They announce themselves, they are understood on first hearing, and they are remembered afterward. Where Bach's melodies generate material through continuous development — a spinning wheel that never stops — Handel's melodies are statements. They arrive, they land, they breathe. This is not a limitation but a mastery of a different kind: the art of the memorable.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Why It Matters for Composition |
|---------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| Vocal orientation | Every melody sings naturally, even in instrumental works | Write it as if a voice will perform it — test by humming |
| Immediate memorability | Theme states itself completely in 4 bars | If a listener cannot recall the theme after one hearing, it is not Handelian |
| Broad arching phrases | Long lines (4-8 bars) with clear peak and cadence | The phrase has a shape: rise, crest, descend, land |
| Diatonic emphasis | Mostly stepwise with strategic leaps | The leaps are events, not texture; steps are the connective tissue |
| Triadic motion | Frequent chord-outlining (arpeggiated) figures | The melody implies its own harmony — no ambiguity |
| Syllabic clarity | Designed around text rhythm, even in instrumental works | One note per syllable for declamation; melisma reserved for key words |
| Repeated notes | Declamatory effect for emphasis | Repetition is rhetoric: "Hallelujah, Hallelujah, Hallelujah" |
| Sequence as extension | Sequences lengthen phrases without losing identity | The melody moves forward by repeating its shape at new pitch levels |
| Ornamental da capo | A section return expected to be elaborated | The bones are the same; the flesh is richer |
| Text painting | Melodic shape mirrors word meaning | "Rise" ascends, "fall" descends, "run" melismatizes — the melody IS the text |

## Interval Preferences

| Interval | Usage | Frequency | Emotional Quality |
|----------|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Steps (2nds) | Primary motion, lyrical flow | Very high | Smooth, singing, connected |
| 3rds | Triadic motion, warm character | High | Warmth, consonance, ease |
| 4ths (ascending) | Opening leaps, strength gestures | Moderate | Confidence, horn-call quality |
| 5ths (ascending) | Heroic leaps, fanfare openings | Moderate | Grandeur, open-air quality, triumph |
| 6ths (ascending) | Expressive yearning | Moderate | Tenderness, longing, reaching upward |
| Octave | Dramatic emphasis | Moderate | Power, declaration, "listen to me" |
| 7ths+ | Extreme drama only | Low | Reserve for rage arias, extraordinary moments |

### Opening Interval as Character Signal

| Opening Interval | What It Signals | Example Context |
|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Ascending 5th | Heroic, triumphant, festive | "See the conqu'ring hero comes" |
| Ascending 4th | Confident, horn-like, noble | Fanfare themes, ceremonial works |
| Ascending 6th | Yearning, expressive, tender | Love arias, devotional passages |
| Descending step | Gentle, pastoral, sighing | Siciliano melodies, laments |
| Repeated note | Declamatory, urgent, rhetorical | Choral openings, text declamation |
| Ascending octave | Dramatic, commanding | Rage arias, royal proclamations |

## Aria Melody Types

The da capo aria was Handel's primary vehicle, and each affect demanded its own melodic vocabulary.

| Aria Type | Melodic Character | Meter | Intervals | Rhythm | Typical Range |
|-----------|------------------|-------|-----------|--------|---------------|
| Rage/Fury | Wide leaps, fast scalar runs, angular | 4/4 | 7ths, octaves, rapid steps | Driving sixteenths, syncopation | Wide (12th+) |
| Lament | Descending contour, chromatic inflections | 3/4 | Steps, descending 3rds | Slow, sighing, dotted | Narrow (octave) |
| Pastoral | Lilting, gentle, stepwise | 6/8, 12/8 | Steps, 3rds | Dotted compound rhythms | Moderate (10th) |
| Triumphant | Fanfare figures, dotted rhythms, triadic | 4/4 | 4ths, 5ths, triadic | Dotted, martial | Wide (12th) |
| Love/Tender | Smooth, flowing, stepwise with expressive leaps | 3/4 | Steps, 3rds, one 6th leap | Even, legato | Moderate (10th) |
| Bravura | Virtuosic runs, rapid coloratura passages | 4/4 | Scalar runs, arpeggios | Extended sixteenth melismas | Very wide (2 octaves) |

### Rage Aria Melody

```abc
X:1
T:Handel Rage Aria — Angular Leaps and Runs
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:Dm
%% Wide leaps announce fury; scalar runs intensify it
D4 z4 A4 z4|d8 c4B4|A4 d4 f4 a4|d'8 z8|
%% The octave leap (D to d) and the ascending run are characteristic
```

### Pastoral Melody (Siciliano)

```abc
X:2
T:Handel Pastoral — Lilting Siciliano
M:12/8
L:1/8
K:Bb
%% Gentle compound meter; steps and 3rds; the melody breathes
d3 c2B A3 G2F|B3 A2G F3 E2D|
C3 D2E F3 G2A|B6 z6|
%% Notice: the melody descends, rests, then rises — an exhale and inhale
```

### Triumphant/Declamatory Theme

```abc
X:3
T:Handel Declamatory Theme — Repeated Notes into Fanfare
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:D
%% Repeated notes build momentum; triadic leap releases it
D2D2 D2D2|D4 F2A2|d4 d2c2|d6 z2|
A2A2 A2A2|A4 d2d2|c2B2 A2G2|F6 z2|
%% The repeated D is rhetorical: "hear this, hear this" — then the leap to F-A-d
```

### Vocal-Style Lyrical Melody

```abc
X:4
T:Handel Lyrical Vocal Melody
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:G
%% Arching line: rises, peaks, descends, cadences — the complete gesture
d4 B2|A4 G2|E2 G2 A2|B6|
d4 e2|d2 c2 B2|A2 B2 c2|d6|
B4 A2|G4 F2|E2 D2 E2|G6|
A4 B2|A2 G2 ^F2|G6-|G6|
%% Peak at bar 6 (e); cadence at bar 8 (d); final resolution at bar 16 (G)
```

## Phrase Construction

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| 4-bar units | The default building block; most themes are 4 bars that can combine into 8 |
| Clear cadences | Every phrase ends with a recognizable cadence — no open-ended spinning |
| Antecedent-consequent | First phrase ends on V (question); second phrase ends on I (answer) |
| Sequential extension | When a phrase needs to be longer, Handel sequences the material at new pitch levels |
| Breathing room | Rests between phrases; the melody does not continuously generate like Bach |
| Peak placement | Melodic peak typically in the third quarter of the phrase — tension before resolution |

### Phrase Shape Diagram

| Bar | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|-----|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contour | Rising | Continuing rise | PEAK | Descent | Rising again | Peak 2 | Descent | CADENCE |
| Tension | Low | Building | High | Releasing | Rebuilding | High | Releasing | Resolution |
| Harmony | I | IV or vi | ii or IV | V (HC) | I | IV | V(sus) | I (PAC) |

## Choral Melody Writing

Handel's choral melodies serve different purposes than his aria melodies.

| Context | Melodic Approach | Key Difference from Aria |
|---------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Homophonic chorus | Simple, declamatory, rhythmically unanimous | Melody is shared by all voices; clarity over beauty |
| Fugal chorus subject | Short, rhythmically distinctive, easy to track | Must be recognizable when 4 voices overlap |
| Unison passage | Bold, wide-ranging, forceful | All voices on one line — the combined weight IS the effect |
| Soprano lead (SATB) | Aria-like melody, but simpler to support harmonization | Inner voices fill; soprano carries the tune |

## Melodic Ornamentation

| Context | Ornament Types | Density | Purpose |
|---------|---------------|---------|---------|
| First statement of theme | Minimal — trill at cadence | Low | Let the melody speak for itself |
| Da capo return (A') | Trills, turns, additional runs, grace notes | High | The singer/player "owns" the theme now |
| Slow movement peaks | Turns on long notes, appoggiatura | Moderate | Expressiveness without obscuring the line |
| Cadential arrivals | Trill from upper note | Always | Baroque convention; signals structural arrival |

## Contrast with Bach's Melody

| Aspect | Handel | Bach |
|--------|--------|------|
| Primary character | Vocal, singable, memorable | Instrumental, motivic, generative |
| Phrase structure | Clear 4-bar units with cadences | Continuous spinning, phrase elision |
| Ornamentation | Structural (cadences, da capo) | Pervasive (written-out decoration) |
| Sequence use | Extends phrases, builds momentum | Develops motifs, creates architecture |
| Leaps | Strategic, for dramatic effect | Frequent, part of continuous texture |
| Text relationship | Direct painting, syllabic | Dense, melismatic, theologically layered |
| Breathing room | Rests between phrases | Minimal rests, continuous flow |
