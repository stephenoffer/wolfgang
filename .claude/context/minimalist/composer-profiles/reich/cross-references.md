# Steve Reich — Cross-References

## Reich vs. Other Minimalists

### Reich vs. Philip Glass

| Dimension | Reich | Glass |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Core technique | Phasing, interlocking patterns | Additive/subtractive process, arpeggiation |
| Texture | Interlocking rhythmic parts | Layered arpeggiated chords |
| Harmony | Diatonic stasis (1–2 chords) | Modal cycling (3–4 chords) |
| Melody | Emergent from pattern interaction | Arpeggio-derived |
| Ensemble | Percussion-centered; mallet instruments | Keyboard-centered |
| Opera | 2 documentary video operas | 25+ narrative operas |
| Film | Minimal | Extensive (50+ films) |
| Rhythm | Primary interest; complex, interlocking | Steady, grouped, additive |
| Cultural root | West African drumming, Balinese gamelan | Indian classical music |
| Training | Juilliard, Mills (Berio) | Juilliard, Paris (Boulanger) |

### Reich vs. Arvo Pärt

| Dimension | Reich | Pärt |
|-----------|-------|------|
| Rhythm | Hyper-rhythmic; constant pulse | Non-rhythmic; uniform note values |
| Harmony | Diatonic, simple, stable | Single triad (absolute stasis) |
| Silence | Almost never; pulse continuous | Structural element equal to sound |
| Texture | Dense interlocking (6–18 parts) | Transparent two-voice (M + T) |
| Emotional tone | Kinetic, communal, energetic | Still, devotional, interior |
| Process | Phasing, gradual addition | Stepwise additive expansion |
| Cultural root | African/Balinese rhythm | Medieval European sacred music |

### Reich vs. Terry Riley

| Dimension | Reich | Riley |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Notation | Fully notated; exact | Instruction-based; performer choice |
| Process | Predetermined, strict | Open, improvised |
| Ensemble | Fixed forces, rehearsed | Flexible, sometimes solo |
| Duration | Determined (15–90 min) | Open-ended (potentially hours) |
| Drone | Not used | Central (tape drone) |
| Indian influence | Secondary (African primary) | Primary (raga, drone, improvisation) |

## African and Balinese Influence

### West African (Ewe/Ghana)

| Ewe Element | Reich Equivalent | Example Work |
|------------|------------------|--------------|
| Master drum patterns | Fixed rhythmic cells | *Drumming* |
| Interlocking kagan/kidi/sogo | Interlocking mallet/piano parts | All ensemble works |
| Bell timeline | Anchor pattern | *Clapping Music* |
| 3-against-2 polyrhythm | Cross-rhythm from phase offset | *Piano Phase* |
| Call-and-response | Gradual addition (one voice at a time) | *Music for 18 Musicians* |

### Balinese (Gamelan)

| Gamelan Element | Reich Equivalent |
|----------------|------------------|
| Kotekan (interlocking parts) | Interlocking mallet patterns |
| Metallic timbres (bronze) | Vibraphone, glockenspiel, crotales |
| Colotomic structure (gong cycles) | Cue patterns for section changes |
| Layered speeds (fast/medium/slow) | Multiple tempo layers in *Drumming* |

```abc
X:1
T:Gamelan Influence — Interlocking Metallic Patterns

L:1/16

V:2 name="Vibraphone 2 (kotekan sangsih)"
z C z E z G z C z E z G z C z E |

```

## Reich and Technology

| Technology | Usage | Period |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Tape loops | Phasing discovery; *It's Gonna Rain*, *Come Out* | 1965–66 |
| Multi-track recording | *Electric Counterpoint* series (solo + pre-recorded) | 1982–2003 |
| Sampling | Speech samples in *Different Trains*, *City Life* | 1988–95 |
| Video | *The Cave*, *Three Tales* (video operas) | 1993–2002 |
| Digital | Modern recording integration | 2000+ |

## Reich vs. John Adams (Post-Minimalism)

| Dimension | Reich | Adams |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Harmony | Diatonic stasis | Full tonal vocabulary; functional |
| Melody | Emergent, not composed | Composed, lyrical, developmental |
| Rhythm | Process-driven, mechanical | Pulse-based but flexible |
| Orchestra | Ensemble-focused; late orchestral | Full symphonic from early career |
| Form | Process-as-form | Adapted Classical forms |
| Emotion | Process creates emotion | Directly expressive |
| Relationship | Adams = "the next generation" of minimalism | Adams studied/admired Reich |

## Influence on Electronic Music

| Genre | Reich's Influence |
|-------|------------------|
| Ambient techno | Repetitive loops, gradual evolution (The Orb, Aphex Twin) |
| IDM | Phase-like processes, pattern manipulation (Autechre) |
| Post-rock | Interlocking guitar patterns (Tortoise, Battles) |
| DJ culture | Loop-based music, layering, gradual build |
| Sampling/hip-hop | *Different Trains* technique prefigures found-sound |
| Generative music | Eno cites Reich; process-based composition algorithms |

## Reich in Context of 20th-Century Music

| Movement | Reich's Relationship |
|----------|---------------------|
| Serialism | Studied with Berio; rejected serial method |
| Cage/indeterminacy | Rejected — Reich insists on precise notation |
| Jazz (Coltrane) | Admires modal jazz; rhythmic energy influence |
| World music | Active study (Ghana, Bali); authentic engagement |
| Academic avant-garde | Rejected; "uptown" establishment was the enemy |
| Electronic music | Pioneer (tape pieces); influenced entire genre |
| Post-minimalism | Adams, Muhly, Dessner descended from Reich |

## Comparative Process Techniques

| Composer | Process | Audibility |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Reich | Phasing, gradual addition | Perceptible — you hear it happening |
| Glass | Additive note-count | Perceptible — you count the notes |
| Pärt | Stepwise expansion | Perceptible — you follow the steps |
| Riley | Modular improvisation | Semi-perceptible — performer choice |
| Young | Sustained duration | Perceptible — you hear the drone evolve |

```abc

% Three minimalist textures side by side
```
