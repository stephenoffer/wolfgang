# Steve Reich — Index

**Era:** Minimalist | **Nationality:** American | **born 1936**

**Key Works:** Piano Phase, Violin Phase, It's Gonna Rain, Come Out, Music for 18 Musicians, Tehillim, Different Trains, The Cave (documentary opera), City Life, Electric Counterpoint, Drumming

**Style Summary:**
- The process composer: "I am interested in perceptible processes. I want to be able to hear the process happening throughout the sounding music." The process IS the music — not a compositional device but the content itself.
- Phasing: two instruments play the identical pattern, but one gradually moves out of phase with the other, cycling through all possible rhythmic relationships. The constantly shifting rhythmic patterns emerge from a single simple pattern.
- Interlocking rhythmic canons: multiple performers play complementary patterns that fill in all rhythmic space together. Each part alone is sparse; together they are dense.
- Constant eighth-note pulse: a relentless stream of even eighth notes, with melody emerging from selective accenting within the stream. The melody is the emphasis, not a separate layer.
- Simple diatonic harmony: where Glass uses modal cycling, Reich is often pure C major (no chromatic inflection). The harmony is deliberately uninteresting — all complexity is rhythmic.
