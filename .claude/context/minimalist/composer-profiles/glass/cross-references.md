# Philip Glass — Cross-References

## Glass vs. Other Minimalists

### Glass vs. Steve Reich

| Dimension | Glass | Reich |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Core technique | Additive/subtractive process | Phasing, gradual process |
| Texture | Arpeggiated keyboard, layered | Interlocking rhythmic patterns |
| Harmony | Modal cycling (3–4 chords) | Diatonic stasis (1–2 chords) |
| Melody | Chord-derived arpeggio patterns | Emergent from selective accenting |
| Rhythm | Constant stream; grouped additively | Constant pulse; phased canons |
| Ensemble | Keyboards + winds + voice | Percussion + keyboards + voice |
| Opera/theater | 25+ operas; primary genre | 2 video operas; secondary |
| Film scoring | Extensive (50+ films) | Minimal film work |
| Training | Boulanger (Paris) | Juilliard, Mills College |
| Cultural influence | Indian music (Ravi Shankar) | West African drumming, Balinese gamelan |
| Emotional tone | Warm, accessible, hypnotic | Energetic, precise, communal |
| Personal rivalry | "He's a nice enough fellow, but..." | "Phil and I don't have much in common" |

### Glass vs. Arvo Pärt

| Dimension | Glass | Pärt |
|-----------|-------|------|
| Repetition | Hypnotic; dissolves time | Devotional; deepens spiritual attention |
| Harmony | Modal cycling | Single triad (total stasis) |
| Silence | Almost never — pulse continues | Structural element equal to sound |
| Melody | Arpeggio patterns | Stepwise M-voice |
| Dynamics | mp–mf steady | pp–mp; almost always soft |
| Spiritual intent | Secular (Buddhist influence) | Sacred (Orthodox Christian) |
| Process | Additive note-count | Additive step-count |

### Glass vs. Terry Riley

| Dimension | Glass | Riley |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Notation | Fully notated; precise | Often improvised; instruction-based |
| Ensemble | Fixed instrumentation | Flexible; solo or any group |
| Harmony | Modal cycling, fixed chord sets | Modal, drone-based, improvisatory |
| Process | Systematic, predetermined | Intuitive, performer-directed |
| Duration | Determined by score | Open-ended |
| Influence on Glass | Riley's *In C* (1964) was a catalyst | |

## The Boulanger Lineage

| Student | What Boulanger Taught | How It Appears |
|---------|----------------------|----------------|
| Glass | Rigorous counterpoint, clarity, formal discipline | Clean part-writing within repetitive textures |
| Copland | Orchestration, open intervals, formal clarity | Spacious American orchestral sound |
| Carter | Rhythmic complexity, polyrhythm | Metric modulation (opposite direction from Glass) |
| Piazzolla | Counterpoint, formal structure | Tango + classical formal rigor |

**Boulanger's mark on Glass:** The counterpoint training ensures that Glass's simple patterns are always precisely voiced. The intervals are clean; the doublings are deliberate. This rigor distinguishes Glass from lesser minimalist imitators.

## Glass and Film Scoring

### Glass vs. John Williams (Film)

| Dimension | Glass | Williams |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Orchestral style | Process-driven, repetitive | Post-Romantic, developmental |
| Leitmotif | Repeating patterns per scene | Character-specific melodic tags |
| Harmony | Modal cycling, simple | Full tonal range, chromatic |
| Emotional approach | State-based (creates mood) | Narrative (follows story) |
| Orchestration | Keyboard-centered + strings | Full symphonic with brass prominence |

### Glass vs. Hans Zimmer (Film)

| Dimension | Glass | Zimmer |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Electronics | Minimal; keyboard-acoustic | Central; hybrid electronic-orchestral |
| Ostinato | Arpeggiated patterns | Rhythmic power-chord patterns |
| Harmonic motion | Modal cycling | Drone + minimal changes |
| Dynamic range | Narrow (mp–mf) | Extreme (pp–fff) |
| Scale | Intimate to moderate | Massive, cinematic |

## Influence on Later Music

| Domain | Glass's Influence |
|--------|------------------|
| Film scoring | Arpeggiated piano + strings scoring style (The Hours → every indie drama) |
| Electronic music | Repetitive structures, cycling patterns (Aphex Twin, Boards of Canada) |
| Indie rock | Repetitive guitar arpeggios (Radiohead, Sigur Rós) |
| Neo-classical | Piano arpeggiation technique (Nils Frahm, Ólafur Arnalds, Ludovico Einaudi) |
| Video game music | Cycling modal harmony for ambient exploration |
| Theater/dance | Music as continuous texture beneath performance |

## Glass in Context of 20th-Century Music

| Movement | Glass's Relationship |
|----------|---------------------|
| Serialism | Explicitly rejected; "I threw all that out" |
| Cage/Fluxus | Parallel NYC scene; shared anti-establishment stance, different methods |
| Pop/rock | Admires; collaborated with Bowie, Eno, Aphex Twin |
| Jazz | Not a primary influence; some collaboration (Marsalis) |
| World music | Indian classical as foundational influence |
| Neo-Romanticism | Later works share accessibility; lacks Romantic expression |
| Post-minimalism | Adams, Muhly, Dessner descended from Glass |
