# Philip Glass — Biography

## Life Timeline

| Year | Event | Musical Impact |
|------|-------|----------------|
| 1937 | Born in Baltimore, Maryland | Father's record shop: early exposure to all genres |
| 1952 | Enters University of Chicago (age 15) | Studies math and philosophy; structural thinking |
| 1958 | Enters Juilliard (composition) | Studies with William Bergsma, Vincent Persichetti |
| 1964 | Studies with Nadia Boulanger in Paris | Rigorous counterpoint training; Boulanger's clarity |
| 1965–66 | Assists Ravi Shankar in Paris (film transcription) | **Discovers Indian rhythmic additive structure** |
| 1967 | Returns to New York; forms Philip Glass Ensemble | Keyboards, winds, soprano voice: the Glass sound |
| 1968 | *Music in Fifths*, *Music in Similar Motion* | Early stripped-down minimalist process pieces |
| 1976 | *Einstein on the Beach* (with Robert Wilson) | Opera breakthrough; 5 hours, no plot, additive structures |
| 1980 | *Satyagraha* (opera on Gandhi) | Second opera: more tonal, Sanskrit text |
| 1983 | *Akhnaten* (opera) | Completes "Portrait Trilogy"; most tonal of three |
| 1982 | *Glassworks* | First crossover album; popular accessibility |
| 1982 | *Koyaanisqatsi* (Godfrey Reggio film) | Film scoring career begins |
| 1988 | *Metamorphosis* (solo piano) | Most intimate piano works |
| 1992 | *Low Symphony* (from Bowie/Eno album) | Symphonic treatment of pop material |
| 2002 | *The Hours* (film score) | Oscar nomination; piano-driven film scoring |
| 2012–17 | Symphonies 9–12 | Late symphonic period; orchestral expansion |

## Formative Influences

| Influence | What Glass Absorbed | How It Appears |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| Nadia Boulanger | Rigorous voice-leading, clarity of line, form | Clean part-writing even in repetitive textures |
| Ravi Shankar | Additive rhythmic structure (Indian tala) | Additive process: 3+3+3 → 3+3+3+4 → 3+3+3+4+5 |
| Alla Rakha (tabla) | Cyclic rhythmic patterns, subdivision mastery | Constant eighth-note pulse with grouped subdivisions |
| Samuel Beckett | Repetition as dramatic structure, minimalist theater | Repetition as content, not decoration |
| Sol LeWitt, Richard Serra | Visual art minimalism: simple rules, complex results | Process as art; the system generates the work |

## The Philip Glass Ensemble

| Instrument | Role | Personnel (classic lineup) |
|-----------|------|---------------------------|
| Electric keyboards (2–3) | Primary harmonic/melodic texture | Glass himself on keyboards |
| Soprano voice | Floating melody above keyboards | Joan La Barbara (early), others |
| Saxophones/flutes | Melodic doubling, color | Various woodwind players |
| Sound engineer | Live mixing as instrument | Kurt Munkacsi |

The ensemble IS the Glass sound: amplified keyboards creating a wall of arpeggiated triads, with wind instruments and voice floating above.

## Cultural Context

| Context | Impact |
|---------|--------|
| 1960s New York art scene | Cross-pollination: visual art, theater, dance, music |
| Indian classical music encounter | Rhythmic thinking replaces Western harmonic thinking |
| Boulanger's rigor | Clean technique prevents minimalism from becoming sloppy |
| Downtown vs. Uptown NYC | Glass firmly "downtown": populist, accessible, anti-academic |
| Opera as total art form | Glass sees opera as primary vehicle; 25+ operas |
