# Arvo Pärt — Cross-References

## Pärt vs. Other Minimalists

### Pärt vs. Philip Glass

| Dimension | Pärt | Glass |
|-----------|------|-------|
| Repetition purpose | Spiritual meditation; each repetition deepens | Hypnotic trance; repetition dissolves time |
| Harmonic content | Single triad, no changes | Modal cycling, 3–4 chords rotating |
| Melody | Stepwise M-voice, never leaps | Arpeggiated patterns, chord-derived |
| Texture | Two voices (M + T), transparent | Dense arpeggiated keyboard, layered |
| Dynamics | pp–mp; soft almost always | mp–mf steady; mechanical consistency |
| Silence | Structural element, equal to sound | Absent; the pulse never stops |
| Process | Additive/subtractive stepwise expansion | Additive/subtractive note-count expansion |
| Cultural root | Orthodox/medieval Christian | Indian classical music (Ravi Shankar) |
| Emotional tone | Devotional, grief, luminous | Energetic, meditative, propulsive |

### Pärt vs. Steve Reich

| Dimension | Pärt | Reich |
|-----------|------|-------|
| Core technique | Tintinnabuli (2-voice system) | Phasing (2 identical patterns out of sync) |
| Rhythm | Non-rhythmic; note values uniform or text-driven | Hyper-rhythmic; constant pulse is the content |
| Harmony | One triad, no change | Diatonic, simple but more varied than Pärt |
| Texture | Transparent, sparse (2–3 voices) | Dense interlocking patterns (6–18 players) |
| Silence | Prominent structural role | Almost never; pulse is continuous |
| Process visibility | Audible: you hear the stepwise addition | Audible: you hear the phase shift happening |
| Ensemble | Strings, choir, organ, piano | Percussion, keyboards, winds, voices |
| Cultural root | Medieval European sacred music | West African drumming, Balinese gamelan |
| Emotional tone | Still, devotional, interior | Kinetic, energetic, communal |

### Pärt vs. La Monte Young

| Dimension | Pärt | Young |
|-----------|------|-------|
| Duration | 3–70 minutes | Hours to days |
| Pitch content | Diatonic scale + tonic triad | Single interval or drone |
| Tuning | Equal temperament (standard) | Just intonation (non-standard) |
| Sacred vs. secular | Explicitly sacred | Secular (Eastern philosophy) |
| Notation | Fully notated | Often improvised/instruction-based |

## Medieval and Early Music Connections

| Source | What Pärt Borrowed | How It Appears |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| Gregorian chant | Stepwise monophonic melody, modal scales | M-voice construction |
| Pérotin (Notre Dame) | Sustained tone + moving voice above | T-voice as sustained triadic presence |
| Ockeghem | Strict intervallic rules between voices | T-voice position rules (1st sup., 2nd sup., etc.) |
| Machaut (Ars Nova) | Isorhythm: fixed rhythm + fixed pitch series | Additive process as formal principle |
| Orthodox znamenny chant | Spiritual function of repetition | Ritual time-sense in tintinnabuli |

```abc
X:1
T:Pérotin Parallel — Sustained Tone + Moving Voice
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:Dm
%%staves {1 2}
V:1 name="Vox organalis (M-voice)"
D E F G | A G F E |
V:2 name="Vox principalis (drone/T)"
D4 | D4 |
% 12th-century organum and 20th-century tintinnabuli share this structure
```

## Sacred Music Context

| Composer | Shared With Pärt | Different From Pärt |
|----------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Górecki | Radical consonance, slow tempo, spiritual weight | More Romantic, emotional surges, larger dynamics |
| Tavener | Orthodox faith, single-chord stasis, choral focus | More ornate, Byzantine chant influence, richer texture |
| Messiaen | Catholic mysticism in music | Vastly more complex harmonically; birdsong, color |
| Hildegard von Bingen | Medieval monophony; spiritual melody as prayer | No systematic method; intuitive |

### The "Holy Minimalist" Group

| Composer | Relationship to Pärt |
|----------|---------------------|
| Górecki (*Symphony No. 3*) | Fellow traveler; both use consonance as spiritual act; Górecki more Romantic |
| Tavener (*The Protecting Veil*) | Fellow Orthodox-inspired; Tavener more decorative, less systematic |
| Pärt | Most austere of the three; strictest compositional system; least decoration |

## Influence on Later Music

| Domain | Pärt's Influence |
|--------|-----------------|
| Film scoring | Tintinnabuli pieces used in 100+ films; spawned "contemplative" scoring style |
| Ambient/electronic | Brian Eno, Stars of the Lid cite Pärt; drone + simplicity aesthetic |
| Neo-classical piano | Max Richter, Ólafur Arnalds, Nils Frahm echo Pärt's reductive approach |
| Sacred music | Revived interest in liturgical composition among contemporary composers |
| Sound art | Concept of silence-as-music influenced installation art |

```abc
X:2
T:Pärt's Influence — Neo-Classical Piano Style (Richter/Arnalds-like)
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:Am
%%staves {1 2}
V:1 name="Melody (M-voice derived)"
E D C | B, A, z |
V:2 name="Arpeggiated triad (T-voice derived)"
A,CE | A,CE |
% The neo-classical piano genre inherits Pärt's M+T structure with added arpeggiation
```

## Comparative Harmonic Language

| Composer | Chord Vocabulary | Harmonic Motion |
|----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Pärt | 1 triad | None (stasis) |
| Glass | 3–4 modal chords | Cycling (no resolution) |
| Reich | 1–2 diatonic chords | Static or very slow |
| Young | 1 interval/drone | None (stasis) |
| Riley | Modal scale over drone | Improvised cycling |
| Adams | Full tonal vocabulary | Functional but slow |

## Pärt in Context of 20th-Century Music

| Movement | Pärt's Relationship |
|----------|-------------------|
| Serialism (Schoenberg, Webern) | Studied, used (Period 1), then explicitly rejected |
| Darmstadt avant-garde (Boulez, Stockhausen) | Never adopted; too cerebral |
| Minimalism (Reich, Glass, Riley) | Parallel development; different cultural roots |
| Neo-Romanticism (Górecki, Penderecki late) | Shares consonance, rejects Romantic expression |
| Spectralism (Grisey, Murail) | No direct connection, but shared interest in resonance |
| New Complexity (Ferneyhough) | Diametrically opposed — maximum vs. minimum complexity |

```abc
X:3
T:Comparative Textures — Three Minimalists
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Am
%%staves {1 2 3}
V:1 name="Pärt style (M+T)"
A,B,CD EDCB, |
V:2 name="Glass style (arpeggio)"
ACEA ACEA |
V:3 name="Reich style (interlocking)"
A,zCz EzCz |
% Three approaches to minimal material: stepwise, arpeggiated, interlocking
```
