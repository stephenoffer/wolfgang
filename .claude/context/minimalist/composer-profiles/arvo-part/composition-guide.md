# Arvo Pärt — Composition Guide

## Fingerprints
Any section claiming Pärt's style needs ≥3 of these 5 present.

1. **Tintinnabuli — two-voice system** — Every piece uses two simultaneous voices: a **T-voice** (tintinnabuli voice) that arpeggiates only the notes of a single triad (the tonic triad, in any order), AND an **M-voice** (melodic voice) that moves stepwise in the diatonic scale. The two voices move independently — the T-voice rings like a bell while the M-voice steps. The relationship between them IS the harmony.
2. **Stepwise diatonic movement only** — The melodic voice (M-voice) moves only by step. No leaps in the melody. The entire musical content is generated by where the melodic voice starts and how it moves through the scale — up or down, and for how many steps.
3. **Silence as structural material** — Pärt uses silence (complete silence, no instrument playing) as a structural event equal in weight to any note. A bar of silence between phrases IS a phrase. The silences are not rests — they are the music breathing.
4. **Extreme simplicity as transcendence** — The surface is maximally simple: one scale, one triad, stepwise motion. The depth comes from the relationship between the two voices and the spiritual attention brought to each note. Complexity would destroy it — the simplicity IS the spiritual practice.
5. **Single tonal center, no modulation** — A Pärt tintinnabuli piece stays in one key, built on one triad, throughout. There is no development, no modulation, no harmonic tension-and-release. The piece exists in a single tonal space and deepens within it.

## The Tintinnabuli "Peak" — Different from All Other Styles

In Pärt's world, "peak" is not loudness or register height. **The peak is the moment of greatest register extremity combined with silence** — either the silence before the highest note, or the silence after it. The tension is not harmonic; it is positional. The M-voice has climbed as far as it will go in this piece, and then it is surrounded by space.

**Design the peak before writing bar 1:**

1. **Choose the highest note the M-voice will reach.** This is the piece's ceiling — the M-voice will not go higher anywhere. Place this note with care. It should feel like a point of stillness, not a climax.
2. **Place silence immediately before or after the peak.** A full bar of rest before the highest note = anticipation. A full bar of rest after it = resonance.
3. **Decide the T-voice's register at the peak.** At the highest M-voice note, the T-voice should be in its lowest register — this creates the widest interval gap, which IS the expressive moment.
4. **Then work backward.** What is the stepwise path that brings the M-voice to this note? How many bars of ascent? How many descent bars after? This is the shape of the entire piece.

**The approach trajectory (in bars):**
- Bars 1–N/3: M-voice ascends by step from the home pitch, T-voice in middle register, both pp
- Bars N/3–2N/3: M-voice reaches highest point, T-voice drops to bass register → **this is the peak**
- Silence (one full bar rest)
- Bars 2N/3–N: M-voice descends back toward home, T-voice returns to middle register, both pp or ppp

**After the peak:** The descent is not an anticlimax — it is the resolution. The music breathes out. The T-voice stays in the bass as the M-voice descends, so the interval narrows back to the familiar opening sonority. This narrowing IS the conclusion.

## Pattern Directives

**Tintinnabuli texture:**
- Choose a home triad: e.g., A minor (A-C-E).
- **T-voice:** write only A, C, or E in any register, in any order. The T-voice arpeggiates the triad but doesn't follow a pattern — it can be A3-E4-C4-A4, or simply A3 repeated. The T-voice is NOT the melody — it is the bell resonance.
- **M-voice:** write stepwise motion in A natural minor (A-B-C-D-E-F-G-A). No leaps. The melody goes up for several notes, then down, then up — purely stepwise.
- Write both voices explicitly, note by note. The tintinnabuli style requires noting every note.

**Rhythm in tintinnabuli:**
- Often: note values are consistent (all quarter notes, or all eighth notes) — the rhythm is not the expressive parameter.
- Or: the note values follow the syllable lengths of a text (if setting text), giving an irregular but natural rhythm.
- Silences: write as explicit rests that last at least one bar.

**Dynamics:**
- Pärt's dynamic range: pp to mp. Never forte, almost never forte.
- Occasional single note at mf for structural weight, never an extended loud passage.
- The music is almost always soft. The spiritual gravity comes from the simplicity and the softness, not from volume.

**Text-setting (when applicable — Spiegel im Spiegel, Für Alina, Miserere):**
- The M-voice follows the rhythm of the spoken text (one note per syllable).
- The T-voice follows a separate rhythm (one note per beat, or sustained).
- The voices are independent rhythmically — they align on certain beats but not on others.

## Anti-patterns (what sounds wrong)

- **Harmonic complexity.** A Pärt piece with chord progressions, secondary dominants, or modulation is not Pärt — it's a mistake. The single triad is the only harmony allowed.
- **Melodic leaps.** The M-voice NEVER leaps. A melody that jumps a third is already wrong in tintinnabuli style.
- **Development or variation.** Pärt does not develop themes or vary them in the traditional sense. The piece begins with an idea and inhabits it completely, without change. There is no "development section."
- **Loud dynamics.** A Pärt piece at ff is wrong in all but the most specific contexts. The spirituality of the style lives in the quiet.
- **Complex rhythm.** Complex rhythmic patterns, syncopation, or cross-rhythms are antithetical to tintinnabuli. The rhythm should feel inevitable, not interesting.
- **Multiple tonal centers.** One triad. One key. Always.
- **Busy accompaniment at the peak.** At the moment of greatest register extremity, the T-voice must not be ornamented or active. It is a bell — it rings and sustains. If it is moving busily while the M-voice peaks, the peak is buried.
- **Peak without silence.** A tintinnabuli peak that is not surrounded by silence on at least one side is not a peak — it is just a high note.

## ShortScore Field Recommendations

**Tintinnabuli two-voice texture:**
- `rh` or `vln1` (M-voice): stepwise diatonic melody.
  ```json
  {"p": "A4", "d": "q"},
  {"p": "B4", "d": "q"},
  {"p": "C5", "d": "q"},
  {"p": "D5", "d": "q"}
  ```
- `lh` or `vln2` (T-voice): triad arpeggiation.
  ```json
  {"p": "E3", "d": "q"},
  {"p": "A3", "d": "q"},
  {"p": "C4", "d": "q"},
  {"p": "A3", "d": "q"}
  ```
- Both voices written note-by-note. No formula references.

**Peak measure — highest M-voice note + lowest T-voice register:**
```json
{
  "m": 18,
  "_feel": "Peak — the M-voice has arrived at its ceiling. The T-voice is in the bass. Silence follows. Hold the note.",
  "voices": {
    "vln1": [{"p": "E5", "d": "w", "dyn": "mp"}],
    "vc":   [{"p": "A2", "d": "w"}]
  }
}
```

**Silence as structural event:**
```json
{
  "m": 19,
  "_feel": "The silence after the peak — let it last its full duration. Do not rush the next phrase.",
  "voices": {
    "vln1": [{"p": "rest", "d": "w"}],
    "vc":   [{"p": "rest", "d": "w"}]
  }
}
```

**`_feel` annotations for tintinnabuli:**
```json
{"bar_num": 1,  "_feel": "Begin as if from silence — the first note is already happening, not beginning"}
{"bar_num": 8,  "_feel": "The ascent is nearly complete — one step left to the ceiling"}
{"bar_num": 18, "_feel": "Peak — the M-voice has arrived. The T-voice in the bass. Silence will follow."}
{"bar_num": 19, "_feel": "Structural silence — not a rest. The music is still present in the resonance."}
{"bar_num": 20, "_feel": "Descent begins — the same simplicity, but now returning home"}
{"bar_num": 32, "_feel": "Final cadence — the M-voice returns to its origin. The T-voice rests on the tonic."}
```

**Dynamics:**
- `"dyn": "pp"` at the beginning of every piece.
- Changes: only to `p` or `mp` for weight, returning immediately to `pp`.
- `"expr": "senza espressione"` — without expression, allowing the structure to speak.

**Register:**
- M-voice: comfortable, middle register (C4–C6 for soprano range, or similar for piano).
- T-voice at peak: an octave or two BELOW the M-voice's highest note. The wide gap creates the expressive distance.
- T-voice in normal phrases: can be interwoven or just below the M-voice.
