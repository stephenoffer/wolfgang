# Arvo Pärt — Biography

## Life Timeline

| Year | Event | Musical Impact |
|------|-------|----------------|
| 1935 | Born in Paide, Estonia | |
| 1957 | Enters Tallinn Conservatory (Heino Eller) | Trained in Soviet-approved idiom |
| 1960 | First film scores; early serial experiments | Collage technique mixing tonality + serialism |
| 1962 | *Nekrolog* — first Estonian twelve-tone work | Soviet authorities condemn it |
| 1964 | *Collage über B-A-C-H* | Juxtaposes Baroque quotation with serialism |
| 1968 | *Credo* — banned by Soviet authorities | The crisis: faith + serial technique irreconcilable |
| 1968–76 | **Eight years of silence** | Studies Gregorian chant, Notre Dame organum, Franco-Flemish polyphony |
| 1976 | *Für Alina* — first tintinnabuli piece | The breakthrough: two voices, one triad, stepwise motion |
| 1977 | *Fratres*, *Cantus*, *Tabula Rasa* | Tintinnabuli method fully realized |
| 1980 | Emigrates to Vienna, then Berlin | Exile from Soviet Estonia; ECM Records partnership |
| 1984 | *Te Deum* | Large-scale tintinnabuli choral work |
| 1989 | *Miserere* | Expansion of sacred choral language |
| 2000s | Becomes most-performed living composer | Global recognition; film soundtrack usage |
| 2010s | *Adam's Lament*, *Swansong* | Continued refinement of tintinnabuli method |

## The Silence Period (1968–1976) — Compositional Significance

| What He Studied | What He Extracted | How It Appears in Tintinnabuli |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| Gregorian chant | Stepwise motion, single line, modal purity | M-voice: purely stepwise diatonic melody |
| Notre Dame organum | Two voices moving against sustained tone | T-voice as sustained triadic presence |
| Machaut, Ockeghem | Strict contrapuntal rules, intervallic control | Rigid T-voice/M-voice relationship rules |
| Orthodox liturgy | Spiritual function of repetition, ritual time | Silence as structural event; non-developmental form |
| Bell acoustics | Overtone series of bells, resonance patterns | Tintinnabuli = "little bells"; triadic T-voice mimics bell partials |

## Three Creative Periods

| Period | Years | Characteristics | Key Works |
|--------|-------|-----------------|-----------|
| Soviet avant-garde | 1960–68 | Serialism, collage, neo-classical | *Nekrolog*, *Perpetuum Mobile*, *Collage über B-A-C-H*, *Credo* |
| Silence | 1968–76 | No public compositions; chant study | (none published) |
| Tintinnabuli | 1976–present | Two-voice system, sacred texts, radical simplicity | *Für Alina*, *Fratres*, *Tabula Rasa*, *Passio*, *Te Deum*, *Miserere* |

## Cultural Context

| Factor | Influence on Style |
|--------|-------------------|
| Soviet censorship | Drove him away from both approved-Soviet AND Western-serial idioms |
| Estonian Lutheran/Orthodox heritage | Sacred texts as primary compositional material |
| Cold War exile | Music as spiritual practice detached from political systems |
| ECM Records aesthetic | Crystalline recording quality shaped reception of tintinnabuli |
| Post-Vatican II sacred music crisis | Pärt offered a radical alternative: ancient + new simultaneously |
