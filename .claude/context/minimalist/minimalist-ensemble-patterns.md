# Minimalist Ensemble Patterns

Ensemble patterns for the Minimalist and Post-Minimalist period (c. 1960-present). These patterns appear in works by Reich, Glass, Part, Adams, Riley, and their contemporaries. Each entry includes a name, reference, ABC notation, usage context, and variation suggestions.

---

## Reich Phasing Techniques

### MN1. Phase Pattern (Two Voices Gradually Shifting)
**Ref:** Reich Piano Phase; Clapping Music
```abc
X:900
T:Minimalist Phase Pattern
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:C
V:V1 name="Piano I (fixed)" clef=treble
E4C4D4E4 | E4C4D4E4 | E4C4D4E4 | E4C4D4E4 |
V:V2 name="Piano II (shifting)" clef=treble
E4C4D4E4 | z2E4C4D4E2 | z4E4C4D4 | E2z2E4C4D2 |
```
**Context:** The signature minimalist technique; two identical patterns gradually desynchronize, creating emergent melodies and rhythms that exist in neither part alone.
**Variation:** Use longer patterns (12-16 notes) for richer interference; apply to pitched instruments for harmonic phasing; phase rhythm only (same pitch).

### MN2. Resultant Patterns from Phasing
**Ref:** Reich Drumming; Six Pianos
```abc
X:901
T:Minimalist Resultant Melody
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:C
V:V1 name="Marimba I" clef=treble
C4E4G4c4 | C4E4G4c4 |
V:V2 name="Marimba II (offset)" clef=treble
z4C4E4G4 | c4z4C4E4 |
V:Res name="Resultant (heard)" clef=treble
C4[CE]4[EG]4[Gc]4 | [cC]4E4[CG]4[Ec]4 |
```
**Context:** The emergent pattern created by two phasing voices — what the listener actually perceives. Performers may emphasize these resultant melodies.
**Variation:** Highlight resultant patterns with a third instrument; gradually bring resultant melodies to the foreground.

## Glass Arpeggiation

### MN3. Continuous Broken Chord Pattern
**Ref:** Glass Glassworks; Einstein on the Beach; Koyaanisqatsi
```abc
X:902
T:Minimalist Glass Arpeggiation
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:Am
V:Pno name="Piano/Keyboard" clef=treble
A,4C4E4A4 | A,4C4E4A4 | A,4D4F4A4 | A,4D4F4A4 |
V:Pno2 name="Piano LH" clef=bass
A,,4E,4A,,4E,4 | A,,4E,4A,,4E,4 | D,4A,,4D,4A,,4 | D,4A,,4D,4A,,4 |
```
**Context:** Hypnotic repetition with glacial harmonic change; the unchanging figuration pattern becomes a vehicle — harmony shifts beneath like tectonic plates.
**Variation:** Change the arpeggiation direction every 4-8 bars; add one note to the pattern per cycle (additive); layer multiple keyboards at different octaves.

### MN4. Additive Process (Glass)
**Ref:** Glass Music in Twelve Parts; 1+1
```abc
X:903
T:Minimalist Additive Process
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:C
V:V1 name="Keyboard" clef=treble
%% Cycle 1: 3 notes
C4E4G4 z4 | C4E4G4 z4 |
%% Cycle 2: 4 notes (add one)
C4E4G4c4 | C4E4G4c4 |
%% Cycle 3: 5 notes
C4D4E4G4 c4z4z4z4 |
%% Cycle 4: 6 notes (full)
C4D4E4G4 A4c4 |
```
**Context:** The pattern grows by adding one note per cycle; the listener perceives gradual expansion. Fundamental to Glass's compositional method.
**Variation:** Subtractive process (reverse — remove notes); add and subtract simultaneously in different voices; change which note is added.

## Part Tintinnabuli

### MN5. Tintinnabuli Texture (T-voice + M-voice)
**Ref:** Part Fur Alina; Spiegel im Spiegel; Fratres
```abc
X:904
T:Minimalist Tintinnabuli
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:Am
V:Tvoice name="T-voice (stepwise)" clef=treble
!pp! A B c | d e d | c B A | A2 z |
V:Mvoice name="M-voice (triad)" clef=treble
!pp! A E A | E A E | A E A | A2 z |
V:Bass name="Bass (pedal)" clef=bass
!pp! A,2 A, | A,2 A, | A,2 A, | A,2 z |
```
**Context:** The T-voice (tintinnabuli voice) moves stepwise; the M-voice sounds only notes of a single triad (A minor = A, C, E). The result is austere, bell-like, sacred.
**Variation:** M-voice selects nearest triad note above or below T-voice; alternate 1st and 2nd position; expand to multiple T-voices in canon.

### MN6. Tintinnabuli with Expanding Phrases
**Ref:** Part Cantus in Memory of Benjamin Britten; Tabula Rasa
```abc
X:905
T:Minimalist Expanding Tintinnabuli
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Am
V:V1 name="Voice 1 (expanding)" clef=treble
!pp! A2 z6 | A2B2 z4 | A2B2c2 z2 | A2B2c2d2 |
V:V2 name="Voice 2 (triad M-voice)" clef=treble
!pp! E2 z6 | E2A2 z4 | E2A2E2 z2 | E2A2E2A2 |
V:Bell name="Tubular Bell" clef=treble
A,4 z4 | A,4 z4 | A,4 z4 | A,4 z4 |
```
**Context:** Each phrase adds one note, creating an arch of expanding and contracting lines; the bell tolls the fundamental — time passing, a ritual unfolding.
**Variation:** Layer multiple voices at different speeds (Cantus technique); reverse the process (contracting); use descending instead of ascending scale.

## Adams Post-Minimalist Patterns

### MN7. Post-Minimalist Harmonic Richness
**Ref:** Adams Harmonielehre; Shaker Loops
```abc
X:906
T:Post-Minimalist Rich Arpeggiation
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:Em
V:Vn1 name="Violin I" clef=treble
!f! E4G4B4e4 | E4G4B4e4 | D4F4A4d4 | D4^G4B4d4 |
V:Vn2 name="Violin II" clef=treble
!f! B,4E4G4B4 | B,4E4G4B4 | A,4D4F4A4 | ^G,4B,4D4^G4 |
V:Vla name="Viola" clef=alto
!f! G,4B,4E4G4 | G,4B,4E4G4 | F,4A,4D4F4 | D,4^G,4B,4D4 |
V:Vc name="Cello" clef=bass
!f! E,4 B,,4 E,4 B,,4 | E,4 B,,4 E,4 B,,4 | D,4 A,,4 D,4 A,,4 | E,4 B,,4 E,4 B,,4 |
```
**Context:** Minimalist repetition meets Romantic harmonic motion; the figuration pattern is minimalist but the chord changes carry emotional weight and direction.
**Variation:** Accelerate the harmonic rhythm gradually; add brass chorale above the string figuration; crescendo into full orchestral tutti.

### MN8. Pulsing String Texture (Adams/Reich)
**Ref:** Adams Short Ride in a Fast Machine; Reich Different Trains
```abc
X:907
T:Post-Minimalist String Pulse
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:D
V:Vn1 name="Violin I (pulsing)" clef=treble
!mf! D4D4D4D4 | D4D4D4D4 | E4E4E4E4 | ^C4^C4^C4^C4 |
V:Vn2 name="Violin II (offset pulse)" clef=treble
!mf! z4 D4D4D4 | z4 D4D4D4 | z4 E4E4E4 | z4 ^C4^C4^C4 |
V:Vla name="Viola (counter-rhythm)" clef=alto
!mf! A,4 z4 A,4 z4 | A,4 z4 A,4 z4 | B,4 z4 B,4 z4 | A,4 z4 A,4 z4 |
V:Vc name="Cello (bass)" clef=bass
!mf! D,8 D,8 | D,8 D,8 | E,8 E,8 | A,,8 A,,8 |
```
**Context:** Propulsive, kinetic energy; the pulsing repeated notes create a sense of forward motion like a machine or locomotive.
**Variation:** Gradually add instruments to the pulse; shift the offset pattern; add a lyrical melody above the pulse texture.

## Repetition and Transformation

### MN9. Gradual Transformation (Pattern Morphing)
**Ref:** Reich Music for 18 Musicians; Riley In C
```abc
X:908
T:Minimalist Gradual Transformation
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
V:V1 name="Voice 1" clef=treble
%% Pattern 1
C2E2G2c2 | C2E2G2c2 |
%% Pattern 2 (one note changed)
C2E2A2c2 | C2E2A2c2 |
%% Pattern 3 (another note changed)
C2F2A2c2 | C2F2A2c2 |
%% Pattern 4 (fully transformed)
D2F2A2d2 | D2F2A2d2 |
```
**Context:** Over many repetitions, one note at a time changes; the listener barely notices each shift but the cumulative effect is a complete harmonic transformation.
**Variation:** Transform two parameters simultaneously (pitch + rhythm); use longer patterns for subtler change; apply to multiple voices asynchronously.

### MN10. Subtractive Process
**Ref:** Reich Clapping Music; Rzewski Coming Together
```abc
X:909
T:Minimalist Subtractive Process
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:C
V:V1 name="Voice" clef=treble
%% Full pattern
C4E4G4c4 |
%% Remove last note
C4E4G4 z4 |
%% Remove another
C4E4 z4 z4 |
%% Skeleton
C4 z4 z4 z4 |
```
**Context:** The inverse of additive process; the pattern gradually erodes, creating increasingly sparse texture — disappearance, dissolution.
**Variation:** Remove notes from the middle outward; combine additive in one voice with subtractive in another; replace removed notes with rests or new pitches.

## String Quartet Minimalism

### MN11. Minimalist String Quartet Texture
**Ref:** Reich Different Trains; Glass String Quartet No. 3
```abc
X:910
T:Minimalist String Quartet
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:D
V:Vn1 name="Violin I (arpeggiation)" clef=treble
!mf! D4F4A4d4 | D4F4A4d4 | D4G4B4d4 | D4G4B4d4 |
V:Vn2 name="Violin II (counter-arpeggio)" clef=treble
!mf! A4D4F4A4 | A4D4F4A4 | B4D4G4B4 | B4D4G4B4 |
V:Vla name="Viola (rhythmic)" clef=alto
!mf! D2D2 z2D2 D2D2 z2D2 | D2D2 z2D2 D2D2 z2D2 | G,2G,2 z2G,2 G,2G,2 z2G,2 | G,2G,2 z2G,2 G,2G,2 z2G,2 |
V:Vc name="Cello (bass pulse)" clef=bass
!mf! D,4 z4 D,4 z4 | D,4 z4 D,4 z4 | G,,4 z4 G,,4 z4 | G,,4 z4 G,,4 z4 |
```
**Context:** Standard minimalist quartet texture; interlocking arpeggiated patterns create a shimmering, perpetual-motion surface.
**Variation:** Phase the two violins against each other; add harmonics for ethereal quality; gradually increase bow pressure from pp to ff.

## Ensemble Synchronization

### MN12. Unison Pulse (Full Ensemble)
**Ref:** Adams Short Ride in a Fast Machine; Reich Music for Large Ensemble
```abc
X:911
T:Minimalist Unison Pulse
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Bb
V:Ww name="Woodwinds" clef=treble
!ff! B2B2B2B2 | B2B2B2B2 | c2c2c2c2 | B2B2B2B2 |
V:Br name="Brass" clef=treble
!ff! B,2B,2B,2B,2 | B,2B,2B,2B,2 | C2C2C2C2 | B,2B,2B,2B,2 |
V:Str name="Strings" clef=treble
!ff! [B,DF]2[B,DF]2[B,DF]2[B,DF]2 | [B,DF]2[B,DF]2[B,DF]2[B,DF]2 | [CEG]2[CEG]2[CEG]2[CEG]2 | [B,DF]2[B,DF]2[B,DF]2[B,DF]2 |
V:Perc name="Percussion"
!ff! B,,2B,,2B,,2B,,2 | B,,2B,,2B,,2B,,2 | B,,2B,,2B,,2B,,2 | B,,2B,,2B,,2B,,2 |
```
**Context:** Maximum rhythmic energy; the entire ensemble locked into the same pulse creates overwhelming physical impact — the audience feels it in their chest.
**Variation:** Add a single instrument playing a counter-rhythm; gradually desynchronize into polyrhythm; drop to a single section for contrast.

### MN13. Interlocking (Hocketing)
**Ref:** Reich Sextet; Music for Mallet Instruments
```abc
X:912
T:Minimalist Hocket Pattern
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:C
V:Mar1 name="Marimba I" clef=treble
!mf! C4 z4 E4 z4 | G4 z4 c4 z4 |
V:Mar2 name="Marimba II" clef=treble
!mf! z4 D4 z4 F4 | z4 A4 z4 B4 |
V:Vib name="Vibraphone" clef=treble
!mf! z8 z4 G4 | z4 E4 z8 |
```
**Context:** Notes of a melody are distributed across instruments; no single player has the tune — it exists only in the ensemble. Medieval technique reborn in minimalism.
**Variation:** Accelerate the hocket; use in pitched percussion vs strings; gradually consolidate hocketed melody into single instrument.
