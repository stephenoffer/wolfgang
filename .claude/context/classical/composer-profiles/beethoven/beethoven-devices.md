# Beethoven Idiomatic Device Catalog

Companion to `beethoven-lh-vocabulary.md`. These are the gestures that make a
passage sound like Beethoven rather than like well-behaved Classical music. Weave
several into a movement — but each in service of the argument, never as a
checklist.

The organising fact: Beethoven builds a whole movement out of a very small cell,
and the drama comes from what he does to it. A piece with a beautiful theme and no
development of it is not in this style however Classical the harmony.

## Melodic and rhythmic devices

1. **The tiny cell** — a motif of three or four notes, often only a rhythm.
   Everything grows from it. Write the cell first, then build the theme out of
   repetitions of it.

2. **Motivic fragmentation** — the theme is stated, then only its first half,
   then only its first bar, then only its rhythm. Progressive shortening builds
   tension better than anything else in the vocabulary.

3. **Sforzando on a weak beat** — `C5q:sfz` on beat 2 or 4, or off the beat
   entirely. Displaces the metre without changing it. One of the most reliable
   Beethoven fingerprints.

4. **Syncopation across the barline** — a note tied from the last beat of one bar
   into the next, so the downbeat is not attacked. Sustained over several bars it
   makes the metre genuinely ambiguous.

5. **Sudden dynamic contradiction** — `ff` followed immediately by `p` with no
   transition, or a long crescendo that arrives at `pp`. The second is the more
   characteristic and the more startling.

6. **Repeated-note insistence** — the same pitch struck three, four, six times.
   Not decoration; it is the argument refusing to move on.

7. **The long crescendo over a pedal** — eight to sixteen bars of intensification
   over an unchanging bass, arriving at a structural downbeat.

8. **Registral extremes juxtaposed** — a phrase in the deep bass answered
   immediately two octaves up, with nothing in between.

9. **Silence as a downbeat** — a general pause where the ear expects the strongest
   beat of the phrase. A whole bar of nothing, then the answer.

10. **Trill as an event, not an ornament** — a long trill sustained over several
    bars, usually before a return. In the late works it becomes a texture.

11. **Accelerating rhythmic values** — quarters, then eighths, then triplets, then
    sixteenths across a passage. Acceleration without a tempo change.

12. **The wrong-note accent** — a strong beat carrying a chromatic note that is
    not in the harmony, marked `sfz`, resolving afterwards.

## Harmonic devices

13. **Deceptive cadence, then a second attempt** — V goes to vi, the phrase
    starts its cadence again, and only the third attempt lands. Extends a close
    from two bars to eight.

14. **The flattened sixth as a shock** — ♭VI arriving in place of an expected
    chord, usually loud and usually at a structural point.

15. **Third-related keys** — moving to the mediant or submediant rather than the
    dominant. A whole second subject in the mediant is characteristic of the
    middle and late works.

16. **Diminished seventh as a pivot** — the ambiguous chord used to leave a key
    abruptly and arrive somewhere remote.

17. **Prolonged dominant preparation** — sixteen or more bars on the dominant
    before a recapitulation, so the return feels earned rather than scheduled.

18. **Neapolitan approach at a moment of pathos** — ♭II6 before the dominant, in
    a minor-key slow movement.

## Structural devices

19. **The false return** — the theme comes back in the wrong key or too early,
    and is corrected.

20. **Coda as a second development** — the coda is not a wind-down but the
    movement's real climax, longer than the exposition's closing group.

21. **Thematic transformation across movements** — the same cell appears in a
    later movement in a different character. In a multi-movement work this is
    what makes it one work.

22. **Written-out accelerando by note value** into a final cadence.

## What to avoid

- **A theme that is never developed.** The defining failure.
- **Even, unrelieved dynamics.** The range is `pp` to `ff` and the changes are
  often abrupt.
- **Regular four-bar phrases throughout.** Beethoven extends, elides and
  interrupts constantly.
- **Decorative ornamentation.** His ornaments are structural — a trill that lasts
  four bars, a turn that becomes a motif — not surface prettiness.
