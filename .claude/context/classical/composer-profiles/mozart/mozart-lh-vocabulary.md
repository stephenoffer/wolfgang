# Mozart Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

The #1 thing that makes Classical piano writing sound mechanical is a
**static bass note held under perpetual figuration, the same idiom every
bar**. Mozart never does this. His left hand is an active agent that
*changes idiom at phrase boundaries* and *thins out at cadences*. Use this
catalog. **Vary it — a phrase should use 2-3 different LH idioms, and
adjacent phrases should not share the same default.**

Shorthand reminder: each hand's bar sums to the meter (4 beats in 4/4).
A sustained pedal UNDER figuration = a full-bar first LH event then the
figure (e.g. `A2w [A2,E3,A3]e ...`). But do NOT default to that — it is
ONE idiom among many below, and the most overused.

## LH idiom catalog (4/4, adapt the pitches to your harmony)

1. **Alberti (proper)** — low-high-mid-high, the bass note RE-ARTICULATED,
   not a held drone: `A2e E3e C3e E3e A2e E3e C3e E3e`. Light, transparent.
   Best for lyrical themes. Don't run it for more than ~4 bars unbroken.

2. **Oom-pah (the Classical default, lighter than Alberti)** — bass on
   beats 1 & 3, chord on 2 & 4: `D2q [D3,F3,A3]q A2q [D3,F3,A3]q`. The
   single most idiomatic Classical accompaniment. Bass LEAPS between root
   and fifth; the off-beat chord is mid-register.

3. **Oom-pah-pah / waltz-feel in 4** — bass then two chords:
   `D2e [F3,A3]e [F3,A3]e ...`

4. **Broken / murky octaves (Sturm und Drang motor)** — the D-minor
   storm engine, alternating octave tremolo: `D2s D3s D2s D3s ...` or
   `D2e D3e D2e D3e D2e D3e D2e D3e`. Use for dramatic, driving passages —
   this is what K.466 uses, NOT static-pedal Alberti.

5. **Repeated chords (pulsing)** — `[D3,F3,A3]e` ×8, or quarter pulses.
   Gives weight without melodic figuration. Good under a busy RH.

6. **Walking / scalar bass** — quarter or eighth notes stepping through
   the harmony: `D2q E2q F2q G2q` — drives transitions and sequences.

7. **Sustained / held** — LH holds a chord or octave for the whole bar
   while the RH carries everything: `[D2,D3]w` or `[D3,A3,D4]w`. ESSENTIAL
   for breathing — use it where the RH is active or at a phrase peak.

8. **Unison / octave doubling with RH** — both hands strike the same
   line for a bare, dramatic tutti stroke (theme statements, climaxes):
   LH mirrors the RH an octave or two below.

9. **LH rest / drop-out** — the LH is SILENT for a beat or a bar (rest),
   letting the RH ring or breathe. Mozart uses silence constantly. A
   `rest` in the LH is not a bug.

10. **Two-voice / contrapuntal bass** — a real melodic line in the LH
    answering the RH (varied recaps, developments).

## Phrase breathing rules

- **Four-bar units.** Most Mozart phrases group as 2+2 or 4+4. The
  antecedent (first half) should end LIGHTER than the consequent —
  thin the texture, hold the LH, or rest.
- **Cadences breathe.** At a HC or PAC, do NOT keep the figuration motor
  running into and out of the cadence. Thin to chords or octaves; let a
  beat of relative silence frame the arrival. A "feminine" ending
  (resolution on a weak beat, then rest) is very Mozart.
- **Texture follows the melody inversely.** When the RH is busy
  (sixteenths, runs), the LH simplifies (held chord, oom-pah). When the
  RH holds a long note or rests, the LH can move (fill the gap). Never
  both maxed out every bar.
- **Reserve perpetual sixteenths.** Continuous sixteenth figuration is
  for specific brilliant/cadential/stormy moments, not the default. Most
  bars move in eighths or a mix.

## Right-hand reminders (lighter touch)

- **Leaps and arpeggios, not just scales.** If a phrase is >55% stepwise
  it sounds like an étude. Mozart leaps (3rds, 6ths, octaves), arpeggiates
  triads, and uses appoggiaturas that LEAP in and resolve by step.
- **Appoggiaturas on strong beats** resolving down by step — the Mozart
  "sigh." Put the dissonance ON the beat, resolve off it.
- **Rests and held notes** in the melody too — a tune that never stops
  isn't a tune.
- **Ornaments at arrivals**: trills/turns at cadences and phrase peaks,
  grace-note appoggiaturas leaning into strong beats.

## When the density gate pushes back

A deliberately thin bar (held LH, oom-pah, a rest) may trip the density
floor. That is EXPECTED for breathing. Waive it honestly:
`allow=[{'check':'density_low_lh','reason':'cadential breath — LH thins to
held octave to frame the HC, per Mozart phrase breathing'}]`. Do not
re-thicken a bar that is supposed to breathe just to satisfy the floor.
