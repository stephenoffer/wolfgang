# W.A. Mozart — Melodic Style

Mozart thinks vocally in every instrument. A violin line, a clarinet solo, a piano right hand — each is written as if a singer were performing it. The melody breathes at natural intervals, rises to peaks proportionate to the phrase, and cadences with the inevitability of a spoken sentence reaching its period. This vocal quality is what makes Mozart melodies sound "effortless" — they follow the logic of the human voice, which every listener unconsciously understands.

## Core Melodic Character

| Feature | Description | Artistic Function |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Vocal origin | Every melody could be sung by a competent soprano or tenor | Instant physical connection — the listener's body understands the phrase |
| Italianate cantabile | Long, lyrical lines derived from Italian opera aria tradition | Beauty of line as primary value; the melody IS the content |
| Phrase symmetry (with subversion) | 4+4 bar periods as default; occasional 4+5 or 4+6 extensions | Symmetry creates expectation; the subversion creates art |
| Dialogue between voices | Melody passed between instruments like operatic conversation | Each instrument is a character; the music is social, not monologic |
| Grace in ornamentation | Appoggiaturas, turns, trills as emotional inflection, not display | The ornament sighs, the ornament laughs — never merely decorates |
| Peak placement | Melodic climax typically at 3/4 of phrase length | Natural speech rhythm — the most important word comes near the end |
| Stepwise motion with strategic leaps | Predominantly conjunct; leaps reserved for expressive moments | A leap is an event: an octave leap = surprise, a sixth = yearning |

## Melodic Construction Principles

### The 8-Bar Period

The fundamental Mozartian unit: antecedent (4 bars, ends open) + consequent (4 bars, ends closed). The consequent begins like the antecedent but diverges to reach a PAC.

| Bar | Antecedent Function | Consequent Function |
|-----|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | Opening gesture — establishes direction, rhythm, character | Same as bar 1 (recognition) |
| 2 | Response — if bar 1 rose, bar 2 falls or continues | Same as bar 2 (familiarity) |
| 3 | Approach to cadence — melodic rise toward dominant | DIVERGENCE — different approach, heading toward PAC |
| 4 | Half cadence — melody on scale degree 2 or 5 (open) | PAC — melody on scale degree 1 (closed) |

### Intervallic Vocabulary

| Interval | Frequency | Expressive Meaning | Context |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| Step (2nd) | Very high | Singing, natural flow | Default melodic motion — the voice walks |
| Minor 3rd | High | Gentle, warm | Arpeggio fills, phrase connections |
| Major 3rd | Moderate | Bright, open | Theme openings, fanfare references |
| Perfect 4th | Moderate | Questioning, open | Upward at phrase starts ("Dove sono") |
| Perfect 5th | Low-moderate | Noble, horn-call | Theme openings in concertos |
| Minor 6th | Low | Yearning, ache | Expressive peaks — the interval of longing |
| Octave | Rare | Dramatic, commanding | Opera arias at emotional peaks; energetic allegro themes |
| Diminished intervals | Rare | Pain, urgency | G minor works, operatic distress |

```abc
X:1
T:Mozart Melodic Intervals — Stepwise Default with Expressive Leaps
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
%% Bars 1-2: stepwise (conjunct) — natural, flowing
E2F2 G2A2|G2F2 E4|
%% Bar 3: minor 6th leap (C to A) — yearning, the emotional peak
C2A2 G2F2|E8|
%% The leap in bar 3 is the expressive event; everything else frames it
```

## Melodic Types by Genre

| Genre | Melodic Character | Typical Shape | Example |
|-------|------------------|--------------|---------|
| Piano concerto, lyric theme | Singing, intimate, ornamental | Rise -> peak -> decorated descent | K.488 Adagio |
| Symphony, first theme | Energetic, rhythmically profiled, compact | Upward gesture -> continuation -> cadence | K.550 opening |
| String quartet, first theme | Conversational, motivic, flexible | Statement -> imitation -> response | K.387 opening |
| Opera buffa aria | Chattering, syllabic, fast | Repeated notes -> quick scale -> cadential flourish | Figaro "Non piu andrai" |
| Opera seria aria | Long-breathed, melismatic, soaring | Sustained note -> ornamental descent -> cadence | Idomeneo "Fuor del mar" |
| Slow movement | Deeply vocal, ornamental, Adagio cantabile | Simple melody -> increasingly ornate repetitions | K.467 Andante |
| Rondo/Finale | Dance-like, periodic, light | 8-bar balanced period, often in 2/4 or 6/8 | K.331 Rondo alla Turca |

## The Art of Melodic Dialogue

Mozart's textures are conversations, not monologues. Even when one instrument has "the melody," others respond, comment, or counter-argue.

| Dialogue Type | Texture | Operatic Equivalent |
|--------------|---------|-------------------|
| Call and response | Theme stated by one voice, answered by another | Recitative exchange |
| Overlapping entries | Second voice enters before first finishes | Ensemble — characters talking over each other |
| Parallel thirds/sixths | Two voices move together in consonant intervals | Duet — lovers in agreement |
| Contrary motion | Voices move in opposite directions | Argument — dramatic tension |
| Echo with variation | Second statement ornaments or alters the first | Character reflecting on what was just said |

```abc
X:2
T:Mozart Dialogue — Violin I and Violin II
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:G
V:V1 name="Vln I"
V:V2 name="Vln II"
%% Violin I states; Violin II answers — a conversation
[V:V1] B2d2 g2f2|e4 z4|z4 B2A2|G8|
[V:V2] z8|e2g2 b2a2|g4 z4|d2c2 B4|
%% Each instrument has its own phrase shape, own breathing — two characters, one scene
```

## Ornamentation as Expression

| Ornament | Mozart's Usage | Emotional Function |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Appoggiatura (leaning tone) | Very frequent — the most Mozartian ornament | A sigh: the dissonance leans into the resolution |
| Turn | Moderate — at phrase peaks and cadences | Grace, elegance — the melodic equivalent of a smile |
| Trill | At cadences, especially on penultimate note | Anticipation before resolution — the held breath before arrival |
| Grace note (acciaccatura) | Moderate — quick, light | Playfulness, wit — a flicked brushstroke |
| Slide (Schleifer) | Rare — slow movements | Tenderness — the approach is as important as the arrival |
| Written-out cadenza | Concerto cadenzas, fermata elaborations | The character speaks alone — the most personal moment |

```abc
X:3
T:Mozart Appoggiatura — The Sigh
M:3/4
L:1/8
K:F
%% The appoggiatura (A resolving to G, then E resolving to F) IS the melody
%% Without them, only bare chord tones remain — with them, the line breathes
A2G2 E2|F6|
%% A->G = appoggiatura on the 3rd; E->F = appoggiatura on the tonic
%% This is the Countess's sigh, Pamina's lament, the Clarinet Concerto's farewell
```

## Melodic Contour Archetypes

| Archetype | Shape | Where Used |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Rising-then-falling arc | Rise to peak then descent | Standard lyric theme (K.488 Adagio) |
| Repeated-note launch | Repeated notes then rise | Buffa themes, energetic allegros (K.550 mvt I) |
| Descending scale | Stepwise descent | Cadential approaches, closing themes |
| Arch with late peak | Gradual rise, late peak, quick descent | Passionate themes (K.466 Romance) |
| Oscillating | Rise-fall-rise-fall | Playful themes, minuets |
| Cascading sequence | Motif descending in sequence | Development episodes, transitions |

## Corpus Melody Statistics (6,987 bars, 18 sonatas, 69 movements)

### Melody Direction Distribution
| Direction | % of bars | Implication |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Static (no clear direction) | 40.5% | The most common state — melody hovers, oscillates, or repeats |
| Descending | 34.4% | More common than ascending — Mozart's melodies fall more than they rise |
| Ascending | 25.1% | The least common — rising arpeggios are the exception, not the rule |

**Key Insight**: The "rising arpeggio" opening is a minority case. Mozart's melodies are more often static or descending. A convincing Mozart melody should default to hovering or gently falling, with rising motion reserved for specific expressive moments (phrase launches, approach to peaks).

### Melody Density
- **Average**: 6.9 events/bar (RH)
- **Peak frequency**: 4-6 events/bar (44% of all bars)
- **Allegro movements**: 8-13 events/bar
- **Adagio movements**: 3-5 events/bar

### Ornamental Features
- **Grace notes**: Present in 9.7% of bars (~1 grace note every 10 bars)
- **Dotted rhythms**: Present in 21.2% of bars (~1 in 5 bars uses dotted figures)

### Key Mode Distribution
- **Major**: 86.1%
- **Minor**: 13.9%
Minor is the contrast color, not the norm. Use it for shadow, not as a default.

### Register
- **Mean MIDI pitch**: 73.7 (approximately D5)
- **Range**: MIDI 29-89 (centered in octave 5)

## Data-Driven Findings (from 69-movement corpus analysis, March 2026)

### Intervallic Motion (corpus-wide, 20 movements, ~22,000 intervals)
- Stepwise (2nds): 62% — the default melodic motion
- Small leaps (3rds): 19% — for arpeggio fills, phrase connections
- Medium leaps (4ths-5ths): 8% — for theme openings, horn-call references
- Large leaps (6ths+): 9% — reserved for expressive peaks and dramatic moments
- Octave: 2% — rare, for drama

**Implication**: Melody should be ~60% stepwise with ~20% 3rds and ~10% expressive leaps. If stepwise exceeds 80%, the melody is too scale-like. If leaps exceed 25%, it loses singability.

### Grace Note Distribution
- Mean: 0.254 per bar (1 grace note every ~4 bars)
- Range by work: 0.01/bar (K.457) to 0.90/bar (K.279)
- Earlier sonatas use MORE grace notes; mature sonatas use fewer but more strategically
- Most common placement: phrase entry (beat 1), before cadential notes

### Phrase Length
- Mean: 10.6 beats across corpus
- Range: 3.9 (short motivic fragments) to 36.9 (long development passages)
- Standard phrase: 8 beats (2 bars of 4/4) — the classic Mozart period unit
- Phrases MUST include rests at boundaries (rest ratio target: 16%)

### RH Dyad Analysis (parallel 3rds and 6ths)
- Parallel 3rds: 811 instances across 20 movements = ~40 per movement
- Parallel 6ths: 204 instances = ~10 per movement
- Most common context: cadential passages, closing themes, dialogue sections
- 3rds outnumber 6ths 4:1

### Register Data
- Mean register center: MIDI 66.6 (F#4/Gb4) — slightly above middle C
- Typical high: MIDI 87-89 (Eb6-F6)
- Typical low: MIDI 31-38 (G1-D2)
- Full range: ~52 semitones (over 4 octaves)
