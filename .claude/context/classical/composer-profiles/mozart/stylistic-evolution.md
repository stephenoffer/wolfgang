# W.A. Mozart — Stylistic Evolution

"Which Mozart?" matters enormously. The 8-year-old writing his first symphony in London and the 35-year-old composing the Requiem on his deathbed share a name, but their music inhabits different emotional universes. The evolution is not linear progress but a deepening — each phase retains everything that came before while adding new dimensions of complexity, darkness, and humanity.

## Period Overview

| Period | Years | Age | Character | Key Works |
|--------|-------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| Child Prodigy | 1762-73 | 6-17 | Brilliant imitation; absorbing every European style | Symphonies K.16-134, Bastien und Bastienne, Mitridate |
| Salzburg Maturation | 1773-81 | 17-25 | Personal voice emerging; Sturm und Drang influence; growing frustration | Violin Concertos, Sinfonia Concertante K.364, Idomeneo, Mass in C minor |
| Vienna Freelance Peak | 1781-86 | 25-30 | Supreme confidence; every genre mastered; psychological depth | Piano Concertos K.449-491, Figaro, "Haydn" Quartets |
| Final Period | 1787-91 | 31-35 | Darkness and light intertwined; contrapuntal density; farewell | Don Giovanni, last 3 Symphonies, Magic Flute, Clarinet Concerto, Requiem |

## Detailed Phase Comparison

### Melody

| Aspect | Child Prodigy (1762-73) | Salzburg (1773-81) | Vienna Peak (1781-86) | Final Period (1787-91) |
|--------|------------------------|-------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Source | J.C. Bach galant style | Italian opera + personal voice | Fully individual, operatically informed | Stripped, essential, sometimes stark |
| Phrase length | Regular 4+4 periods | 4+4 with occasional extensions | Flexible; 4+5, 4+6 subversions | Maximum flexibility; phrases breathe freely |
| Ornamentation | Conventional, surface | Expressive, purposeful | Integrated — ornament IS the melody | Spare — each ornament devastates |
| Chromatic content | Minimal | Emerging (b6, passing tones) | Characteristic (the "minor shadow") | Pervasive — chromaticism enters the DNA |

K:G

```

M:3/4
L:1/8
K:A
%% The clarinet concerto slow movement — every note is a farewell
c2B2A2|E6|F2E2D2|C6|

```

### Harmony

| Aspect | Child Prodigy | Salzburg | Vienna Peak | Final Period |
|--------|--------------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Chromaticism | Rare, decorative | Emerging (Sturm und Drang) | Surgical — one note changes everything | Structural — late chromatic language |
| Key relationships | Close keys only (I, V, vi) | Wider range; minor explored | Full tonal spectrum; Neapolitan, dim7 | Remote modulations; enharmonic |
| Minor mode | Uncommon | G minor emerges (K.183) | Shadow within major (Fingerprint #5) | Minor and major interpenetrate |
| Dissonance level | Minimal | Moderate (K.364 slow movement) | Controlled intensity (K.465 intro) | Extreme in context (Requiem, K.491) |

### Orchestration

| Aspect | Child Prodigy | Salzburg | Vienna Peak | Final Period |
|--------|--------------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Winds | Doubling strings | Beginning independence | Full characters; wind serenades | Maximum economy; each note counts |
| Clarinet | Not used | Encountered in Mannheim (1777) | Increasingly prominent | Central voice (K.622, K.581) |
| Texture | Thick, imitative of models | Cleaner, more personal | Transparent, chamber-like | Spare — silence is an instrument |
| String writing | Conventional | Sinfonia Concertante virtuosity | Quartet mastery; dialogue | Quintet depth (K.593, K.614) |

### Form

| Aspect | Child Prodigy | Salzburg | Vienna Peak | Final Period |
|--------|--------------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Sonata form | Textbook, balanced | Personal modifications | Dramatic, operatic influence | Contrapuntal integration (Jupiter) |
| Concerto | Simple ritornello | Growing solo-orchestra dialogue | Full dramatic dialogue; wind independence | K.595 — simplicity beyond complexity |
| Opera | Seria conventions | Idomeneo stretches seria limits | Da Ponte comedies transcend genre | Magic Flute invents new form |
| Finale | Light, rondo | More substantial | Equal to first movement | Jupiter fugal finale; Requiem |

## Critical Transitions

### Transition 1: The Sturm und Drang Awakening (1773)

Mozart hears Haydn's Op. 20 quartets and the "Sturm und Drang" symphonies. Minor keys suddenly become available as vehicles for genuine emotional intensity, not just conventional contrast.

| Before (pre-1773) | After (1773-75) |
|-------------------|----------------|
| Minor keys rare, used conventionally | G minor Symphony K.183 — personal tragedy in orchestral form |
| Harmony purely functional | Diminished 7ths, chromatic passing tones for emotional effect |
| Melody always charming | Melody can be agitated, driven, dark |

### Transition 2: The Mannheim/Paris Expansion (1777-79)

Exposure to the Mannheim orchestra and Parisian taste transforms Mozart's orchestral thinking. The death of his mother in Paris adds genuine grief to his emotional palette.

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| Standard 4-part string writing | Orchestral crescendo, dynamic contrast, wind independence |
| No clarinets | Clarinet enters the sonic vocabulary |
| Grief expressed conventionally | A minor Sonata K.310 — real anguish in keyboard writing |

### Transition 3: The Vienna Transformation (1781-82)

Breaking from Colloredo, discovering Baron van Swieten's Bach/Handel library, and composing for Vienna's sophisticated audiences together catalyze Mozart's full maturity.

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| Galant melody + simple accompaniment | Contrapuntal inner voices; fugal techniques integrated |
| Conventional wind writing | Winds as independent characters (Serenades, Concertos) |
| Entertainment music | Psychologically complex art (Figaro, K.465 "Dissonance") |

### Transition 4: The Darkening (1787-88)

Leopold's death, Don Giovanni's composition, financial collapse, and the three last symphonies written in six weeks mark the final transformation.

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| Darkness as contrast | Darkness as cohabitant — light and shadow simultaneously |
| Major keys dominant | G minor Symphony K.550 — tragic masterpiece in minor |
| Chromaticism as color | Chromaticism as structural force |
| Melody as aria | Melody as confession |

T:Mozart Darkening — G minor Symphony K.550 Theme

## Period Identification Guide

| If the music has... | It is likely... |
|-------------------|----------------|
| Clean galant style, parallel thirds, no chromaticism | Early (pre-1773) |
| Sturm und Drang minor, agitated rhythms, Haydnesque development | Salzburg (1773-81) |
| Piano concerto with wind dialogue, operatic themes, Alberti bass | Vienna Peak (1781-86) |
| Clarinet prominence, spare texture, chromatic darkness within simplicity | Final Period (1787-91) |
| Contrapuntal density + vocal melody simultaneously | Very late: Jupiter, Requiem, K.614 |
