# Joseph Haydn — Stylistic Evolution

"Which Haydn?" is the essential question. The composer of the early galant divertimenti is almost unrecognizable next to the creator of The Creation. Haydn evolved continuously across five decades, and the stylistic distance between his first symphony and his last is greater than the distance between Mozart's earliest and latest works.

---

## Period Overview

| Period | Years | Works | Character |
|--------|-------|-------|-----------|
| Apprentice/Early | 1750-1765 | Syms. 1-20; early quartets | Galant, light, conventional |
| Sturm und Drang | 1766-1774 | Syms. 44-52; Quartets Op. 20 | Dark, intense, minor keys, contrapuntal |
| Mature Classical | 1775-1790 | Syms. 53-92; Quartets Op. 33-64 | Witty, balanced, monothematic mastery |
| London | 1791-1795 | Syms. 93-104; late piano trios | Grand, public, expanded orchestration |
| Late Vienna | 1796-1803 | The Creation; Quartets Op. 76-77 | Sublime, visionary, distilled |

---

## Early Period (1750-1765)

The young Haydn absorbed the Viennese galant style — light, charming, entertainment music. These works are competent but rarely distinctive; the Haydn personality has not yet emerged.

| Feature | Early Characteristic |
|---------|---------------------|
| Form | Three-movement plan (fast-slow-fast); no minuet yet |
| Melody | Galant phrases; balanced, predictable 4+4 bars |
| Harmony | Simple I-V-I; few surprises; secondary dominants rare |
| Texture | Melody + accompaniment; violin I dominates |
| Orchestration | Small: strings + 2 oboes + 2 horns |
| Humor | Absent or mild; charm, not wit |

```abc
X:1
T:Early Haydn — Galant Style (Light, Balanced)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:G
%% Symmetrical, pleasant, predictable
!p!G2B2 d4|c2B2 A4|B2A2 G2F2|G4 z4|
```

## Sturm und Drang Period (1766-1774)

A dramatic transformation. Haydn turns to minor keys, angular themes, contrapuntal density, and emotional intensity. The cause is debated — personal crisis, aesthetic influence from literature, or simply artistic restlessness — but the result is unmistakable.

| Feature | Sturm und Drang Characteristic |
|---------|-------------------------------|
| Keys | Minor keys dominant: D minor, F minor, C minor, E minor |
| Mood | Passionate, turbulent, sometimes anguished |
| Texture | Counterpoint returns; fugal finales (Op. 20) |
| Rhythm | Driving, syncopated, aggressive |
| Dynamics | Extreme contrasts; ff outbursts |
| Form | Monothematic sonata begins to appear |
| Orchestration | Strings intensified; unison passages as dramatic gesture |

### Key Sturm und Drang Works

| Work | Key | Distinctive Feature |
|------|-----|-------------------|
| Symphony 44 "Trauer" | E minor | Relentless minor-key intensity; canon in slow movement |
| Symphony 45 "Farewell" | F# minor | Unprecedented key; musicians leave one by one in finale |
| Symphony 49 "La Passione" | F minor | Opens with slow movement; Baroque gravity |
| Quartet Op. 20/5 | F minor | Fugal finale in 2 subjects; counterpoint as emotional argument |
| Quartet Op. 20/2 | C major | Fugue in 4 subjects — contrapuntal tour de force |
| Piano Sonata Hob.XVI/20 | C minor | Beethovenian intensity decades before Beethoven |

```abc
X:2
T:Sturm und Drang Haydn — Fierce, Minor-Key Drive
M:4/4
L:1/16
K:Emin
%% Unison strings, driving rhythm, no escape
!f!E4E4 E4G4|B4B4 B4e4|d4^c4 d4e4|B8 z8|
%% Syncopated inner voice creates tension
!f!E4 z4 G4 z4|B4 z4 e4 z4|
```

## Mature Classical Period (1775-1790)

The Haydn the world knows: witty, balanced, endlessly inventive. The Sturm und Drang intensity has been absorbed and channeled into structural humor. Op. 33 ("written in a new and special way") marks the birth of the mature style.

| Feature | Mature Characteristic |
|---------|----------------------|
| Form | Monothematic sonata perfected; false recapitulations regular |
| Melody | Simple, folk-like, infinitely developable |
| Harmony | Wit through surprise: deceptive cadences, remote key detours |
| Texture | Four-voice conversation fully realized (quartet = 4 equals) |
| Dynamics | Subito contrasts as comedy: the structural joke is born |
| Humor | Central to the style; not decoration but substance |

### What Changed from Sturm und Drang

| Dimension | Sturm und Drang | Mature Classical |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Emotional mode | Intensity, darkness | Wit, light, balance |
| Minor keys | Dominant | Used for contrast within major |
| Counterpoint | Explicit fugal writing | Absorbed into texture (implied, not displayed) |
| Themes | Angular, dramatic | Simple, folk-like, developmental |
| Audience | The composer himself | Connoisseur listener who gets the jokes |

```abc
X:3
T:Mature Haydn — Wit and Surprise (Op. 33 style)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
%% Simple theme — almost too simple...
!p!C2E2 G2c2|B2A2 G4|
%% Expected continuation... but SURPRISE!
!f!_A,8|
%% Quiet recovery
!pp!G,2B,2 D2G2|F2E2 D4|C8|
```

## London Period (1791-1795)

Public concert life transformed Haydn's art. The London symphonies are grander, louder, more dramatic, and more virtuosic than anything before. The wit is amplified for large audiences; the orchestration exploits the full Classical palette.

| Feature | London Characteristic |
|---------|---------------------|
| Orchestra | Full Classical: fl, 2 ob, 2 cl, 2 bsn, 2 hn, 2 tpt, timp, strings |
| Slow introductions | Standard: Adagio/Largo curtain-raiser before every Allegro |
| Dynamics | Maximum range: ppp to fff; the Surprise Symphony lives here |
| Development | Longer, more adventurous; remote key explorations |
| Finales | Bigger, more complex; sonata-rondo form preferred |
| Public address | Themes are bolder; gestures bigger; effects unmistakable |

### London vs. Esterhazy Comparison

| Element | Esterhazy Symphonies (44-90) | London Symphonies (93-104) |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Opening | Direct Allegro | Slow introduction → Allegro |
| Theme character | Subtle, chamber-like | Bold, public, memorable |
| Development length | 30-50 bars | 50-80 bars |
| Orchestration depth | Strings + basic winds | Full woodwind choir + brass |
| Coda | Brief | Extended, may develop new material |

```abc
X:4
T:London Haydn — Grand Slow Introduction (Symphony 104 style)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Dm
%% Adagio: dark, imposing, mysterious
!f!D4 D4|!p!F2E2 D2^C2|D2F2 A2d2|!pp!c2_B2 A4|
%% Transition to Allegro (modulate to D major)
K:D
!p!A,4- A,4|
%% Allegro erupts
!f!D4 F2A2|d4 A4|
```

## Late Vienna Period (1796-1803)

The final Haydn: The Creation, The Seasons, the Op. 76 and Op. 77 quartets. A synthesis of everything learned — the wit of the mature period, the grandeur of London, and a new dimension: the sublime. The Creation's "Representation of Chaos" and the depiction of Light are music that transcends entertainment.

| Feature | Late Characteristic |
|---------|---------------------|
| Scale | Oratorio: massive choral + orchestral forces |
| Harmony | Richest yet: chromatic, colorful, sometimes audacious |
| Word-painting | The Creation: nature depicted in sound (sunrise, animals, storms) |
| Quartets | Op. 76: the summit of quartet writing; Op. 77: unfinished perfection |
| Spirituality | Religious conviction expressed directly (not just liturgical duty) |
| Economy | Maximum effect from minimum material; every note essential |

```abc
X:5
T:Late Haydn — The Sublime (The Creation, "Representation of Chaos" style)
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:Cm
%% Chromatic, searching, mysterious — nothing like early galant Haydn
!pp!C2_D2 E2F2|_G2F2 _E2D2|C2B,2 _A,2G,2|C,8|
```

| Dimension | Early (1750s) | Sturm und Drang (1770s) | Mature (1780s) | London (1790s) | Late (1800s) |
|-----------|--------------|------------------------|----------------|----------------|-------------|
| Complexity | Simple | Dense | Balanced | Grand | Distilled |
| Mood | Pleasant | Fierce | Witty | Spectacular | Sublime |
| Keys | Major | Minor | Both | Major + shadows | Chromatic |
| Themes | Galant tunes | Angular motifs | Folk simplicity | Bold gestures | Compressed cells |
| Form | Conventional | Experimental | Perfected | Expanded | Transcended |
