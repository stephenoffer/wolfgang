# Haydn Left-Hand Vocabulary & Phrase Breathing

Haydn's left hand has a sense of humour. It stops when you expect it to continue,
it enters when you expect silence, and it is often *thinner* than the music seems
to require. Generated "Haydn" is usually indistinguishable from generated Mozart:
a steady Alberti under a graceful tune. The difference is that Haydn is
constantly doing something slightly unexpected with the accompaniment, and that
he is willing to leave it out.

Shorthand reminder: each hand's bar sums to the meter. A sustained pedal under
figuration is a full-bar first event then the figure — one idiom among many.

## LH idiom catalog (adapt pitches to the harmony)

1. **Two-part texture, bass as a real line** — often just a single melodic line
   under the melody, no chords at all: `C3q D3q E3q F3q`. Haydn writes far more
   two-voice keyboard texture than Mozart, and it is the most under-used idiom in
   generated Classical writing.

2. **Oom-pah, dry** — `C2q [E3,G3]q C2q [E3,G3]q`. Present, but lighter and more
   often staccato than in Mozart. The dots matter: without them it is heavy.

3. **Alberti** — used, but Haydn tires of it faster. Two to four bars, then
   something else.

4. **The sudden rest** — `rest_h [G2,B2,D3]h`, or a whole bar of silence in the
   middle of a phrase. Haydn's signature. A joke needs a beat of nothing before
   the punchline.

5. **Repeated notes as a drone** — the same bass note pulsing under changing
   harmony above: `G2e ×8`. The rustic, folk-derived sound of the finales;
   suggests a drone instrument.

6. **Bare octaves for emphasis** — `[C2,C3]h` at the start of a phrase or a
   development, with nothing in between. Stark and attention-getting.

7. **Off-beat entries** — the accompaniment begins on beat 2 rather than beat 1:
   `rest_q [C3,E3,G3]q [C3,E3,G3]q [C3,E3,G3]q`. Displaces the whole bar's
   feeling without changing a note of the melody.

8. **Walking bass into a cadence** — quarters stepping down to the dominant over
   two bars. Straightforward, and it is what makes his cadences sound inevitable.

9. **Hand crossing / bass above the melody** — occasionally the left hand plays
   *above* the right for a bar or two. A colour effect, and unmistakably his.

10. **Unison passages** — both hands on the same line, often at the start of a
    development or after a fermata. Bare and dramatic in a way the galant style
    otherwise avoids.

11. **Held chord under a running right hand** — `[C3,E3,G3]w`. Simple, and it
    gives the right hand room to be virtuosic.

## The surprise as a technique

Haydn's characteristic gestures are *interruptions*:

- A phrase that should be four bars is five, because one bar is repeated.
- A cadence arrives, then arrives again, then a third time.
- The dynamic drops to `pp` for one bar in the middle of a forte passage.
- A general pause — everything stops for a beat or a whole bar.
- The music stops on a dominant and simply starts again somewhere unexpected.

At least one of these belongs in any Haydn-style movement. They are the
difference between the style and generic Classical.

## Where the idiom changes

- **Between the two halves of a period.** The consequent often has a different
  accompaniment from the antecedent — that is the variety Mozart gets from
  ornamenting the melody instead.
- **At a development.** Usually thinner and more contrapuntal, often two voices.
- **At a false recapitulation.** Haydn brings the theme back in the wrong key and
  in the wrong texture, then corrects himself.

## Breathing

More silence than any other Classical composer. Rests in the left hand at phrase
ends are the norm, not the exception, and a whole bar's silence in both hands is
a device he uses in nearly every fast movement.

## What to avoid

- **Unrelieved Alberti.** The commonest way to write Mozart by accident.
- **Perfect four-bar regularity.** Haydn's phrases are five bars, or seven, or
  four with the last bar repeated. Hyper-regular phrase rhythm is not his.
- **A texture that never thins to two voices.** He drops to a bare duet
  constantly, and it is the most economical way to make a texture change.
- **Solemnity.** Even the slow movements have wit in them. If nothing in the
  piece surprises, it is not this composer.
